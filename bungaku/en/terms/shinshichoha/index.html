What is Shinshichōha (New Thought School)?

Category
Literary Movement

A group centered on Akutagawa Ryūnosuke, Kikuchi Kan, Kume Masao, and Matsuoka Yuzuru, who gathered around the third and fourth incarnations of "Shinshichō" in the mid-Taishō era. Siding neither with Naturalism's flat depiction of things as they were nor with the Shirakaba school's optimistic idealism, they were also called the Shingenjitsushugi (New Realism), or Shingikōha (New Technicalist) or Shinrichōha (New Intellectualist) school, for their fondness for reworking reality through an intellectual eye and building short stories with a clear theme and skillful craftsmanship. Akutagawa, a disciple of Natsume Sōseki, dissected human psychology with a sharp eye in historical fiction drawing on the classics — "Rashōmon," "Hana" ("The Nose"), "Jigokuhen" ("Hell Screen") — while Kikuchi probed the realities of human life in plays and stories with clear-cut themes, such as "Chichi Kaeru" ("Father Returns") and "On to no Kanata ni" ("Beyond Forgiveness and Enmity"). It stood alongside the Shirakaba school as one of the mainstreams of Taishō literature, but effectively came to a close with Akutagawa's suicide in 1927, and Kikuchi went on to found "Bungei Shunjū" and become an elder statesman of the literary establishment.
