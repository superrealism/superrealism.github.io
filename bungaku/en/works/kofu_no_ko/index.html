About this work

A short story set at the construction site of a power station in a mountain gorge. Because the boring must be finished during the winter, while the river is low, the miners go on setting dynamite day and night even in a blizzard. If they meet the deadline the company gets a hundred thousand yen and the foreman thirty thousand — and what is consumed for that arithmetic is dynamite and the lives of the miners. Just after one of them lights the fuse, another goes down with an attack of his chronic illness. Hayama's own experience of working at civil engineering sites, in a dry hand that strikes at the chest in the last line.

Summary

The excavation for the power plant had reached fifty feet below the waterline. If it was not finished during the winter, they would have to wait another full year. Aiming for a bonus of a hundred thousand yen from the company to the crew, and thirty thousand from the crew to the foreman, the miners kept gnawing at the rock day and night, even through the blizzard. At six in the evening came the final blast of the shift. In the blizzard, with the laborers already withdrawn, Akiyama — ten years a miner — and Kobayashi — thirty years a miner — went to light the twenty-three fuses. But Akiyama, who had already been enduring a sharp pain in his lower back before the lighting even began, seemed wrong somehow. 'Hey! It's already half burned down!' Kobayashi urged him on, and right before his eyes, Akiyama sank down onto the hoisting path. Kobayashi hoisted him onto his back, but on a forty-five-degree slope hard enough to climb even alone, he could not run carrying another man. Before long, with a roar like machine-gun fire, shards of rock came raining down.

Even when suppertime came, the fathers did not return. Two boys, both ten years old, grew tired of waiting in the row house and dashed out to the construction site in their bare tabi feet. The compressor, the smithy, the substation — nowhere to be found. The two small shadows headed upstream, toward the hoisting shed.

Characters

  • Akiyama　A miner of ten years' standing, with six children. Struck by sharp pain in his lower back just before the blast
  • Kobayashi　A veteran miner of thirty years' standing. Hoists the fallen Akiyama onto his back and climbs the hoisting path
  • The two children　The ten-year-old sons of Akiyama and Kobayashi. Dash out to the construction site in search of their fathers

The opening (original text)

The excavation for the power plant had gone forward. It now reached fifty feet below the waterline. Three pumps worked on without a break, day and night, hot enough to scorch their own motors. The miners at the excavation now worked around the clock. At five in the morning, nine in the morning, noon, three in the afternoon, and six in the evening, tremendous blasting roars rose up from every quarter — the intake, the waterway, the power plant, the dam. Shards of rock the size of pickle-stone weights punched through the roof of a farmhouse several blocks away and came crashing down into its sunken hearth.

Kōfu no ko by Hayama Yoshiki (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Even so, the children, their small hearts pounding fit to burst, gasping for breath, went on trying to raise up the corpse. Had the men of the workers' camp not grown worried when neither the fathers nor the children came home, and come out searching for them, the boys would surely have gone on so stubbornly trying to lift that head, again and again, until they met the very same fate as their fathers. The hot-blooded laborers of the camp, seeing the children in the pitch-dark blizzard, straining with all their might to raise the corpse's head, every one of them wept.

Kōfu no ko by Hayama Yoshiki (Aozora Bunko)
