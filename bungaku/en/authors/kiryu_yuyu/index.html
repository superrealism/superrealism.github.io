Biography

Born in 1873 in Kanazawa, Ishikawa Prefecture; his real name was Masaji. After graduating from the law department of Tokyo Imperial University, he moved between the Shimotsuke Shimbun, the Osaka Mainichi Shimbun, and the Osaka Asahi Shimbun before being brought in as chief editorial writer of the Shinano Mainichi Shimbun in 1910. He later served as chief editorial writer of the Shin Aichi Shimbun before returning to the Shinano Mainichi in 1928. His 1933 editorial "Laughing at the Kantō Air Defense Grand Maneuvers" provoked fierce backlash from military figures, and under pressure from a boycott campaign led by the Imperial Reservist Association, he was forced to resign. After moving to Moriyama on the outskirts of Nagoya, he published a private magazine, "Tazan no Ishi" ("A Stone from Another Mountain"), and continued to write criticism of the military and the times, even as it was repeatedly banned. He died of cancer on September 10, 1941, just before the outbreak of the Pacific War. He is remembered as the journalist who alone kept resisting with his pen in an age when freedom of speech was being extinguished.
