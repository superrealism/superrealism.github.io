About this work

One of Akutagawa's important early court-period adaptations, published in the magazine Shin Shōsetsu in September 1916. Drawing on the Konjaku Monogatarishū and the Uji Shūi Monogatari, it follows a shabby fifth-rank samurai who has nursed one modest wish — to eat his fill of yam gruel — and then has it granted on an absurdly lavish scale. A wish long cherished loses its colour the moment it is met, and he understands that he was happier wanting it: the fine grain of desire and satisfaction, rendered lightly and ironically against a Heian background. Ranks with Rashōmon and The Nose among his early works.

Summary

The samurai of the Fifth Rank in the service of the regent Fujiwara no Mototsune is a middle-aged man, short, red-nosed and unimpressive, whom everyone about him looks down on. Mocked by his fellow samurai and even by the street boys of the capital, he never once loses his temper, and lives supported only by one small, long-held wish: to drink his fill of yam gruel. Yam gruel in those days was a sweet and costly dish served in small quantities at the great banquets, not a thing a man of his low standing could ever eat until he was full. One New Year, sipping what was left of the gruel after the banquet at Mototsune's mansion, he says to himself, "When shall I ever have my fill of this?" — and Fujiwara no Toshihito, a bold warrior who happens to be there, catches the words. If that is his wish, says Toshihito, he shall have his fill of it; and he carries the man off to his own house at Tsuruga. On the road he sees Toshihito take a wild fox and send it ahead as a messenger, and at Tsuruga, the next morning, mountains of yams and a great cauldron brimming with gruel are set out before his eyes. Faced with the very thing he had longed for so, he feels he could not get a single bowl of it down. The moment he knows he may eat until he is sick of it, the wish of years withers away, and he looks back instead, with fondness, on the man he had been until yesterday, who had not yet had his fill of yam gruel.

Characters

  • The Fifth Rank　A low-ranking samurai in the service of the regent Fujiwara no Mototsune. Unimpressive and despised, he lives on the single wish of having his fill of yam gruel. The hero of the tale
  • Fujiwara no Toshihito　A bold, open-handed warrior, who hears of the wish and serves the man all the yam gruel he can eat at his house in Tsuruga

The opening (original text)

It must have happened at the end of the Gangyō era, or the beginning of Ninna. In either case the period plays no great part in this story. The reader need only know that the background is the Heian court, in the far past. — At that time, among the samurai in the service of the regent Fujiwara no Mototsune, there was one of the Fifth Rank, a certain so-and-so.

The ending (original text)

Along with the relief of knowing that he need drink no more yam gruel, he felt the sweat that covered his face drying, little by little, beginning at the tip of his nose. Clear though it was, the morning wind at Tsuruga was cold enough to go through a man. The Fifth Rank hurriedly clapped a hand to his nose, and at the same moment gave a great sneeze into the silver pot.

Famous passages

But the thing the Fifth Rank had dreamed of—“to have my fill of yam gruel”—came true surprisingly easily.

A sentence announcing that the Fifth Rank's modest dream of eating yam gruel to his heart's content will come true in an unexpected way. It leads to the story's ironic ending: a dream fades the moment it is fulfilled.

Yet he was also the happy man who had cherished, all alone, the desire to have his fill of yam gruel.

A passage near the end, in which the Fifth Rank, having lost his appetite before a vast quantity of yam gruel, looks back fondly on himself when he still cherished that dream. It reveals that holding on to an unfulfilled wish was itself happiness.
