About this work

Outside the window she is pleading with him to open it. But the narrator has not the least intention of letting her into the room — a piece from Yamakawa Masao's series "Close Friends." A woman fresh from her bath snatches up the sheet of paper on the desk on which this cold monologue is being written, reads it, and screams: so you mean to kill me. A dry piece of comedy in which one misunderstanding turns out unexpectedly convenient.

Summary

At the black window she makes a noise, more and more strongly and stubbornly. She writhes and signals to me with her whole body. To come this far alone in the middle of the night must have taken a great resolve on her part. That I understand. But that is her own doing, her own egoism. It is no concern of mine. Tomorrow I shall find her cold body below this window on the hard concrete. — Call me heartless, abuse me as cold-blooded if you will. I have my own world. I do not want it disturbed by the likes of her. ... Just as he had written so far, he was suddenly slapped on the cheek. A woman with a bath towel wrapped round her chest is glaring at him with trembling lips. "What a self-satisfied creature! It takes my breath away." She had gone out of her way to call on him after work, and he writes as though she had forced herself in like some mad hussy. The woman picks up the paper on the desk, reads it again from the beginning, and cries out with her eyes growing wider and wider: "You mean to kill me!" This is the fifth floor; if she were pushed out she could not live. Screaming, the woman flees into the bathroom, locks the door, and begins to beg him not to kill her, saying she will never come again. He picks the paper up from the floor, smooths out the creases and writes on. Beat against it as much as you like. I shall not open the window. Not for anything in the world will I let a moth into my room. — But he stops his hand and considers. There is really no need to let the woman read this part and clear up her mistake.

Characters

  • He (I)　A man sitting at his desk, writing
  • The woman　A woman who works in a bar and has called at the flat on her way home

The opening (original text)

I raised my face. It was she, after all. Through the window her eyes are watching me as if pleading. She wants me to open it. At the black window she makes a noise. Little by little the sound grows stronger and more stubborn. She writhes and signals to me with her whole body.

Hijōna otoko by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

In short, it is enough simply to let her out of the room without a word. Then everything between them is finished. Perhaps this is what is called the turn of the tide, after all. He crumpled the scribbled sheet of paper in his hand. Watching the moth still beating its wings against the window, he threw the paper vigorously into the wastebasket. He threw the corridor door wide open, making a great noise on purpose.

Hijōna otoko by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)
