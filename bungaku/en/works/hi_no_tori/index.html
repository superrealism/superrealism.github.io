About this work

A long work by Dazai Osamu written in pursuit of the life of the actress Takano Sachiyo, and broken off with the single line "The Firebird, unfinished." It was included in the 1939 collection "Ai to Bi ni Tsuite" ("Of Love and Beauty"). The prologue depicts Sachiyo before she became an actress, and her meeting with Susuki Otohiko, a young man who shows her "happiness" for one night and then takes his own life. In this girl born in the mountains of Aizu, with the blood of the Byakkotai in her, who means to live without leaning on a man, is the model of the woman who runs through Dazai's later work.

Summary

The prologue tells of the actress Takano Sachiyo before she became an actress. In the autumn of 1930, Susuki Otohiko, a poor university student who has had a plain black haori made up at a secondhand clothes shop, stands in front of the Imperial Hotel. The girl with him is Sachiyo, nineteen years old. Otohiko tells a beautiful-faced young man that filial duty alone can be a purpose in life, that if one gets along quietly and modestly there are no devils in this world, and at parting leaves him with the words, "You must not imitate me." When the two of them are alone, Sachiyo says outright, "You are going to die, aren't you." She begs him to live, says she will do anything — and Otohiko answers, "It is no use." That night the two of them take a drug in a room at the hotel and are found cold, sitting side by side: Otohiko dead, the woman alive. From there the story goes back to Sachiyo's upbringing in the mountains of Ōu. Her great-grandfather was a physician and her grandfather one of the Byakkotai. Raised among mist and echoes by a mother of great dignity and a foster father who lived timidly with an unspoken wish to be a painter, the girl at last comes out into the world as an actress. The years that follow are piled up in fragments, and the book closes unfinished.

Characters

  • Takano Sachiyo　Born in the mountains of Ōu, she later becomes an actress
  • Susuki Otohiko　A poor university student. He takes the drug with Sachiyo, and only he dies
  • Kazue　A close friend of Sachiyo's, who speaks her mind without mincing words
  • Sukeshichi　One of the men around Sachiyo

The opening (original text)

The prologue sets down the actress Takano Sachiyo before she became an actress. This is a story of some time ago. Susuki Otohiko went into a secondhand clothes shop and said, have you a plain black haori here? "We have one in serge." It was the twentieth of October, 1930, and the leaves of the street trees of Tokyo were beginning to scatter in the wind. "Would serge still not look odd?" "However much colder it gets, a plain black one would never look odd." "Good. Let me see it." "Is it for you to wear yourself?" He had a square student's cap tipped back on his head and a student's uniform with the cuffs worn to rags. "It is." He threw the serge haori that was held out to him over the uniform. "Isn't it short?" He was a thin, lanky student of some five feet seven. "With a serge haori, a little on the short side is rather better." "Is that smart? How much is it?"

The ending (original text)

"I thought it would come to something like that. What a rise in the world. Come, take me there. Whose man is he? Sachiyo must not be allowed to do such a thing." He hailed a taxi and had it driven to Yodobashi. In the car he said: "She is a fool. A fool, a fool, an utter fool. I thank you. It was good of you to tell me." Kazue felt faint with a foreboding of something evil. "I love Sachiyo. I love her, love her, love her. I love her higher than anyone. I have never once forgotten her. I know better than anyone how hard it has been for her. I know all of it. She is a good person. She must not be allowed to rot. Fool, fool. To become a man's kept woman. Fool. Die! I will kill her myself." "The Firebird, unfinished"
