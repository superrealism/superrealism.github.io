About this work

Hamao Shirō's representative work, published in "Shinseinen" in 1929. The whole of it consists of a letter written by Shimaura Eizō, held in custody as the murderer of a beautiful woman, to his old and closest friend, the procurator Tsuchida Hachirō. The man he loved in his student days as an elder brother and as a lover; the self that was led by that eloquence and that logic into becoming "the devil's disciple." Why, having planned to kill his wife, was he found standing beside the body of his mistress? A masterpiece in letters, written from both sides at once — the inner life of the criminal and the logic of the law — by an author who knew the courtroom as procurator and as counsel.

Summary

"I, Shimaura Eizō, a prisoner awaiting trial, send this letter to Procurator Tsuchida, my old friend, who was once closer to me than a brother" — so begins the long letter a man whose name has filled the papers as the murderer of a beautiful woman writes to the old friend who is a procurator in the same courthouse. As students the two had been so close that the men in the dormitory called them "the pair." Tsuchida, three years the elder, was brilliant and eloquent, and Shimaura felt for him as for an elder brother and a lover, and believed everything he said was right. Then Tsuchida's heart turned to another beautiful youth, and the two parted abruptly. Cast off, and looking hard at himself, Shimaura writes that he saw through what sort of man Tsuchida was. You are a devil: it does not content you to eat a man's flesh, you cannot rest until you have dragged his soul down to hell — you are the man who makes a boy who has never tasted drink drink it and watches him suffer, and never touches the cup yourself. And then Shimaura sets down in minute detail how he came to resolve to kill his wife, and what preparations he made. But the arrest, when it came, was over the death of an entirely different woman. In the instant when he sees the corner of a medicine paper showing from the sash of the wife who comes to visit him, a lost memory comes back like a flash of light.

Characters

  • Shimaura Eizō　A prisoner awaiting trial, held as the murderer of a beautiful woman. The writer of the letter
  • Tsuchida Hachirō　A procurator of the district court. The old friend whom Shimaura loved in his student days as an elder brother and a lover
  • Tsuyuko　Shimaura's wife. Visiting him in custody, she unknowingly wakes her husband's memory
  • Ishihara Sueko　The woman found dead. Shimaura stands accused of her murder

The opening (original text)

1 To Tsuchida Hachirō, Procurator of the —— District Court. I, Shimaura Eizō, a prisoner awaiting trial, send this letter to Procurator Tsuchida, my old friend, who was once closer to me than a brother. Procurator, you remember me, of course. Even though I was examined by another procurator and then passed into the hands of the preliminary judge, my name has certainly been reported and sung in the newspapers as the murderer in that case of the beautiful woman which set the world talking; and since you are in the same courthouse, you cannot have failed to see my name in connection with this affair, nor to have heard of it.

The ending (original text)

But my legal responsibility for Tsuyuko I of course cannot escape, nor have I any wish to escape it. I am sorry to have troubled you to read so long a document. Tsuchida, for the third time I call upon you in the name of that friendship. Believe me, I beg you. And believe what I tell you.
