About this work

A short story Akutagawa published in Chūō Kōron in 1920. Set in Nanjing at the end of the Qing, it draws the sad and pure faith of Song Jinhua, a girl who lost her parents young and sells herself to live. Though she has caught a bad disease, Jinhua stubbornly refuses to sleep with foreign clients for fear of passing it on, and one night spends the night with a foreigner much like the Christ of the crucifix. In the morning her disease has strangely healed — a piece in which Akutagawa draws, in rich foreign colour, the faith that lodges in an innocent soul and the bitter irony hidden behind it.

Summary

Late Qing-dynasty Nanjing. Sung Chin-hua is a girl of fifteen or sixteen who, having lost her parents in childhood, lives by selling her body to survive. A devout Christian, she keeps a picture of the crucified Christ hung on the wall of her shabby room, and holds fast to a humble yet pure faith. At some point Chin-hua contracts a terrible disease, but believing that to give herself to a foreign customer would pass the sickness on to another, she stubbornly refuses no matter how much money is offered. Having resolved in her heart to take no customers until she is cured, one autumn night a foreign man comes to her whose face closely resembles the crucified Christ. Though they share no common tongue, their hearts meet, and Chin-hua, believing this must be Christ himself, come in another form to save her, spends the night with him. By the next morning the man has vanished, but Chin-hua's terrible sickness has, strangely enough, been entirely healed. Her face alight with radiant joy, Chin-hua believes without the least doubt that he truly was the living Christ. Later, a Japanese traveler who visits the town once again learns the true identity of that "Christ": a disreputable half-caste foreigner, afflicted with a malignant syphilis, who had abandoned the woman and, in time, gone mad. But he does not try to shatter Chin-hua's beautiful dream. A tale in which Akutagawa, with rich exotic color, depicts the faith dwelling in an innocent soul, and the bitter irony concealed behind it.

Characters

  • Sung Chin-hua　A girl of fifteen or sixteen living by selling her body in Nanjing. A devout Christian who refuses customers so as not to pass on her sickness
  • The Foreign Man　A customer whose face closely resembles Christ. Chin-hua believes him to be Christ's incarnation, but he is in truth a disreputable half-caste foreigner afflicted with syphilis
  • The Japanese Traveler　Visits Chin-hua again some time later and learns the truth of the "Christ," but keeps silent so as not to shatter her dream

The opening (original text)

I It was the middle of a certain autumn night. In a room of a certain house on Chi-wang Street in Nanjing, a pale-faced Chinese girl sat alone, resting her chin on her hand at an old table, listlessly cracking open watermelon seeds set out on a tray. On the table an oil lamp gave off a dim light. That light had more power to lend the room a gloomy, melancholy effect than to brighten it.

Nankin no kirisuto by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

When Chin-hua's story came to an end, he struck a match as though something had occurred to him, and began to puff on a fragrant cigar. Then, feigning great earnestness, he put this awkward question to her: "Is that so. How strange. But — but tell me, have you never once fallen ill again since?" "No, never once." Chin-hua, still gnawing on her watermelon seeds, answered without the least hesitation, her face alight with radiant joy.
