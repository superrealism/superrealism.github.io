Biography

Born in 1884 at the foot of Mount Haruna in Munetaka Village, Gunma Prefecture (now Takasaki City); his real name was Tsuchida Yakuto. He discovered literature at the Holy Trinity Seminary in Tsukiji, and after graduating worked as an evangelist for the Nippon Sei Ko Kai (the Anglican Church in Japan), writing poetry while being posted successively to Akita, Sendai, and Mito. With Hagiwara Sakutarō and Murō Saisei he founded the Ningyo Shisha (Mermaid Poetry Society) and kept in touch through the magazine "Kanjō" ("Feeling"). The radical linguistic experimentation of his second poetry collection, "Seisanryō Garasu" ("Holy Trinity Prism Glass"), went largely unappreciated in its time, and years of tuberculosis, unemployment, and wandering followed, but in his later years he turned to a poetic style celebrating nature and life in plain language. He is known for "Fūkei: Jungin no Mozaiku" ("Landscape: A Mosaic of Pure Silver"), which repeats the refrain "ichimen no nanohana" ("mustard flowers everywhere"), and for the posthumously published poetry collection "Kumo" ("Clouds"). He died in 1924 at the age of 40.
