About this work

A young lord who has lost a struggle within his house and been confined to a ruined mansion, and Denkurō, a petty thief who breaks in. Two men entirely unlike in rank and upbringing begin a strange life together in a leaking, derelict house. A novella in which the kindness of ordinary life that Yamamoto Shūgorō did best crystallizes in its clearest form; the parting scene is reckoned among the most affecting in all his work.

Summary

Having lost in a struggle over the succession of his house, and forbidden even to take his own life, the young lord Narinobu is confined to a ruined lower mansion, and, having lost all will to live, drifts listlessly through his days. One night, someone comes stepping through onto the wide veranda, its floor rotted through — a small, slight thief named Dengurō, brandishing a kitchen knife. He threatens, "Hand over the money," but there is nothing at all in the mansion to steal. Astonished, but learning of Narinobu's circumstances, Dengurō simply stays on, just as he is. He hauls in rice, mends the roof, works for Narinobu's sake, and so the two begin an odd shared life together. The young lord, who knows nothing of the world, is impressed by Dengurō's way of talking and his practical wisdom; and Dengurō, for his part, comes to know, for the first time in his life, the joy of being relied upon by another. A small light comes to glow in the ruined mansion, until the two of them come to think that they might spend their whole lives together like this. But one day, word reaches Narinobu that the state of affairs within his house has changed. A path has opened once more for him to take his place as a samurai. On the last night, Narinobu lays out a feast for Dengurō and bids him farewell. "Just as I want you to go on living as yourself, I've come to want to go on living as myself too." Leaving behind Dengurō, who clings to a post and weeps, "I wanted to stay with you, Nobu-san," Narinobu clenches his teeth and walks out through the gate. The west wind still blew strong, and the night sky was strewn thick with stars.

Characters

The opening (original text)

That sound was first heard from somewhere around the wide veranda. The veranda boards creaked, rather loudly. Narinobu reached out on instinct for the sword laid by his pillow, but the moment his fingers touched the scabbard, a feeling came over him — what does it matter now — and he drew his hand back. — Enough of this. Let it be however it wants to be. I'm sick to death of it all.

Dorobō to wakatono by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"So you're really going, then, Nobu-san," Dengurō's voice came chasing after him from behind. "I won't try to stop you anymore — go on and make something splendid of yourself... I'll be praying for you, so don't go falling ill... and someday, if you ever can, come and see me again, Nobu-san — I'll be waiting." Gritting his teeth, and pressing his hands, in spirit, over his own ears, Narinobu strode straight on, out through the gate. The west wind still blew strong, and the night sky was strewn thick with stars, as though scattered there. — Nobu-san, are you really going, Nobu-san. In Narinobu's ears, Dengurō's sorrowful voice went on ringing, without end.

Dorobō to wakatono by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
