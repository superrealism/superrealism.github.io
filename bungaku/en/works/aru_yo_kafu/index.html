About this work

A short story Nagai Kafū wrote after the war. Suetsuko, a girl of seventeen living with her elder sister's family, sits alone in the evening in the waiting room at Ichikawa station on the government line because she has nowhere else to be. A piece in which Kafū quietly draws the loneliness of a girl watching people waiting for others to come home, looking for a place to put herself.

Summary

Suetsuko goes into the waiting room at the government line's Ichikawa station and sits down. But she has no intention of going to Tokyo or anywhere else. She means only to sit there vacantly for a while. Ever since she began imposing on her elder sister's family this summer, whenever she grows despondent she has taken to slipping out on impulse, going into a cinema, walking through the black market, or resting in the station waiting room. In the waiting room her eye falls on young wives meeting husbands home from work, and men waiting for a woman to return. To Suetsuko, seventeen and with no experience of love, this is neither enviable nor distasteful; she only turns eyes with nowhere to settle toward these people. All that is in her heart is an aimless thought: "I don't want to stay at my sister's. Isn't there somewhere else I could put myself?" A quiet piece from Kafū's late years, capturing the loneliness of a girl with nowhere to go in a scene at a postwar station.

The opening (original text)

Suetsuko went into the waiting room at the government line's Ichikawa station and sat down on a bench. But it was not that she meant to go to Tokyo, or anywhere else. Just as one might sit down on a park bench or a stone by the roadside, she meant only to sit there vacantly for a while.

Aru yo by Nagai Kafū (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

After a while she stopped as if reluctant to go, but the man showed no sign of coming after her. From the top of the pine tree she had stumbled against earlier came the cry of an owl or some such bird. Suetsuko went home alone, downhearted.
