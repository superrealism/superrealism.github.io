About this work

Put on new clogs in the evening and a fox will get into you — a superstition heard at the clog shop leaves an unshakable unease in the small Bunroku's chest. A children's story from Niimi Nankichi's last years, in which not one fox appears. Few of his works draw so deep an affection out of nothing but the exchanges between a mother and her child.

Summary

Seven children were out walking on a moonlit night. Among them was little Bunroku, the child of Kiyosa, who ran the barrel shop — a thin, pale-skinned, spoiled child, shuffling along in his mother's oversized clogs. At his mother's request, the children had gone to a clog shop in Hongō to buy him a new pair. Just as Bunroku put on his new clogs, a bent-backed old woman came into the shop and said, "Well, well, whoever's child this may be, they do say that if you put on new clogs in the evening, a fox will possess you." "That's a lie," "It's just superstition," the children said to one another, but a look of worry still lingered on their faces all the same. The mistress of the clog shop, pretending to strike a match, touched it to the sole of the clogs and cast a charm over them: "There now — no fox or badger will possess you anymore." But even so, Bunroku's unease would not go away. That night, in bed, Bunroku confided in his mother: what should he do, he asked, if he really did turn into a fox? His mother answered quietly. In that case, she said, Mother would become a fox too. And when the hunter came, Mother would act as the decoy, and let herself be bitten and bound, so that while she was being held, Bunroku and his father could run away. "I don't want that, Mother, not that. If you do that, then there won't be any mother left at all." "But there's nothing else to be done. Mother will go along slowly, limping, limping..." — and as Bunroku sobbed, crying "I don't want it, I don't want it," clinging to her breast, his mother slipped a small pillow beneath him.

Characters

  • Bunroku　A spoiled child who puts on new clogs
  • Mother　Answers Bunroku's fears
  • Yoshinori　The oldest of the children, who looks after the rest

The opening (original text)

Seven children were walking on a moonlit night. Among them were both big children and small ones. The moon shone down from above. The children's shadows fell short across the ground. Looking at their own shadows, each child thought: what a big head I have, and how short my legs are.

Kitsune by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"But there's nothing else to be done. Mother will go along slowly, limping, limping..." "I don't want it, I don't want it, I don't want it!" Bunroku shrieked, clinging to his mother's breast. The tears came flooding out. His mother, too, secretly wiped the corners of her eyes with her nightgown sleeve, then picked up the small pillow that Bunroku had kicked away, and slipped it back beneath his head.

Kitsune by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)
