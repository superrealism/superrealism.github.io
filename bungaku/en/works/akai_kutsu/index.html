About this work

Andersen's tale of Karen, a girl whose heart is taken by a pair of red shoes, until at last she can neither take them off nor stop, and wanders the forest dancing. In Kusuyama Masao's version. Although it is a cautionary tale against vanity and pride, it contains the ferocious scene in which the shoes are cut off together with her feet, and stands out even among Andersen's works. Drawn upon again and again in later literature, film and ballet, it is known all over the world.

Summary

Karen, born in a poor house, who went barefoot in summer and in wooden shoes in winter, walked behind her mother's coffin in a pair of shoes of red cloth stitched for her by the shoemaker's wife. An old lady who saw her there took her in, dressed her in fine clothes and brought her up. When the time came for her confirmation, Karen chose a pair of red shoes very like those the princess had worn, and thought of nothing but the shoes even inside the church. One day an old soldier with a red beard says, "What fine shoes for dancing," and taps the soles — and the shoes begin to dance of themselves. Whether she means to go to the ball or to go back and nurse the old lady, her feet will not stop. Night and day, over fields and through woods, Karen dances on, and an angel in white appears before her and pronounces the punishment of the proud. At last she asks the executioner to cut off both her feet, red shoes and all, and lives on with wooden feet and crutches. Even so the red shoes dance before her and bar the church door. Working in the parson's house, and repenting from her heart, Karen comes at last, with the sunshine, to peace.

Characters

  • Karen　The girl whose heart is taken by the red shoes
  • The old lady　She takes Karen in and brings her up
  • The old soldier with the red beard　He taps the soles of the shoes and lays the curse of dancing on them
  • The angel　He appears in white and pronounces the punishment of pride
  • The executioner　At Karen's asking, he cuts off her feet together with the red shoes

The opening (original text)

There was once a little girl, very delicate and pretty; but in summer she had to go barefoot, for she was poor, and in winter she wore great wooden shoes, so that the little insteps of her feet turned quite red, and it was pitiful to see. In the middle of the village there lived an old shoemaker's wife. She sat and sewed, as well as she could, a pair of little shoes out of old strips of red cloth. They were clumsy enough, but they were kindly meant, and they were for the little girl. The little girl's name was Karen.

The Red Shoes by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"This too is God's mercy," said Karen. And the organ sounded, and the children's singing rang out sweet and gentle. The clear sunshine streamed warm in at the window onto the pew where Karen sat. And her heart was so full of sunshine, so full of peace and joy, that it broke. Her soul flew on the sunshine to God, and there was no one there who asked after the red shoes.
