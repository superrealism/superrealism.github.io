About this work

On a winter night, men gathered at the Meiji Club argue about the ideal and the real. Calling realism, which chases the wealth of the world, the beef party, and idealism, which chases dreams, the potato party, they set out their views of life — and then the young Okamoto stands up. What he really wants is neither success nor love, but simply to be astonished at the strangeness of this universe and this life. A central short story by Doppo, drawing in the light form of a debate the soul of a person standing between the ideal and the real.

Summary

On a winter night, six men gather in the dining room of the Meiji Club in Shiba, drinking and debating life. Before long the talk turns to whether one ought to choose "the ideal" or "the real," and in jest the company divides itself into the "Beef Party," who prize the real, and the "Potato Party," who prize the ideal. One man, who once crossed to the wilderness of Hokkaidō carrying an ideal within him, only to be crushed by harsh reality and abandon his dream, now champions the Beef Party, declaring that reality alone comes first. Another confesses that he once longed for love, for honor, for money, but that having gained them all, his heart was left unsatisfied. Just as the argument is running dry, the young Okamoto, who has sat listening in silence all this while, speaks up. There is, he says, one single wish he cannot help but long to see fulfilled. As the others hold their breath, what Okamoto confesses is neither wealth, nor success, nor love, but only this: that he wishes "to feel wonder at the mystery of this universe and this life." He wants to open his eyes once more in astonishment at the sheer strangeness of being alive at all — a strangeness that people, living out their days so placidly, take entirely for granted and pass over without a thought. At these words of Okamoto's, fitting neither idealism nor realism, the men laugh, and yet in his face they catch sight of a grave and hidden anguish.

Characters

  • Okamoto　A young man who longs to "feel wonder" at the mystery of the universe and of life. He can settle comfortably into neither idealism nor realism
  • Kondō　A friend who listens to Okamoto's wish, and catches sight of the anguish hidden behind his words
  • The men gathered at the club　They divide themselves into the Beef Party (realism) and the Potato Party (idealism) and debate life together

The opening (original text)

The Meiji Club too, by the moat in Sakurada-Hongō-chō in Shiba Ward, had a building in the Western style — not a very grand one, but a fairly substantial one all the same. The building still stands today, though its owner has changed, and the Meiji Club itself is no more. This is a tale from the days when the club still flourished. On a winter night of a certain year, lamps were burning, unusually, in the upstairs dining room, and now and then the sound of loud laughter leaked out into the street. As a rule, few people gathered at this club by night, so that even in the ordinary way, the smoke from the stove rose only by day. Yet though eight o'clock had already struck some while before, the men showed no sign at all of breaking up any time soon. Six rickshaws stood lined up beside the entrance, and the pullers, every one of them, seemed to be off in the back somewhere, deep in their usual game of dice.

Gyūniku to bareisho by Kunikida Doppo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"So it's only a thing you say, is it — heh, heh, heh, heh." "Is that so! Then it's only a wish you go about making, is it — ha, ha, ha, ha!" "A kind of hobby, after all, I suppose — ha, ha, ha, ha," Okamoto laughed along with the rest, but Kondō caught sight, in Okamoto's face, of a pain that could not be put into words.

Famous passages

“What I wish for is to be astonished.”

The unexpected wish Okamoto reveals amid the young men's debate over ideals and reality. It holds Doppo's own thought: to shake a heart grown used to habit and to be truly astonished at the world.
