About this work

A short story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Bessatsu Bungei Shunjū in 1964. A bracing piece in which Futago Rokubei, a young samurai with a reputation as a coward, carries out the ordered execution of the domain's finest swordsman by wit alone, without drawing his blade even once. Having volunteered for a duty everyone else was too afraid to take, Rokubei chases the fleeing swordsman and goes on shouting "murderer" wherever they go. By a stratagem that corners his man with shame and isolation rather than force, the weak brings the strong to his knees. Loved as a work that asks again, with humour, what courage and what strength are; later filmed.

Summary

Futago Rokubei, a young samurai of the Fukui domain in Echizen, has somehow — without any real incident behind it — come to be branded the biggest coward in the whole domain, a reputation even he himself has come to believe. Around this time, Nitō Kōken, the domain's swordsmanship instructor, feared as a master of the Heki school, cuts a man down and flees, and the domain must send someone to carry out the lord's order for his execution. But no one who knows the terrifying skill of Kōken's blade will come forward. Rokubei, hoping to clear away his name as a coward and to bring his sister Kane, who has already missed her chance to marry, a good match, volunteers for the task. Since he could never hope to beat Kōken by the sword, what Rokubei uses instead is a stroke no one expects. On finding Kōken, he makes no move to cut him down; instead he trails after him, and wherever people gather — at post towns, along the highway — he keeps shouting at the top of his voice, "A murderer — there's a murderer here!" Exposed to the eyes of the whole world, unable to lodge at any inn, unable to eat or rest in peace, Kōken, for all his skill with a blade, can find nothing to do about it; he wears down by degrees, driven by shame and isolation into a corner. With help along the way from a woman named Oyō, Rokubei at last brings Kōken to surrender without ever once drawing his sword. In place of a head, he cuts off Kōken's topknot to bring back, and so carries out the order for the execution splendidly — and, into the bargain, brings home a bride as well, so that his sister's match is happily settled too.

Characters

  • Futago Rokubei　A young samurai of the Fukui domain in Echizen, reputed to be a coward. He carries out an execution by wit alone, without ever drawing his sword. The protagonist
  • Nitō Kōken　The domain's finest swordsman, who cuts a man down and flees. He is gradually driven into a corner by Rokubei's unexpected stratagem
  • Oyō　A woman Rokubei meets along the road while carrying out his mission. She helps him
  • Kane　Rokubei's sister. Thanks to her brother's feat, she is at last blessed with a good match

The opening (original text)

Futago Rokubei was called a coward. There was no this-or-that fact behind it. Not a single person actually knew of any occasion on which he had shown cowardice, but somehow, without anyone quite noticing when, it had become the settled opinion of the whole domain, until even Rokubei himself came to believe he was a coward. —Since boyhood he had never once quarreled or come to blows with anyone, nor had he ever played at anything dangerous.

Hitogoroshi by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Sorry to trouble you," Rokubei said, "but cut off that topknot for me — I'll take it back in place of your head." "Can a topknot really stand in for a head?" "A raw head would only rot," Rokubei said. "And besides, I don't much care for killing people, or for watching them do away with themselves." To quote once more from the "Henjiroku": Futago Rokubei carried out the order for the execution with complete success, and, into the bargain, brought a bride home with him as well; and thanks to the high reputation this earned him, his sister Kane, too, was happily married off to Daihachirō, son of the domain elder Nakano.

Hitogoroshi by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
