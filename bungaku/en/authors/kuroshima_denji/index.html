Biography

Born in 1898 to a farming family on Shōdoshima, Kagawa Prefecture. He left Waseda University to enlist, served in the Siberian Intervention, and contracted a lung disease. The antiwar fiction born of that experience — "Uzumakeru Karasu no Mure" ("A Swarm of Whirling Crows") and "Sori" ("The Sleigh") — made him a leading writer of proletarian literature, and he was highly regarded as well for "Tongun" ("Herd of Swine") and "Nisen Dōka" ("A Two-Sen Copper Coin"), which depicted the poor farmers of his native place. His work is distinguished by a restraint of strident assertion and a dry touch that captures the lives of ordinary people. Suffering under repression and illness, he died in 1943 at the age of 44.
