About this work

A novella Ranpo serialized in 1931 and 1932 in Tantei Shumi, the supplement to his collected works, and elsewhere. Kitagawa Jiroemon, only son of a multimillionaire, has built an extravagant amusement park on the outskirts of the city of Y. In that strange pleasure garden suggesting a hell, a world of maddened beauty and cruelty unfolds. A piece overflowing with the dreamlike, aesthete imagination of Ranpo, akin to "The Strange Tale of Panorama Island."

Summary

In the southern part of M Prefecture lies Y City, an old-fashioned, gloomy castle town that seems all but forgotten by the world. On the outskirts of this sleepy town, there once lived a man who built an extravagant amusement park beyond all reason. He was the only son of the Kitagawa family, the foremost old house of Y City, said to be worth millions, and he bore the strange name Jiroemon. This eccentric, thirty-three years old, with both parents dead, no wife, and no living relations, poured his boundless fortune into building a bizarre pleasure garden suggestive of hell itself — "Jiro Park." Against the backdrop of this dreamlike amusement park, adorned with a mad, obsessive beauty and cruelty, one wildly aberrant event follows another. In the story's final scene, Jiroemon boards the gondola of a hot-air balloon together with a girl named Ayuko, and cuts the mooring rope to soar up into the sky. Shaking off the pursuing police, he scatters showers of colored paper streamers, shouting banzai as he bids his final farewell to the ruins of his beloved Jiro Park. The balloon ascends endlessly into the heavens, until at last, like a small fish, it vanishes into the depths of the blue sky. A tale steeped in the world of dream and decadent beauty so characteristic of Ranpo, akin in spirit to "The Strange Tale of Panorama Island."

Characters

The opening (original text)

In the southern part of M Prefecture there is a city called Y — an old-fashioned, gloomy town that seems all but forgotten by the world. It is not a place of thriving commerce or industry, nor does it lie along any major route of transport; it is only that, having once been a castle town under the old shogunate, its population remains large enough for it to just barely retain the shape of a city. On the outskirts of this sleepy town of Y, there once lived a man who built an amusement park quite beyond all reason.

Jigoku fūkei by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Jiroemon and Ayuko, leaning their bodies half out of the gondola, threw down every last one of their remaining paper streamers to the ground below, and, their voices joined together, shouted banzai as they bade their final farewell to the ruins of their beloved Jiro Park. The balloon went on rising, endlessly, into the heavens. Piercing through one white cotton cloud after another, it dwindled into the shape of a small fish, its trailing tassels of colored streamers looking just like that fish's fins — joyfully, so joyfully, growing smaller and smaller, until at last it grew faint as a mote of dust, and vanished into the fathomless depths of the blue sky.
