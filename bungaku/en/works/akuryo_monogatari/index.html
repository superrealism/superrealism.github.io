About this work

The opening piece, written by Edogawa Ranpo, of a project continued in relay by three authors. The novelist Ōe Randō visits a doll-maker's workshop to write about it, and is drawn into the strange world surrounding the old craftsman. The scrawl on a scrap of paper slipped into Randō's pocket at the end is itself the handing-over to the next writer. Ranpo's own note tells us that the development was written by Kadota Kikuo and the solution by Yamada Fūtarō.

Summary

The novelist Ōe Randō found himself obliged to write about the workroom of a maker of dolls. He looked into the Britannica and the Americana and the great encyclopedias, but such concrete matters were not to be found there. So he telephoned the tailor in the Ginza where he always has his clothes made and asked where the mannequins displayed in the front of the shop came from — and from there the story begins to move. Following the introduction he is given, Randō calls at the workshop of an old dollmaker with an air about him that is not that of ordinary men. Dolls that copy human skin exactly; hands and faces uncannily lifelike. Having gone in meaning only to gather material, Randō gradually perceives that the relations between this old man and the people about him are not ordinary. ... Coming out after the visit, walking through the dark streets toward the tram station, Randō remembers the touch of a hand upon him as he left. Struck by a thought, he puts his hand into that pocket and finds a small scrap of paper. Hurrying to a street lamp to examine it, there is a scribble in pencil: "This old man is a great villain. Help me. I shall be killed." The opening breaks off here, and the rest is left to the next writer.

Characters

  • Ōe Randō　The novelist who calls on the dollmaker to gather material
  • The old dollmaker　A master of dolls with an air about him that is not that of ordinary men

The ending (original text)

Hurrying to a street lamp and examining the scrap of paper, there was scribbled in pencil the following: "This old man is a great villain. Help me. I shall be killed." [Note] This too was published all at once; the development was written by Mr. Kadota Kikuo and the solution by Mr. Yamada Fūtarō.
