About this work

A Boy Detectives story serialized in Shōnen Kurabu in 1957. It begins with the appearance of the skeleton gentleman, dressed all in grey, and moves to a circus, where the fiend and the Boy Detectives face each other. Members such as young Inoue Ichirō and Noro Ippei — "Noro-chan" — have their moments. A favourite of the series.

Summary

One evening the famous pair of the Boy Detectives Club, the strong Inoue Ichirō and the timid but likable Noro-chan (Noro Ippei), are walking through a lonely quarter of villas in Setagaya when a strange gentleman in a gray overcoat and a gray soft hat comes toward them — and that is their meeting with the dreadful "Skeleton Gentleman." The case then develops with a circus for its stage, and a battle of wits is fought between the fiend with his disguises, the Boy Detectives Club under young Kobayashi, and the great detective Akechi Kogorō. At the end of an adventure of one crisis after another, the boys defeat the fiend at last. "Three cheers for Mr. Akechi! Three cheers for the great detective Akechi!" — at the boys' shouting, and their jumping for joy, even the stern policemen cannot help breaking into smiles, and their laughter rings out through the quiet town. A volume of the Boy Detectives series, splendid for the exploits of the boys.

The opening (original text)

One evening the famous pair of the Boy Detectives Club, Inoue Ichirō and Noro-chan, were walking through a lonely quarter of villas in Setagaya ward. Today it was Inoue who had gone to play at Noro-chan's house, and Noro-chan was seeing him home. As the two were walking through the lonely street, with long concrete walls running along both sides, a gentleman appeared at a corner far ahead and came walking toward them. In a gray overcoat, with a gray soft hat on his head, he came trotting along, leaning on a stick.

Sākasu no kaijin by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Three cheers for Mr. Akechi! Three cheers for the great detective Akechi!" They shouted it, all but jumping into the air. Hearing it, even the stern policemen could not help breaking into smiles, and their laughter rang out through the quiet town.
