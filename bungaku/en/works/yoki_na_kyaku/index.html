About this work

"Did you know Nakai Tensei is dead?" — the life of a certain drinker, told over cheap distilled spirits. Against the disorder of the postwar years, the history of a man who drifted among drink, debts and manuscripts is told at length in the first person to a listener. An unusual contemporary piece for Yamamoto Shūgorō, with the bitterness of being alive hidden under a voice wrapped in self-mockery and humour.

Summary

Do you know that Nakai Tensei is dead? — that drunkard Nakai Tensei; you cannot possibly not know him — so the narrator begins, and tells his memories over a drink. Mixing in verses like "Seven days and seven nights without drinking wine, / without hearing the lyre that Apollo plays," the talk goes back to the days he spent with Tensei. ... In the editorial room, Fukuchi the manager is asleep face down, making a pool of water on his desk. The amiable Mr. Taguchi comes about once in three days and goes away saying, "Well then, Mr. Nakai, I leave it to you." The narrator did everything except write manuscript — because, he says, happily he had neither the skill nor the judgment to write such things. In time, on pay day, he is told flatly by the president that he is dismissed, and is struck with disappointment and astonishment. Soothed in the Kansai speech — "You may have your complaints, but there are things I would say on my side too" — he left the place. And so he parted from Tensei as well. What told him that Tensei was dead was a letter from that same amiable Mr. Taguchi Shirō. Life is hard either way; if one is to suffer, let it be over something real of one's own — and he says he blurted out such a thing, like a piece of affected moralizing, and is embarrassed by it. By the way, have you a plot for a light entertaining story of about eighteen pages? There is my wife's confinement to think of, too — and just as he begins to say it, the rotgut is finished.

Characters

  • I　The man who tells the story over a glass of rotgut
  • Nakai Tensei　The drunkard who has lately died
  • Taguchi Shirō　An amiable man, who sends the news of Tensei's death
