Biography

Born in Ushigome, Tokyo, in 1881. He crossed to Shanghai in 1913 and for more than a decade thereafter went about investigating Chinese manners and customs. Known as a scholar of China, who founded the magazine "Shina Fūzoku" among other ventures, he then turned to introducing the new literature of China. In 1932 he produced for the Kaizōsha publishing house a translated "Complete Works of Lu Xun," making it possible to read together in Japanese such short stories as "The True Story of Ah Q," "My Old Home," and "A Madman's Diary." Lu Xun himself pointed out errors in the translation, but the reception of Lu Xun in Japan began with this work. He died in 1950.
