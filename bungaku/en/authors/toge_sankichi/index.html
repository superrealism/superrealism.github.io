Biography

Born in 1917 in Toyonaka, Osaka Prefecture, and raised in Hiroshima. Frail from childhood, he contracted pulmonary tuberculosis after graduating from Hiroshima Commercial School and wrote tanka, haiku, and poetry through repeated hospitalization and convalescence. On August 6, 1945, he was exposed to the atomic bombing at his home in Hiroshima. Afterward, suffering himself from radiation sickness, he threw himself into the peace movement and into writing poetry, and in 1951 published "Genbaku Shū" ("Poems of the Atomic Bomb"), known for its preface beginning "Chichi o kaese, haha o kaese" ("Give me back my father, give me back my mother"). As one of the earliest poetry collections to indict the horror of the atomic bomb, its words are still carved today on a monument in the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park. He died in 1953 during surgery, at the age of 36.
