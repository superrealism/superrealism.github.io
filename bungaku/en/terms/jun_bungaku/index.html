What is Jun Bungaku (Serious Literature)?

Category
Form / Genre

A term for literature, above all fiction, that places weight on artistic value over entertainment. In the Meiji era it referred broadly to literature aimed at beauty, as against writing for scholarly purposes, and Kitamura Tōkoku used it in that sense as well, but once popular fiction, which won a mass readership from the end of the Taishō era into the Shōwa era, rose to prominence, the word settled into its present meaning as a term distinguishing serious literature from it. Its core is the tradition of the I-novel and the shinkyō shōsetsu (state-of-mind novel), which dig into the author's own real life, with literary magazines such as "Shinchō," "Chūō Kōron," "Kaizō," and "Bungakukai" serving as its venues. With the establishment of the Akutagawa Prize in 1935 for serious literature by unpublished and emerging writers, and the Naoki Prize for popular fiction, the two categories became still more sharply defined. Even so, this distinction was originally peculiar to Japan, and its boundary has continually shifted. After the war, middlebrow fiction, occupying the ground between the two, spread widely, and in 1961 Hirano Ken argued that the very concept of serious literature was merely a historical one, touching off the "pure literature debate" that drew in Itō Sei, Takami Jun, and others.
