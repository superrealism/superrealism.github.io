About this work

On a night of violent wind and rain, Tanaka, left alone to mind a large house in the suburbs, hears a scream and angry voices from the direction of the gate — a very short piece Hashimoto Gorō published in Tantei Club. The next morning he searches the ground round the house minutely for bloodstains and dropped articles, and turns a suspicious eye on the stout master of the house opposite. The thoroughly ridiculous end he arrives at, after three hours of watching.

Summary

The rain came in sheets against the windowpane like lumps of flesh, and with every shudder of the house the electric light went out. Remembering that the dreadful scene in the story he was reading had happened on just such a night, Tanaka mutters, "Something will happen on a night like this." In this wide house in the suburbs he is minding the place tonight entirely alone. ... And then, somewhere about the garden, he certainly heard something like a scream. "Damn you!" in a deep man's voice. About the gate there is a thudding in the earth, mixed with the rain and the wind, as of something being fought over. A sharp shriek, a heavy thudding, a woman's scream. "I'll kill you!" "You dog!" Something heavy seemed to strike the board fence. At the instant when he thought, they have killed him, the light went out, and this time it did not come on for some while. His knees shook, and the inside of his thighs was oddly cold. ... The next morning the sun was shining. Tanaka goes round the whole house from the gate, examining. In rain like that the blood must have been washed clean away. But what have they done with the body? Is there nothing left behind? — When the master of the house opposite speaks to him and he tells him about the night, the man will not take it seriously: "Now that you mention it, I certainly heard something too. But surely not a murder...." Tanaka turned a doubtful eye for a moment on that oddly plump body. Nobody knows the man's trade, and there is a rumor that he was once knocking about Shanghai. He stood inside his gate and glared at the house opposite for three whole hours.

Characters

  • Tanaka　The man who was minding the house alone on the night of the storm
  • The master of the house opposite　An oddly plump elderly man whose trade nobody knows

The opening (original text)

It was a terrible storm. When a gust came roaring, one felt as though the ceiling would be blown away. The rain came in sheets against the windowpane like heavy lumps of flesh, and having struck, ran down it in a waterfall. With every shudder the electric light went out. Now and then the sound of a tram was heard, but each time it seemed to him an emergency whistle after some accident. Something was flapping, and outside the window it was dark past all sounding.

Shōkyoku by Hashimoto Gorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Curse it — it was past eleven in that storm, and when I got this far the cart went down into this hole, and the horse would not move for anything. Shout at him or do what I liked, it was no use, so I had to beat him hard with the end of the rope, poor beast; and every time I struck him he squealed and staggered, and he went into the wall of this gentleman's place, and I was afraid we should break the wall down...." When the two men in their happi coats had gone away, Tanaka slipped out of the gate and went to look at the hole. It was certainly the hole into which a horse-cart had fallen.
