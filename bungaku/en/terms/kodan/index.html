What is Kōdan?

Category
Other

A traditional Japanese art of oral storytelling in which a performer recites war tales, tales of martial valor, and accounts of political affairs to a rhythmic beat. Originally derived from "kōshaku," the lecturing on war chronicles and the like, the performer strikes the lectern with a folding fan while vigorously narrating battles, vendettas, and the exploits of chivalrous outlaws. It was a popular entertainment at variety halls from the Edo period into the Meiji era, and many shorthand transcripts of performances were published. In particular, the "Tachikawa Bunko" series, published from 1911 by the Tachikawa Bunmeidō in Osaka, thrilled boys with tales of ninja and swordsmen — including "Sarutobi Sasuke" and stories of the Ten Braves of Sanada — based on the performances of the kōdan storyteller Tamada Gyokushūsai, and became a great wellspring of popular reading matter in the Taishō and early Shōwa eras. The outlandish yet thrilling world of kōdan storytelling was carried forward into popular period fiction, beginning with Yoshikawa Eiji's "Miyamoto Musashi," and, alongside rakugo, the art of oral storytelling played a great role as the soil in which modern popular literature grew.
