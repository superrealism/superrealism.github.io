About this work

A very short piece published in 1961 by Yamakawa Masao, called a master of the short short. A man who believes he has the power to foresee good and ill fortune is riding a bus with his wife up a mountain road when a shudder runs down his spine and he becomes certain the bus will go over the edge, and gets off over everyone's objections. The ironic ending is prepared brilliantly within a few pages. A central work of his short shorts, drawing the absurdity of fate and foreknowledge with a sharp final turn.

Summary

The man believed he possessed the power to foresee good and bad fortune. Before anything happened, a numbing shiver running down his spine would announce the outcome in advance. School examinations, the test for his job, even his meeting with his wife — this premonition had always proven true. On the day he and his wife rode a large bus climbing a road cut into a mountainside, a shiver like a warning bell had been sounding in his back for some time already. Certain that "this bus is going to fall," he desperately argues with his wife, who laughs it off, and negotiates with the sullen driver, and the two of them get off the bus. The other passengers watch, puzzled, as the young couple insists on walking the rest of the way. The bus rounds a curve, and the two figures vanish behind the slope of the cliff. That evening's paper reported this: a sightseeing bus had lost control of the wheel near Kozaru Pass and gone over, but since it fell only to the road one level below and stopped there, none of the passengers died. However, a young married couple walking on that lower road were crushed beneath the bus and killed instantly. This couple, it was reported, had gotten off that very bus only moments before. The premonition had indeed come true. A masterful piece of short-short fiction that carves out, with brilliant clarity, the irony of fate.

The opening (original text)

On the slope of a small mountain separated by a deep ravine, fresh green had begun to stand out here and there, and the shadows cast by several clouds moved across the mountainside, forming patterns of light and dark. Near and far, the brown undulations of the early-spring mountains stretched on in a continuous line, and the contrast between them and the bright, fresh, deep blue sky was, if one wished to call it so, beautiful, and, if one wished to call it so, gentle — but he was in no state to appreciate any of it.

Yokan by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"—At around two o'clock this afternoon, a large sightseeing bus operated by ×× Kankō lost control of the wheel near Kozaru Pass and went over the edge. Fortunately it fell only to the road one level below and came to a stop there, so there were no fatalities among the passengers. However, a young married couple walking on the lower road were crushed beneath the bus and killed instantly. This couple had gotten off that very bus only moments before."
