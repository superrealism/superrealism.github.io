About this work

A historical story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shōsetsu Shinchō in 1964. Kunugi Kōbei is troubled over how to deal with a certain scandal. Which is right, and which takes more courage — burying the matter in the dark to keep up appearances, or facing it squarely as a human being? A meditative piece asking what real judgment is.

Summary

Karita Sōhei spoke smoothly. It was exactly like a painter working up a picture he was skilled at, without the slightest hesitation. Listening to him, Kunugi Kōbei thinks, "Just like it was with Tateoka." Over the handling of a certain "scandal," Karita puts a certain request to Kōbei. "I cannot do it. There is nothing for it but to say it is impossible," Kōbei says, giving a faint shake of his head. A matter that might well tarnish a samurai house's name and standing — which is right, and which takes more courage, to fear it and try to bury it in the dark, or to face it squarely as a human being? Long after he has gone to bed, Kōbei goes on calling his own past to mind. "Have I really been so cold, so bound up in mere form, all this while?" And he arrives, at last, at a certain resolve. "I will do something for Karita Sōhei's sake. Punishment alone is not the whole of judgment, after all — tomorrow I'll go to Utsubochō and talk it over." Even as he pictures, in his mind, the sharp tongue of the stern councillor Etsukawa, Kōbei quietly smiles to himself. A meditative piece from Yamamoto's last years, asking what true judgment is, and what real, human courage is.

Characters

Karita Sōhei spoke smoothly. It was much like watching a painter draw a picture he was skilled at. Every line, every point, and even the shading and the order of coloring, seemed to come together fluently, without the slightest error or hesitation.
