What is Shinshōsetsu?

Category
Society / Group

A literary magazine published by the Shun'yŎdō company. It was founded in January 1889 by Aeba Kōson and others (its first incarnation), and, after a hiatus, was relaunched in July 1896 under the editorship of Kōda Rohan (its second incarnation). Thereafter, for thirty years, it remained, alongside "Bungei Kurabu," a leading literary magazine of the Meiji and Taishō eras and a principal stage for writers. It published Izumi Kyōka's "Kōya Hijiri" ("The Holy Man of Mount Kōya," 1900) and "Uta Andon" ("The Song Lantern," 1910) and Natsume Sōseki's "Kusamakura" (1906), and in 1907 it published Tayama Katai's "Futon" ("The Quilt"), setting the direction of Naturalist literature. In the Taishō era it also sent Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's "Imogayu" ("Yam Gruel") and Arishima Takeo's "Kain no Matsuei" ("The Descendants of Cain") into the world, but it ended its run under this title in November 1926, and after being retitled "Kurōshio" ("The Black Current") in 1927, it ceased publication before long.
