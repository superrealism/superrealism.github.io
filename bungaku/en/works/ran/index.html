About this work

A historical story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Ie no Hikari in 1948. Suzukawa Shōnosuke, Heizaburō's close friend, promised to settle the matter peaceably, and then cut down Tōroku and killed himself. A single orchid was in the letter box he left behind — a fragrant piece drawing how deep his friend's hidden resolve had been.

Summary

At dusk on an autumn day, Heizaburō, gazing out the window at the somber cedar grove, the leafy thickets of female bamboo, and the sound of quail rising in flight, murmurs to himself, "Yes, I'll meet him and say it plainly" — that impression stayed vivid in his mind for a long time after. His close friend Sugawa Ikunosuke had promised Heizaburō, with a quiet, confident expression, that he would surely settle a certain dispute peaceably. But — Ikunosuke cuts down Tōroku, and then takes his own life. A voice groaning, "Ikunosuke, Ikunosuke..." Heizaburō's shock was beyond words. Had those words been meant to reassure him? Had Ikunosuke already resolved on death, even then? Had he himself simply failed to understand the depth of his friend's thinking? Heizaburō presses his fists into his knees. Just then, a letter box arrives, brought by an urgent messenger from his home province, addressed from Ikunosuke to Heizaburō. Undoing the wrapping cloth and opening the lid of the box, a refined, elegant fragrance strikes Heizaburō's face. Inside, a single orchid blossom lay quietly, all alone. A piece of Yamamoto's, drawing, richly fragrant, the depth of a friend's hidden heart, entrusted not to words but to a single flower.

Characters

  • Heizaburō　Ikunosuke's close friend

The opening (original text)

The autumn sun had already set. Heizaburō, sitting at his desk with brush still in hand, lost in thought, noticed the paper door growing dim and shadowy, and, setting down his brush, rose quietly and opened the window.

Ran by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"It is not usually permitted within the castle, but his lordship has granted his leave, so I bring it to you," said the messenger, holding out a letter box wrapped in a cloth. "An urgent courier brought this from the home province — a letter from Sugawa Ikunosuke, addressed to you." Heizaburō slid forward on his seat and quietly undid the wrapping cloth. As he opened the lid of the box, scattered with the Sugawa family crest, the refined fragrance held within struck his face. ...Inside, a single orchid blossom lay quietly, all alone.
