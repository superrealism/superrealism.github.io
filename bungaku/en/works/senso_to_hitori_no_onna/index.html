About this work

If the war is going to smash everything, then let us make ourselves a wreck now — a novella by Sakaguchi Ango drawing the days under the air raids of a man and a woman who begin living together on that agreement. A strange disappointment that the war has ended after all runs through this work, written the same year as "Discourse on Decadence."

Summary

During the war, Nomura lived together with a woman. Theirs was the same arrangement as husband and wife, but she was not his wife. It had been understood between them from the start: since the war was bound to end in defeat and everything would be smashed to pieces anyway, that very ruin was the true shape of their bond, and neither of them held anything like domestic affection for the other — so it is explained. The woman had run a small bar and had also been a kept mistress; she was, by nature, a promiscuous woman. When sake grew hard to come by and running the bar became difficult, she gave it up without a second thought and moved in with Nomura. "If you get sick of it, you can just leave" — that was what Nomura told her from the very start, when he took her in. Their life together is measured out in air-raid sirens. Incendiary bombs fall, the house next door burns, they flee in confusion, and once the fire is out, they sit down and drink tea. The woman takes no proper joy of the flesh in love, and Nomura, for his part, looks at her only on the premise that he might well be dead by tomorrow. It is precisely because their ruin is certain that the two of them can remain exactly as they are. But then the fire next door dies down, and their own house is left standing, unburned. And the war ends. The air raids stop, the sirens fall silent, and Nomura says, "It hardly seems possible, does it." If ruin is no longer coming, what is to become of this relationship? Nomura begins to wonder whether he still has it in him to simply go on living with her, turning a blind eye to her selfishness. "Let's not talk about the war anymore," the woman says, irritation showing in her voice, and turns over abruptly, lying flat on her back, and closes her eyes, murmuring, "Let it turn out however it turns out." Has the war really ended? — Nomura, gazing at the woman's body, went on thinking, his mind growing only colder and clearer as he did.

Characters

  • Nomura　A man who lives with a woman on the premise of coming ruin
  • The woman　Formerly the owner of a bar. A promiscuous woman without desire for anything more

The opening (original text)

During the war, Nomura lived together with a woman. Theirs was the same arrangement as husband and wife, but she was not his wife. For it had been understood between them from the start: since the war was bound to end in defeat and everything would be smashed to pieces anyway. The ending (original text)

"Let's not talk about the war anymore." A note of irritation showed in her voice. The woman turned over abruptly and lay flat on her back. "Let it turn out however it turns out." She closed her eyes. It was a small, fresh, appetizing body. Has the war really ended? — Nomura, devouring the woman's body with his eyes, went on thinking, his mind growing only colder and clearer as he did.
