About this work

An editorial the chief writer of the Shinano Mainichi Shimbun, Kiryū Yūyū, wrote in August 1933. He dismissed the large-scale air-defence exercise carried out across the Kantō region as no more than a puppet show, and declared that to meet enemy aircraft over the capital at all was already defeat. It brought down the anger of the military, and Yūyū was driven out of the paper.

Summary

In August of the eighth year of Shōwa (1933), a large-scale air-defense exercise was carried out across the skies of the Kantō region, and its progress was broadcast nationwide by radio. Two days later, Kiryū Yūyū took the exercise head-on in an editorial. Were this real warfare, he argued, even the total mobilization of our own aircraft could not shoot down every enemy plane. If two or three slipped through and reached the skies over the imperial capital to drop their bombs, Tokyo — a city of mostly wooden houses — would be reduced to scorched earth in an instant. However much people might tell themselves to stay calm and composed, the instinct of terror could not be overcome when the moment actually came; citizens would flee in panic, fires would break out everywhere, and scenes of horror like those of the Great Kantō Earthquake would surely recur. And so, Yūyū writes, to meet the enemy's planes in the skies of Kantō, in the skies over the capital itself, is in itself nothing less than our own military's defeat. The enemy ought to be repelled out at sea, along the way; any operational plan that assumes, from the very outset, an interception over the capital's own skies is simply absurd. The exercise, however spectacular a sight, is in the end nothing more than a puppet show, he adds — and once infrared technology comes into military use, even blackout measures will lose their meaning. This essay, which would prove an almost exact prophecy of the firebombing of Tokyo twelve years later, provoked a boycott campaign by the Imperial Reservists' Association, and Yūyū was forced to leave the Shinano Mainichi Shimbun. Afterward, from the outskirts of Nagoya, he published his own personal magazine, Tazan no Ishi, continuing to criticize the military until just before his death.

The opening (original text)

Air-defense exercises have been held before, once in Osaka as well — but the Great Kantō Air-Defense Exercise now under way since the ninth of this month, the day before yesterday, was carried out, true to its name, across the skies of the Kantō region encompassing the whole area around Tokyo, and the number of aircraft taking part in it was very great indeed — a truly large-scale affair. And since this exercise was broadcast nationwide through AK, not only the citizens of Tokyo but the entire nation must have foreseen, and felt with keen force, how immense the damage, and how utterly indescribable the horror, would be were this ever to become real warfare.

Kantō bōkū daienshū o warau by Kiryū Yūyū (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

From this standpoint too, the blacking-out of lights among the citizens, throughout the city, is nothing but a piece of pure absurdity. In short, as the Zeppelin raids on London during the European war made plain, aerial warfare is a matter in which whoever strikes from the air wins, and whoever is struck from the air loses. And so repelling the attacker before that strike can ever be delivered must be the very first principle of air defense.

Kantō bōkū daienshū o warau by Kiryū Yūyū (Aozora Bunko)
