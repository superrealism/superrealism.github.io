About this work

A short story of the Rokumeikan era, suggested by Pierre Loti's travel piece on a ball in Edo. In 1886 the young Akiko attends her first brilliant ball at the Rokumeikan, and the evening she spends with the French officer at her side is drawn in splendid colour and in melancholy. The frame is memorable: years later, an old woman, Akiko recalls it without ever knowing that the officer was the famous writer Loti. Loved as a crystallization of the glitter of youth and the sadness of time passing.

Summary

On the night of the third of November in the nineteenth year of Meiji, Akiko, a young lady of seventeen, goes with her father to her first ball at the Rokumeikan. Dressed like a Western flower, she catches the eye of a French naval officer, dances a waltz with him and walks arm in arm through the garden. Watching the fireworks go up into the night sky, the officer murmurs quietly that he had been thinking of fireworks — of fireworks like our lives. That brilliant night is set in Akiko's heart as a sweet memory. Long years pass, and it is the Taishō era. On a train, the aged Akiko tells the memory of that night to a young novelist who happens to be traveling with her. And when he tells her that the officer's name, Julien Viaud, was that of the famous writer Pierre Loti, the old lady only repeats quietly that it was not a Monsieur Loti but a Monsieur Viaud.

Characters

  • Akiko　A young lady of seventeen at her first ball at the Rokumeikan, who looks back on that night as an old woman
  • The French officer　The naval officer who dances with Akiko and speaks of the frailty of life over the fireworks. He is in fact the writer Pierre Loti

But the old Madame H only looked at the young man's face with a puzzled air and murmured, over and over: "No, it was not a gentleman called Loti. It was a gentleman called Julien Viaud."

Famous passages

I was thinking of the fireworks — of fireworks that are like our life.

Words spoken by the French naval officer who dances with Akiko at the Rokumeikan ball, as he gazes up at fireworks in the night sky. In the midst of a glittering evening, this remark likening the brevity of life to fireworks is justly celebrated.
