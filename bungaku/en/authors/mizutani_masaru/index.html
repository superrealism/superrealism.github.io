Biography

Born in Tokyo in 1894. After graduating from Waseda University he edited "Ryōyū" for the children's publisher Kodomosha and served as chief editor of "Shōjo Gahō," establishing himself as a poet of children's songs and a writer for children. In 1928 he founded the coterie magazine "Dōwa Bungaku" with Chiba Shōzō and others. He left many works retelling the masterpieces of the world in children's language — Alcott's "Little Women," De Amicis's "From the Apennines to the Andes," Fabre, and Tolstoy. He died in 1950 at the age of 55.
