What is Monogatari (Tale Literature)?

Category
Form / Genre

Japanese narrative literature born in the Heian period, told in kana prose. It begins with "The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter" (ninth–tenth century), a tale of the exotic wanderings of an old bamboo cutter and a shining princess, and, praised within "The Tale of Genji" itself as "the ancestor from which tales first arose," it is the oldest surviving work of pure fiction. After passing through "uta monogatari" (poem tales) such as "The Tales of Ise," which wove poems into the story of a man's amorous career resembling Ariwara no Narihira, court tales reached their zenith with Murasaki Shikibu's fifty-four-chapter "The Tale of Genji" (early eleventh century), depicting the life of Hikaru Genji and the human drama of the court — an unparalleled long novel with no counterpart anywhere in the world. Thereafter tale literature branched into historical tales narrating the past, war tales depicting the turbulence of battle, and pseudo-classical tales that looked back longingly on the court tale, and, alongside waka, formed the core of classical literature. Its method — depicting the subtleties of the human heart through fictional characters and plot — can be called a distant wellspring of the modern novel.
