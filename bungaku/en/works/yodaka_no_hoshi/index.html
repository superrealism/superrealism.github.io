About this work

A children's story unpublished in Miyazawa Kenji's lifetime, which reached the world in the collected works of 1934. The nighthawk, shunned by the other birds for his ugliness and forced by the hawk even to change his name, is tormented by his own existence — by the insects whose lives he takes in order to live — and at last flies on and on toward the far side of the sky until he burns as a pale blue star. The sorrow of the weak, self-sacrifice, and a piercing question about the karma of everything that lives: the clearest sadness in all Kenji's stories.

Summary

The nighthawk is a truly ugly bird. His face is blotched as though smeared with bean paste, and the other birds dislike him and abuse him to his face day after day. One day the hawk comes and tells him it will not do for him to carry the hawk's name: he must change it to Ichizō, go around announcing his new name, and if he does not, he will be seized and killed. Sunk in grief, the nighthawk flies through the night sky and thinks of the winged insects and beetles that go into his mouth. He takes many lives in order to live, and he in his turn will be killed by the hawk — and the thought becomes more than he can bear. So he says goodbye to his younger brother the kingfisher and begs the sun, and then the stars, "Please take me to where you are"; but nowhere is he given a hearing. Gathering the last of his strength, the nighthawk gives one cry high like a hawk's and climbs straight up into the sky, on and on, his breath freezing in the cold. And when he came to himself, he saw that his body had become a beautiful blue light like the fire of phosphorus, burning quietly. The nighthawk's star is burning still.

Characters

  • The nighthawk　A bird shunned for his ugliness, who climbs toward the far side of the sky and becomes a star
  • The hawk　The bird of prey who presses him to change his name
  • The kingfisher　The nighthawk's younger brother

The opening (original text)

The nighthawk is a truly ugly bird. His face is blotched here and there as though smeared with bean paste, and his beak is flat and split back as far as his ears. His legs are so unsteady that he cannot walk six feet. The other birds had only to look at the nighthawk's face to feel sick of him.

The ending (original text)

After a while the nighthawk opened his eyes wide. And he saw that his own body had become a beautiful blue light like the fire of phosphorus, burning quietly. Just beside him was Cassiopeia. The blue-white light of the Milky Way was just behind him. And the nighthawk's star went on burning. It went on burning for ever and ever. It is burning still.

Famous passages

The nighthawk was, in truth, a very ugly bird.

A children's story that opens by unhesitatingly calling a despised bird “ugly.” Contemplating the sin of eating in order to live, the nighthawk becomes a star.

And the Nighthawk Star went on burning. It went on burning forever and ever. It is burning still, even now.

The ending. The nighthawk, scorned as ugly, keeps climbing into the sky until it becomes a blue star. The line saying it is still burning leaves behind both salvation and sorrow.
