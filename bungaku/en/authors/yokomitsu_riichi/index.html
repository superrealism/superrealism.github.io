Biography

Born in Aizu, Fukushima Prefecture, in 1898. He dropped out of the English literature department of Waseda University, and, discovered and mentored by Kikuchi Kan, made his literary debut in 1923 with "Nichirin" ("The Sun") and "Hae" ("Flies"). With Kawabata Yasunari and others he founded the magazine "Bungei Jidai" and became the central figure of the "New Sensationist School" (Shinkankakuha), which challenged established realism with bold, keenly sensory figures of speech. With "Kikai" ("Machine"), published in 1930, he established a New Psychologist style that fixed its gaze on the inner workings of the human mind, and was hailed as "the god of literature." Later, after a period of observation in Europe, he took up work on the epic novel "Ryoshū" ("Travel Sorrow"), but it remained unfinished. Called to account after the war for his wartime speech and writing, he died in 1947, disheartened, of a perforated gastric ulcer, at the age of 49.
