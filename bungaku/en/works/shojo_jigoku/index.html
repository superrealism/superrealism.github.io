About this work

A linked set of three stories in the form of letters and records — "Nothing at All," "Murder Relay" and "The Woman from Mars" — published in 1936, late in Yumeno Kyūsaku's life. In each, a young girl falls inescapably into ruin because of her innocence or her disordered mind. The first, in which the beautiful nurse Himekusa Yuriko is possessed by a terrible compulsion to lie, is the best known. A central work of the irregular detective story, in which Kyūsaku draws such girls with fantasy and dread.

Summary

"A Girl's Hell" consists of three stories told in the form of letters and memoranda, each the tragedy of a young girl who sinks to the edge of ruin because of her own innocence or of a psychology she cannot get free of. The heroine of the first and best-known, "It's Nothing at All," is Himekusa Yuriko, a young nurse of a sweet and spotless appearance. She was an excellent nurse whom everyone loved; but within her lived a dreadful compulsion to lie, an inability to keep from telling stories with no foundation whatever as though they were fact. The lies she spins, each of them "nothing at all," weave in time a hell of fiction that draws in the people around her, the newspapers, even the police. Driven at last into a corner by her own lies, Yuriko takes her own life, as though to underwrite the fiction with her death. Letters exchanged between the two doctors who attended her uncover, little by little, the strange workings of the girl's mind and the truth of the tragedy. "The Murder Relay" and "The Woman from Mars," which follow, likewise draw girls struggling as they sink into their hells, in colors of the grotesque and the fantastic. It is a central work of the "irregular" detective story, in which Kyūsaku draws, with a shudder all his own, the tragedy of girls whom their very innocence cannot save.

Characters

  • Himekusa Yuriko　The heroine of the first story, "It's Nothing at All." A young nurse of spotless appearance, possessed by a dreadful compulsion to lie, who is driven by her own lies to take her life
  • Usuki Rihei and Shirataka Hidemaro　The two doctors who attended Yuriko, who uncover in their letters the workings of her mind and the truth of the tragedy
  • The girls　Those of "The Murder Relay" and "The Woman from Mars," each sinking in her own way to the edge of ruin

The opening (original text)

It's Nothing at All To my elder brother Shirataka Hidemaro Usuki Rihei I am the man who had the honor of meeting you briefly the other day at the Kōjutsu-kai at the Marunouchi Club; like yourself I am a graduate, junior to you, of the department of otolaryngology at Kyushu Imperial University. Forgive the rudeness of sending you so strange a letter out of the blue. Himekusa Yuriko has taken her own life.

The ending (original text)

In this way I repay the debt I owe you for having made a woman of me. And with it I want to let Tonomiya Aiko, the lover I love from my heart, be a true daughter to her parents. Unless I settle everything in this way, I cannot return to the nothingness I came from. Please accept the parting gift of the woman from Mars — the charred body of a girl.
