Biography

Born in Takanawa, Tokyo, in 1872; his real name was Keiji. After graduating from a Tokyo prefectural middle school, he worked as a newspaper reporter writing theater criticism, and also wrote plays, joining forces with the kabuki actor Ichikawa Sadanji II to produce one new kabuki play after another, including "Shuzenji Monogatari" ("The Tale of Shuzenji") and "Banchō Sarayashiki" ("The Dish Mansion at Banchō"), becoming the leading playwright of the genre. Over his career he left roughly two hundred plays, and he was also the founder of Japan's detective and period-mystery fiction with his linked stories "Hanshichi Torimonochō," which cast an Edo-period detective, the informant Hanshichi, as its great sleuth. He was also known as a master of the ghost story, writing many tales of the uncanny, including those collected in "Seiadō Kidan" ("Tales of the Blue Frog Hall"). He died of illness in 1939 at the age of 66.
