About this work

An unusual late work written in the form of a diary, published in Chūō Kōron in November 1955. The narrator, who lives at Atami, comes up to Tokyo, sees a strip show and the French film Les Diaboliques, eats Kyoto food, and returns to Atami — two days recorded exactly. But at midnight, half asleep and half awake, the afterimages of the film, the food and the excrement mix together and flow at last into the story from the Shiji of the human swine. A piece in which cruelty rises up in the middle of the ordinary life of old age.

Summary

On the eighth of August, with my high blood pressure at last improving, I take my household up to Tokyo from Atami. At the music hall of the Nichigeki, which I have long been pressed to visit, I watch a strip show, my eye caught by the body of a girl named Harukawa Masumi who appears in a bathtub scene. The next day I go to see the much-talked-of French film Les Diaboliques in Hibiya, in which a headmaster's wife and his mistress conspire to drown him in a bathtub and sink him in a swimming pool, only for the corpse to vanish — I pore over the film's contrivance in detail, tallying up its improbabilities. That night, back in Atami and in bed, I cannot sleep, my stomach swollen from having eaten too much. As I take my sleeping draught, images begin to overlap one after another — pike conger in a thick kudzu sauce, a bathing girl, the eyes of the corpse in the tub, and the red liquid in my own chamber pot — until at last I become Emperor Xiaohui of Han, and am shown a certain thing.

Characters

  • I　An old novelist living in Atami who travels up to Tokyo despite his high blood pressure
  • My wife　The narrator's wife, who of late has taken to groaning in the grip of nightmares
  • Miss Tamako　My wife's younger sister, who presses the narrator to take her to see the strip show
  • Lady Qi　A consort of Emperor Gaozu of Han, as told in the Records of the Grand Historian, whose limbs were cut off by Empress Lü, who made her into a "human swine"

The opening (original text)

August 8th. Morning: up to Tokyo on the Ideyu. The party of four — I, my wife, Miss Tamako, and Fuji. Through July we had a spell of heat rare even for recent years, but late on the night of the 4th of this month it rained for the first time in a long while, the temperature dropping some nine degrees Fahrenheit and giving great relief, so that I thought we were surely past the worst of it — yet this morning the heat has come back again. Having already bought our tickets for the Ideyu last night, we set out braving it all the same.

Kasanka mangan sui no yume by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Ah — this is Lady Qi," I say, and cannot help covering my eyes. I know now that the noble lady at my side is Empress Lü, and that I myself am Emperor Xiaohui. ……… I wake with a start to find it is half past four in the morning, a faint light showing beyond the shoji. The sound of the drum from the Kōa Kannon up on the mountain drifts on. My stomach is still swollen and aching. My wife, I find, has somehow fallen into a peaceful sleep beside me. Just where had I crossed over into a true dream, I wonder — was it from the point where Simone Signoret's face began to twist and crumble? ……… Turning such thoughts over, I begin to drift back to sleep.

Kasanka mangan sui no yume by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)
