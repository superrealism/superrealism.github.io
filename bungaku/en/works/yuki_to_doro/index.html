About this work

A historical story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Ōru Yomimono in 1954. Oshino, a woman who feigns a fainting fit in the amusement quarter of Asakusa and takes money off men who know nothing of the world. Set in the world of women in that back trade, it draws a human pattern in which what is clean as snow and what is dirty as mud cross each other.

Summary

"I only made like I was going faint, that's all" — Oshino, near the gate of Denbōin, together with her companion Okatsu, overtakes some meek-looking young man from behind and lets herself stagger and collapse against him. That was her trick for squeezing money out of unsuspecting "marks." Otsune, an older woman of standing among them, scolds her, "That's a sin, you know — you'll come to a bad end one day." But young Chiyono finds it all great fun and begs to hear more. Set in the amusement quarter of Asakusa, this piece follows women who make a tough, precarious living there — a place where things pure as snow and things filthy as mud mingle inseparably. Chased even by the collector for her daily installment payments, Oshino gives a wry laugh: "What's taken off you gets taken off you, sure enough — this daily-payment business isn't easy either, is it." A piece of Yamamoto's, drawing the sorrows and joys of women living in the back streets, with an eye at once distanced and warm.

Characters

  • Oshino　A woman who fakes fainting to squeeze money out of men
  • Otsune　An older woman of standing among them

The opening (original text)

"It's not that he's good-looking, exactly — it's more that he's got this meek, weak-willed sort of look right there on his face, that kind. Still young, too." "Enough of that now, will you," Otsune said, applying powder to the back of her neck. "That's a sin, you know. And besides, the world isn't so full of easy marks that you can keep this up — you'll come to a bad end one day."

Yuki to doro by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Just as Otsune was starting to say this, the lattice door slid open and a woman's hoarse voice was heard. "Excuse me, is Sister Oshino in?" "Yes, I'm here," Oshino said, putting down her pipe and standing up. "What's taken off you gets taken off you, sure enough — this daily-payment business isn't easy either, is it."
