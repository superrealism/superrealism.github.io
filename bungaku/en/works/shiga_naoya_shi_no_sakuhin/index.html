About this work

Of all writers now living I respect Shiga most — Kikuchi Kan's essay on Shiga Naoya, written with unstinting admiration. It takes in the difference from the realism of naturalism, the gravity of a pared-down expression, and the flat assertion that his short stories are at a world level. A short, heated essay in which one writer speaks about another of his own time.

Summary

"Among the writers of our own day, I respect Mr. Shiga above all others" — so Kikuchi Kan declares at the very start of this essay. In the six or seven years since the founding of Shirakaba, he has grown disillusioned with, or simply lost interest in, most other writers, but his feeling for Shiga Naoya alone has never wavered. And so, he says, what follows is not criticism, but simply an account of what he feels. What Kikuchi sees at the core of Shiga's writing is realism — but a realism nothing at all like that of the naturalist school. Beside writers who pile up trivial detail after trivial detail, Shiga's expression shows a severe, disciplined selectivity, sparing his pen almost to a fault. He depicts only what must be depicted, driving straight at the very heart of each event, and it is this force, Kikuchi writes, that lends his prose its tension. As for his view of life, Shiga clouds nothing with sentiment or theory, but looks straight at living fact itself, Kikuchi observes. He then turns to specific works, praising "The Old Man," "An Incident," and "Seibei and the Gourds" without reserve, while candidly noting that "Akanishi Kakita" and "The Righteous" fall somewhat short by comparison. It is not at all rare, he says, for Shiga's stories to surpass the foreign short-story collections translated by Ōgai; the Japanese literary world assumes without question that anything foreign must be good, but that, he declares hotly, is simply absurd. "In any case, that Mr. Shiga should be here among us, a person of my own generation, strikes me as a thing both deeply reassuring and a matter for real joy."

The opening (original text)

Among the writers of our own day, I respect Mr. Shiga above all others. And not only do I respect him — his work, too, is what I love best of all. To speak plainly, exactly as I believe it: I hold Mr. Shiga to be one of the most outstanding writers now active in the Japanese literary world.

Shiga naoya shi no sakuhin by Kikuchi Kan (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

There is still much more I could say, but let me stop here. In any case, that Mr. Shiga should be here among us, a person of my own generation, strikes me as a thing both deeply reassuring and a matter for real joy. One last word, before I close: I have written this essay for no other purpose than to express my own respect and affection for Mr. Shiga's work.

Shiga naoya shi no sakuhin by Kikuchi Kan (Aozora Bunko)
