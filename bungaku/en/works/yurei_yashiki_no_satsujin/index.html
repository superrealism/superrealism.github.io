About this work

A detective story for boys that Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shōnen Shōjo Tankai in 1930. The boy detective Haruta Ryūsuke unravels by lucid observation and reasoning the ghost murder at the mysterious White Mansion that is filling the newspapers. A bracing piece exposing the trick of a ghost worked with a parakeet, and the secret of the treasure hidden in the house.

Summary

After dinner, Dr. Haruta asks young Ryūsuke, "That mysterious White Mansion affair has been making such a fuss in the papers lately — what sort of case was it, exactly?" Young Ryūsuke, biting into an apple, begins to tell the whole story of the strange case. A "white ghost" said to appear at the White Mansion, and then a murder. But its true nature was nothing supernatural. Ryūsuke picks up the thread of the truth from a black feather he found caught in the frame of a revolving window, and a parakeet he found in the garden. The culprit, it turns out, had tied a piece of white silk cloth to the parakeet's leg to make it look like a "white ghost." Ryūsuke goes further still, discovering that the mansion had once been a lower residence of the former Lord Hasegawa, and that a chief retainer had once made off with twenty thousand ryō in hidden gold — and so brilliantly solves the case. The gold that had been buried beneath the White Mansion is returned to the Hasegawa family, half of which is given to Ryūsuke as funds for his research. A rousing piece showcasing a boy detective's sharp deduction.

The opening (original text)

"So, Ryūsuke!" Dr. Haruta, sipping coffee after dinner, tossed aside the newspaper he had been reading and spoke to young Ryūsuke. "That mysterious White Mansion affair has been making such a fuss in the papers lately — what sort of case was it, exactly?"

The ending (original text)

And so the ghost-murder case that had set the world abuzz was, by Ryūsuke's hand, happily solved. I might add, too, that the gold buried beneath the White Mansion was returned to the Hasegawa family, and that the Hasegawa family in turn gave half of it to Ryūsuke as funds for his research.
