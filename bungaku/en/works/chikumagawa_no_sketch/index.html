About this work

A collection of sketches Shimazaki Tōson wrote from his six years and more as a teacher at Komoro in Shinshū, serialized in the magazine Chūgaku Sekai in 1911. The lives of the people of an upland town on the bank of the Chikuma river, and the turning of the seasons, are set down like sketches with a poet's eye and a painter's fine observation. It conveys the original landscape of the Komoro years in which he prepared his novel The Broken Commandment, and is loved as the starting point of his prose.

Summary

Tōson, who had made his name as a poet, lived from the thirty-second year of Meiji as a teacher at the Komoro Gijuku, in a highland town looking out over the Chikuma river. This book is a record, written as sketches from life, of what he saw and heard in those Komoro years. The fields spreading over the skirts of Mount Asama, the spring when the apple trees flower, the rough winds of winter, mornings sunk in the river mist — through the turning seasons, the plain lives of farmers and packhorse men, of the blacksmith and the pupils at the school, are copied down in an unadorned style. Here Tōson set himself the task of leaving behind the ornamented language of poetry, of looking at things as they are, and of training a prose in which speech and writing are one. Together with memories of the days when he walked with his pupils and his painter friends from the upper reaches of the Chikuma down to its lower course, it records a precious apprenticeship in prose that prepared the novelist Shimazaki Tōson to come.

The opening (original text)

My dear Mr. Yoshimura — Itsuki — now that I am setting at the head of this book a piece addressed to you in place of a preface, I want after all to call you by the familiar name I have been used to calling you. I have at last been able to put together the memorial of that life on the heights which I have long meant to offer you. Itsuki, the tie between you and me is deep and long. I was taken into your family's house while still a boy, before you were born; and after you were born I carried you as an infant and walked about with you on my back.

Chikumagawa no suketchi by Shimazaki Tōson (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

In the end I lived seven years up on the heights. Among the things I cannot forget are the days when Osanai Kaoru, Arishima Ikuma, Aoki Shigeru, Tayama Katai and Yanagita Kunio came to the house at Babaura. I often went off on journeys with Samejima, a bachelor of science at the Komoro Gijuku, or with the watercolorist Maruyama Banka, and with the pupils of the school, from the upper reaches of the Chikuma river down toward its lower course. These sketches are the keepsake of a life at Komoro that is full of memories in many senses.

Chikumagawa no suketchi by Shimazaki Tōson (Aozora Bunko)
