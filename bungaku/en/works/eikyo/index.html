About this work

A short story Nakajima Atsushi published in Seikai Ōrai in 1942 together with "The Ox Man," one of his Chinese pieces drawn from the Zuo Commentary. Set in the state of Wei in the Spring and Autumn period, it draws the arrogant and pitiless crown prince Kuaikui through exile and wandering, restoration and fall. The impermanence of a human world that waxes and wanes like the moon, and the pitiless human pattern around power, cut by Nakajima in a dignified Chinese-flavoured style.

Summary

In the Spring and Autumn period, in the reign of Duke Ling of Wei. The crown prince Kuaikui hears, on one occasion, farmers working the fields singing a strange song that mocks him. Kuaikui, arrogant and hot-tempered, takes it as an insult and flies into a rage; after a reckless scheme, he is driven from the state and wanders in exile among other lands. After his father's death, when Kuaikui's own son Zhé ascends the ducal throne of Wei, a wretched struggle for the state begins between father and son. After his long wandering Kuaikui at last returns home, seizes the throne, and becomes Duke of Wei — but his pitiless conduct alienates the hearts of the people, and he too, in the end, is driven to a miserable death. Even as he is killed, still clinging to a jade disc, he is met with mockery: "Do you think, if I kill you, the jade will simply vanish somewhere?" Like the moon that wanes once it has waxed full, this piece of Chinese material draws, in Nakajima's dignified, Chinese-flavoured prose, the impermanence of a human world that repeats its cycles of glory and decline, and the pitiless human drama that surrounds power.

Characters

  • Kuaikui (later Duke of Wei)　Crown prince of Wei. Driven from the state for his arrogance and pitilessness, seizes the throne after long wandering, but loses the people's hearts and meets a miserable end
  • Duke Ling　Ruler of Wei, Kuaikui's father
  • Jishi　The man who kills the cornered Duke of Wei (Kuaikui) and takes his jade disc

The opening (original text)

In the autumn of the thirty-ninth year of Duke Ling of Wei, the crown prince, acting on his father's orders, once went as an envoy to Qi. On the way, passing through the state of Song, he heard farmers working the fields singing a strange song.

Eikyo by Nakajima Atsushi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Jishi drew his barbarian blade from its scabbard and approached him, grinning. "Do you think, if I kill you, the jade will simply vanish somewhere?" Such was the end of the Duke of Wei.

Eikyo by Nakajima Atsushi (Aozora Bunko)
