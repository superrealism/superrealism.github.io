What is Kōdan Zasshi?

Category
Society / Group

A popular entertainment magazine founded by Hakubunkan in 1915. It took over the title from the kōdan stenographer Imamura Jirō's magazine of the same name, and, set up to rival the rival publisher Kōdansha's "Kōdan Kurabu," it began as an entertainment magazine offering kōdan, rakugo, and true stories. Period fiction written from the outset as reading matter gradually became the center of its pages, and popular fiction writers including Shirai Kyōji, Kunieda Shirō, Mikami Otokichi, and Yokomizo Seishi wrote vigorously for it. Watanabe On published the stylish short piece "Ā Kazoku-sama da yo to Watashi wa Uso o Tsuku no de Atta" (1929), and Unno Jūza published the full-length novel "Hae Otoko" ("The Fly Man," 1937), blending the grotesque with science. From wartime into the postwar period it was an important venue for Yamamoto Shūgorō, who published many period stories in it, from "Susu Gayu" ("Millet Gruel," 1943), depicting hunger and a samurai's pride, to "Nagaya Ten'ichibō" (1950). Hakubunkan also published "Taiyō," "Bungei Kurabu," and "Shinseinen," and this magazine sustained the popular-fiction wing of that stable. It ceased publication in 1954.
