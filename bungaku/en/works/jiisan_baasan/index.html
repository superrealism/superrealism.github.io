About this work

A short story Mori Ōgai published in Shinshōsetsu in 1915, one of his historical works, drawn from an actual event of the Edo period. Into a small retirement house at Ryūdochō in Azabu move a dignified old couple with snow-white hair, living on the best of terms. They are Minobe Iori and Run — married young, then separated for thirty-seven years while Iori was exiled after a fatal quarrel, and only now, in old age, reunited. A fine piece in which Ōgai drew, in a concise and feeling historical style, an affection between husband and wife unchanged across a great span of years.

Summary

In the spring of Bunka 6, a dignified old man with hair gone entirely white, yet still unbent at the waist and wearing a fine pair of swords, comes to live alone in a small retirement cottage on the grounds of the Matsudaira estate in Azabu Ryūdo-chō. Before long, a refined old woman, her hair too gone entirely white and done up in a small round chignon, arrives to live with him. The neighbors look on with smiles at the old couple, living together as affectionately as children playing house. This old man — Minobe Iori — and this old woman — Run — were, in fact, husband and wife, married when they were young. But Iori, on the road during his lord's alternate attendance in Edo, had killed a fellow retainer over some trifling matter, and was banished for the long span of thirty-seven years to Maruoka in Echizen. During all that time, Run served for thirty-one years as a lady-in-waiting in the Kuroda household, keeping her fidelity and waiting for her husband's return. The years flowed on, and when at last, through a general pardon, Iori was permitted to return to Edo, Run welcomed him with joy, and in the house at Ryūdo-chō, the two were reunited after fully thirty-seven years apart. Drawing on historical fact from the Edo period, this is a piece in which Ōgai, in a spare, chronicle-like style, depicts the sight of an old couple, parted for so many long years, once again living together in affection.

Characters

  • Minobe Iori　A dignified old samurai with hair gone white. In his youth he committed a fatal act of violence and was banished for thirty-seven years to Maruoka in Echizen. Reunited with Run in his old age
  • Run　Iori's wife. During her husband's banishment, serves for thirty-one years as a lady-in-waiting in the Kuroda household, keeping her fidelity and waiting for his return

The opening (original text)

It was around the time the spring of Bunka 6 was drawing to its close. In Azabu Ryūdo-chō, on the grounds just south of what is now the barracks of the Third Infantry Regiment, within the estate of the daimyo Matsudaira Sashichirō Norifusa, lord of Okudono in Mikawa province, carpenters had gone in and were repairing a small vacant house. When the neighbors asked who was to live there, they were told that a certain Miyashige Kyūemon, a retainer of the Matsudaira house, was having a retirement cottage made.

Jiisan bāsan by Mori Ōgai (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The following year, Bunka 6, her husband Iori, who for thirty-seven years since the first year of An'ei had made his living in his place of banishment at Maruoka in Echizen province by teaching penmanship and swordsmanship, was informed that, "on the eighth day of the third month, in memorial observance for the late Lord Shunmei-in, and by the merciful wish of the shogunate, his indefinite custody is hereby pardoned," and so was permitted to return to Edo. Hearing this, Run came joyfully from Awa to Edo, and in the house at Ryūdo-chō, the two were reunited after thirty-seven years apart.
