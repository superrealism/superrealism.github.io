Biography

Born in Kanazawa in 1889, the illegitimate child of a foot-soldier commander of the Kaga domain; his real name was Terumichi. Soon after birth he was taken in by the common-law wife of the head priest of a Shingon-sect temple, and at seven he was formally adopted into the Murō family. Growing up without ever knowing his birth mother's face, that lonely upbringing became the undercurrent of his literature throughout his life. He dropped out of higher elementary school and began writing poetry while working at a courthouse, moving to Tokyo in 1913. With Hagiwara Sakutarō and others he founded the poetry magazine "Kanjō" ("Feeling"), and with the poetry collections "Ai no Shishū" ("A Collection of Poems of Love") and "Jojō Shōkyokushū" ("A Collection of Small Lyric Pieces") he brought fresh energy to the world of lyric poetry as a poet of sentiment. In 1919 he published the autobiographical novels "Yōnen Jidai" ("Childhood"), "Sei ni Mezameru Koro" ("The Time of Awakening to Sex"), and "Aru Shōjo no Shi made" ("Until a Certain Girl's Death") one after another in "Chūō Kōron," and was recognized as a novelist as well. He was also close friends with Akutagawa Ryūnosuke. After the war he established his standing as a novelist with "Ani Imōto" ("Brother and Sister"), "Anzukko," and "Kagerō no Nikki Ibun" ("A Sequel to the Diary of the Mayfly"), and died in 1962 at the age of 72. He was also the poet remembered for the line "One's hometown is a thing to be longed for from afar."
