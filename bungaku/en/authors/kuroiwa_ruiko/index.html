Biography

Born in 1862 in Aki District, Tosa Province; his real name was Kuroiwa Shūroku. After running into trouble with the authorities for his involvement in the Freedom and People's Rights Movement, he became a newspaper reporter and in 1892 founded the newspaper "Yorozu Chōhō," whose outspoken pages made it a sensation. At the same time he adapted a stream of Western detective and adventure novels, dazzling readers with plainspoken prose that boldly Japanized the originals — "Iwaya no Ō" ("The Count of Monte Cristo"), "Ā Mujō" ("Les Misérables"), "Yūreitō" ("The Ghost Tower"), "Hakuhatsuki" ("The White-Haired Demon") — and laid the groundwork for popular fiction in Japan. His influence on later writers, beginning with Edogawa Ranpo, was considerable. He died in 1920.
