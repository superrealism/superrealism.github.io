What is Shi to Shiron (Poetry and Poetics)?

Category
Society / Group

A quarterly poetry magazine founded by the Kōseikaku publisher in September 1928. Centered on Haruyama Yukio, eleven poets, including Kitagawa Fuyuhiko, Anzai Fuyue, Iijima Tadashi, Kondō Azuma, and Takenaka Iku, joined as founding coterie members. Taking the stance that a poem is not an outpouring of feeling but a construction made of language, the magazine championed building poems intellectually, with sentiment set aside, and its distinguishing feature was the weight it placed on poetics alongside poetic works: it vigorously introduced avant-garde currents from Europe and America — Nishiwaki Junzaburō's Surrealist poetics among them, as well as Dadaism, Futurism, and the New Psychologist school — becoming the base of Japanese Modernist poetry. It opened its pages beyond poetry as well, and Kajii Motojirō's "Sakura no Ki no Shita ni wa" ("Under the Cherry Trees") and "Kigakuteki Genkaku" ("Instrumental Hallucination") were published in its second issue. In 1930, Kitagawa Fuyuhiko, Miyoshi Tatsuji, Yodono Ryūzō, and others, disliking the current's drift away from reality, broke off to found "Shi, Genjitsu" ("Poetry, Reality") — Kajii's "Yami no Emaki" ("A Dark Picture Scroll") and "Aibu" ("Caress") appeared there instead — and the original magazine ceased publication in December 1931 after 14 issues, continuing the following year under the retitled name "Bungaku."
