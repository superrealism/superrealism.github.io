About this work

The unknown young Kōda Rohan kept Yoda Gakkai reading all night and made Yamada Bimyō break into a smile, and so came into the world — Uchida Roan tells lightly, with anecdotes about the first work, how Rohan came to contend for the throne of the literary world with Tsuyu Dandan and The Elegant Buddha.

Summary

A short piece in which Uchida Roan tells briefly how Kōda Rohan came into the world. He begins with the day when Yamada Bimyō, then chief editor of Miyako no Hana, broke into a smile and said, "A great man has appeared!"; and he gives the anecdote of a young student who came suddenly to the old master Yoda Gakkai, flung down a manuscript with "If you find it interesting, write me a preface; if it is worthless, burn it in your stove," and went away. Rebuffed flatly by the old man, the student left; and when Gakkai afterward began to read the manuscript he was drawn in by its extraordinary invention and read it through the night, and repenting of his rudeness wished to write a letter of apology — only to find that he knew neither the writer's address nor his name; and going to Awashima Kangetsu, who had sent him, he learned for the first time that it was the first attempt of a young man named Kōda Rohan. That work was Rodandan, first serialized in Miyako no Hana and then published as a volume; and what raised Rohan's name at a bound to contend for the throne of the world of letters was Fūryūbutsu. Roan writes that he read Rodandan with the sort of interest one takes in a conjuring trick, but that when he read Fūryūbutsu he felt as though he were drifting with Shuun in five-colored clouds; and he praises it as a work that, short as it is, ought to be counted among the great literature of the world.

Characters

  • Kōda Rohan　The young writer who with Rodandan and Fūryūbutsu came at a bound to contend for the throne of the literary world
  • Yoda Gakkai　The great master of the day, who read Rohan's manuscript through the night, recognized his gift, and looked after him
  • Yamada Bimyō　Chief editor of Miyako no Hana, who rejoiced at Rohan's appearance with "A great man has appeared"
  • Awashima Kangetsu　The man who sent Rohan to Gakkai
  • Uchida Roan　The author

The opening (original text)

Once, meeting Yamada Bimyō, who was then chief editor of the Kinkōdō's Miyako no Hana, he broke into a smile and said at the first word, "A great man has appeared!" When I asked what sort of man, he said that some days before a student had come suddenly to the old master Yoda Gakkai and said that he had written a novel and would he write a preface for it. Since Gakkai was in those days the great master of both the hard and the soft styles, there was no end of people waiting at his gate to ask for prefaces to their books, and being weary of the trouble of receiving them all one by one, he rebuffed him flatly, saying that his preface would not make the man's work shine, and that if the work were a masterpiece it would shine without any preface of his.

Rohan no shusse banashi by Uchida Roan (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

But when I read Fūryūbutsu, for some while after I had finished I felt as though I were floating in a daze with Shuun among five-colored clouds. Never since have I felt so lost to myself, as in a dream. Short as it is, it ought to be counted among the great literature of the world. There is much to be said about Rohan; but four or five pages could never say it all, and so I lay down my pen here.

Rohan no shusse banashi by Uchida Roan (Aozora Bunko)
