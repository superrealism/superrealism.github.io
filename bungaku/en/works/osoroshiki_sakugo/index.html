About this work

An early short story Ranpo published in Shin Seinen in 1923. Mr Kitagawa walks the street with a strange gait, nothing turning in his head but the thought that he has won — and behind his madness lies a terrible chain of coincidence involving a letter and a gold medal exchanged by mistake. A piece striking at the precariousness of human psychology, in what Ranpo himself called the blind spot of the brain.

Summary

"I won, I won..." — in Mr. Kitagawa's mind, nothing turned but the sheer consciousness of having won, spinning round and round like a windmill, and he wandered the streets with a strange, unsteady gait, not even aware of where he was walking. What was it that drove him to madness? The story traces back the chain of that terrible "mix-up" and lays it bare. It began with a single letter, and a gold medal enclosed within it. A postscript to the letter noted that the person in the photograph attached to the enclosed medal was said to be a fearsome murderer — though it seemed hard to believe that a man he saw often, on friendly terms, could really be such a wicked criminal. But the photograph fitted into that medal had, through some mistake, been swapped with that of an entirely different person. This mix-up calls up a suspicion the man himself never anticipated, and drives Mr. Kitagawa, who habitually spoke of the "blind spot of the brain," to the very edge of madness. Whether that letter had been sincere, or a piece of wit seizing on the mistake, remained a secret known only to the man himself, forever. An early piece of Ranpo's, striking at the precariousness of the human heart and the terror of pure coincidence.

The opening (original text)

"I won, I won, I won..." In Mr. Kitagawa's mind, nothing turned but the sheer consciousness of having won, spinning round and round like a windmill. He thought of nothing else at all. He had not the faintest idea, just then, where he was walking, or where he meant to go. In fact, he was not even conscious of the very fact that he was walking.

Osoroshiki sakugo by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Whether this letter had been Mr. Nomoto's sincere intention, or a piece of wit on his part seizing on the mistake with the medal, was a secret known to no one but Mr. Nomoto himself, forever. And so, what a terrible chain of coincidence it was that became the direct cause of Mr. Kitagawa's madness. It was, in the end, the working of what he himself had habitually called, as though it were a mere saying, the "blind spot of the brain."

Osoroshiki sakugo by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)
