About this work

A tale of the Brothers Grimm in which a woodcutter's son and daughter, abandoned in the forest in a year of famine to save food, are taken prisoner by a man-eating witch who lives in a house of cake, and use their wits to come home alive. In Kusuyama Masao's version, the contrast between the first journey, when white pebbles are dropped along the way, and the second, when the crumbs of bread are eaten by birds, and the scene in which a bone is held out in place of a finger to deceive the witch, carry over into Japanese the whole frame of the original, in which cruelty and quick wit live side by side. Through opera and picture books it has been loved all over the world.

Summary

A poor woodcutter's family who live beside a great forest can no longer come by their daily bread in a famine that has struck the whole country. The stepmother proposes leaving the children deep in the forest, and the father, unwilling as he is, gives way at last. Hansel, who has overheard them, fills his pocket with white pebbles that shine in the moonlight, drops them along the way, and brings his sister Gretel safely home. But the famine does not end, and when they are taken out a second time the door has been locked and he cannot gather pebbles. Hansel scatters crumbs of bread instead, and the birds eat them all, so that the two children really do lose their way in the forest. On the third day a white bird leads them to a little house with walls of bread and windows of sugar. What welcomes the two of them as they eat at it hungrily is a half-blind witch who lures children to her and eats them. Hansel is shut in a cage and fattened, and Gretel made to do the work. Hansel holds out a thin bone instead of his finger so that the witch believes him still thin, and at last, when the witch presses Gretel to look into the oven, Gretel pushes her in instead. With their aprons full of pearls and jewels, the children cross the water on the back of a duck and come home to their father.

Characters

  • Hansel　The woodcutter's son. The brother who marks the way home with white pebbles and crumbs of bread
  • Gretel　His sister, who in the end pushes the witch into the oven
  • The woodcutter　The father who, out of poverty, abandons his children in the forest
  • The wife　The stepmother who proposes abandoning them
  • The witch　The half-blind man-eater who lures children to her house of cake

The opening (original text)

1 Before a great forest there lived a poor woodcutter, with his wife and his two children, in a little hut. Of the two children the boy was called Hansel and the girl Gretel. They had lived meanly and had hardly enough to put between their teeth even before this; but one year a great famine came upon the country, and the woodcutter could no longer come by his daily bread. Lying in bed at night and turning over what was to become of them, he tossed about with worry, and sighed, and said to his wife: "What is to become of us? How are we to feed our poor children?"

Hansel and Gretel by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

And the wife had died. Gretel shook out her apron, and the pearls and jewels went rolling all about the room; and then Hansel thrust a hand into his pocket and took out handful after handful and scattered them there. And with that all their cares and troubles were swept clean away. The three of them lived on together in nothing but happiness. And so my tale has prospered. Look, there runs a little mouse; whoever catches it may make himself a great fur cap out of it.

Hansel and Gretel by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)
