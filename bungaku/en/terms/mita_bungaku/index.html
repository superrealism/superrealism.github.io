What is Mita Bungaku?

Category
Society / Group

A literary magazine founded in May 1910, when, in the course of reforming the literature department of Keio University's University Division, the department secretary Ishida Shintarō consulted with Professor Mori Ōgai and they founded it together. Nagai Kafū was brought in as founding editor-in-chief, with Ueda Bin as advisor, and Kafū serialized the essay "KŌcha no Ato" ("After Tea") from the inaugural issue, publishing pieces that criticized the government's censorship system and drew notice. The travel essay "Hiyorigeta" ("Fair-Weather Clogs," 1914), walking the streets of Tokyo, was also published in this magazine. Kafū soon stepped down as editor-in-chief after clashing with the university administration over, among other things, an issue carrying Tanizaki Jun'ichirō's work being banned from sale, but as a pioneer among university-affiliated literary magazines, alongside Waseda University's "Waseda Bungaku," it went on sending many writers into the world.
