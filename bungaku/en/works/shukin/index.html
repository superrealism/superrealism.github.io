About this work

A short story with Hasegawa Kinzō, a Tokyo Imperial University professor suggesting Nitobe Inazō, for its protagonist, asking sharply about the way of the samurai in Japan and the theatricality hidden inside it. A mother who comes to tell him of her son's death keeps a smile on her face while, in her lap, she grips a handkerchief hard enough to tear it — and in that restrained grief the professor finds the bushido beauty of a Japanese woman. But afterwards he reads Strindberg on stagecraft and is assailed by the doubt that it may be no more than acting to a pattern. A piece looking with a cool eye at what civilization and morality actually contain.

Summary

Hasegawa Kinzō, a professor at Tokyo Imperial University, sits on his veranda reading Strindberg's treatise on the art of drama and turning his thoughts to the civilization of Japan. Just then a lady, the mother of one of his students, comes to call. When he asks her business, she tells him, quite composedly and even with a smile, that her son died of illness only a little while before, without the least sign of distress. The professor, impressed by her composure, happens to glance under the desk to pick up a fan he has dropped, and notices that the woman is gripping a handkerchief in both hands on her lap, trembling as though about to tear it in two. Her face laughs, her hands weep — and in this the professor sees, deeply moved, the bushido beauty of a Japanese woman holding her feelings in check. That night, however, when he opens Strindberg again, he finds a passage condemning as a hackneyed, vulgar technique the very acting that "expresses sorrow with the hands while the face laughs." Was that mother's conduct, too, no more than acting after all? The professor, left with an unaccountable unease, sits gazing fixedly at the light of a Gifu lantern.

Characters

  • Hasegawa Kinzō　A Tokyo Imperial University professor. Holds forth on Japanese civilization and bushido, but finds his convictions shaken (said to be modelled on Nitobe Inazō)
  • Nishiyama Atsuko　The mother of the dead student. Bears her grief while keeping a smile and gripping her handkerchief tight
