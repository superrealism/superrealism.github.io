About this work

A blind younger brother who plays the flute in a port town, and his beautiful sister who dances to it. One day the story of the island of swans, told to the sister by a foreign sailor whose ship has come into the port, divides the fate of the two. A children's story in which Ogawa Mimei laid a longing for foreign parts over the feeling between brother and sister. The title is given as it appeared at the time of publication.

Summary

By the roadside of a harbor town, a boy of about ten was playing a flute. At one moment its notes sounded mournful, like the autumn wind rustling withered leaves, and at the next, sweet as the voice of a small bird in the forest. Those who gathered to look found the boy frail, and blind besides. Beside him, his beautiful sister — sixteen or seventeen, barefoot in a pale blue robe — danced lightly, as though petals were scattering in the wind, singing low as she moved. To hear her singing was to feel one's heart race off toward some far, distant sky, wandering, forlorn and sorrowful, through a forest swept by a lonesome wind. No one knew where the two of them had come from, nor where they meant to return. One day, a sailor who had come to port by ship from a distant country spoke to the sister and told her of a far-off island. Beyond a silver sea, past mountains that towered white and gleaming with snow, lay an island where countless swans made their home, and the sight of them wheeling through the evening sky was said to be a splendid one. Before long, the brother vanished. The sister searched the whole harbor and pressed the sailor for answers, but it seemed that her brother had gone off to the island of swans. "There must be another self of mine, somewhere in this world — a self kinder and better than I am. And it was that self who has taken my brother away," the sister thought, wringing her long hair between both hands, her heart nearly breaking with regret. When she said she wished to go to that island herself, the sailor pointed off toward the harbor and told her it was not a place one could go to easily. The summer day was drawing to its close, and the sky, as it had the day before, burned a deep crimson red.

The opening (original text)

A boy who looked to be no more than about ten years old was playing a flute. At one moment its notes sounded mournful, like the autumn wind rustling withered leaves, and at the next, lovely and sweet, like the cry of a small bird singing in a beautiful green wood on a mild spring day.

The ending (original text)

"Where is this island, then? I do so want to go there, somehow," the sister said. At this, the sailor pointed off toward the harbor and answered, "Far, far away, thousands of leagues off, there lies a silver sea. One must cross it and come up onto land, and then cross over mountains, range upon range of them, shining white with snow — it is not a place one can go to easily." Just then the summer day was drawing to its close, coloring the sea, and the sky, as it had the day before, could be seen burning a deep crimson red.
