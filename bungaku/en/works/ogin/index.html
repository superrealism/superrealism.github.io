About this work

One of Akutagawa's stories about Japan's Christians, holding both irony and pathos on martyrdom and apostasy. Ogin, a girl raised a Christian in the village of Urakami near Nagasaki, is drawn before the fire by feeling for the parents who died without knowing the teaching of the Lord, and chooses deliberately to give up her faith. In her preference for love of her own dead over "correct belief," one sees Akutagawa's eye relativizing doctrine and the stories of martyrs. Read as a companion to "The Martyr."

Summary

In the Genna and Kan'ei years, when the persecution of the Christians was fierce, there lived in the village of Urakami Yamazato near Nagasaki a girl named O-Gin. Her parents had drifted there from Osaka and died without ever knowing the teaching of God; after their death she was taken in by a devout farmer, Joan Magoshichi, and his wife, was baptized, and grew up a pious believer. In time the household was found out, and Magoshichi, O-Sugi and O-Gin were all condemned to be burned. At the execution ground, Magoshichi and his wife are glad to be martyred; O-Gin alone collapses in tears before the stake. What had crossed her mind was her father and mother, who died heathen and must have fallen into hell, and who lie under the pine tree. If she goes to heaven she will never see them again — and thinking that, she cannot hold to her faith, and says that she will recant. Hearing her, her foster parents give up their faith as well and follow her. The three of them lived on; but this apostasy was handed down to later ages as the most shameful of stumbles.

Characters

  • O-Gin　A Christian girl raised in the village of Urakami, who recants before the fire out of love for the parents who died outside the faith
  • Joan Magoshichi　The devout farmer who took O-Gin in and had her baptized, and who gives up his faith after her
  • O-Sugi　Magoshichi's wife, O-Gin's foster mother

The opening (original text)

It was in Genna, or in Kan'ei — at any rate, long ago. In those days anyone who held to the teaching of God was, as soon as he was found out, burned at the stake or crucified.

Ogin by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Magoshichi fell at last. This story has been handed down to later ages as the most shameful of stumbles among all the sufferings of the many believers of our country.
