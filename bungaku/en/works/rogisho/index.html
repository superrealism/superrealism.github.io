About this work

Okamoto Kanoko's central late work, published in Chūō Kōron in 1938. Hiraide Sonoko, an aged geisha once famous under the professional name Kosono, takes into her house Yuki, a poor young man with an uncommon gift for electrical work, and tries to let him give himself to the inventions he loves without worrying about money. But given his freedom the young man only grows listless and low, and the old geisha, irritated, becomes the more attached to him. A thirst for life undiminished by age, and an old woman trying to live out her own unfulfilled dream through a young man, drawn with voluptuousness and humour. It closes on the poem — that year by year her sorrow deepens, and the more splendid her life becomes.

Summary

Hiraide Sonoko, an old geisha once famous in the quarters, went by the professional name of Kosono, and her stylish figure may still be seen at midday in the department stores. To her house, where she lives at ease on the fortune she has made, there comes one day a young workman named Yuki to repair the electrical fittings. Yuki is deft with his hands and fond of inventing, and has the misfortune of being unable, out of poverty, to carry on his researches as he would. Grudging that his gift should be wasted, the old geisha offers to have him live in her house and to see to all his needs, so that he may give himself to whatever he likes to invent. Freed from the anxiety of money, Yuki, once his freedom is given him, grows listless and sunk in gloom as though he had lost his purpose, and at times stays away from the house and behaves almost like a rake. The old geisha frets at a young man who will not go as she wishes, and though she fusses and scolds over him, she cannot hold back her growing attachment to him. To keep a young man's talent and vitality near her was also the expression of her own violent thirst for life, undiminished in age, and of a wish to live her own dream over again through a young man. The strange life the two of them share, wavering between age and youth, protection and bondage, is drawn by the author with a voluptuous sensuality and a light humor; and the story closes quietly with a poem the old geisha made: "Year by year my sorrow grows deeper, and the more brilliantly my life comes into flower."

The opening (original text)

Hiraide Sonoko is the old geisha's real name, but like the name in the family register of a kabuki actor it does not quite settle onto the person. Nor, on the other hand, does her professional name of Kosono alone suit the dignity she has today, as she comes gradually back to the plain feeling of a private person. Here it will be best simply to call her the old geisha. People often see her in the department stores at midday.

The ending (original text)

In midsummer, from the old geisha, who ought by then to have been introduced to a certain lady and to be learning haiku, there came, unusually, a sheaf of poems for the author of this story to correct. The author happened at the time to be taking the cool of the evening after his meal on the veranda from which one looks at the fountain in the inner garden that the old geisha had had made for him long ago in return for lessons in poetry; and taking the sheaf from the servant there, and listening to the sound of the water in the pond, he examined with great curiosity these poems from the old geisha, the first in a long while. Among them there is one in which her present state of mind may be seen, and I give it here. That I have made some alterations to the original is not so much a matter of the manners between teacher and pupil as simply to make the sense clearer to the reader. They are confined to matters of phrasing, and I guarantee that the substance of the original is not injured. Year by year my sorrow grows deeper, and the more brilliantly my life comes into flower.

Famous passages

With every passing year my sorrow deepens, and all the more my life comes into bloom.

A poem placed at the end of the work. It sings of the old geisha's very way of life — that both sorrow and the brilliance of life deepen the more years one accumulates.
