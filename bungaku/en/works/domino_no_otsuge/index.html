About this work

An early work Kusaka Yōko published in the coterie magazine VIKING in 1950 — written in her late teens by a writer born into a distinguished family who took her own life at twenty-one. A father gasping with chronic asthma, a mother at her devotions night after night, and Shinjirō throwing himself into gambling: in a house falling apart, only the narrator tries to read her own fate in the domino tiles. In prose that seems to tremble at the nerves, the leaning toward ruin that ran through this writer's short life is already marked.

Summary

Coming home with her footsteps muffled, "I" change quickly out of my Western clothes and open the sliding door of the room where my father lies. Beside the dim light of a lamp my father is gasping with his chronic asthma. It always comes on damp days. Though it is autumn, that year there had not been one clear, dry day; the days went on gloomy and chilly, with something clinging to the skin. My father only looks at my face without a word, and though I am used to that look I am cowed by it all the same, and bow politely and back awkwardly out of the room. Inside the house it is stifling. Every night my mother shuts herself in the room of the household altar to perform her devotions, and raises a cry like the cry of an animal. Into such a house comes Shinjirō, a man who has thrown himself into gambling and pleasure. "I" draw near him, and away, and am drawn to him again. Laying out the dominoes and telling my fortune by them, I think: when one throws oneself into a world of bold gambling, something like a white flash passes through the brain, and answers in the air to the same flash within Shinjirō, and throws up a purple spark. It is only then that I understand him, and feel that I am burning my life.

The opening (original text)

One day — With my footsteps muffled I went in from the entrance to my own room, and changing quickly out of my Western clothes I opened the sliding door of the room where my father lay. With the dim light of the lamp drawn close to his pillow, my father is gasping there. His chronic asthma always comes on upon a damp day like today.

Domino no otsuge by Kusaka Yōko (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

I cried out. The flow of time seems to have stopped. Something like a white flash such as comes when one throws oneself into a world of bold gambling runs stitching through the brain. There is the same flash within Shinjirō too, and for an instant they seem to answer each other in the air and throw up a purple spark. And — ah, it is only then that I can understand Shinjirō, and feel for myself that I am burning my life. Only then — Just at that moment an uncanny cry like the cry of an animal rises from the room of the household altar. My mother is beginning her devotions for the night.
