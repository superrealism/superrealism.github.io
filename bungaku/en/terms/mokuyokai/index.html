What is Mokuyōkai (Thursday Society)?

Category
Society / Group

A literary salon gathered around Natsume Sōseki, beginning around 1906, when Sōseki, his fame growing and the resulting stream of visitors interfering with his work, set aside Thursday afternoons as a day for callers on the advice of his disciple Suzuki Miekichi. It came to be called the "Mokuyōkai" ("Thursday Society") from the members' gathering every Thursday at Sōseki's home in Ushigome, later known as Sōseki Sanbō. Koyama Toyotaka, Suzuki Miekichi, Morita Sōhei, and Abe Jirō were called the "Four Guardian Kings," and a colorful range of figures gathered as well, including the physicist and essayist Terada Torahiko, Uchida Hyakken, Abe Yoshishige, and Iwanami Shigeo, who would go on to found Iwanami Shoten. Suzuki Miekichi made his debut with "Chidori" ("Plover"), highly praised by Sōseki, and later founded the children's magazine "Akai Tori," and Terada Torahiko opened a distinctive terrain in essays such as "Donguri" ("Acorns"), bringing a scientist's observant eye to the form. During this period Sōseki serialized "Sanshirō" and "Kokoro" in the "Asahi Shimbun." In November 1915, Akutagawa Ryūnosuke joined together with Kume Masao, and Sōseki lavishly praised Akutagawa's then-unknown "The Nose," pushing him out into the literary world. These newcomers would go on to become the Shinshichōha (New Thought school) that carried Taishō literature. The society dissolved naturally with Sōseki's death in 1916, but it remains in the history of modern literature as a place that nurtured many gifted figures.
