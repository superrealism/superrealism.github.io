What is Shiki (The Four Seasons)?

Category
Society / Group

A poetry magazine Hori Tatsuo founded as a quarterly in May 1933. It broke off after two issues, but in October 1934 it was relaunched as a monthly under the joint editorship of Miyoshi Tatsuji, Maruyama Kaoru, and Hori Tatsuo, and continued to 81 issues by 1944. In an age when proletarian literature and Modernism had each in turn retreated, it is distinguished by keeping its distance from both political ideology and the journalism of the literary establishment, seeking to defend an authentic lyric poetry grounded in a proper understanding of European poetry. Poets of the preceding generation, including Hagiwara Sakutarō and Murō Saisei, contributed as well, and Tachihara Michizō, discovered by Hori Tatsuo, who rang the sonnet beautifully in Japanese, Nakahara Chūya, drawn to French Symbolist poetry and Rimbaud, and Tsumura Nobuo and Itō Shizuo gathered here, becoming the center of a revival of lyric poetry in the 1930s. Poets affiliated with the magazine came to be called the "Shiki school." Miyoshi Tatsuji had already shown a refined lyricism in "Sokuryōsen" ("The Surveying Ship," 1930), and Tachihara Michizō's "Wasuregusa ni Yosu" ("Dedicated to the Forget-Me-Not," 1937) and Nakahara Chūya's posthumous poetry collection "Arishi Hi no Uta" ("Songs of Days Gone By," 1938) represent that delicate, musical poetic style.
