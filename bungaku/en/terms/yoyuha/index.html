What is Yoyūha / Kōtōha (School of Leisure / School of Detachment)?

Category
Literary Movement

A literary-historical term for the position of Natsume Sōseki and Mori Ōgai, who, at the end of the Meiji era, when Naturalism had strengthened its current of confessing to the dark side of life with a naked bluntness, kept their distance from it and viewed life from a height with intellect and composure. Also called the Kōtōha. Sōseki, in "Kusamakura" (1906), set out a realm of beauty and detachment from human feeling, and in "Wagahai wa Neko de Aru" ("I Am a Cat"), essayed a playful, sardonic critique of civilization, distinguishing his own style, marked by leisure and composure, from Naturalism. Ōgai, too, having served as both an army surgeon and a writer after his time in Germany, held fast to an intellectual style throughout dignified historical and biographical works such as "Sanshō Dayū." This spirit was carried on as well by disciples such as Suzuki Miekichi, who gathered around Sōseki's Thursday salon, and Suzuki went on to found the children's literature magazine "Akai Tori" and the children's-story movement. As one representative pole of anti-Naturalism, opposing Naturalism's flat depiction and its refusal of ideals or resolution, it is spoken of alongside the Aesthetic school and the Shirakaba school.
