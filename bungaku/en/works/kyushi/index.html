About this work

A short story Kunikida Doppo published in Bungei Kurabu in 1907, a central late work of his naturalism. Set at a cheap eating house near Kudanzaka, it copies down the exchanges of day-labourers living hand to mouth, and draws evenly, with sentiment refused, how one of them, Bunkō, falls ill and can no longer work, collapses and dies where he falls, without a soul taking any notice — a death in destitution. A central short story pressing on us the reality, without redemption, of those living at the very bottom of society.

Summary

A cheap eating house near Kudanzaka. On an evening at the end of spring, day laborers and casual hands — the kind who live from day to day — sit sharing cheap sake bought with a few small coins. Into this comes Bunkō, crossing the threshold with visible effort. His companions worry over his condition, for Bunkō has fallen ill, and can no longer work as he once did. With no one to rely on, growing more destitute by the day, Bunkō still drags his body about in search of day labor, if only to go on living — but his weakened body will not obey him. In time he collapses by the roadside, and, with no hand to help him, breathes his last, alone. Passengers on a train running briskly by in the morning sun glance, through the window, at the "object" covered with a straw mat beside the road. His corpse, its name and family register unknown, is given, as is customary, a temporary burial. That was the end of Bunkō — "Just as the laborer had said, Bunkō, unable to bear it any longer, simply collapsed." A masterwork of naturalism, in which Doppo, with a stark, unsentimental hand, carves out the unredeemable death met, unmourned by anyone, by one who lives at the very bottom of society.

Characters

  • Bunkō　A day laborer who falls ill and can no longer work. With no one to rely on, he grows steadily more destitute, and dies, unattended, collapsed by the roadside
  • The laborers at the cheap eating house　Fellow day laborers, living from day to day just as Bunkō does. They worry over him, but are themselves poor as well

The opening (original text)

There is a shabby eating house near Kudanzaka. On an evening at the end of spring, a man crossed its threshold, looking as though it cost him great effort. Three customers were there already. The lamp had not yet been lit, and the figures lined up on the dim earthen floor showed only dimly. The three customers already there, and the one man who had just come in, were all, every one of them, the lowest sort of laborers — day laborers, casual hands, and the like. They looked like men who could scarcely afford even cheap sake, except on an especially good day.

Kyūshi by Kunikida Doppo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Just then a train bound for Akabane came running briskly by, the morning sun falling full upon its windows. And the fireman, the driver, and the passengers alike all leaned out to look at the "object" covered with a straw mat. Since the name and family register of this "object" were unknown, it was given, as was customary, a temporary burial. This was the end of Bunkō. Just as the laborer had said, Bunkō, unable to bear it any longer, simply collapsed. (The End)
