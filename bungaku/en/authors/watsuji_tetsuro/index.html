Biography

Born in 1889 in Kanzaki District, Hyōgo Prefecture (now Himeji City). He studied philosophy at Tokyo Imperial University and took part in the second "Shinshichō" alongside Tanizaki Jun'ichirō and others. Starting from studies of Nietzsche and Kierkegaard, he went on to broaden his interests to ancient Japanese culture and Buddhist art. Known for the travel writing of "Koji Junrei" ("Pilgrimage to Ancient Temples"), which described the old temples and Buddhist statues of Nara in fresh, vivid prose, and for "Fūdo" ("Climate and Culture"), which examined the relationship between climate and human beings, he became a leading philosopher, ethicist, and cultural historian of modern Japan. He died in 1960.
