About this work

A short story published in the magazine Taikan in March 1922. Against the building of the light railway between Odawara and Atami, eight-year-old Ryōhei, who longs to ride the handcars, is taken along by two labourers until he is far from home and must run back alone through the falling dark. The turn from a child's exhilaration to sudden helplessness is famous, and together with the closing glimpse of Ryōhei as a grown man recalling that day it has long been a staple of Japanese school textbooks.

Summary

Ryōhei was eight in the year the work of laying the light railway between Odawara and Atami began, and every day he went out to the edge of the village to watch the handcars, longing to push one just once. One evening he pushed one in secret and was scolded by the labourers; then, a little more than ten days later, he met two young labourers who let him push with them. On and on they went, past orange groves and bamboo thickets and along a road with the sea in sight, and Ryōhei was in raptures — but go as far as they might, there was no sign of turning back. When they told him in front of a teashop, "You go on home now. We're stopping over on the other side tonight," he was dumbfounded for a moment and close to tears. The sun was going down. Ryōhei threw off his coat and his straw sandals and ran and ran along the road he had come. The moment he burst in at his own gate he broke at last into loud crying, and though the grown-ups around him asked what was the matter, when he thought back on the loneliness of running that long road no amount of crying was enough. At twenty-six, come up to Tokyo with a wife and child and working as a proofreader at a magazine office, Ryōhei still finds himself remembering the boy he was that day, for no reason at all. Before him, worn out with the drudgery of living, a road with dim thickets and slopes still runs on, thin and unbroken.

Characters

  • Ryōhei　An eight-year-old boy who longs to ride the handcars. The protagonist
  • The two young labourers　They let Ryōhei push the handcar with them, and at dusk tell him to go home alone

The opening (original text)

It was in the year Ryōhei turned eight that work began on laying the light railway between Odawara and Atami. Every day Ryōhei went out to the edge of the village to watch the work. The work — though all it amounted to was carrying earth on handcars: that was what was fun, and that was what he went to see.

Famous passages

It was the year Ryōhei turned eight that construction began on the light railway between Odawara and Atami.

An opening that fixes time and place by way of the boy's age. The anxiety of the long walk home after riding a handcar too far becomes a memory he never forgets, for the whole of his life.

Before him, worn out by the toil of the world, there still runs—just as it did then—a single thin path through dim thickets and up slopes, breaking off and running on.

The close of the story. The grown-up Ryōhei recalls the forlorn road home after riding a handcar too far as a boy, and the story ends by laying life's anxieties over that path.
