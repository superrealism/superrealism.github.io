About this work

A memoir Ozaki Kōyō dictated late in life and published in the magazine Shinshōsetsu in January 1901. He tells how the literary society he helped to found, the Ken'yūsha, came into being — from his meeting with Yamada Bimyō to the launch of its journal Garakuta Bunko. A first-rate document of literary history.

Summary

Kōyō begins with the remark that although he has long wished to put together a chronicle of the Ken'yūsha, his memory has faded and the materials are scattered; and then he tells how it began. About the fifteenth year of Meiji, while at the Second Middle School of Tokyo Prefecture, Kōyō became friendly with Yamada Bimyō (Yamada Taketarō), a boy reputed for his many talents. Out of that friendship a circle grew, in which congenial companions such as Ishibashi Shian gathered and showed one another what they had written. This came in time to be named the Ken'yūsha, and led to the founding of its magazine, the Garakuta Bunko. Kōyō speaks fondly of the beginnings of the society and of the changes its magazine went through, touches on how over the long years his companions scattered into various roads — some into business, some into official posts, some no one knows where — and lays down his pen thinking of what is still to come for the Ken'yūsha.

Characters

  • Yamada Bimyō (Yamada Taketarō)　The many-talented schoolfellow with whom Kōyō became friendly at the Second Middle School of Tokyo Prefecture, and who was the occasion of the founding of the Ken'yūsha
  • Ozaki Kōyō　The narrator himself, recalling how the Ken'yūsha was founded

The opening (original text)

I have long had it in mind to make a chronicle of the Ken'yūsha; but since what was written has been scattered and lost, and since there are many facts that have gone out of my memory altogether, I could never write anything adequate with my own pen alone. Unless I inquire of the people who were coming and going in those days and gather the facts from every side, I cannot write anything that deserves the name of a history.

Ken'yūsha no enkaku by Ozaki Kōyō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

As for the history of the Ken'yūsha, I mean to set it out in much greater detail another day; here I have only sketched the changes of its magazine, and even that is not at all to the point. Since I had made no preparation to speak, I beg your pardon for as much as this.

Ken'yūsha no enkaku by Ozaki Kōyō (Aozora Bunko)
