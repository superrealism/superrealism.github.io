About this work

A short piece published in Shinchō in January 1918. Having lost his wife Yasuko to tuberculosis two years before, the author writes in the form of a letter to the three small sons she left. He tells them without concealment how their mother fell ill and died, and closes: the road ahead is long, and dark; but do not be afraid, for the road opens before those who are not afraid. Love and encouragement from father to sons, in dignified prose. Long used for recitation and as a school text.

Summary

A time will come when you are grown and read this writing — so the father begins, speaking to his three small sons. Last year you lost for ever the only mother you had. The marriage, the life in Sapporo, your births. After the third son was born, tuberculosis had settled in your mother's chest. The diagnosis, the move to Kamakura for her health, and then the long illness in a hospital room. Even on her deathbed she spoke of wanting to see you, and to the end would not, for fear of infection. In the letter she left a few days before she died, her love for you, small as you were, overflowed. Having watched a single thread of smoke go up from the crematorium chimney, your father is about to start out again in the world along with you. However great a failure my own life may be, you will find the road you are to walk from the tracks I leave. "The way ahead is long. And it is dark. But you must not be afraid. The road opens before those who are not afraid. Go. Bravely. Little ones."

Characters

  • The father (I)　The narrator, who sets down memories of his dead wife and gives heart to his small children
  • You　The three small sons who have lost their mother
  • Your mother　The wife, struck down by tuberculosis, who leaves her love for her children in the letter she writes

The opening (original text)

When you have grown up and become men in your own right — whether your papa will still be alive then or not, I cannot say — I think there will be a chance for you to open out and look through the things your father left behind. And at that time this small piece of writing too will appear before your eyes. Time goes on and on.

The ending (original text)

Little ones. Take the blessing of your unhappy and at the same time happy father and mother into your breasts, and set out on the journey of human life. The way ahead is long. And it is dark. But you must not be afraid. The road opens before those who are not afraid. Go. Bravely. Little ones.

Chiisaki mono e by Arishima Takeo (Aozora Bunko)

Famous passages

O little ones. Take to your journey through the human world clutching to your hearts the blessing of your father and mother, unhappy and yet happy both. The road ahead is long. And it is dark. But you must not be afraid. For those who do not fear, the road opens before them. Go. Go bravely. O little ones.

The closing of a letter addressed to his young children, who had lost their mother. A father's prayer, hammered home in a series of short imperatives, has continued to encourage readers ever since.
