About this work

A children's story published in the magazine Akai Tori. Kaminagahiko, a young woodcutter as gentle as a woman, lives at the foot of Mount Katsuragi in Yamato. A fine flute player, he takes three marvellous dogs given him by the god of the mountain and rescues two abducted princesses: a simple, warm and happily ending adventure.

Summary

Long ago, at the foot of Mount Katsuragi in the province of Yamato, there lived a young woodcutter named Kaminagahiko. He was called so because his face was gentle as a woman's and his hair, too, was long as a woman's. He was very good at the flute, and when they heard it the birds and beasts and even the grasses and trees listened enraptured. One day Kaminagahiko is given three wonderful dogs by the god of the mountain: Sniff, Leap and Bite. With the strength of these dogs he sets out on an adventure to rescue the two daughters of the Minister of Asuka, who have been carried off by ogres. But then two young samurai appear, plotting to steal the credit of his deed... A simple, warm-hearted fairy tale. Their scheme is seen through by the dogs, and Kaminagahiko becomes the Minister's son-in-law. Which of the two princesses became his bride, however, "being a matter of long ago, is not clearly known even now" — so the tale ends. A charming adventure written by Akutagawa for children.

Characters

The opening (original text)

Long ago, at the foot of Mount Katsuragi in the province of Yamato, there lived a young woodcutter named Kaminagahiko. His face was gentle as a woman's, and his hair besides was long as a woman's, and so he had been given this name. Kaminagahiko was very good at the flute, so that even when he went up the mountain to cut wood, in the intervals of his work he would take out the flute he carried at his waist and enjoy the sound of it by himself.

Inu to fue by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

What happened after that need hardly be told. Kaminagahiko was given a great many rewards, and became the son-in-law of the Minister of Asuka; and the two young samurai, chased about by the three dogs, fled headlong out of the mansion. Only, which of the princesses became Kaminagahiko's bride — that alone, being after all a matter of long ago, is not clearly known even now.

Inu to fue by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)
