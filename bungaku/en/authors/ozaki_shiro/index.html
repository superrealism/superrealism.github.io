Biography

Born in 1898 in Yokosuka Village, Hazu District, Aichi Prefecture (now Kira-chō, Nishio City). By an odd coincidence, his home village was the domain of Kira Kōzuke-no-suke, the villain of the Chūshingura legend, and this later gave him grounds for works seeking to clear that name of its infamy. He studied politics at Waseda University but dropped out, threw himself into the socialist movement, and then turned to writing after winning a prize-fiction contest. The autobiographical epic novel "Jinsei Gekijō" ("The Theater of Life"), serialized in a newspaper from 1933, was an unprecedented sensation, portraying the youth of Aonari Hyōkichi and the chivalrous outlaws he lived among, and made him a nationally popular writer. He continued to produce many robust, warmly humane works drawing on history, biography, and autobiography, and was beloved as a writer who bridged popular and serious fiction. He was also known as a devoted sumo enthusiast, and was Kawabata Yasunari's lifelong close friend. He died in Tokyo in 1964 at the age of 66.
