About this work

A grotesque detective story for boys that Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shin Shōnen in 1937. Sugihara Atsuo, a science student, goes home on bad news from his native place deep in Hokkaido, and faces the uncanny business of a bottomless marsh and the dark secret of his family. A piece full of suspense, set in a valley where corpse-fires flicker.

Summary

A telegram reaches Sugihara Atsuo, a student at Tokyo University of Science, from his sister back in his home village deep in Hokkaido: "MOTHER DEAD FATHER DYING COME AT ONCE." What awaited Atsuo, who hurried home not knowing what to make of it, was the uncanny business called the "corpse-fire of Sesshō Valley," and a dark secret bound up with his family. A corpse-fire flickering by the edge of a bottomless marsh, one inexplicable event after another — Atsuo, relying on his scientific knowledge, closes in on the truth of the uncanny happenings. At the close of the story, the old man Heizō holds out a hat that had been floating on the marsh and says, "However much we search, we can't find the master from the west house — this hat was floating on the bottomless marsh, so it may well be that he's..." "So be it. That is the fittest grave there could be for my uncle. God rest his soul in peace." Cause and effect come full circle, and evil sinks its own reward into the bottomless marsh. An early piece of Yamamoto's for boys, set deep in Hokkaido and drawing out the uncanny and the thrilling.

Sugihara Atsuo, a student at Tokyo University of Science, received a telegram from his sister back in his home village deep in Hokkaido, to the effect — (MOTHER DEAD FATHER DYING COME AT ONCE, URGENT) Atsuo murmured, his face full of bewilderment. "What on earth is this?"
