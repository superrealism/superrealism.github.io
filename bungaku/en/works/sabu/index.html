About this work

A long novel serialized in Shūkan Asahi in 1963. Set among the mounters and paperers of Edo, it draws the friendship between Eiji, a skilled but proud young craftsman, and his slow, clumsy fellow-worker Sabu. Framed for the theft of a customer's goods and sent to the labour camp, Eiji sours and grows wild against the world. That hardened heart is thawed, little by little, by Sabu's almost witless honesty and by the feeling of the women who worry over him. That no one lives alone, and that it is a fine thing to trust and to forgive, set in the world of the downtown craftsman: one of Yamamoto's central works.

Summary

Eiji and Sabu are fellow apprentices, living in at the Hōkodō, a paperer's shop in Shitaya in Edo. Eiji is good-looking and skillful, with a strong will to win. Sabu is slow in body and slow in mind and is forever being made a fool of, but he looks up to Eiji as to an elder brother. Just as the two are about to become full craftsmen, Eiji is suspected of stealing an article left in his charge at a customer's house, and unable to clear himself he is sent to the labor camp on Ishikawajima. Cast down by a crime he did not commit, Eiji comes to hate the world and everyone in it, and rages behind an armored heart. He quarrels with the rough men of the camp and grows stubbornly isolated; and still Sabu believes in him without a doubt, and comes over to the island again and again to see him. To O-Sue, who is in love with him, and to O-Nobu, who worries over him like a sister, and to the unchanging sincerity of the women around him, Eiji at first keeps his back turned. But as he sees how the people he meets in the camp live, and feels the kindness of those who will not give him up, the ice of his hardened heart begins little by little to melt. In time the truth behind the false charge comes to light, and Eiji, who has learned what it means to trust and forgive and hold one another up, gains the strength to live again and leaves the camp. At the end of the story, from outside the rain shutters, Sabu's voice rings out.

Characters

  • Eiji　A young craftsman at the Hōkodō, a paperer's shop. The hero of the work, skillful but proud, sent to the labor camp on a false charge of theft and turning bitter against the world
  • Sabu　Eiji's fellow apprentice. Slow in body and mind and despised by others, but honest to the point of foolishness, believing in Eiji and looking up to him always
  • O-Nobu　A woman who worries over Eiji like a sister and goes on supporting him
  • O-Sue　A girl who is secretly in love with Eiji

The opening (original text)

On an evening when a fine rain hung like mist, Sabu was crossing the Ryōgoku bridge from west to east, crying as he went. He wore a kimono of futako stripe, a narrow Kokura-weave sash, and a faded black apron, and he was wet from the head down. Because he kept rubbing at his face, sodden with rain and tears, with the back of his hand, there were black smudges around his eyes and on his cheeks. He was a squat, thickset fellow with a round face and a pointed head. — When he had crossed the bridge, Eiji came after him from behind.

Sabu by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Just then there came a knocking at the rain shutters at the front. "Ei-chan," said a voice outside. "Are you there, Ei-chan? I'm back." "It's Sabu," said Eiji. "Forgive me, Ei-chan," came the voice from outside the shutters. "My mother was in such a state, we thought she'd breathe her last any minute, any minute, and it went on being put off until today. I'm sorry, Ei-chan, forgive me. It's me, open up here, it's Sabu."

Sabu by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
