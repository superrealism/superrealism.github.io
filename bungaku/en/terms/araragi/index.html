What is Araragi?

Category
Society / Group

A tanka magazine and poetry society whose roots lie in "Ashibi," the organ of the Negishi Tanka Society formed in 1903 by disciples of Masaoka Shiki. After "Ashibi" ceased publication, Itō Sachio, Warabi Shin'ichirō, and others founded "Araragi" in October 1908; the following year it merged with Shimagi Akahiko's magazine "Himuro" and took the name "Araragi." Committed above all to carrying Shiki's principle of "shasei" (sketching from life) through fully into tanka, it built, after Sachio's death, the largest force in the tanka world under the leadership of Saitō Mokichi and Shimagi Akahiko and came to be known as the Araragi school. Nagatsuka Takashi, also a disciple of Sachio, is known as the author of the novel "Tsuchi" ("The Soil"), which depicted rural life in a style of thoroughgoing sketch-based realism. The magazine continued for nearly a century, until it ceased publication in December 1997, and is counted among the coteries with the greatest influence on modern and contemporary tanka.
