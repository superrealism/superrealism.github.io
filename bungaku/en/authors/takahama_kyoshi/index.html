Biography

Born in 1874 in Matsuyama, Iyo Province (now Matsuyama, Ehime Prefecture); his real name was Takahama Kiyoshi. He studied haiku under his fellow townsman and senior, Masaoka Shiki, who also gave him his pen name, "Kyoshi." After Shiki's death he took over the editorship of the haiku magazine "Hototogisu," and, championing "kyakkan shasei" (objective sketching from life) and "kachō fūei" (the poetic celebration of birds, flowers, and the natural world), built one of the great schools of modern haiku. Ranked alongside Kawahigashi Hekigotō as a haiku poet, he also tried his hand, at Shiki's urging, at sketch-based prose, and left novels such as "Ikaruga Monogatari" ("The Tale of Ikaruga") and "Haikaishi" ("The Haikai Master"). He is also known for publishing Natsume Sōseki's "Wagahai wa Neko de Aru" ("I Am a Cat") in "Hototogisu," launching it into the world. He died in 1959 at the age of 85 and received the Order of Culture.
