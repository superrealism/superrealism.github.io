What is Dōjin Zasshi (Coterie Magazines)?

Category
Other

A magazine that like-minded people pool their money to edit and publish themselves. In Japan this begins with the Meirokusha's "Meiroku Zasshi" (1874–75), and as a literary magazine, the pioneer is generally taken to be Ozaki Kōyō and the Ken'yūsha's "Garakuta Bunko" (from 1885). Because it offers a place to write freely, unbound by the demands of commercial publishers, new currents in modern literature have often arisen from just such magazines. Mushanokōji Saneatsu and Shiga Naoya's "Shirakaba" ("White Birch"), Akutagawa Ryūnosuke and Kikuchi Kan's "Shinshichō," Yokomitsu Riichi and Kawabata Yasunari's "Bungei Jidai," Kajii Motojirō and others' "Aozora," and Yasuda Yojūrō and others' "Nihon Roman-ha" are all examples of coterie magazines that set literary history in motion. The path by which unknown writers honed their skills through criticism from fellow members, and works published there were then discovered by literary magazines and prizes, endured for a long time, and the Akutagawa Prize has always considered works published in coterie magazines eligible for selection. That "Bungakukai" ran a "Coterie Magazine Review" column surveying magazines from around the country for more than half a century, beginning in 1951, speaks to this tradition.
