About this work

A short story Oda Sakunosuke published in Shin Bungaku in 1946. Holding no more than sixty-three sen, the narrator sets out to walk the railway line from Osaka to Tokyo to see the woman he loves — and he looks back in a light voice on a reckless life full of wandering and failure, against the downtown quarters of Osaka. A father who was a rakugo performer, the women who left, and a present that has come to nothing. A fine piece of Oda's Osaka writing, setting down in humour and sadness the ordinary person who lives as precariously and as easily as an advertising balloon floating in the sky.

Summary

At that moment, "I" had only sixty-three sen to my name. Clutching that small sum, I resolved to walk all the way from Osaka to Tokyo along the railway line — driven by nothing but the single-minded wish to see Fumiko, the woman I loved, who was in Tokyo. It was, by any sane measure, out of my mind, but reckless impulse and a longing for the wandering life were, from birth, simply my nature. The story follows, in a light, easy telling set against the backdrop of Osaka's working-class quarter, the wandering, failure-strewn course of "my" life from birth onward: a father who had been a rakugo storyteller, women who left me one after another, a string of jobs I drifted through — hospital handyman, canvasser for a savings company. Unable to master polite speech and often provoking others to anger without meaning to, "I" go on living, unbothered, in my own carefree way. Having sent my father off and laid his bones to rest, "I" find myself, for a moment, looking back on my present, going-nowhere life. But by now I have no great ambitions left, and my heart, if anything, has grown all the calmer for it. A fine piece of Oda's "Osaka fiction," depicting, with humor and pathos, the fragile yet buoyant lives of ordinary people, drifting like an ad balloon adrift in the sky.

Characters

  • I　A man of Osaka, born with a reckless streak and a wandering nature. Chases after Fumiko, the woman he loves, drifting carefree through one job after another
  • Fumiko　The woman "I" longs for. Lives in Tokyo
  • Father　A rakugo storyteller. "I"'s father, whose final years are spent together, and who is eventually sent off

The opening (original text)

At that time, I had only sixty-three sen on me. And with only sixty-three sen to my name, I was resolved to walk all the way from Osaka to Tokyo — which, thinking back on it now, seems hardly the act of a sane man, but at the time, to me, it seemed the most natural thing in the world.

Ad Balloon by Oda Sakunosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Leaving the teahouse, I walked toward the cable-car landing, listening to the cicadas, and as I watched my father's aged wife's wrinkled face, trotting along to keep up with me, a thought suddenly came to me — that I ought to be a good son to this old woman, after all. And before I knew it, I found myself humming, under my breath, "Today too, up in the sky, a balloon..."
