What is Shigarami Sōshi?

Category
Society / Group

A monthly literary magazine founded and edited by Mori Ōgai in October 1889, upon his return from studying in Germany. The title carries the spirit of "damming up the currents of the literary establishment," like a weir across a river. Ōgai wielded modern Western aesthetics to develop vigorous criticism of the literary establishment, and from 1891 into the following year he waged the "Botsurisō Debate" with Tsubouchi Shōyō over whether art requires ideals. Against Shōyō's stance ("botsurisō") of wishing to assess a work as it was, Ōgai, drawing on Hartmann's aesthetics, argued that art cannot do without ideals, lighting the fuse of full-scale literary criticism in modern Japan. It also served as a stage for creative writing and translation: Ōgai's own Romantic novel "Utakata no Ki" ("A Record of Foam on the Waves," 1890) and his translation of Andersen into dignified classical prose, "Sokkyō Shijin" ("The Improvisatore," from 1892), were serialized here. It ceased publication in August 1894 after 59 issues in all, with Ōgai's departure for service in the First Sino-Japanese War, and its activity was carried on by the successor critical journal "Mesamashigusa."
