About this work

The man at the bicycle shop is going back to the country, and after him a man who has come from the sea takes over the shop — a small piece by Ogawa Mimei drawing, through the eyes of the town's children, a life in which people leave and people arrive. The passage in which a man who used to dive in the sea breaks his health on land and comes to want to go back to it stays quietly with one.

Summary

The man who runs the bicycle shop is moving back to the countryside. He is a man Seikichi and Shōji have grown quite fond of, who has mended their tricycle and fixed their rubber ball when it went flat, and who is a friend, too, of Isamu, the boy of that household. His father has decided to go back, he explains, because his own grandfather in the countryside has grown too old to be left alone. "Neither I nor my mother wants to go," Isamu says, but Seikichi and Shōji both think Isamu's father is doing right by his own parent. An uncle, a relative of the family, is to take over the shop, and Isamu says he will come back to visit and play. Before long, the new man who has come to run the shop turns out to be someone who once worked diving in the sea. Though his body was toughened by years of diving work, once he came fully up onto land his body no longer obeyed him — "It's a frightening thing, isn't it, how a man who's spent his life diving loses the use of his body once he comes all the way up onto land." So he says, and confides that he has come to want to go back to the sea and dive again — that this, he thinks, will make his body strong once more. "When that happens, you two come and visit me. There's always a breeze on the beach, and it's cool there in summer. I'll feed you shrimp, octopus, all kinds of fresh fish." He says the shop will likely be taken over again by the family who ran it before. Hearing this, Shōji smiles and says, "Then we'll be able to play with Isamu again after all." But as they leave the shop, Seikichi, walking along, glances back at Shōji and says, "I'm sad to say goodbye to the man." Dragonflies were flying overhead.

Characters

  • Seikichi and Shōji　Children of the town
  • Isamu　The bicycle-shop owner's son. Moves back to the countryside
  • The New Man　A man who once worked diving in the sea

The opening (original text)

The man who ran the bicycle shop was going to move back to the countryside. He was a man Seikichi and Shōji had grown quite fond of. He had mended their tricycle for them, and fixed their rubber ball when it went flat. And they were friends, too, with Isamu, the boy of that household. The man loved dogs and cats. It seems that good people are always fond of living things.

Umi ga yonda hanashi by Ogawa Mimei (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Then we'll be able to play with Isamu again after all," Shōji said, smiling. As they left the shop, "I'm sad to say goodbye to the man," Seikichi said, glancing back at Shōji as they walked. Dragonflies were flying overhead.
