Biography

Born in 1875, the eldest son of a druggist family in Handa Village, Chita District, Aichi Prefecture (now Handa City); his real name was Katō Isoo (born Oguri). From boyhood he immersed himself in Edo-period popular fiction, and going to Tokyo in 1891 he became a disciple of Ozaki Kōyō in the Ken'yūsha, rising to prominence as one of the outstanding talents of Kōyō's school alongside Izumi Kyōka, Tokuda Shūsei, and Yanagawa Shunyō. While inheriting his master's mixed classical-colloquial style, he came under the growing influence of Naturalist writers such as Tayama Katai and leaned increasingly toward realism, establishing a style that lovingly depicted the lives of ordinary people. The trilogy "Seishun" ("Youth"), serialized in the Yomiuri Shimbun in 1905-1906, was widely read by young readers and became his signature work; other notable works include "Fukagawa Nyōbō" ("The Wife of Fukagawa") and "Renbo Nagashi" ("A Drifting Love"). In 1900 he married into the family of a wealthy Toyohashi household and took the name of his adoptive family; he spent his later years in quiet retirement in Toyohashi. He died in 1926, at the age of 50, of pericarditis complicated by pneumonia.
