About this work

A short story Arishima Takeo published in the coterie magazine Shirakaba in 1910. The title is the slang term for the dock labourers who knock the rust off a ship's hull with a chisel. Set at Kherson, a Russian port on the Black Sea that the author saw on his way to study in America, it draws in a strong hand, through the narrator's eyes, the rough labourers sweating over the cargo work and the huge Yakov Ilyich, who stands out strangely among them. A central early work by Arishima, who began as a writer of the Shirakaba group, in which the vitality of those living at the bottom and his eye for the base of society already show strongly.

Summary

In the scorching heat of a Russian summer, at the port of Kherson. "I" have spent a week now among the band of dock laborers known as "kankanmushi" — those who chisel the rust from ships' hulls. Amid these rough men, "I" feel an irresistible pull toward Yakov Ilyich, a giant of a man with an extraordinary body and strange countenance. In the searing heat and sweat of the loading yard, amid endless curses and brawls, the laborers chafe against the authority of the foremen and police, yet go on, wordlessly, hammering at the iron. Some incident sets the whole yard into an uproar, and under the strict watch of a police inspector and his constables who rush to the scene, the laborers have their dangerous tools confiscated and are moved onto the great barge. Held down by authority, yet smoldering underneath with resentment and rebellion — one of them, glared at by a constable, mutters "damn it" as though spitting, and lowers his head in disgust. Arishima, with a dry, sinewy pen, carves out the wildness and pathos of the lowest laborers living in a foreign port.

Characters

  • I　The narrator, who has joined the loading yard for a week now. Observes the rough laborers around him
  • Yakov Ilyich　A giant of a man with an extraordinary body and strange countenance, giving off a mysterious pull
  • Yefim　One of the kankanmushi (dock laborers)

The opening (original text)

The waters of the Dnieper estuary seemed to boil under the unrelenting heat of August, every muddied, mingled color driven into hiding; only fierce, single hues, like something out of a Monet canvas, dyed sea and sky and ship and man alike, dazzlingly vivid, and over the whole of it the midsummer light poured down as though wrapping it in a single embrace. It was just the hour for the midday meal, the sun at its very zenith, the shadows of things shrunk small and dense as though scorched into the ground, and the heat was such that even to look at it made one's eyes ache and sting. Having put away my lunch box, I sat facing the sea, side by side with a companion of mine, on the gunwale of the great barge moored fast alongside the cargo ship Odessa Maru. Companion, or should I call him boss — in any case, he was someone I had come to know since arriving here a week before.

The ending (original text)

"Heh — making such a fuss over nothing," a voice said, and turning to look, I saw a man, a triangular cloth tied over his round black cap and knotted beneath his chin, glancing about him with a look of discontent — but when he noticed a constable looking down at him, he stared back at him steadily, and, as though spitting something out, "Damn it," he said, and, with a look of disgust, lowered his head.
