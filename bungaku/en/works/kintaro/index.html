About this work

One of Japan's best-known folk tales, ranking with Momotarō and Issun-bōshi, retold for children by Kusuyama Masao. A boy of prodigious strength, raised by a mountain witch on Mount Ashigara in Sagami Province, plays with a bear, a monkey and a hare for retainers, until he is found by Usui Sadamitsu, a retainer of Minamoto no Yorimitsu, and goes up to the capital to take the name Sakata no Kintoki and become one of Yorimitsu's Four Guardian Kings. The line that runs from the familiar figure with an axe on his shoulder astride a bear to the legend of the real warrior Sakata no Kintoki is set down neatly in a very few pages.

Summary

Deep in the mountains of Ashigara in Sagami Province there lived a boy named Kintarō with his mother, a mountain witch. He was strong from birth, and at seven or eight could lift a millstone or a bale of rice husks without trouble and was not thrown in wrestling even by grown men. When there was no one left to match him he would run about the forest all day, shouldering the great axe his mother had given him and felling cedars and pines for the fun of playing at woodcutter. One day a bear sprang at him, telling him not to spoil the forest; Kintarō hooked its leg and threw it, and the bear put both paws to the ground in apology and became his retainer. Monkeys and hares and even deer joined the company, and Kintarō became captain of the whole mountain, making them wrestle and laying felled trees across the valleys for bridges. And there came past Usui Sadamitsu, a retainer of Minamoto no Yorimitsu and one of his Four Guardian Kings. Seeing the child's strength with the axe and the beasts at his heel, Sadamitsu begs the mountain witch to let him make a warrior of him. Sorry as she is to part with him, the mother sends him up to the capital. Taken to Yorimitsu's house, Kintarō is told that Kintarō is no name for a samurai, and that since his father was a Sakata he should call himself Sakata no Kintoki — and in time he becomes one of the Four Guardian Kings.

Characters

  • Kintarō　The boy of prodigious strength raised by the mountain witch on Mount Ashigara, later Sakata no Kintoki
  • The mountain witch　Kintarō's mother
  • The bear　Thrown by Kintarō, it becomes his retainer
  • Usui Sadamitsu　One of Minamoto no Yorimitsu's Four Guardian Kings. He finds Kintarō and takes him to the capital
  • Minamoto no Yorimitsu　He gives Kintarō the name Sakata no Kintoki

The opening (original text)

1 Long ago there was a strong boy named Kintarō. He was born deep in the mountains of Ashigara in Sagami Province, and lived there with his mother, the mountain witch. Kintarō was strong from the day he was born, and by the time he was seven or eight he could lift a millstone or a bale of rice husks without turning a hair. He was not thrown in wrestling by most grown men either. When there was no one left in the neighborhood to match him it grew dull, and Kintarō would run about the forest all day long. He would walk with the great axe his mother had given him over his shoulder, felling huge cedars and pines wherever he liked, and amuse himself playing at woodcutter.

Kintarō by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

After many, many days on the road, Sadamitsu brought Kintarō back to the capital. He went to Yorimitsu's house and said: "Deep in the mountains of Ashigara I found this child, and have brought him with me." And he showed Kintarō to Yorimitsu. "Well now, this is something rare — a fine strong-looking boy," said Yorimitsu, stroking Kintarō's head. "But Kintarō is an odd name for a samurai. If your father was a Sakata, then from today you shall call yourself Sakata no Kintoki." And so Kintarō took the name Sakata no Kintoki and became Yorimitsu's retainer. And when he grew up he became a great warrior, and with Watanabe no Tsuna, Urabe no Suetake and Usui Sadamitsu he came to be called one of Yorimitsu's Four Guardian Kings.
