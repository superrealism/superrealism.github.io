What is Gazoku Setchū (Mixed Elegant-Colloquial Style)?

Category
Style & Technique

A prose style that fuses elegant, classical Japanese diction in the narrative passages with everyday colloquial language in dialogue, blending refined literary language with the plain speech of ordinary life within a single text. It traces back to Ihara Saikaku in the early modern period, and in the 1890s Ozaki Kōyō learned from Saikaku and refined it, making it the standard style of Ken'yūsha writers. Kōyō deployed a florid, realistic mixed style, capturing the subtleties of human feeling, in "Ninin Bikuni Iro Zange" ("Two Nuns' Confessions of Passion") and his unfinished last work "Konjiki Yasha" ("The Golden Demon"). Higuchi Ichiyō, who likewise learned from Saikaku, dissolved the vivid spoken language of the old downtown into an elegant prose with the rolling rhythm of seven-five meter in "Takekurabe" ("Growing Up") and "Nigorie" ("Troubled Waters"), achieving one peak of this style. It also connects to the dignified classical style of Kōda Rohan's "Gojū no Tō" ("The Five-Storied Pagoda") and other works. As a style bridging the tradition of early modern literature and the psychological depiction of the modern novel during the transitional period before genbun'itchi prose became widespread, its significance in literary history is considerable.
