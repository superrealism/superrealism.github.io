About this work

A boys' novel by Satō Kōroku, serialized in "Shōnen Kurabu" from 1927 into the following year, which was an explosive success. The title comes from "Aa Gyokuhai ni Hana Ukete," the dormitory song of the old First Higher School. Aoki Senzō, fifteen, who lives by selling tofu in the town of Urawa, endures insult with neither money nor parents, and keeps a thirst for learning hidden in his chest. Through his meetings with the middle-school boy Yanagi Kōichi, with the hermit-like Mokumoku-sensei, and with the rough Sakai Iwao, the boys learn what a true hero is. With this serial the circulation of "Shōnen Kurabu" is said to have leapt from three hundred thousand to four hundred and fifty thousand.

Summary

On a spring morning Aoki Senzō, the little tofu boy, hurries toward the next town with his carrying pole on his shoulder. He lost his father at two and was raised with his mother on his uncle's charity. According to his uncle, the large house where a doctor now lives was built long ago with his father's money. "I want to win this house back and put my mother in it" — so Senzō thinks whenever he stands in the onion field. But at fifteen he has not grown, and in town he is caught by Sakai Iwao, son of the deputy at the town hall and mad about judo, who lifts him by the ears, eats the skin off his grilled tofu and says he will pay next time. Boys go to middle school because they have money, boys go to middle school because they have parents; am I to carry tofu and blow a trumpet all my life? Senzō's chest burns with anger as he blows the trumpet with the tears running down. Before long he meets Yanagi Kōichi, a middle-school boy. Boys of different circumstances and different tempers, they quarrel, they fight, and they come to acknowledge each other. "Tonight your speech has taught me what a true hero is" — and the story moves toward a night on which the song of the jeweled cup is sung aloud.

Characters

  • Aoki Senzō (the little one)　A boy of fifteen who works at a tofu shop. With neither money nor parents, he will not give up his hope of learning
  • Yanagi Kōichi　A middle-school boy. After quarreling with Senzō, he becomes his closest friend
  • Sakai Iwao　Son of the deputy at the town hall. A rough boy, who later begins to study furiously
  • Yasuba　A student of the First Higher School, who expounds to the boys what a hero is
  • Mokumoku-sensei　The man who guides the boys

The opening (original text)

1 The little tofu boy is hurrying along the paths between the paddies toward the next town. The fresh morning sun of spring leaves the wood and pours down a rain of golden light on the green barley fields, on the yellow rape fields, on the paddies red with milk vetch; the grasses on the path wake from the night's dew and lift their heads lightly; the violets open their pale purple doors, and the dandelions hold up their orange crowns.

The ending (original text)

Readers, the number of installments has its limit, and here I lay down my pen. If you wish to know how the people of this story fared, ask about those who were in the dormitory of the First Higher School six years ago. From which window, and in what voices, did Aoki Senzō and Yanagi Kōichi put out their spirited faces and sing of the jeweled cup? And ask at the same time how a young man nicknamed the Savage — Sakai Iwao, who entered a year behind them — studied so furiously that he went up to the university at the head of his class.
