About this work

A detective story Oguri Mushitarō published in the magazine Shin Seinen in 1933, notable as the first appearance of his prodigiously learned detective Norimizu Rintarō, who takes on the inexplicable death of a temple abbot in a locked room. A bold trick turning on a buddha's halo and on rays of light, worked through with wide knowledge of religion, art and optics: the starting point of Oguri's mysteries, representative of the heterodox school of Japanese detective fiction.

Summary

Kōnosu Tairyū, abbot of the Gōrakuji on Mount Fugen and known also as the painter who threw away his brush, has met a strange death. The body, found still with its hands joined in prayer before the Buddha, has no wound on it, and there is no clear trace of poison. Norimizu Rintarō — formerly director of the bureau of investigation, now a first-rate criminal lawyer and a great detective — is asked by Prosecutor Hasekura to come to the scene, and fixes upon the secret of the images handed down in the temple and of the light that falls into the hall. Unraveling the discords among those concerned, in matters of faith and of art, Norimizu exposes the fearful device of a murder hidden in the phenomenon of the "halo." At the end of a chain of reasoning that makes full use of a wide learning in optics and Buddhist art the truth of the case is revealed, and the criminal, driven into a corner, meets a terrible end before Norimizu's eyes. The first appearance of the great detective Norimizu Rintarō.

Characters

  • Norimizu Rintarō　A great detective, formerly director of the bureau of investigation and now a criminal lawyer, who meets hard cases with an enormous learning. This is his first appearance
  • Prosecutor Hasekura　The prosecutor who asks Norimizu's help in the investigation, and who often acts with him thereafter
  • Kōnosu Tairyū　The abbot of the Gōrakuji on Mount Fugen, known also as the painter Katayama, who dies strangely with his hands joined in a sealed room
  • Shizukuishi Kyōson　A painter spoken of in the same breath as Tairyū, and a friend of Norimizu's, who lives next to the Gōrakuji

The opening (original text)

Norimizu Rintarō, formerly director of the bureau of investigation and now a first-rate criminal lawyer, had to get to the bottom of why a new spirit had departed on the day when the invited spirits go. For on the morning of the sixteenth of July Prosecutor Hasekura had told him by telephone that Kōnosu Tairyū, the abbot of the Gōrakuji on Mount Fugen — or rather, as he is better known, the painter Katayama, who threw away his brush — had met a strange death.

The ending (original text)

No sooner had he said it than Sakurō turned suddenly and ran to the fuse box behind him. The glass burst with a bang, and at the same instant Norimizu shut his eyes without meaning to. A flash pierced his eyelids, and he heard a tearing cry; and an instant later the room held nothing but the smell of scorched hair, and a stillness as at the bottom of water. Taking a high-tension current at the temple, this young avenger never came back to life.
