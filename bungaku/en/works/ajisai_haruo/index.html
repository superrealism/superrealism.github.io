About this work

A woman who lost her husband to drowning, and the man who has gone on caring for her. Behind the quiet talk they hold in the room with the household altar, the presence of the dead man slowly rises — a small uncanny piece by Satō Haruo. Nothing that could be called uncanny ever appears, and yet the last line runs a chill down the back: a piece only a master of the short story could bring off.

Summary

"If he hadn't died so suddenly, the way he did" — the woman says to the man, her eyes brimming with tears. Had it come after a long illness, she says, he surely would have told them plainly, one way or the other, whether what lay between the two of them was permissible or not. Seven years have passed since her husband drowned, and the man has remained unmarried ever since. Her husband had known of the bond between them, though he never spoke of it aloud — and it was surely for that very reason that he had grown all the gentler with her. The thought leaves the woman unable to decide what she ought to do. The man, for his part, carries words of his own that he cannot decide whether to speak or to keep silent. He has thought, more times than he can count, how he wished the husband would simply die — and he cannot say for certain that, even at the very moment the man was drowning, some part of him, far away, had not been thinking exactly that. In the six-mat room before the household altar, the woman sits a little apart from the pillow of her pale, wan six-year-old daughter, plucking softly at a shamisen, her eyes fixed on the tatami. The man, sensing someone approaching along the veranda, sits up — but there is no one there. At that moment the sleeping child suddenly bursts into a fit of piercing sobs, and, without so much as looking at her mother's face, cries out again and again: "Papa! Papa! Papa! ..." She won't sleep anywhere but here, the woman says — anywhere but the room with the altar. The woman turns, on a sudden impulse, to look behind her — but there is nothing there. Only the sickly child, her black eyes grown large and strangely transparent with illness, staring on and on past her mother's shoulder into one corner of the room.

Characters

  • The woman　Lost her husband to drowning seven years ago
  • The man　Has gone on loving the woman all this while
  • The sickly child　A six-year-old girl who will not sleep anywhere but the room with the household altar

The opening (original text)

—If he hadn't died so suddenly, the way he did — if, say, it had come after a long illness instead — I'm sure he would have told us plainly, one way or another, whether it was all right, or whether it must never be, about you and me... That's the feeling I can't seem to shake.

Ajisai by Satō Haruo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The woman found herself turning, on a sudden impulse, to look behind her. But there was, of course, nothing there. Only the sickly child, wasted thin, her black eyes grown larger and more transparent than ever, staring wide-eyed on and on past her mother's shoulder, into the empty air, into one corner of the room—

Ajisai by Satō Haruo (Aozora Bunko)
