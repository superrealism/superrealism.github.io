About this work

A long novel Ranpo serialized in Omoshiro Kurabu in 1955, a late work with an abnormal man called the Shadow Man for its protagonist — a criminal who moves like a shadow along the underside of the world and lives by peeping into other people's secrets. The terrible trade of murder to order, and a world of the grotesque and of crime, are drawn in Ranpo's own weird hand. A scroll of crime seen from the side of evil, differing in flavour from the stories in which a great detective works.

Summary

"The Shadow Man" — a man who moves like a shadow along the underside of the world, and finds an abnormal joy in secretly peeping into other people's secrets. Opening with a scene in which he breaks into a hotel manager's office, this story draws out the dark world of the grotesque and of crime in which the Shadow Man becomes entangled. What eventually comes to light is the existence of a hair-raising underground trade: "murder for hire." A terrifying criminal organization that has carried out more than twenty contracted murders — and how the men at its centre come to tumble down into ruin. At the story's close, a police van carrying three abnormal criminals draws near the moat where the red brick of the Metropolitan Police headquarters can be seen. One pictures a phantom gallows in his mind; another refuses to think any further — carrying the "three separate thoughts of three uncommonly abnormal criminals." A distinctive long work from Ranpo's later years, differing in flavour from his tales of a great detective's deduction, peering instead into the darkness of humanity from the side of evil and crime.

The opening (original text)

A thin man who looked to be thirty-two or thirty-three came in through the entrance of the Chō Hotel and walked straight into the manager's office behind the counter. The manager, short and squat, plump and round, with a little moustache, sat at his desk fiddling with the ledgers. From a cigar, burned down to about half, resting in an ashtray beside him, a thin thread of purple smoke rose almost straight up.

Kage otoko by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The three criminals were picturing a bare, cheerless holding cell in their minds. Then the scene of a prison rose up before them. The Shadow Man and the man with the little moustache thought no further than that, but Suhara, the small man, alone pictured a phantom gallows. Carrying the three separate thoughts of three uncommonly abnormal criminals, the bus was already drawing near the moat where the red brick of the Metropolitan Police headquarters could be seen.
