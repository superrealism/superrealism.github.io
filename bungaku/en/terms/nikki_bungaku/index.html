What is Nikki Bungaku (Diary Literature)?

Category
Form / Genre

Works that entrust literary expression to the form of recording, day by day, the events and movements of the heart. In Japan it developed together with kana writing in the Heian court, and Ki no Tsurayuki's "Tosa Nikki" ("The Tosa Diary," around 935), narrating, under the guise of a woman, his journey home to the capital from Tosa, is regarded as its starting point. Thereafter, women's diaries followed one after another — Fujiwara no Michitsuna's Mother's "Kagerō Nikki" ("The Gossamer Diary"), and diaries by court women such as Izumi Shikibu, Murasaki Shikibu, and Sugawara no Takasue's Daughter, recording, in delicate prose, half a lifetime, love, and the days of court service — building one province of court literature. In the modern era, the diary form, recording fact just as it is, drew renewed attention as a vessel directly reflecting a writer's inner self. Nagai Kafū's "Danchōtei Nichijō," kept for more than forty years, is the crowning achievement of literary diary-keeping, and Hori Tatsuo retold the "Kagerō Nikki" as a modern novel in "Kagerō no Nikki," while Tanizaki Jun'ichirō depicted an old man's obsession in diary form in "Fūten Rōjin Nikki" ("Diary of a Mad Old Man"): the classical diary literature continued to inspire modern writers richly.
