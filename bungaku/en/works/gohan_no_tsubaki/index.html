About this work

A long historical novel serialized from January to September 1959 in the monthly Kōdan Kurabu. Oshino, whose honest and gentle father died in misery because of her dissolute mother, gives herself over to revenge on her mother and her mother's lovers. Around the formal beauty of a series of murders, each scene left with a single mountain camellia — the five-petalled camellia her father loved — and the investigation of the town officer Aoki Sennosuke, it sets out quietly both anger at people who have lost all restraint and the emptiness of private vengeance. Filmed and dramatized many times, and one of Yamamoto's central novels.

Summary

O-Shino, the only daughter of the Musashiya, a drug wholesaler in Nihonbashi, deeply reveres her adoptive father Kihei, who lives wholly for the business. But her mother O-Sono is an unfaithful woman who trades on her beauty and goes from man to man; she leaves the nursing of the consumptive Kihei to her daughter and amuses herself with her lovers at the villa at Kameido. At her father's deathbed O-Shino learns that she is not his child at all, but the child of her mother and Genjirō, the master of the Marume. And her mother is not in the least ashamed of it. Unable to forgive a mother who tramples on the law of human beings and laughs at it, O-Shino sets fire to the villa, buries her mother in it, and disappears, making it appear that she too burned to death. With the eight hundred ryō her father left, she rents a house, traces what has become of the men who had to do with her mother, chooses the five whose guilt is heaviest, lures them one at a time with a woman's charm and kills them, leaving each time the five-petaled camellia her father loved. Aoki Sennosuke, a police officer following the chain of strange killings, comes at last to see that the murderer is O-Shino. But Genjirō, her real father, she does not kill: she understands that to put the truth before him and leave him a burden he can never be rid of is a heavier punishment than death. Killing is neither punishment nor atonement — realizing this, O-Shino writes out for Sennosuke a confession of the six deaths, her mother's among them, and asks to give herself up. But rather than be beaten by the terror of execution and by her own reluctance, she stabs herself in the breast in a private room at a restaurant, leaving the wish that the examination of her body should prove she had remained an unspoiled girl. Sennosuke, coming to her, swears before that dead face, which seems to smile peacefully, that Genjirō at least shall be made to know his own guilt.

Characters

  • O-Shino　The only daughter of the Musashiya, eighteen years old. The heroine, who reveres the father who raised her and gives herself to revenge on her mother and her mother's lovers
  • Kihei　The father who raised O-Shino. An honest man married into the Musashiya who lived only for the business, and who falls ill of consumption
  • O-Sono　O-Shino's mother. An unfaithful woman who trades on her beauty and has no thought for her husband
  • Aoki Sennosuke　A police officer following the chain of murders, who understands what O-Shino means
  • Genjirō　The master of the Marume, a paper dealer in Yorozu-chō in Nihonbashi. O-Shino's real father
  • O-Masa　A servant at the Musashiya's villa
  • Sakichi　A pleasure-boat boatman, who knows what became of her mother's lovers

The opening (original text)

On the sixth of the same first month, four days after Butsuan died, the Musashiya's villa burned down in a fire of its own, and three bodies were taken from the ruins. Perhaps because goods from the oil business they also kept had been stored there, the fire had run on the oil inside the building; even the pillars had burned clean away, and of the three bodies one could hardly tell man from woman.

Goben no tsubaki by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"O-Shino, there is nothing I can say to you," he whispered to her dead face. "Whether what you did was right or not, I do not know; but that you wanted to do it, that you could not help doing it — that much is true. And if it is true that you could not help it, there is no need to repent of having done it." He closed his eyes and went on. "Genjirō of the Marume I take upon myself; I shall make him taste the full flavor of his own guilt. You must be at your father's side by now; you need not torment yourself or fear people's eyes. Rest quietly beside your father. There is no one left to trouble you now."

Goben no tsubaki by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
