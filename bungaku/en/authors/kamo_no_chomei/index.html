Biography

Born around 1155, the son of Kamo no Nagatsugu, a Shinto priest (negi) at the Shimogamo Shrine in Kyoto. Skilled in waka poetry and music (the biwa), he was chosen as a member of the Bureau of Poetry under Retired Emperor Go-Toba, but after failing to advance in the Shinto priesthood as he had hoped, he took Buddhist vows around the age of fifty and adopted the religious name Ren'in. He withdrew to a ten-foot-square hermitage he built on Mount Hino outside Kyoto, and in that seclusion wrote the essay "Hōjōki" ("An Account of My Hut"). "Hōjōki," which contemplates natural disasters and the impermanence of human life, is counted among Japan's three great classical essays alongside Sei Shōnagon's "The Pillow Book" and Yoshida Kenkō's "Essays in Idleness." He also left the treatise on poetics "Mumyōshō," the Buddhist tale collection "Hosshinshū," and the personal poetry collection "Kamo no Chōmei Shū." He was also a poet whose waka were included in imperially commissioned anthologies. He died in 1216.
