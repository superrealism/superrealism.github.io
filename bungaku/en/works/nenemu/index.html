About this work

Nenemu, a monster child who loses his parents to famine and has his little sister carried off by a slaver, rises through his training as far as the office of judge of the world — a long children's story Miyazawa Kenji wrote as a young man. Held to be the prototype of "The Life of Gusukō Budori": an unfinished draft full of invention and humour.

Summary

A great famine strikes the world of monsters. Nenemu's father goes into the forest, and his mother out into the fields, in search of food, and neither ever returns — the two of them have gone off to "the paradise of the monster world." A sharp-eyed man appears before the young Nenemu and his little sister Mamimi, left behind alone, and, as though in exchange for food, puts Mamimi into a basket and carries her away. Nenemu, wandering the forest in search of his sister, collapses, and is taken in by a suspicious gentleman who calls himself a "kelp gatherer," and set to the strange labor of harvesting invisible kelp. Before long Nenemu escapes that life, throws himself into his studies, becomes the greatest prodigy in the whole world, and at last is appointed to the highest post in the monster world: Chief Judge of the World. His duty is to judge cases of "manifestation" — instances in which monsters show themselves in the human world. Wielding his authority with a firm hand as chief judge, praised by his subordinates, Nenemu one day, dancing with joy, finds his own foot straying, ever so slightly, toward the "wrong direction." Before he knows it, he finds himself atop a mountain pass leading from Nepal into Tibet, a Tibetan demon-warding banner fluttering before his eyes. He himself has become a manifestation. "Today I must put myself on trial" — and there, at that line, the manuscript breaks off. An early long work of Kenji's, it already shows, in outline, the very bones that would later be carried forward into "The Biography of Gusukobudori."

The opening (original text)

One: The Independence of Pennennennennennen Nenemu [Several pages of the opening manuscript lost.] ...it was. Indeed, before Lady Kire rose, the eastern sky would fill, amber as beer. But even so, summer came all the same, and every monster began to stir and clamor.

Pennennennennennen Nenemu no denki by Miyazawa Kenji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Ah, thank you. It's nothing now. But in the end, I have manifested myself after all. Today I must put myself on trial. Ah, I shall resign my post. And from tomorrow, for a hundred days, I shall clean the monster world's university. Ah, everything, everything is finished." Nenemu could not help but weep. His thirty subordinates wept aloud together with him. That sound rippled out across the ground, non-non-non-non, and by the time it reached Sanmutori beyond, Sanmutori raised a pillar of red fire and let off its fifth eruption. "Gaan, dorodorodorodoro." A gust of wind blew through, and the broken Clarais flowers trembled and shook. [No further manuscript.]

Pennennennennennen Nenemu no denki by Miyazawa Kenji (Aozora Bunko)
