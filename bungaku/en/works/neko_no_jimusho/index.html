About this work

A children's story published in the magazine Getsuyō in 1926, subtitled as a fantasy concerning a small government office. In the sixth cat office, which studies the history and geography of cats, a "stove cat" whose body is grimed with soot from birth is bullied without reason by the other clerks and shut out from among them. Through to the closing of the office at a single roar from a passing lion, Kenji indicts the unreason of bullying and discrimination, quietly but sharply, in the form of a fable.

Summary

Near the station of the light railway stood the Sixth Cat Office. Its business was chiefly the study of the history and geography of cats, and the four clerks in their short black satin coats were respected by everyone and envied by all. The chief was a large black cat; the clerks were a white cat, a tabby, a calico, and the fourth clerk, the hearth cat. A hearth cat is one that feels the cold and always sleeps inside the cooking stove, so that its whole body is dirty with soot. Because of the look he was born to, the hearth cat was disliked and bullied by the other clerks. One day, taking advantage of his staying away with a cold, the others speak ill of him to the chief and gradually shut him out. Against their groundless charges the hearth cat can only weep. And then a great lion, passing by and seeing all this, cannot bear it and roars at them: "What are you doing? With that sort of thing there is no need of geography or history. Stop it. Hah. I order this office dissolved." And so the cat office was abolished. "I am half in agreement with the lion." It is a fable in which Kenji indicts the injustice of bullying and discrimination.

Characters

  • The hearth cat　The fourth clerk, whose body is dirty with soot because he sleeps in the cooking stove, and who is bullied by the other clerks for the look he was born to
  • The chief (the black cat) and the three clerks　The cats who dislike the hearth cat and shut him out
  • The lion　Who passes by, sees the injustice of it, roars at them and orders the office dissolved

The opening (original text)

Near the station of the light railway there was the Sixth Cat Office. It was chiefly a place for studying the history and geography of cats. The clerks all wore short black satin coats, and they were so much respected that whenever one of them gave up his post for any reason, all the young cats round about would flutter and scramble for a chance to take his place.

Neko no jimusho by Miyazawa Kenji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"What are you doing? With that sort of thing there is no need of geography or history. Stop it. Hah. I order this office dissolved." And so the office was abolished. I am half in agreement with the lion.

Famous passages

I half agree with the lion.

The last line, added by the narrator after the lion dissolves the cats' office, where bullying has run rampant. In the reservation “half” lies Kenji's complicated feeling, which will not let the story end as a simple tale of good rewarded and evil punished.
