About this work

A short story written by Yokomitsu Riichi, the central figure of the New Sensationalists, out of his own experience of nursing his wife as tuberculosis wasted her. Published in the magazine Josei in August 1926. In a house by the sea in Shōnan it renders, in acute sensory figures and restrained dialogue, the days of a wife long in bed and a husband taken up with caring for her. It follows quietly the weariness and the love, the quarrels and the reconciliations of a couple facing a death that comes closer by the hour, and closes with the spring that arrives at last after winter, carried in a bouquet said to come by coach. Loss raised into poetry, and a requiem for the wife he loved.

Summary

In a house among the pines by the shore, the husband lives at the side of his wife, long confined to her bed. She has tuberculosis and grows weaker by the day. Driven between earning their living by his writing, the nursing of an invalid, and the sense of a death coming closer, the husband grows irritable, and weariness and quarrels throw their shadow between them. Even so, the two look at the small things around them — the tortoise in the garden, the pine needles, the waves coming in and going out — exchange aimless words, and get through the long winter caring for each other. Both know that death is near and neither says it outright, and under their restrained talk a deep affection passes between them. Then the winter ends, plum and camellia bloom in the garden, and one day when the light has begun to hold a hint of spring, the husband buys a bunch of flowers on his way home for his wife. "These flowers came riding in a carriage, scattering spring along the shore ahead of everything else" — and taking the bouquet, she holds it to her thin breast with both arms, buries her pale face in the bright flowers, and closes her eyes in rapture.

Characters

  • The husband　A man who goes on writing while nursing his sick wife in a house by the shore. The central figure, swinging between irritation and deep love for a dying wife
  • The wife　Struck down with tuberculosis and long confined to bed, she exchanges quiet words of care with her husband out of a failing body

The opening (original text)

The pines along the shore had begun to sound in the winter wind. In a corner of the garden a clump of small dahlias was shriveling. From beside the bed where his wife lay, he watched the dull shape of the tortoise in the garden pond. When the tortoise swam, the bright reflection thrown up from the surface of the water wavered over the dry stones.

Haru wa basha ni notte by Yokomitsu Riichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"These flowers came riding in a carriage, scattering spring along the shore ahead of everything else." Taking the bouquet from him, his wife held it to her breast with both arms. And then, burying her pale face among the bright flowers, she closed her eyes in rapture.

Famous passages

These flowers came riding in a carriage along the seashore, the very first of all, scattering spring as they came.

The close of the story. The words the husband speaks as he hands his bedridden wife a bouquet that has arrived for her. This line, announcing the coming of spring to a wife near death, gives the work its title.
