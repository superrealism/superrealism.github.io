What is Shizen-shugi (Naturalism)?

Category
Literary Movement

A literary current that sought to depict the reality of human beings and society just as it was, with idealization stripped away. It derives from the Naturalism championed by France's Zola, which observed human beings scientifically through heredity and environment, but in Japan it developed along its own distinctive path, one that prized the unvarnished confession of the writer's own life. Shimazaki Tōson lit the fuse with "Hakai" ("The Broken Commandment," 1906), depicting the anguish of a young teacher of outcaste birth, and the direction was fixed when Tayama Katai candidly exposed a middle-aged writer's infatuation with a young female disciple in "Futon" ("The Quilt," 1907); under the banner of "flat description" — "depicting reality just as it is" — and of having neither an ideal nor a resolution, it dominated the literary establishment of the late 1900s. Tokuda Shūsei, Masamune Hakuchō, Iwano Hōmei, and Chikamatsu Shūkō were its leading practitioners, and Kunikida Doppo, who depicted the nature of Musashino in fresh prose, is counted among its forerunners as well. This stance of staring straight at reality deepened the realism of the modern novel and became the wellspring of Japan's distinctive I-novel, but the reaction against its narrow range of subject matter and its gloom gave rise to the various currents of anti-Naturalism — the Yoyūha, the Aesthetic school, the Shirakaba school — and it went into decline once the Taishō era began.
