Biography

Born in 1868 in Kanda, Edo, Musashi Province (now part of Chiyoda Ward, Tokyo); his real name was Taketarō. While a student at the First Higher Middle School he founded the literary group Ken'yūsha with Ozaki Kōyō, Ishibashi Shian, and others, entering the literary world through the coterie journal "Garakuta Bunko." In 1887 he published the historical novel "Musashino," drawn from the strife of the Nanboku-chō period, in the Yomiuri Shimbun, drawing attention as a pioneering attempt at genbun'itchi, prose using the copula "da" or "de aru" close to spoken language. With subsequent works such as "Kochō" ("The Butterfly") and "Ichigo Hime" ("Princess Strawberry"), his distinctive genbun'itchi style and dialogue continued to strike contemporary readers, and, alongside Futabatei Shimei, he stood for a time at the forefront of the genbun'itchi movement. He later experimented further, shifting to a "desu" style, and was also a pioneer of Japanese dictionary-making, compiling the "Nihon Dai Jisho." He was also a vigorous poet and critic, but, weighed down by his falling-out with Kōyō and personal misfortune, he gradually drifted from the literary world, and died in obscurity in 1910 at the age of 42.
