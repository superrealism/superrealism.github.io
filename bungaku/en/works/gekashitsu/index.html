About this work

One of Izumi Kyōka's important early stories, published in the magazine Bungei Kurabu in June 1895. A countess refuses anaesthesia before her operation, and the surgeon holds a secret love for her: a novel of ideas, and one of the works that made Kyōka's name. Its spare classical prose and dramatic turn have kept it alive as a story of single-minded love across the boundaries of rank.

Summary

"I," a painter, press my friend Takamine, a doctor of medicine, to let me watch the operation on the Countess Kifune. Fearing that under anaesthetic she may let out her secret in her delirium, the countess stubbornly refuses to be put under. Takamine, having no choice, is about to put the knife in without anaesthetic when the countess grips his hand hard and asks whether he does not know her. In fact, nine years before, Takamine as a student and the countess had done no more than pass each other once in a botanical garden. When Takamine answers that he has not forgotten, the countess smiles, draws the knife into her own breast with her own hand, and dies. Months and years pass, and Takamine too, having never taken a wife, dies as though following her, on the same day of the year as she.

Characters

  • I　A painter. The narrator, who prevails on his friend Takamine to let him be present at the operation
  • Takamine　A doctor of medicine. He performs the operation on the Countess Kifune
  • The Countess Kifune　The patient. She refuses anaesthetic and takes her own life with a secret still unspoken

The opening (original text)

In truth it was out of curiosity; but making my being a painter my instrument, and framing some pretext or other, I forced Takamine, a doctor of medicine and as good as a brother to me, to let me see the operation on the Countess Kifune, on which he was to use his knife on a certain day at a hospital in the city of Tokyo. Leaving my house a little after nine that morning, I took a rickshaw at speed to the hospital. As I went straight toward the surgery, two or three good-looking women who might have been maids of a noble house came out through the door and passed me in the middle of the corridor.

The ending (original text)

Outside the garden two tall, well-fed horses were standing, and three grooms were resting by a carriage with ground glass in its windows. In the nine years that followed, until that affair at the hospital, Takamine never said one word to me even about that woman; and although in age and in position he was a man who ought to have had a household, he had no wife to keep his house, and his conduct was more strictly correct than it had been in his student days. I shall say no more. The Aoyama cemetery and the Yanaka cemetery — the places differ, but they died one after the other on the same day. I put the question to the religious men of the world: are those two guilty of a sin, so that they may not go to heaven?

Famous passages

“Does it hurt?” “No — because it is you, because it is you.”

A single exchange on the operating table between a countess who has refused anesthesia and the surgeon operating on her. This brief exchange reveals a relationship built on a single instant of love, hidden for nine years.
