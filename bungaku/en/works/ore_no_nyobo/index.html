About this work

"There you go doing what nobody asked for; kindly stop" — morning in the tenement begins with the high-pitched scolding of the wife of the painter Hirano Matagorō. A nagging, sharp-tongued wife and a painter piling up debts. While the other tenants watch the pair with warmth, things roll off in an unexpected direction. A piece of Yamamoto Shūgorō's human-feeling writing in which the whole tenement blows its nose at the words "I'm glad you came back, Oishi."

Summary

Morning in that tenement begins with the shouting heard from the house of the painter Hirano Matagorō. "There you go meddling again; leave it alone, what are you doing there?" — a shrill, wonderfully fluent, rapid scolding. At that signal noises begin here and there, and the whole tenement wakes with "Get up, will you; it has begun at the master's place." ... Gohei, the agent of the tenement, lives with his wife O-Katsu and sells brushes and ink and paper and inkstones; but that is pocket money for a retired man, and it seems he is supported monthly by a son who keeps a paper wholesaler's in Kanda. He is a little talkative, and it is trying that he presses verse-making and challenges anyone at all to chess; but behind the scenes he looks well after those in trouble. Matagorō is behind with his rent and owes him this and that besides, and Gohei has never once shown a sour face. He even encourages him: "Whatever a man does, he cannot do good work unless he knows real hardship." In time something happens to Matagorō. Some said that a man called the young prodigy of the Kanō school and marked by the world as the leader of a new way in painting could bear no more with those nerves of his; but Gohei alone says, "Mr. Hirano will certainly come back," and declares that he will keep the house untouched and wait however many years it takes. At the close of the story Matagorō, holding up his wife who has come home, laughs with forced brightness and says: "Come, let us go inside — and after so long, let us have a good, brisk scolding again."

Characters

  • Hirano Matagorō　A painter living in the tenement
  • O-Ishi　Matagorō's sharp-tongued wife
  • Gohei　The agent of the tenement, who looks after those in trouble

The ending (original text)

"Say nothing. I shall no doubt have to go on being poor; and the only one who will be poor for my sake is you — thank you for coming back, O-Ishi." O-Ishi began to weep violently again, and weeping, clung to Matagorō's hand.
