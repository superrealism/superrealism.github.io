About this work

A detective adventure story for boys that Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shōnen Shōjo Tankai in 1930. Haruta Ryūsuke, a boy who boasts that he can break any cipher, takes on a spy conspiracy over the invention of a submarine after picking up a sheet of coded writing. An early piece for boys, full of cipher-breaking and breathless action.

Summary

At midday in July, students practicing football on the grounds of the prefectural middle school find a strange scrap of paper. "Looks like some kind of cipher!" — everyone runs to the school building to show it to Haruta Ryūsuke, a boy who has always boasted that he can solve any cipher another person makes. Handed the coded message beneath the wisteria trellis, Ryūsuke sets at once to decoding it. What that scrap of paper leads to is a terrifying spy conspiracy surrounding a secret submarine invention by Ryūsuke's father, Dr. Haruta. Facing the enemy's evil designs on the film recording the doctor's invention, on Major General Yamakawa's important documents, and on Ryūsuke's own kidnapped little sister, the boy detective Ryūsuke fights back with code-breaking skill, quick wit, and courage. Even "American Sōta," once a delinquent youth, is bested and reformed by Ryūsuke, becoming a stalwart ally. After a breathless, thrilling struggle, the film, the documents, and his beloved sister are all safely recovered, thanks to Ryūsuke's exploits. "My boss is Ryūsuke, and I'm American Sōta — bring on your arrows, bring on your guns!" — so Sōta, now Ryūsuke's loyal assistant, always tells his friends. An early boys' adventure by Yamamoto, full of ciphers and daring.

Characters

  • Haruta Ryūsuke　A boy skilled at breaking ciphers
  • American Sōta　A young man who reforms and becomes Ryūsuke's assistant
  • Dr. Haruta　Ryūsuke's father, inventor of the submarine

The opening (original text)

"What could this be?" The midday July sun beat down on the grounds of Prefectural Middle School No. X. In that dazzling glare, four or five second-year students, absorbed in fierce football practice, now stood clustered in a corner of the track, gathered around a scrap of paper, arguing heatedly about something.

Ayaushi! Sensuikan no himitsu by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The film recording Dr. Haruta's invention, Major General Yamakawa's important documents, and Ryūsuke's own beloved little sister were all safely recovered, thanks to young Haruta Ryūsuke's exploits. After that, American Sōta gave up his life as a delinquent and went to work as Ryūsuke's loyal assistant. And he always tells his friends the same thing: "Heh — my boss is Ryūsuke, and I'm American Sōta. Bring on your arrows, bring on your guns!"
