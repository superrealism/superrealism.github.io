What is Keimō-shugi (Enlightenment Thought)?

Category
Literary Movement

An intellectual movement in the early Meiji era that introduced Western modern thought, scholarship, and institutions to promote the civilization of the nation. Growing out of the body of Western-learning scholars from the closing years of the shogunate, it was centered on the Meirokusha, formed in 1873 by Mori Arinori and others, and enlightenment through practical, accessible prose was carried out on the pages of its organ, the "Meiroku Zasshi." Fukuzawa Yukichi, in "Gakumon no Susume" ("An Encouragement of Learning," 1872–76), declared that "Heaven does not create one person above or below another," and, in plain prose, appealed broadly to the general public for the importance of practical learning and the independence and self-respect of the individual, becoming an unprecedented bestseller. Nakamura Masanao's translation of "Self-Help" as "Saigoku Risshihen" is likewise counted among works of the same current. This movement was more a matter of thought and education than literature proper, but in preparing the ground for Tsubouchi Shōyō's theory of realism — the argument that fiction should escape the moralistic "reward good, punish evil" of gesaku and depict human feeling and social conditions realistically — it holds a place at the starting point of modern Japanese literature.
