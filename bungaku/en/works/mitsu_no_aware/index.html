About this work

A novella Murō Saisei published in Shinchō in 1959: an unusual late work, full of fantasy and sensuality, suggested by a single goldfish and written entirely in dialogue. It consists only of the playful talk between an old writer, called uncle, and Akako, the red goldfish he keeps, who has taken the form of a girl. Age and youth, life and death, the real and the visionary dissolve together as sweetly as honey, and the life of the voluptuous, endearing goldfish girl is sung as something frail. Counted among the high points of Saisei's work.

Summary

"Good morning, uncle" — so calls out "me," a voluptuous, endearing girl named Akako. But her true nature is that of a single red goldfish, kept by the old writer she calls "uncle," who has taken the form of a girl. Almost the entirety of this story is composed of nothing but the playful, teasing dialogue between the old writer and Akako, the goldfish girl. Akako, precocious like a young woman, alluring, and yet innocent, clings to old "uncle," cajoling him, teasing him, and exchanging words about life and death. Around the two of them pass the ghost of a dead woman — Auntie Tamura, who appears every evening at five — and various other shadows of people as well. Age and youth, life and death, reality and illusion melt sweetly together as honey in their dialogue, and the life and death of the goldfish girl are sung as something frail. To one who is vanishing into the darkness of the burned-out ruins, Akako calls out, "Even when spring comes, I won't be dying, so..." A distinctive masterpiece full of fantasy and sensuality, written entirely in dialogue by the old writer Saisei in his last years, taking a single goldfish as his inspiration.

Characters

  • Akako　A voluptuous, endearing girl. In truth a single red goldfish kept by the old writer, taken the form of a girl
  • Uncle　The old writer who keeps Akako. Exchanges playful, teasing dialogue with the goldfish girl
  • Auntie Tamura　The ghost of a dead woman who appears every evening at five
