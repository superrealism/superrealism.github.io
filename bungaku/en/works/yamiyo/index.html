About this work

A novella Higuchi Ichiyō serialized in the magazine Bungakukai from July 1894. A girl living alone in the ruined mansion of a fallen great house takes in a man who has collapsed at her gate, and from that plots revenge on the enemy who ruined her father. The cold heart of a woman who uses the devotion of an innocent young man as an instrument of revenge, and the dark end it comes to: a strikingly gloomy work even among Ichiyō's.

Summary

In the deserted, decaying Matsukawa mansion, Oran lives with only the elderly couple Sasuke and Osoyo — the daughter of a father who ruined himself through failed speculation, a beautiful yet cold and enigmatic girl. One night, a young man named Takagi Naojirō, struck down by a passing carriage before the mansion's gate, collapses there; the old manservant takes him in. Born into the house of a renowned physician but orphaned early and spurned by the world, his heart grown twisted, Naojirō comes to adore Oran, who nurses him, as though she were a bodhisattva in the flesh, and in time vows to give his very life to her. But what Oran had kept hidden in her heart was a vow of revenge against Namisaki Hyō, an old acquaintance who had once fed off the Matsukawa family and driven her father to take his own life, and who now thrives as a prosperous member of the Diet. Learning of Naojirō's single-minded love for her, Oran, in order to carry on her father's dying wish, entrusts to Naojirō the task of assassinating Namisaki. Whispering to him that it matters not whether he succeeds, only that he escape and return to the mansion, she sends him off; and Naojirō, swearing that he will neither flee nor go into hiding, vanishes into the dark of night. He strikes at Namisaki before his gate, but his blade, dulled, only grazes his cheek, and the assassination attempt fails; the culprit is never identified, and even the rumor of it dies away. Three months later, the mansion has been renovated and passed to a new master, and both Oran and Naojirō have vanished, no one knows where.

Characters

  • Oran　The daughter of a ruined mansion, her father having taken his own life after a failed speculation. A cold woman who uses the love of the young man she nurses back to health, Naojirō, as a tool in her revenge against her father's enemy, Namisaki
  • Takagi Naojirō　A young man Oran rescues after he collapses before the mansion's gate. Though born into the house of a renowned physician, he was orphaned and grew twisted of heart, and vows to give his life to Oran
  • Namisaki Hyō　A member of the Diet and an old acquaintance who once fed off the Matsukawa family and drove Oran's father to suicide. The target of Oran's revenge
  • Sasuke and Osoyo　An elderly couple serving in the Matsukawa mansion

The opening (original text)

The mansion's grounds, said to run to who knew how many tsubo, were shut in behind a great gate that had stood closed ever since some storm of years past had left it looking as though it might, at any moment, come crashing down. No pine tree stood there, but grass grew thick upon the roof tiles, its name — "shinobu," the fern of longing — asking whose past it was that lingered here. In the season when the autumn of Miyagino, land of the crying stag, might well have been transplanted whole into this little grove of bush clover, alone in boasting its brocade of color, there had once been a time when nobles of the cloud-borne court above had sat upon a mat spread out to view the moon.

Yamiyo by Higuchi Ichiyō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Some said it must have been a hot-blooded young partisan of one faction or another; others said that someone belonging to such-and-such a club had come under suspicion and been hauled off by the authorities — but in the end no one could say whose doing it truly was, and within a month even the rumors had left no trace behind. The wound took a good half-month to heal, a pity for so fine a man's handsome face — though even if a scar remained, it would be a strange thing indeed to boast of a wound taken face-on. While the clever gentleman, the shrewd gentleman, rejoiced in his ten-thousandfold good fortune, the one who had once again botched the deed and now lived as a man shunned by the light of day, though he still walked this earth, found the whole world too narrow to hold him — what, then, became of Naojirō? Had he sunk beneath some river, or hidden himself away in the mountains? Or had his heart undergone some sudden change, so that he became, at last, an honest man in truth? That much remains uncertain. And stranger still is what became of the Matsukawa mansion. Some three months after these events, the gate stood handsomely repaired, its broken paving stones set right once more, and day after day gardeners and carpenters came and went in numbers — a sign, surely, that the house had passed to a new master. And as for old Sasuke and his wife, and Oran too — where might they have gone? The world is a wide place, after all, and by this time the railway ran the length of the whole country.

Yamiyo by Higuchi Ichiyō (Aozora Bunko)
