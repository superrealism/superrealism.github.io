What is Chūō Kōron?

Category
Society / Group

A general-interest magazine founded in 1887 in Kyoto as "Hanseikai Zasshi," the organ of a self-cultivation society for young Nishi Honganji Buddhist monks called the Hanseikai, and retitled "Chūō Kōron" in 1899. Under the celebrated editor Takita Choin it reached a golden age in the Taishō era, expanding its literary pages and repeatedly bringing in up-and-coming writers such as Tanizaki Jun'ichirō, Shiga Naoya, and Akutagawa Ryūnosuke, building a reputation as the gateway to the literary establishment. A great many representative works of modern literature were published in its pages, including Mori Ōgai's historical fiction "Okitsu Yagoemon no Isho" ("The Testament of Okitsu Yagoemon") and "Takasebune" ("The Boat on the Takase River"), Kikuchi Kan's "On to no Kanata ni" ("Beyond Forgiveness and Enmity"), and Tanizaki's "Shunkinshō" ("A Portrait of Shunkin") and "Sasameyuki" ("The Makioka Sisters"). In 1944 it was forced to cease publication in the wake of the Yokohama Incident, a case of wartime press suppression, but it was revived in 1946 after the war and continues publication today.
