Famous passages by Arishima Takeo

Well-known lines from the works of Arishima Takeo.

6 passages

Descendants of Cain

Trailing a long shadow across the ground, leading his gaunt horse by the reins, he walked on in dogged silence.

An opening that depicts a family drifting to a tenant farm in Hokkaido through nothing more than a long shadow and a gaunt horse. It begins the rough-hewn life of a man who can settle into neither the land nor its people.

Chiisaki mono e

O little ones. Take to your journey through the human world clutching to your hearts the blessing of your father and mother, unhappy and yet happy both. The road ahead is long. And it is dark. But you must not be afraid. For those who do not fear, the road opens before them. Go. Go bravely. O little ones.

The closing of a letter addressed to his young children, who had lost their mother. A father's prayer, hammered home in a series of short imperatives, has continued to encourage readers ever since.

Umareizuru nayami

My friend, spring is coming. After winter, spring comes.

The ending. Words the author addresses to a young man in a Hokkaido fishing village, torn between his passion for painting and the hardships of making a living. A prayer for the spring that surely comes after suffering.

Oshiminaku ai wa ubau

The expression of love may give unsparingly. But the substance of love is that which takes unsparingly.

The passage from which the book takes its title. Overturning the common notion that love is something given, it holds that the substance of love is a power that takes — drawing others into the self in order to grow. The heart of Arishima's thought on individuality.

A Bunch of Grapes

When I was small, I loved to draw.

It opens with a recollection of a boyhood spent loving to draw. A story of the sin of theft and of forgiveness, it has long been a favorite in school textbooks.

Every autumn the bunches of grapes still turn a beautiful purple, bloomed with their fine white powder, but nowhere can I find again those beautiful white hands, like marble, that once received them.

The closing. The white hands of the teacher who forgave his theft and shared his grapes with him are recalled each autumn, together with the grapes that turn color once more.
