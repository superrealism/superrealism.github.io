About this work

A girl who comes for a single summer to a villa in a mountain village, and a red dragonfly that settles on the bamboo of the hedge. A children's story in which Niimi Nankichi draws a summer of meeting and parting through the dragonfly's eyes. The last call — goodbye, my red dragonfly — and the dragonfly left behind at the empty house leave a quiet resonance.

Summary

In an early-summer mountain hamlet, a red dragonfly perched, as always, on the bamboo of the hedge where he liked to rest. The morning-glory seeds that the villa's master had planted the summer before had sprouted again. To the house, its rain shutters closed in loneliness now that no one lived there, three or four people came one day: a pretty young lady wearing a hat with a red ribbon, her mother, and a young houseboy carrying their luggage. The red dragonfly, working up his nerve, alighted on the ribbon of the young lady's hat. Braced to be caught, he found instead that she leapt up with delight, crying, "Oh, it's landed on my hat! How wonderful!" From then on, for the whole of that summer, the red dragonfly lived close by the young lady's side. He heard her singing a song — "Dragonfly, dragonfly, red dragonfly, danger lurks among the plume grass" — perched on her shoulder, and was even chased, now and then, by the mischievous houseboy. Before long summer ended, and the feel of autumn grew stronger. One day, the young lady wore the very same red-ribboned hat she had worn on her first arrival at the villa, and a pretty dress besides. To the red dragonfly perched on her shoulder, she spoke in a small, thin voice, almost as if she were crying: "My red dragonfly... my dear red dragonfly... I'm going back to Tokyo. This is goodbye." Everyone set off walking, and the young lady looked back again and again, saying, "Goodbye, my red dragonfly..." At last her figure could no longer be seen. On lonely autumn evenings, the red dragonfly perches on the tip of a plume-grass stalk, remembering that dear young lady still.

Characters

  • The red dragonfly　A single dragonfly that perches on the hedge's bamboo
  • The young lady　A girl who spends one summer at the villa
  • The houseboy　A young man who carries their luggage

The opening (original text)

The red dragonfly circled the sky some three times, then alighted lightly on the single stalk of bamboo in the hedge where he always rested. Noon in the mountain hamlet is quiet. And the mountain hamlet in early summer is, in truth, wrapped entirely in green. The red dragonfly gave his eyes a quick, round turn.

Akatonbo by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Goodbye, my red dragonfly..." The dear young lady said this, looking back again and again. But at last, everyone's figures could be seen no more. From now on, will this house stand empty again, I wonder — the red dragonfly quietly tilted his head. On lonely autumn evenings, the red dragonfly perches on the tip of a plume-grass stalk, remembering that dear young lady still.

Akatonbo by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)
