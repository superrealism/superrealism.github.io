Biography

Born in 1903 into an Ainu family in Noboribetsu, Horobetsu District, Hokkaido. As a small child she moved to Asahikawa to live with her grandmother Monashinouku, and grew up steeped in the Ainu language and its kamuy yukar (sacred narrative songs) as part of daily life. At nineteen, an encounter with the linguist Kindaichi Kyōsuke led her to take up the work of transcribing the vanishing oral literature herself in Roman letters and translating it into Japanese. "Ainu Shin'yōshū" ("A Collection of Ainu Kamuy Yukar"), known for its preface beginning "Long ago, this vast Hokkaido was a free land for our ancestors," is the first book of Ainu literature set down by an Ainu hand. The day after she completed the manuscript, in 1922, she died of heart disease at the age of nineteen.
