What is Shaseiha (the Sketch School)?

Category
Literary Movement

A movement, from the mid-Meiji era on, that reformed haiku, tanka, and prose based on the principle of "shasei" (sketching from life), depicting a subject just as it is. Masaoka Shiki, in the pages of the newspaper "Nippon," criticized the formulaic verse of the old school and the old-school waka, and, in the 1898 "A Letter to Practitioners of Tanka," called for a return to the "Man'yōshū," lighting the fuse of tanka reform. In his final years he established, from his sickbed, the sketch-based prose of works such as "Byōshō Rokushaku" ("A Six-Foot Sickbed"), calmly depicting the flowers in his garden and his daily life. After Shiki's death, the lineage of the Negishi Tanka Society was carried on by Takahama Kyoshi and Kawahigashi Hekigotō through the haiku magazine "Hototogisu," and by Itō Sachio, Nagatsuka Takashi, and Saitō Mokichi through the tanka magazine "Araragi," and Nagatsuka Takashi depicted the reality of a farming village with sketch-based brushwork in the novel "Tsuchi" ("The Soil," 1910), while Saitō Mokichi carried the principle of shasei forward to later generations through anthologies of fine poems such as "Man'yō Shūka." A plain, realist style, it also resonates with the genbun'itchi movement of the same era.
