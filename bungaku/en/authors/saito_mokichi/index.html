Biography

A tanka poet and psychiatrist born in 1882 in Kanaame Village, Minami-Murayama District, Yamagata Prefecture (now Kaminoyama). He went to Tokyo at fifteen and was adopted into the distantly related Saitō family, who ran a hospital in Asakusa. While studying at the Tokyo Imperial University School of Medicine, he came under the guidance of Itō Sachio, led by Masaoka Shiki's theory of "shasei" (sketching from life), and entered the world of tanka. He joined the magazine "Araragi," founded in 1908, and became a central poet there alongside Shimagi Akahiko and others. His first tanka collection, "Shakkō" ("Red Light"), published in 1913, shocked the poetry world with its vital, sketch-based songs contemplating life and death, and stands as a pinnacle of modern tanka. He continued to publish collections that deepened his ideal of "shasei" — "Aratama," "Tsuyujimo," "Shiroki Yama" ("White Mountains") — while as a physician he studied in Europe and, on his return, served as director of the Aoyama Brain Hospital. He was also a vigorous writer of essays and criticism, and his "Man'yō Shūka," an accessible commentary on the finest poems of the Man'yōshū, is widely read to this day. He died in 1953 at the age of 70.
