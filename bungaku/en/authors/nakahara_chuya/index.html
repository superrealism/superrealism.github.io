Biography

Born in 1907 into a doctor's family in Yoshiki District, Yamaguchi Prefecture (now Yuda Onsen, Yamaguchi City). His younger brother's death awakened him to literature, and during his middle-school years in Kyoto he received his baptism in French Symbolist poetry and Dadaism from Tominaga Tarō. He moved to Tokyo in 1925 and formed a lifelong friendship with the critic Kobayashi Hideo. Living a wandering, dissolute life, he kept writing poetry and in 1934 published his first poetry collection, "Yagi no Uta" ("Songs of the Goat"). He is also known for his translations of Rimbaud's poetry. In 1936, the shock of losing his eldest son, Fun'ya, brought on a mental breakdown, but he continued to write the poems of his later years, which sublimated his grief. He died suddenly in 1937 of tubercular meningitis at the age of 30. Soon after his death, his second poetry collection, "Arishi Hi no Uta" ("Songs of Days Gone By"), was published through the efforts of Kobayashi Hideo, to whom he had entrusted the manuscript. In his lifetime he left more than 350 poems, and with a distinctive rhythm boldly fusing the colloquial and the classical, he remains beloved today as a towering figure in the history of Shōwa-era poetry.
