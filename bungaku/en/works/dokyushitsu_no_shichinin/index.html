About this work

Between two tables in a billiard hall, a man with a large mole between his brows lay dead of a single thrust to the heart — a short story Hashimoto Gorō published in the magazine Tantei. The windows and doors were shut and not one of the seven men present had left the room. And yet the weapon, apparently a slender dagger, was found nowhere. The narrator, who has worked out the truth, explains quietly why he nevertheless held his tongue.

Summary

A billiard room early in the evening. In the middle of a lively game at two tables, a man with a large mole between his brows had been killed between the tables. A single thrust through his mojiri coat, and the blood welling out about the heart. ... A policeman came running from the box, the party from the district station arrived, and a formal investigation began. The victim was one Fukuhara, thirty-six or seven, a regular with no settled trade. That he was killed by another is plain; but though the room was searched from end to end and all seven men strictly examined, the weapon could not be found. The windows were shut, the entrance closed by a frosted-glass door, there was no crack in the floor, and even the cupboard at the desk was searched. Not one of the seven had left the room in that time. — What the officer fixed upon was the dark-skinned new player who had crouched down close beside the victim to pick up a piece of chalk. Until lately, they said, he had been throwing knives in a circus in the character of an Indian. From the depth and the direction of the wound, the weapon had not been thrust in by hand: it had been thrown. "Where in the world did you hide the weapon?" But the man recovers himself and denies it. Unless the weapon is found, the officer can go no further. ... The next day, walking toward the billiard room with a talkative friend, "I" say: in that case there was one, and only one, person who went out of the room.

Characters

  • I　One of the billiard players who was present
  • The man with the mole (one Fukuhara)　The regular who was killed
  • The man from the South Seas　The new player who used to throw knives in a circus
  • The girl marker　The beautiful girl of seventeen who keeps the score

The opening (original text)

... At the other table four men were playing a lively three-handed game, laughing loudly. On my side there were three. Having lost a close game the round before, I was sitting out and watching the match between the hateful man in the mojiri coat with the big mole between his brows, who had gone on playing for five rounds now, and the new opponent, a dark-skinned man who looked as if he had been somewhere in the South Seas. And then the new opponent dropped his chalk by some chance, and bent down to pick it up.

Dōkyūshitsu no shichinin by Hashimoto Gorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

My friend came running after me. And, gentlemen, did he not say in no small voice: "I say, I say — I left it as it was, but there was a dagger in that grass. I did not look closely enough to see whether there was blood on it, but it was a new one. It seemed to have been stuck into the grass with the hilt upward. Shall we go and look again?" "No, no, you are mistaken. It must be a scrap of tin." So I said, and did not so much as look round.
