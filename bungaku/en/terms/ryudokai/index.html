What is Ryūdokai?

Category
Society / Group

A discussion society of literary figures held regularly in the latter half of the 1900s at the Western-style restaurant Ryūdoken in Azabu, Tokyo. Its source was a gathering that Kunikida Doppo, Tayama Katai, Yanagita Kunio, and others held at various changing venues, and it came to be known by this name once it settled at the Ryūdoken in November 1904. A broad range of writers spanning different schools and genres came together — Yanagita Kunio, Tayama Katai, Kunikida Doppo, Shimazaki Tōson, Tokuda Shūsei, Masamune Hakuchō, Oguri Fūyō, Kawakami Bizan, Osanai Kaoru, Kanbara Ariake, and Hasegawa Tenkei — but it gradually took on more of the character of a gathering for Naturalist writers. The significance of this society in nurturing the mood, around 1907, in which a string of landmark works of Naturalist literature were born in quick succession — Tayama Katai's confession of infatuation for a female disciple in "Futon" ("The Quilt"), Shimazaki Tōson's depiction of a teacher of outcaste birth in "Hakai" ("The Broken Commandment"), and Kunikida Doppo's fresh nature writing in the celebrated "Musashino" — is considerable. Around 1907, once members' interest shifted to the Ibsen Society, formed to study Ibsen, the society dissolved naturally.
