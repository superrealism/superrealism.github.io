Biography

Born in 1886 into a doctor's family in Maebashi, Gunma Prefecture. From his days at Maebashi Middle School he immersed himself in tanka and poetry and fell deeply under the spell of Kitahara Hakushū's verse. He never held a steady job, devoting himself to poetry under his family's protection. In 1913 he met Murō Saisei, who became a lifelong friend, and together they founded the poetry magazine "Kanjō" ("Feeling"). In 1917 he published his first poetry collection, "Howling at the Moon," which broke free of the classical literary forms that had dominated verse until then and, with its keen sensibility and the rhythms of free colloquial verse, marked an epoch in modern Japanese poetry. Its successor, "The Blue Cat," sang of urban ennui and melancholy and earned him renown as the poet who perfected colloquial free verse; he also left the prose-poem novel "Cat Town" and critical works such as "Principles of Poetry" and "Yosa Buson: A Poet of Nostalgia." He is known as "the father of modern Japanese poetry." He died of pneumonia in 1942 at the age of 55.
