Anonymous: Life and Major Works

Biography

An entry gathering works whose author has not come down to us. In classical literature, not a few works passed through many hands before reaching their final form, or lost their original shape as they were passed down orally, so that no single author can be identified. "The Tale of the Heike" is one such work: completed in the early Kamakura period, it was told and retold by itinerant biwa-playing monks as heikyoku recitation, giving rise to numerous variant texts along the way. Yoshida Kenkō writes, in section 226 of "Essays in Idleness," that in the reign of Retired Emperor Go-Toba a scholar named Shinano no Zenji Yukinaga composed it and had a blind monk named Shōbutsu recite it — long the most authoritative clue to its origin, though it remains unconfirmed. Works born from the hands of nameless authors, and etching into the Japanese heart a sense of impermanence and the beauty of ruin, occupy an important place in Japanese literature.
