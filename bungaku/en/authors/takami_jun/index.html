Biography

Born in 1907 in Mikuni-chō, Sakai District, Fukui Prefecture (now Sakai City); his real name was Takama Yoshio. He joined the leftist movement while studying English literature at Tokyo Imperial University and, after graduating, was arrested and renounced his beliefs (tenkō). "Kokyū Wasureubeki" ("Old Friends Not to Be Forgotten," 1935), reflecting that experience, was a candidate for the first Akutagawa Prize and drew the literary world's attention at once. He went on to become known for a style that captured the ordinary people and the mood of the entertainment district of Asakusa with a light, deft touch, as in "Ikanaru Hoshi no Moto ni" ("Under Whatever Star"). He was also highly regarded for his diary literature, including "Haisen Nikki" ("Diary of Defeat"), a detailed record kept during the war. In his later years he broke new ground with the novel "Iya na Kanji" ("An Unpleasant Feeling") and the poetry collection "Shi no Fuchi yori" ("From the Depths of Death"), written amid his struggle with esophageal cancer. He devoted himself to establishing the Japan Modern Literature Museum and served as its first director. He died in 1965 at the age of 58.
