Biography

Born in 1882 to a large landowning family in Hōfu, Yamaguchi Prefecture; his real name was Shōichi. When he was ten his mother drowned herself, a memory that scarred him for life. He dropped out of Waseda University with nervous exhaustion, and the family sake brewery went bankrupt; he parted from his wife and child and wandered from place to place. In 1925 he took holy orders at a temple in Kumamoto and became caretaker of the Miture Kannon Hall, and the following year he set out on a mendicant pilgrimage with a sedge hat and begging bowl. He composed free-verse haiku, studying under Ogiwara Seisensui, that sang of the very act of walking as life itself: "Wakeittemo wakeittemo aoi yama" ("Pushing on and on, still the green mountains") and "Massugu na michi de samishii" ("A straight road, and lonely"). He left the haiku collection "Sōmokutō" ("Pagoda of Grasses and Trees"), and died in 1940 at the Issōan hermitage in Matsuyama, at the age of 58.
