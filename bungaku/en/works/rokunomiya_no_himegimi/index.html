About this work

A short story Akutagawa published in the magazine Hyōgen in August 1922, one of his court pieces, drawn from the Konjaku Monogatarishū. Born into an old-fashioned princely house left behind by the times and raised knowing nothing of the world, the lady of the sixth ward goes on waiting for one man, comes down in the world and dies frailly. The unhappy fate of a court woman with no means of living for herself, who lived wholly passively, quietly reflected in a shapely archaic prose: a fine piece representing his court works.

Summary

The Princess of the Sixth Ward was born into a noble house left behind by the times, old-fashioned in its ways. Deeply cherished by her father and mother, she knew nothing of the world, and passed modest, unremarkable days knowing neither sorrow nor joy. In time, through the offices of the gentlewoman who attended her, a young man began to visit her in secret, and the two quietly exchanged their feelings. But the man was to accompany his father to a distant provincial post in Michinoku, and had to set out for that far country. Trusting his promise that he would surely return, the princess did nothing but wait for him. The months and years passed; her parents, on whom she had relied, died before her, and her circumstances grew steadily more straitened. New proposals of marriage arose, but the princess, who knew nothing but waiting, would not be moved by any of them. Years later, the man, at last returned to the capital, sought her out, only to find her utterly changed — she had already fallen into poverty, and lay wasted by illness on her deathbed. Collapsed almost like one fallen dead on the road, the princess is urged to recite the nembutsu by a passing holy man of great virtue, Naiki no Shōnin (Yoshishige no Yasutane), and guided by his voice, breathes her last, whether or not she attains rebirth in paradise uncertain. A tale in which Akutagawa, drawing his material from the Konjaku Monogatarishū, renders with quiet precision the fleeting and pitiable life of a court lady who knew only how to wait for another, and possessed no means of living for herself.

Characters

  • Princess of the Sixth Ward　Daughter of a noble house of the old fashion. Knowing nothing of the world, she does nothing but wait for the man who once visited her, and falls into ruin, dying a fleeting death
  • The Man　A young man who once visited the princess. He accompanies his father to a distant provincial post in Michinoku, and keeps the princess waiting long for his return
  • Naiki no Shōnin (Yoshishige no Yasutane)　A holy man of great virtue who happens by at the princess's deathbed and urges her to recite the nembutsu

The opening (original text)

The father of the Princess of the Sixth Ward had been born into an old branch of the imperial family. But he was a man of the old fashion, given to falling behind the times, and had risen no higher in office than Assistant Minister of War. The princess lived together with such parents in a lofty-roofed mansion near the Sixth Ward, from which she took her name — the Princess of the Sixth Ward. Her father and mother doted on her. Yet, in the old way, they never took it upon themselves to arrange a match for anyone. They only waited, hoping that someone might come forward to court her. The princess too, following her parents' teaching, passed her modest mornings and evenings. It was a life that knew no sorrow, but at the same time knew no joy either. Yet the princess, who knew nothing of the world, felt no particular dissatisfaction with it. "As long as my father and mother are well and in good health, that is enough" — so the princess thought.

The ending (original text)

"Are you not the Holy Man Naiki? However did you come to be in such a place—" His lay name was Yoshishige no Yasutane; known to the world as the Holy Man Naiki, he was, among even the disciples of the Holy Man Kūya, a monk of noble and exceptional virtue.
