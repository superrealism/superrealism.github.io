About this work

Kikuchiyo, brought up as the heir of a daimyo house, has a secret that can never be told — an unusual work by Yamamoto Shūgorō, drawing the inner life of someone required to live as a man. In circumstances known only to the nurse Matsuo, Kikuchiyo turns an eye also to the poor lives outside the mansion. The restrained hand never breaks, down to the closing scene in which the light of a late-spring dawn falls on the paper screens.

Summary

Kikuchiyo was born the first child of Makino Echigo-no-kami Sadayoshi. The mother was a daughter of Matsudaira Izumi-no-kami Noriyoshi. Sadayoshi served as commissioner of temples and shrines and was in good favor, and the upper mansion was in Marunouchi; but Kikuchiyo was brought up chiefly at the middle mansion at Hamachō in Nihonbashi or the lower mansion at Onagizawa in Fukagawa. In charge of the upbringing was Higuchi Jirobei, formerly second in the council of elders, and the personal care was in the hands of a nurse named Matsuo. The father Sadayoshi came without fail five times a month to see the child — a man with a thick beard and large eyes and a face that looked forbidding, but a gentle person with a dimple in one cheek when he laughed — and the story begins from this quiet setting-out. ... As Kikuchiyo grows, the weight of what has been laid upon the child becomes plain. The nurse Matsuo brings up the past: "There was a time when you slipped out of the mansion with young Chikara of the branch house and went swimming." At the foot of the hill on which the mansion stands, in a damp corner shut in by bamboo and coppice, there were three half-ruined hovels; and seeing the mansion's samurai there pressing a man with "No, it will not do; get out at once," Kikuchiyo stops and speaks to them. Inside the hut a very thin man sat with his head bowed. One bound by rank and by sex and by an appointed part comes into touch with the life outside it. At the end of the story Kikuchiyo seizes Hanzaburō's hand and presses a cheek violently against it and pleads: "Make Kikuchiyo a woman.... There is no one but you." The hard, stiffened posture of Hanzaburō softened of itself, and his hand went round the shoulder and stroked quietly. And when at length Kikuchiyo was raised up and turned away to wipe the tears, a faint light of late-spring dawn was falling on the paper of the window.

Characters

The ending (original text)

Trembling, Kikuchiyo seized his hand and pressed a cheek violently against it. What came after can be remembered only as in a dream. Hanzaburō's hard, stiffened posture softened of itself, and at some point his hand went round Kikuchiyo's shoulder and stroked quietly, gently, comfortingly.
