About this work

The brothers Ichirō and Narao, caught in a blizzard on a mountain road, are made to walk through a hell-like dim-lit country and are saved by the person of the shining bare feet who appears there — the piece in which Miyazawa Kenji turned the thought of the Lotus Sutra most directly into a children's story. The brothers' talk, left in dialect, and the pitiless ending stay with one for good.

Summary

Ichirō and Narao, two brothers, had spent time at a mountain hut with their charcoal-burner father, and were to walk home following the man leading a horse loaded with charcoal, in time for Monday school. The well-packed snow path felt good underfoot, and the sky was a deep, clear blue. But partway along, the weather suddenly turned, and a violent blizzard struck the two of them. Narao collapsed, and Ichirō too began to be buried in snow. — When he came to, the two of them found themselves being made to walk through an unfamiliar, dim country. Over the coarse, ash-gray fields grew black thorns, and blood ran from the feet of the people made to walk there barefoot. Demon-like "false people" cracked their whips, and children, weeping and wailing, marched on in a line. Ichirō tried to shield his younger brother, tried to take the pain upon himself instead, but there was nothing he could do. In the fathomless suffering of the "dimly lit country," the two of them had their sins counted up against them, and were driven onward. Just then, everything all at once grew bright, and a single figure appeared. That person's bare feet were dazzling light itself. Walking over the thorns left them unhurt, and the demons, struck by the light, vanished away. That person drew Ichirō and Narao close, and said to them, "Why are you crying? You need not suffer anymore." Before long Ichirō was pulled back to reality. In the dazzling blue sky the faces of the villagers floated into view, and he found himself buried in snow, holding Narao tightly in his arms. "What's happened to your brother?" the hunter cried out. Narao's face was red as an apple, and his lips still held, faintly, the same smile as when he had parted from Ichirō in the country of light a moment before. But his eyes were closed, his breath had stopped, and his hands and chest had already grown cold as ice.

Characters

The opening (original text)

One: The Mountain Hut Ichirō woke because the birds were singing so noisily. It was already fully morning. From the corner of the hut, three bars of blue sunlight slanted straight across, passing over the brothers' heads to shine on the thatch-woven wall beyond, on the brush knife and the leg-wrappings hanging there.

The ending (original text)

The hunter cried out. As Ichirō was helped up, he looked once more at Narao's face. That face was red as an apple, and his lips still held, faintly, the same smile as when he had parted from Ichirō in the country of light a moment before. But his eyes were closed, his breath had stopped, and his hands and chest had already grown cold as ice.
