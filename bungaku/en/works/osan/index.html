About this work

A short story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Ōru Yomimono in 1961. Osan, a woman so made that in the act of love she loses herself and calls out another's name. Taking it for infidelity, her husband, the cabinetmaker Santa, leaves for the Kansai, and Osan, left alone, is passed from man to man and ruins herself. Weaving into Santa's return journey along the Tōkaidō a woman who cares for him and a boy who lives on the highway, it alternates his recollection of tracing what became of Osan with the present. The sadness of human nature, and the remorse of those who keep running, quietly gathered up in the bindweed flowers by the road.

Summary

Santa, a joiner well known in Edo, gets hopelessly drunk at a celebration in his master's house and, on an impulse, ends up sleeping with Osan, a woman who works the account books there. Though a match had already been arranged for her, Santa woos her away and sets up house with her — but Osan possesses an extraordinary, innate sensuality, and in the midst of lovemaking she loses herself completely, crying out other men's names for no reason at all. Santa takes this as proof that she has another man, and begins to torment her over it. Pretending he has work waiting for him in Kamigata, he leaves Edo, and once alone Osan quickly comes apart, drifting from man to man. More than two years later, moved by pity for her, Santa returns to Edo. On the road he falls in with Ofusa, a woman who has fallen for him at first sight, and Isa, a boy who lives along the highway, and with them he traces Osan's path. When he calls on her former lover Sakuji, he finds that Sakuji too has been driven to ruin by Osan's sensuality. When Santa at last finds where she has been living, he learns that she was brutally murdered out of jealousy by her last man, and buried in an unclaimed grave. On the grave where she lies, the bindweed she once called a flower nobody ever looks after — and found endearing for just that reason — blooms quietly. Santa repents of the way he kept running from her, and murmurs to himself that as long as one is alive, there is no such thing as an end.

Characters

  • Santa　A joiner (alcove cabinetmaker) well known in Edo. He mistakes Osan's sensuality for infidelity and keeps running from her. The protagonist
  • Osan　Santa's wife. A woman whose extraordinary, innate sensuality drives her to lose herself and ruin her own life
  • Ofusa　A woman at an inn on the Tōkaidō who falls in with Santa and comes to care for him
  • Isa (Isaburō)　A boy with no parents or home, living along the highway. He helps Santa
  • Sakuji　A cook Osan once lived with. He is driven mad by her and falls into ruin

The opening (original text)

Is this really happening, is it really all right for it to be like this, Osan said. That was when the two of them first became lovers. And if this is real, she also said, I could die tomorrow and be content. Put into words it sounds ordinary, a thing said too late to matter, but at the time Osan was trembling all over, pitifully, so that her teeth chattered against each other.

Osan by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"I think I'll head for Sendai after all," Isa said, backing away. "Seems that suits me better anyway. So long, mister." Santa watched him without a word. Isa said so long once more, then spun around and broke into a run toward Senju. White dust rose from beneath his feet, and his small figure grew rapidly more distant. "I wonder if Ofusa's waiting," Santa murmured under his breath. "—As long as a person's alive, there's no such thing as an end, is there."

Osan by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
