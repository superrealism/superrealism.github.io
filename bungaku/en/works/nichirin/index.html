About this work

A novella Yokomitsu Riichi published in Shinshōsetsu in 1923; with "The Fly" of the same year, it made his name in effect. Set in the ancient Japan of the age of Yamatai, it draws the kings of the various states hurling their desire and ambition at each other over Himiko, a woman of matchless beauty born in the state of Na, until it comes to war between Na and Yamatai. Its style, deploying a gorgeous vocabulary, a vivid sense of colour and bold figures, foretells the New Sensationalists to come: an ambitious work by a young writer, drawn from history and yet full of the force of myth.

Summary

Ancient Japan, in the age of Yamatai. Along the shore of the Ariake inlet, the land of Na faces the rising power of Yamatai. Himiko, a woman of matchless beauty born in Na, is placed, by the very brilliance of her looks, at the center of a whirlpool of desire and rivalry among the kings of every land. The ambitions and passions of the kings who seek to possess her, and of the young men of neighboring lands, clash violently, and scheming and betrayal, plunder and war, follow one upon another without end. Tossed between love and hatred, between the lust to possess and be possessed, Himiko too finds herself caught fast in the threads of a strange and fated destiny. At last, amid the full-scale collision of Na and Yamatai, Himiko, entangled in the very love and hatred that surrounds her, is driven, in bitter irony, to bring about the death of Chōra, the man who adored her, and collapses in tears, clinging to his corpse. As the war cries of the advancing army of Yamatai roll and thunder across the sky, the tale comes to its close. Set in ancient Japan and rendered in gorgeous prose and vivid color, this tale of kings' desire and one woman's fate already showed the brilliance of the young Yokohashi, and became the breakthrough work that led onward to the Shinkankaku-ha, the New Sensationalist school.

Characters

  • Himiko　A woman of matchless beauty born in the land of Na. Her beauty places her at the center of the desires and rivalries of the kings of every land
  • Chōra　A man entangled in the love and hatred surrounding Himiko. He meets his death at Himiko's own hand amid the warfare between Na and Yamatai
  • The kings of the various lands　Vie with one another for Himiko, clashing again and again in war, driven by ambition and passion

The opening (original text)

Prologue A band of maidens, water jars balanced on their heads, sang as they made their way from the spring on the hillside, disappearing into the grove of silk trees. On the rocks that ringed the spring behind them, the still-unwithered flowers of the great bulrush lay trampled together with shards of broken water jars. And the sun, already tilting toward the west, now cast its failing light, weak and spent, over the Ariake inlet spreading far below the foot of this hill, over the white dunes that wound and curved along both ends of the horizon before vanishing from sight.

Nichirin by Yokomitsu Riichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Himiko clutched her head and collapsed in tears upon the sword. "Great Elder Brother, great elder brother, forgive me. It was for your sake that I struck down Chōra. It was for your sake that I took my revenge. Ah, Chōra, Chōra, forgive me. It was for my sake that you were killed." Leaving behind Chōra, Han'e, and Himiko, from the distant forest, where the army of Yamatai, pursuing the soldiers of Na, pressed on toward the land of Na itself, the roar of their war cries rose once more, higher into the sky.
