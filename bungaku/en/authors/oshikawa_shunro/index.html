Biography

Born in 1876 in Matsuyama, Ehime Prefecture, the eldest son of Oshikawa Masayoshi, a leader in Japan's Christian circles; his real name was Masaari. While a student at Tokyo Semmon Gakkō (now Waseda University), "Kaitei Gunkan" ("The Undersea Battleship"), which he published in 1900 as part of "Kaitō Bōken Kitan" ("A Strange Tale of Maritime Adventure"), captured the fervent enthusiasm of young readers and made him an overnight sensation. He went on to publish one work after another blending the sea, adventure, and scientific fantasy — "Shin Nihontō" ("The New Japan Island"), "Tōyō Bukyōdan" ("The Eastern Knights-Errant") — becoming the fountainhead of Japanese adventure fiction and science fantasy. As chief editor of the magazines "BŌken Sekai" ("The World of Adventure") and "Bukyō Sekai" ("The World of Chivalry") he trained younger writers and illustrators, while also founding the sporting social club Tengu Club and working energetically to spread sports, above all baseball. The pen name Shunrō was given to him by his mentor, Iwaya Sazanami. He died young on November 16, 1914, at the age of 38.
