About this work

A tax office chief who sets out to crack down on illicit brewing is fêted by the villagers until he is carried off and shut up in the Ihatov Illicit Brewing Company in the hills — an unusual, longish story that Miyazawa Kenji did not publish in his lifetime. Villagers who laugh with more delight the worse you speak of them, an illicit distillery fitted out with modern equipment in perfect seriousness, and the virtues of drink argued with reference to Horibe Yasubei. Within the comic frame, the mismatch of timing between the countryside and officialdom comes up as comedy.

Summary

Invited to a lecture meeting for the prevention of illicit brewing, the head of the tax office stands in the assembly hall of the elementary school and, in perfect seriousness, praises sake and then exposes the tricks of the illicit brewers one by one. Brewing it in the miso tub and laying a board over it; hiding it in the dry grass of the stable; making it in the loft and coming down with one's eyes bright red. But at abuse that ought to have stung them, the villagers, far from growing angry, only clap their hands and laugh with enjoyment. The superintendent's calculation — to count how many turn red with anger and how many go pale with fear, and so to reckon the number of illicit stills in the village — comes to nothing. Even when he plays his last card and says that seven informers are among them, the whole hall bursts out laughing, and the superintendent goes down from the platform in a whirl. ... At the welcoming party that follows, splendid trays are set out, and clear sake on which the tax has certainly been paid goes round. But as the drink takes hold, the superintendent notices that the smell of the sake has changed. It is not cloudy; but it is not from any brewer of this district. Shaking off the honorary village head and the others who try to hold him, the superintendent flees by bicycle with his subordinate Shiratori; and in time the two of them are carried into the mountains by the villagers and shut up in the modern works of the "Ihatov Illicit Brewing Company," equipped with everything from cultures of lactic bacteria to pipettes. Four days later there was a sudden noise outside.

Characters

  • The head of the tax office　A stout man who sets out to catch the illicit brewers
  • Shiratori　The subordinate who attends the superintendent
  • The honorary village head　The village worthy who takes the superintendent to the welcoming party
  • The village councillor　One of the well-wishers who gives the feast at his fine house

The opening (original text)

1. A lecture meeting for the prevention of illicit brewing "In the examinations of the English universities they say that even a cow gains weight if you give it drink. And this is truly the root of human energy. That sake is compressed liquid bread is a fine saying indeed. That Horibe Yasubei could take vengeance on thirty men at Takadanobaba was truly because he had a great deal of energy from drink. Gentlemen, for the sake of the nation and of the world, drink deeply."

Zeimushochō no bōken by Miyazawa Kenji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The superintendent was walking beside the honorary village head. "What day is it today?" he asked, turning round suddenly to Shiratori. "The fifth, sir." "Ah, four days already since that day. The buds on the trees have grown in that little while." The superintendent looked up at the sky. A damp white cloud of spring came drifting up over the hill, and the scent of the spicebush came floating on the wind. "Ah, a good smell," said the superintendent. "A good smell indeed," said the honorary village head.
