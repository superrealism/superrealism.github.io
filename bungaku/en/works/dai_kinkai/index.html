About this work

A Boy Detectives story Ranpo serialized in Shōnen Kurabu in 1939 and 1940. A strange Western house built by an eccentric uncle on a hill at Ogikubo, and the secret of the great hoard of gold hidden in it. The sixth-year pupil Miyase Fujio and young Kobayashi carry out an adventure at the risk of their lives, with the great detective Akechi Kogorō also at work: a rousing treasure-hunting story.

Summary

Miyase Fujio, a sixth-grader, lived in a spacious Western-style mansion standing atop a lonely hill in Ogikubo, at the northwestern edge of Tokyo. The house had been built by an eccentric uncle who had lived out his whole life a bachelor, keeping to himself and doing nothing but tinker with antiques, and it was built in a strange, old-fashioned style, like some castle. Hidden within that strange house lay one great secret — the "great gold hoard." Around that immense hoard of gold, sinister thieves begin to move in the shadows, and young Miyase Fujio finds himself swept up in a life-risking adventure together with young Kobayashi, boy assistant to the great detective Akechi Kogorō. Facing danger after danger, the two boys win through by wit and courage, and at last discover the great gold hoard and capture the thieves. Fujio's father, Mr. Miyase, resolves not to keep for himself the enormous sum the government grants him in exchange for the gold, but to use it for the good of the world, building schools and hospitals. The great detective Akechi's fame rose all the higher for it — but what delighted the public even more was the breathless tale of adventure told by the two boys, Kobayashi and Fujio. Their names came to ring out to every corner of Japan. A rousing tale of a boys' treasure hunt.

The opening (original text)

Miyase Fujio, a sixth-grader, was minding the big house all by himself. Fujio's house stood atop a lonely hill in Ogikubo, at the northwestern edge of Tokyo. This house had been built by Fujio's uncle, but when that uncle passed away — leaving behind no wife and no children — it became the property of Fujio's father, and for about a year now Fujio's family had been living there.

Dai kinkai by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

But what delighted the public far, far more than these two grown men's fine deeds was the breathless tale of adventure told by young Kobayashi and young Fujio. For it was thanks to the two boys' own life-risking adventure that the great gold hoard had been found at all, and the thieves captured — and so their fame grew tremendous, until the names of the two boys, Kobayashi and Miyase, rang out to every corner of Japan.
