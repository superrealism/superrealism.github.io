About this work

An essay by Unno Jūza, the science-fiction and detective writer known for Exploring Mars. He introduces cheerfully, with examples, the various kinds of cheating that go with mahjong. A light piece setting down a human psychology that hates dishonesty and cannot help finding humour in it.

Summary

"Cheating is a dishonest device, and thus not something a gentleman should practice" — so Unno Jūza cautions, even as he goes on to introduce, with examples, the various kinds of cheating rife in the world of mahjong. Though being cheated oneself brings vexation enough for tears, one cannot help laughing at the foolishness of someone else who gets taken in, and even feels a kind of respect for the rogue who worked the trick — Unno pokes lightly at that absurdity of human psychology. The trick of switching tiles, signals exchanged between confederates (scratch the head for the white dragon, rub the nose for the prevailing wind), ways of cheating with the counting sticks... the techniques of cheating are listed off with evident relish. And at the end, a single piece of advice: of the time spent at the table, only one or two parts in ten should go to watching one's own tiles — the other eight or nine must always go to watching the other three players. A delightful essay of Unno Jūza's, cautioning against dishonesty while telling, with plenty of humor, the underside of mahjong.

The opening (original text)

Cheating is a dishonest device. Thus it is not something a gentleman should practice. In mahjong, which of late reigns over the world of games in Japan, all manner of cheating is also possible. The Japan Mahjong League, of course, condemns cheating as well.

Mājan inchiki monogatari by Unno Jūza (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

There is no end to it if I go into detail, but of the time spent at the table, only one or two parts in ten need go to watching one's own tiles. The other eight or nine must always be spent watching the conduct of the other three players.

Mājan inchiki monogatari by Unno Jūza (Aozora Bunko)
