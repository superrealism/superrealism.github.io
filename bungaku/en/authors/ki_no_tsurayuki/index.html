Biography

Born around 872, a leading poet and court official of the early Heian period, and the son of Ki no Mochiyuki. By order of Emperor Daigo, he compiled the first imperially commissioned anthology of waka, the "Kokin Wakashū," together with Ki no Tomonori, Ōshikōchi no Mitsune, and Mibu no Tadamine, and in its preface, the "Kana Preface," he set out the essential nature of waka poetry, writing that "Japanese poetry has the human heart as its seed, and puts forth countless leaves of words." He was one of the Thirty-Six Immortals of Poetry, and no poet has more verses included in the imperial anthologies. From 930 he served as governor of Tosa Province, and the account he wrote in kana prose of his journey home, framed as though narrated by a woman, "Tosa Nikki" ("The Tosa Diary"), is Japan's oldest work of diary literature in kana and exerted enormous influence on the later diary, essay, and monogatari traditions. He died around 945.
