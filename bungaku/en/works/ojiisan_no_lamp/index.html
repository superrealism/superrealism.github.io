About this work

The title story of the only collection Niimi Nankichi put together in his lifetime, published in 1942. Minosuke, an orphan, is struck by the brightness of a lamp he sees in town, makes his living bringing lamps to his village and grows into a man. But times change; electric light reaches the village and lamps become useless. Minosuke, who first clings to the old thing and fights, thinks better of it, says goodbye to his lamps himself, and starts a new trade. A warm study of progress and of a man who accepts it, and one of Nankichi's central stories.

Summary

Pressed by his grandson, the old man begins to tell the story of his own young days. Minosuke was an orphan with no one belonging to him; and while working as a rickshaw puller he saw a lamp for the first time in the town, and its brightness struck him to the heart. Wanting to light his dark village, he bought a few lamps with what little money he had and began peddling them from village to village. Unable even to read, he taught himself his letters in order to learn what his trade required; and in time he prospered as a lamp seller, opened a shop, took a wife and made a life of his own. But the world moved on, and at last electricity was to be brought to the village. Learning that lamps would be useless and his living gone, Minosuke resisted fiercely at first, and in his resentment against the district head who was bringing the electric light in he even went to set fire to the man's house. But he could not find a flint, and when he found himself about to buy matches instead, the irony of it showed him how foolish he was to cling to old things. Minosuke hung every lamp he had for sale in the trees by the pond and lit them, and threw stones to break them one by one, taking a clean farewell of his old trade. Then he changed his business to bookselling and went on living in the new age. His tale finished, the old man tells his grandson that when an old thing is no longer of use, the right course is to give it up cleanly and go on to a new road.

Characters

  • Minosuke　An orphan with no family, who made his way as the merchant who brought lamps to the village, and who faces the coming of the age of electric light
  • The grandfather　The teller of the tale, who tells his grandson how he lived his young days as Minosuke

The opening (original text)

Tōichi, who had crawled into a corner of the storehouse playing hide-and-seek, came out carrying a lamp. It was a lamp of an unusual shape. A thick bamboo tube some eighty centimeters long made the stand, and on top of it was fastened the little part where the flame burned; and the chimney was a slender glass tube. To anyone seeing it for the first time it hardly looked like a lamp at all.

Ojiisan no ranpu by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Tōichi said nothing, and for a long while looked at his grandfather's face, small but full of spirit. Then he said: "You were a fine man, Grandfather." And he looked at the old lamp beside him with something like longing.

Ojiisan no ranpu by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)

Famous passages

“This is how I'll quit my trade.”

The words of Minosuke, who has realized that the age of electric light has come, as he hangs the lamps he sold on the trees by a pond, lights them, and breaks them one by one with stones. It lingers as the graceful way a man left behind by his times takes his leave.
