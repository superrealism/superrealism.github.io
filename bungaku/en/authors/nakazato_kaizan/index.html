Biography

Born in 1885 in Hamura, Nishitama District, Kanagawa Prefecture (now Hamura, Tokyo); his real name was Yanosuke. After working as a telephone operator and substitute teacher, he joined the "Miyako Shimbun" in 1906 and began his literary career as a reporter. His lifelong masterpiece, "Daibosatsu Tōge" ("The Great Bodhisattva Pass"), which he began serializing in that paper in 1913, ran across various publications for the next 28 years; through the wanderings of the nihilistic swordsman Tsukue Ryūnosuke, it depicted the karma of human existence as what he called a "Mahayana novel," and was praised by Kikuchi Kan and Tanizaki Jun'ichirō, becoming a forerunner of popular fiction. In his youth he was drawn to socialism and Christianity, and is also known for his friendship with Kōtoku Shūsui and others. He remained unmarried his entire life, and died in 1944 of typhoid fever at the age of 59, leaving "Daibosatsu Tōge" unfinished.
