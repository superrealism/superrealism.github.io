About this work

A children's story by Yumeno Kyūsaku. Before winter, the mushrooms hold a farewell meeting. When those useful to human beings lament that they get picked, the poisonous ones jeer that if you are no use no one troubles you, so be poisonous — a fable asking, with irony and humour, what it means to be useful.

Summary

One night, as winter draws near, the mushrooms — matsutake, shiitake, hatsutake and the rest — gather together and hold a farewell meeting. The shiitake rejoices, saying, "Human beings even make farms for us," while the matsutake laments tearfully, "We get picked before our caps have even opened, and our whole line is wiped out." As everyone is sympathizing, the poisonous mushrooms at the back burst out laughing. The largest of the fly agarics stands up and says: "You're all fools. It's exactly because you're useful to the world that you get picked so much. If you weren't any use at all, nobody would bother you. Look at us — because we're a poison to the world, we go on thriving." The mushrooms, impressed, think to themselves that indeed, if you're poisonous, you have nothing to fear. But the next morning, a family comes out mushroom-hunting — "My, don't all the poisonous mushrooms have such nasty shapes." "I'll get rid of them for you." And the poisonous mushrooms, large and small alike, are trampled to smithereens, right down to their roots. A fable of Yumeno Kyūsaku's, asking with irony and humour what it means to be useful, and what it means to live.

The opening (original text)

One night, the mushrooms — hatsutake, matsutake, shiitake, wood ear, white mushroom, gantake, nametake, shimofuritake, shishitake, nezumitake, kawahagitake, kome-shōro, mugi-shōro and all the rest of them — gathered together and held a meeting. First of all, the hatsutake stood up and made a greeting. "Friends. It's been getting colder and colder these days, so it's about time we all had to withdraw back into the earth. Tonight is our farewell banquet, so please, everyone, make whatever speech you like, to your heart's content."

Kinoko kaigi by Yumeno Kyūsaku (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"My, don't all the poisonous mushrooms have such nasty shapes." "Yeah, I'll get rid of them for you." And with that, one after another, the poisonous mushrooms, large and small alike, were trampled to smithereens, right down to their roots.
