About this work

A novella Tanizaki published in the magazine Kaizō in 1936. In an ordinary household between Osaka and Kobe, it draws with humour the manoeuvring over a single cat between Shōzō, an easygoing husband who dotes on his female cat Lily, and two women — his first wife Shinako and his second wife Fukuko. An unusual work in which the idea of a cat, rather than another woman, as the rival in love is worked up with lively Kansai dialogue and close description of the animal, conveying the enjoyable side of Tanizaki, who here treats a relationship that could easily be grim as comedy.

Summary

Shōzō, who runs a small hardware shop in Ashiya, dotes on Lily, the female cat he has kept for years, more than he does his wife Fukuko. Then a letter arrives for Fukuko from Shinako, Shōzō's former wife, driven out of his house, asking that Lily be given over to her. Fukuko, who has long been jealous of her husband's doting on the cat, suspects some scheme behind Shinako's request, but out of sheer spite presses Shōzō all the more to give Lily up. Weeping, Shōzō sends Lily off to Shinako, but only grows more attached to his memory of the cat as his catless life goes on. Shinako, for her part, finds herself growing fond of Lily — a cat she was supposed to hate — even as she lives with her, and her scheme of using the cat as bait to lure Shōzō back begins to go astray. One morning, visiting Shinako's house in secret from his wife, Shōzō is at last reunited with Lily — but at the sense that Shinako herself is about to return home, he bolts and flees headlong, as though pursued by something fearsome.

Characters

  • Shōzō　Owner of a small hardware shop in Ashiya. An easygoing idler who dotes on his cat Lily
  • Lily　The female cat Shōzō has doted on for years. Becomes the object of the two women's rivalry
  • Shinako　Shōzō's former wife. Out of spite and lingering attachment after being driven out, offers to take Lily in
  • Fukuko　Shōzō's current wife. Jealous of her husband's doting on the cat, she forces him to give Lily up
  • Orin　Shōzō's mother. A shrewd old woman who drove out Shinako and brought in Fukuko

The opening (original text)

Fukuko dear please forgive me I borrowed the name Yuki-chan for this letter but it isn't really from Yuki-chan of course once I say that you'll already know who I am won't you — no, no, the very moment you tore open this envelope you must already have thought to yourself 'ah, it must be that woman,' and no doubt you're angry, thinking what nerve, using a friend's name without asking and sending me a letter, what a shameless person — but Fukuko dear please understand, if I'd written my real name on the back of the envelope I know perfectly well he would have found it and snatched the letter away before it ever reached you, and since I was determined that you and only you should read this there was nothing else for it but to do as I've done, but please set your mind at ease, I have no intention at all of reproaching you or pouring out my complaints to you.

A Cat, a Man, and Two Women by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Just then there came the sound of hurried footsteps, and Hatsuko flung the sliding door open in a rush. "Sis is already right round the corner!" "Eh?! That's terrible!" "You can't go out the back! Go round to the front — round to the front! I'll bring your sandals for you! Quick, quick!" He went tumbling headlong down the stairs, dashed to the front entrance, and jammed his feet into the wooden sandals Hatsuko had thrown down onto the earthen floor for him. And the very moment he slipped out into the street, he caught, out of the corner of his eye, a glimpse of Shinako's retreating figure turning toward the back entrance, missing him by a hair's breadth — and he took off running, headlong, in the opposite direction, as though pursued by something fearsome.
