About this work

A short story about a kindness received in his poor student days, published in May 1920. A doctor of medicine who catches sight of a madwoman in scarlet crêpe at a rainy station recalls his youth. Osen, the woman of a kept house, who saved him when he was a student with barely anything to eat, even by selling herself. Following a memory in which hunger, shame and kindness are mixed, the story arrives at a sickbed in a first-class room. The title comes from a bowl of noodles she once gave him to eat.

Summary

At the rainy Mansei Bridge station, what caught Hata Sōkichi's eye was a woman sitting there, her long scarlet crêpe undergarment soaked with mud. From that figure, seemingly a madwoman, Sōkichi's memory travels back more than twenty years. In his days as a poor student, he had been fed by the followers of a man named Kumazawa, while being sent running on errands to Kumazawa's kept woman's house. Days of hunger, taunted by Amaya and the others from the same tenement, going up and down the stone steps. One day, when Sōkichi tried to put a razor to his own throat behind the Myōjin shrine, it was Osen who caught him and held him back. She swore she would give up Kumazawa and save Sōkichi, even at the cost of her own body and life. Sōkichi, grown to become a doctor of medicine, visits the madwoman, now admitted to a first-class hospital room, as one private individual.

Characters

  • Hata Sōkichi　A doctor of medicine who rose up from a poor student. Never forgets the kindness shown him in his youth
  • Osen　Kumazawa's mistress. A woman who casts herself aside to save Sōkichi
  • Kumazawa　A man who looks after Sōkichi and other students, rumoured to be on his way to becoming a great businessman
  • Amaya　A man who lived in the same tenement as Sōkichi. Taunts Sōkichi

The opening (original text)

What caught the eye first — it is a little hard to say, but — was, in any case, scarlet crêpe. A long undergarment, blazing red as fire, shaded here and there with vermilion. The woman had not tucked up her hem on purpose. She had hitched the front of it high, and from around where her knees pressed together, the crimson hung damp and clinging, wrapping her white ankles — but it seemed she had pulled it up like that only out of some uneasy sense of having gotten soaked and chilled.

Baishoku kamonanban by Izumi Kyōka (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Startled by this unscheduled visit, one day his assistants and the white-clad nurses came hurrying, scattered and yet quiet, toward him — and Dr. Hata Sōkichi, the doctor of medicine, waved them gently aside, right and left. "No, no — I am visiting as a private individual... please, everyone, go on." And then the doctor, alone in the first-class room, before the madwoman, her knees and chest bare and careless, a razor placed, of all things, in her hand — knelt at her bedside, buried his forehead against her breast, clung to her tightly, and, weeping streams of tears, smiling all the while, as though he had forgotten himself and the whole world and reverted to a simple child, let the tears run helplessly down into his beard.
