About this work

A short story published in Bungei in 1939. It turns on the sushi shop Fukuzushi in a hilly quarter of the city, the middle-aged gentleman Minato who eats there, and Tomoyo, the daughter of the house. A strange fastidiousness about food had kept Minato from taking any pleasure in eating — until, as a child, his nervous mother pressed a single piece of sushi for him with her own hands, and in it he found the sensuality and the joy of being alive. That sweet memory is slowly retraced. A central story of Kanoko's, written in her rich hand on food and the senses.

Summary

In a quarter of many slopes and cliffs, on the border between the low town and the hillside districts of Tokyo, there is a sushi shop called Fukuzushi. Tomoyo, the daughter of the house, feels a faint fondness for Minato, a well-bred middle-aged gentleman who among all the regulars always eats his sushi quietly and alone. One day, when she asks him, Minato begins to tell her a memory of his childhood. A nervous and fastidious boy, he found something raw or unclean in every kind of food and could take no pleasure in eating. Anxious for him, his mother went one day into the kitchen, washed her hands clean, pressed white rice for him and made him beautiful sushi with her own hands. Egg, then white-fleshed fish — piece after clean and lovely piece coming from his mother's hands, which he put into his mouth for the first time in his life, timidly and then eagerly. That mouthful was the moment when he woke to the pleasure of eating and of living. Ever since, sushi has been a special food for him. Before long Minato stops coming to the shop altogether, and Tomoyo comes to think only, vaguely, that he must be going to some other sushi shop somewhere. Food and mother and a sensuous feeling for life are drawn together in one of Kanoko's finest stories.

Characters

  • Minato　A well-bred middle-aged gentleman who comes to Fukuzushi. Fastidious about food and unable to enjoy eating, he found the pleasure of living in the sushi his mother once made for him
  • Tomoyo　The daughter of the sushi shop Fukuzushi, who feels a faint fondness for the regular Minato and hears the memory of his childhood
  • Minato's mother　Who washed her hands clean and made beautiful sushi for her nervous, fastidious son, and led him to the pleasure of eating

The opening (original text)

There is a quarter, on what might be called the border between the low town and the hillside districts of Tokyo, that is full of slopes and cliffs. To anyone who has turned aside from the bustle of the main street it gives the feeling of another world. That is to say, a small street of the sort into which people tired by the stimulation of the main streets and the new roads slip now and then, to get away from it and change the air —

Sushi by Okamoto Kanoko (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Nowadays, whenever Tomoyo remembers Minato, she does no more than think vaguely: "He must have moved somewhere, and be going to some other sushi shop — there are sushi shops everywhere, after all."
