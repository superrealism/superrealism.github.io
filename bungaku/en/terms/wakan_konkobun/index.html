What is Wakan Konkōbun (Mixed Sino-Japanese Style)?

Category
Style & Technique

A prose style fusing wabun (kana prose) with the grammar and vocabulary of kanbun-kundoku (Chinese texts read in Japanese word order). It combines the strengths of graceful, sentiment-rich wabun with the powerful, concise style of kanbun-kundoku, creating a dignified cadence that mixes native Japanese words with Sino-Japanese vocabulary. Early traces appear in the late-Heian anecdote collection "Konjaku Monogatarishū," and it reached its full maturity in the medieval war tales. "The Tale of the Heike," opening with the stately mixed Sino-Japanese prose of "The sound of the bell of Gion Shōja echoes the impermanence of all things," sings epically of battle and the impermanence of human life, and, chanted by blind biwa-playing priests, was beloved by a wide audience. Kamo no Chōmei's "Hōjōki," recording the impermanence of a chaotic age and the quiet of a hermit's life in lucid, taut prose, is likewise celebrated as a masterpiece of mixed Sino-Japanese style. This style was used widely in the setsuwa, war tales, and essays of the medieval period, and, becoming a model for the written language of later ages, it left a lasting influence on the pseudo-classical prose and Sino-Japanese-inflected writing of the Meiji era as well.
