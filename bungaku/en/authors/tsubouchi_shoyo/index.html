Biography

Born in 1859 in Ōta-juku, Kamo District, Mino Province (now Minokamo, Gifu Prefecture); his real name was Yūzō. After graduating from Tokyo University he taught at Tokyo Semmon Gakkō (now Waseda University), and in the critical work "Shōsetsu Shinzui" ("The Essence of the Novel") he argued for a realism that rejected the old convention of rewarding good and punishing evil, laying the theoretical foundation of modern Japanese literature. He was also known for the novel "Tōsei Shosei Katagi" ("The Character of Present-Day Students"), which put his own theory into practice, and his name has come to symbolize the dawn of modern literature. In his later years he devoted himself to reforming the theater, founding the Bungei Kyōkai and pioneering the shingeki (new theater) movement, while single-handedly completing a translation of the complete works of Shakespeare and firmly establishing Shakespearean literature in Japan. He died in 1935 at the age of 75.
