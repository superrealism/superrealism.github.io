Biography

Born in 1889 to a police officer's family in Tsuruoka, Yamagata Prefecture. He graduated with top marks from the Army Academy and Army War College, and studied the history of warfare in Germany. As a staff officer of the Kwantung Army he was the chief architect of the 1931 Manchurian Incident. Drawing on his Nichiren Buddhist faith and his own theory of the history of war, he propounded the "Final World War Theory," holding that humanity's world would be unified only after one last great war, and advocated forming an East Asia League. Opposing the expansion of the war with China and Tōjō Hideki, he was pushed out of the army, and after the defeat he preached a movement for village self-government in his home province. Opinions of him as a soldier remain divided, but his highly original theory of civilization is still read today. He died in 1949 at the age of 60.
