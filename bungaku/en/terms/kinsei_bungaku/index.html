What is Kinsei Bungaku (Early Modern Literature)?

Category
Historical Period

The literature of the Edo period, from the early seventeenth century to the mid-nineteenth century. Townspeople, rather than warriors, became its principal bearers, and the spread of woodblock printing and lending libraries produced a broad readership — the great difference from earlier ages. The first half centers on the Kamigata region (Kyoto and Osaka): in the Genroku era, Ihara Saikaku published the ukiyo-zōshi "Kōshoku Ichidai Otoko" ("The Life of an Amorous Man," 1682), vividly capturing the desires and manners of townspeople; Matsuo Bashō raised haikai to the art of "sabi" and "karumi" and left the travel journal "Oku no Hosomichi"; and Chikamatsu Monzaemon, in the puppet play "Sonezaki Shinjū" ("The Love Suicides at Sonezaki," 1703), depicted the bind of duty and human feeling. The latter half centers on Edo: works generally classed as gesaku flourished in variety — Ueda Akinari's yomihon "Ugetsu Monogatari" ("Tales of Moonlight and Rain," 1776), Jippensha Ikku's comic "Tōkaidōchū Hizakurige" ("Shanks' Mare"), and Kyokutei Bakin's great work "Nansō Satomi Hakkenden" ("The Eight Dog Chronicles"), completed over twenty-eight years. In kokugaku (National Learning), Motoori Norinaga found "mono no aware" at the heart of "The Tale of Genji." Modern literature began with Tsubouchi Shōyō's criticism of gesaku's moralizing, but not a few modern writers have looked back on this age, as in Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's "Gesaku Zanmai" and Uchida Roan's "Hakkenden Dan'yo."
