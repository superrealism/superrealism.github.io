About this work

Ogawa Mimei's best-known work, published in the Tokyo Asahi Shimbun in 1921 and taken as a landmark of Japanese children's literature. A mermaid in the northern sea, wishing that at least her child might be raised among people with some kindness in them, bears it on land. But human greed betrays that wish cruelly. Written in a beautiful and lyrical hand, it holds both a trust in human gentleness and a quiet anger at the coldness that tramples it. Sorrowful and full of symbol.

Summary

A mermaid heavy with child, living in the blue sea of the north, has heard that human beings are kind and tender-hearted; and wishing that her child at least might be raised in the bright and busy town of men, she gives birth on the stone steps of a shrine by the shore. The child is found by an old couple who sell candles at the foot of the hill, and is brought up with great care. She grows into a beautiful, gentle-hearted girl, a fish from the waist down; and when she paints fish and shells on the white candles they take on a strange power, so that word goes round that whoever carries the stub of a candle offered at the shrine on the hill will come to no harm at sea, and the shop prospers. Then one day a showman from the south offers a great sum of money for so rare a creature. The old couple refuse at first, but they are worked on by talk of a mermaid bringing ill luck, and by the money, and in the end they promise to sell the girl. Her tearful pleading is not heard, and she is carried away, leaving behind a few candles she has painted red. That night, after a pale woman with wet hair has come to buy a red candle and gone away again, the sea rises in a storm such as had never been known, and the ship carrying the girl goes down. From then on, every night a red candle burned there was a storm; the god whose favor had been so sure became the demon gate of the town, and before long the town at the foot of the hill was destroyed.

Characters

  • The mermaid girl　The child a mermaid of the northern sea bore on land, wishing her a human town. She makes the candle shop prosper with the fish and shells she paints, and is sold to a showman
  • The mermaid mother　Who wishes her child happiness, bears her on land and returns to the sea
  • The old man and the old woman　The couple who sell candles at the foot of the hill. They take the girl in and raise her, and then lose their hearts to money and superstition and sell her
  • The showman　A man of the sideshows from the south, who tries to buy the mermaid as a rare exhibit

The opening (original text)

Mermaids do not live only in the southern seas. They lived in the northern sea as well. The color of the northern sea was blue. Once a mermaid came up on a rock and rested there, looking at the scene around her. The moonlight leaking from between the clouds shone lonely on the waves. Whichever way she looked there were terrible waves without end, moving in long swells. What a lonely scene it is, the mermaid thought. We are not so very different from human beings in our shape. Compared with fish, or with the fierce creatures of all kinds that live deep down in the sea, how much closer we are to human beings in heart and in form. And yet we must live along with the fish and the beasts in this cold, dark, dispiriting sea — how can that be, she wondered.

The ending (original text)

On a pitch-dark night of rain, with no stars showing, there were those who saw the light of a red candle drifting over the waves, climbing higher and higher, and at last flickering on its way toward the shrine at the top of the hill. Within a few years the town at the foot of that hill fell into ruin and ceased to be.

Famous passages

Mermaids do not live only in the seas of the south. They lived also in the northern sea.

A children's story that opens by overturning a common assumption in a single line. It depicts the sorrow of a mermaid of the northern sea, who entrusts her daughter to human hands — a masterpiece of original Japanese children's literature.

Before many years had passed, the town at the foot of that mountain fell to ruin, and vanished away entirely.

The fate that comes to the town that sold off the mermaid's daughter. A line that states the cause and effect plainly closes the story with a quiet dread.
