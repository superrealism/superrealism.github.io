What is Shūkan Asahi?

Category
Society / Group

A weekly magazine founded by the Asahi Shimbun company in February 1922 as "Junkan Asahi" (a thrice-monthly), which became a weekly and took its new name that April. Alongside "Sunday Mainichi," founded the same year, it was a pioneer of Japanese weekly magazines, and as a newspaper company's weekly it combined current-affairs reporting with reading matter, becoming an important venue for popular literature. Before the war it carried works such as Yoshikawa Eiji's "Jirokichi Gōshi" (1931), and after the war, in the years when Ōgiya Shōzō served as editor-in-chief, its circulation passed one million copies in 1954. The work that represents the magazine in this period is Yoshikawa Eiji's epic "Shin Heike Monogatari" ("The New Tale of the Heike"), serialized over seven years from 1950, which won a broad readership with its grand depiction of the rise and fall of the Taira clan. Yamamoto Shūgorō also contributed many short stories, beginning with "Ashi wa Mite Ita" (1947), and his novel "Sabu" (1963), portraying the friendship of two young craftsmen, was serialized here as well. In the fiction contest "Hyakumannin no Shōsetsu" ("A Novel for a Million Readers") the magazine announced in 1949, Matsumoto Seichō's first work, "Saigō Satsu," won third place and became his starting point as a writer, and from 1971 Shiba Ryōtarō's travel essays "Kaidō o Yuku" were serialized until the author's death. The magazine ceased publication with an issue released in May 2023, closing a history of 101 years.
