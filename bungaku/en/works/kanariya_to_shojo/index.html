About this work

In a small eating house in a port town there was a girl who had been deaf and mute from birth, with a single canary for a pet — a very short piece Yamakawa Masao wrote for the evening edition of the Chūbu Nippon Shimbun. She takes the linked-finger promise she made with a young man going to the deep-sea fleet for an eternal vow. In time the ship is lost, and she walks into the sea at dawn. A clear trill sounding for an instant between the roars of the surf announces a terrible fulfilment.

Summary

The girl had large eyes, and a habit of looking at people fixedly, desperately, as though she would eat them up. Deaf and dumb from birth, she works in an eating house in a port town that is a base for deep-sea fishing. A single canary is her only pet; the bird is in its cage in the loft at night, and by day it sits on her shoulder. One spring day a young man comes into the shop with the smell of the sea all over him. The canary suddenly settles on his shoulder, their eyes meet, and the next moment both of them were laughing without knowing which had begun. ... The young man came almost every day. In the girl's eyes there was a shining of shyness and joy, and the canary, which had never taken to anyone but her, began to rub its wings again and again against his cheek. But the girl can speak her heart only with her eyes. Spring passed, and the young man had to set out again for a distant sea. On the night before sailing the girl stood before him and appealed with her whole soul in her eyes, without blinking. Come back. Without fail. And be with me. The young man was a little at a loss, but laughed and nodded two or three times. He had understood it only as an encouragement to come home safe. The girl flushed scarlet and took his hand and linked her little finger with his and shook it hard. The canary went back and forth between their shoulders, singing. — News of the wreck came two months later. Every night the girl came to stand on the beach with the canary on her shoulder, gazing at the sea. And one day it came to her: he is not on the other side of the sea; he has dissolved into the sea.

Characters

The opening (original text)

The girl had large eyes, and the slightly brownish color of her hair suited her fair cheeks well. But she had a habit of always looking at people with those large eyes fixedly, desperately, as though she would eat them up. Unhappily, she was deaf from birth, and dumb as well. That town was a port, one of the bases for the fishing boats that go out to the deep sea. She worked at a small eating house in the town, near the shore.

Kanariya to shōjo by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

One of the men of the search party cried out as he stood gazing blankly at the sea where night was beginning to close in. On that dark, brimming surface he had seemed to see the wordless, joyful shining of the eyes of that girl who was always looking at one so desperately. And then, for an instant, all of them heard, threading through the roar of the breaking waves, the high singing of that canary.... But it may have been that they had mistaken for it the sound of the halyards ringing in the wind.
