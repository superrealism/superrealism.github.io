Biography

A writer of monogatari and a poet of the mid-Heian period. Her birth and death dates are unknown, but she is thought to have been born in the early 970s (probably around 973) and to have died in the mid-1010s. She was born in Kyoto, the daughter of Fujiwara no Tametoki, known as a scholar and poet, and showed her gifts from childhood in her love of Chinese classical texts. She married Fujiwara no Nobutaka and had one daughter (later known as Daini no Sanmi), but was soon widowed. It is said that she began writing "The Tale of Genji" in the solitude that followed. On the strength of the tale's reputation she entered court service as a lady-in-waiting to Empress Shōshi, consort of Emperor Ichijō and daughter of Fujiwara no Michinaga, where she displayed such erudition that she was nicknamed "Our Lady of the Chronicles of Japan." She left "The Diary of Lady Murasaki," recording her observations from court service, and "The Poetic Memoirs of Murasaki Shikibu," a self-selected collection of poems from throughout her life. "The Tale of Genji," spanning fifty-four chapters in all, is considered one of the world's oldest full-length novels, and has remained a wellspring of Japanese literature for a thousand years since.
