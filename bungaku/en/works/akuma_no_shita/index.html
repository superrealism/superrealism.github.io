About this work

The best known of the stories left by the painter Murayama Kaita, who died young. One night in May a telegram arrives for "I" from his friend, the young poet Kaneko Eikichi, and it makes no sense: "KUDANZAKA 301 KANEKO." Hurrying to Kudanzaka, "I" learns instead that Kaneko has died at home with a fire-tong driven into his chest. Solving the riddle of the number, he digs up a package wrapped in black oilpaper, and inside is Kaneko's memoir. The truth about a poet with strangely thick lips, known to everyone as an unaccountable oddity — what happened to his tongue, and what that tongue came to want. A piece of Kaita's own, where the weird and the decadent run together.

Summary

On a clear night in early May, "I" receives a telegram he cannot make sense of: "KUDANZAKA 301 KANEKO." Kaneko Eikichi is a young poet he met two years before at a party full of eccentrics, a man known for lips as strangely thick as a copper rod eaten by verdigris, and for a way of living no one could account for. No one is waiting at Kudanzaka, and when "I" calls at the house in Tomizaka, Kaneko has killed himself by driving a fire-tong through his heart. "I" realizes that the number in the telegram counts the stone covers over the gutter along the slope, and from under the three hundred and first cover, counting up from the bottom, digs out a package in black oilpaper. Inside is a single notebook. — Born to a Hida timber merchant's mistress, Kaneko was an odd child from the first, with a strange habit of putting wall plaster, slugs, and caterpillars into his mouth. Recovering at Kamakura from the illness he fell into after his mother's death, he noticed that food had no savor at all. Looking in a mirror, he found that his tongue had grown to three sun five bu and turned into a fearful piece of flesh bristling all over with needles. The devil in the mirror tells him: a devil's tongue will be satisfied with nothing but a devil's food. Shutting himself up in a poor village in Izu, he piles one monstrous meal upon another, and his craving moves toward a line that must not be crossed. And at the end of the memoir is set down what happened on the night he resolved to die.

Characters

  • I　The narrator. One of Kaneko Eikichi's few friends, who solves the riddle of the telegram and finds the memoir
  • Kaneko Eikichi　A young poet of twenty-seven. He had strangely thick lips and lived a secretive, unaccountable life
  • Kaneko Gorō　Kaneko's half-brother, who has a crescent-shaped birthmark on the sole of his right foot

The opening (original text)

(1) It was a clear night in early May. About eleven o'clock, as I stood in the garden gazing up into the blue depth of the sky, a voice called suddenly from outside the gate: "Telegram!" I took it and read the following words: "KUDANZAKA 301 KANEKO." "What can this be? And what is 301?" It struck me as very strange indeed. Kaneko was the name of a friend, and of the strangest of all my friends at that.

The Devil's Tongue by Murayama Kaita (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Ah, I can live no longer. Friend, what I meant to leave written down is all of this. Have pity on me, I beg you. The document ended there. From the handwriting and from the contents alike, I could not help doubting Kaneko's sanity. When his body was examined his tongue did have needles on it, as described; but the devil's face was, I suppose, no more than a poet's fantasy.
