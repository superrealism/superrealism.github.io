Biography

Born in Shitaya, Edo, in 1868; his real name was Uchida Mitsugu. He studied English at Rikkyo School and other institutions and formed a close friendship with Futabatei Shimei. In 1892 he produced the first full-scale Japanese translation of Dostoevsky's "Crime and Punishment," introducing its social awareness and psychological depth and sending a shock through the literary world. Mixing with writers around the Ken'yūsha, he published the novel "Kure no Nijūhachinichi" ("The Twenty-Eighth of the Old Year"), which cut sharply into the dark corners of society, and "Shakai Hyakumensō" ("A Hundred Faces of Society"), a satire of Meiji society. Known for his voracious reading and encyclopedic knowledge, he also played a major role, as a consultant to the Maruzen bookstore, in importing and introducing Western books. In his later years he wrote vigorously in essays and criticism, including the memoir "Omoidasu Hitobito" ("People I Remember"), which vividly portrayed his Meiji-era literary contemporaries, and, through later reappraisal by critics such as Tsubouchi Yūzō, has come to be recognized as a leading critic representing the Meiji era. He died in 1929 at the age of 61.
