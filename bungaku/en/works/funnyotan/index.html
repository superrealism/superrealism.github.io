About this work

A novella depicting the hard struggle of a man who runs a night-soil collection business in a small city in Kyushu; it received the sixth Akutagawa Prize (1938). When it appeared in the coterie magazine "Bungaku Kaigi" the author was on active service at the China front, and the ceremony was an extraordinary one, held at Hangzhou, where Kobayashi Hideo, then an army press officer, handed him the prize. Officials of the city hall, obstruction tied to the political parties, betrayal by a rival in the trade, days hounded by money — the comedy and the tragedy of a filthy trade and of human beings are written out to the end in a hand where laughter and anger are mixed, and it comes at last to a tremendous scene in which night soil is scattered over the whole town.

Summary

Komori Hikotarō keeps a night-soil collection business under the sign of the Eiseisha in a small city in Kyushu. The truck is worn out, the tubs are rotten and leak, and when he goes to collect his fees he is beaten down on the price and scolded for coming at a bad hour. Even so, with the help of his benefactor Akase Haru­kichi and the backing of the easygoing Abe, he writes a petition to secure his footing as a business designated by the city, and goes about handing it to councilors and men of influence. But Sugiyama, the head of the city sanitation section, cannot move against the pressure of the political parties. Over the forming of a collectors' union, his rival Tomoda works behind the scenes, and the evidence of the bought-off Amano twists the whole matter around. Repairs for the truck, wages for his men, money for drink — hounded from every side, Hikotarō's gears begin little by little to slip. When he is betrayed by a man he trusted and his last rope is cut, something bursts in him. Knocking over the tubs he has piled up and scattering night soil over the whole town, reciting the long nonsense name of Jugemu at top speed and standing with his legs planted like a temple guardian, Hikotarō shines in the evening sun like a demon of gold.

Characters

  • Komori Hikotarō　The man who keeps the night-soil collection business Eiseisha
  • Akase Harukichi　The benefactor of Hikotarō's business
  • Abe　An easygoing man who writes the petition for Hikotarō
  • Sugiyama　Head of the city hall's sanitation section, caught between the pressures of the parties
  • Tomoda　A rival in the trade, who works behind the scenes against the forming of the union
  • Uhei　Akase's mountain watchman, whose wife is said to be possessed by a fox

The opening (original text)

Somewhere it must be raining already; in the direction of the thunderheads that reared up white and swelling above him there was a faint roll of distant thunder. Coming down the slope, Hikotarō put a hand to the brim of his straw hat and looked up at the sky and thought there might be a shower; and when he looked around at the ground shimmering with heat, a single gust of cold wind came, it seemed to him, and rattled the leaves of the watermelon patch. The melons of every size lying about in the red earth were covered with dust and showed a bald sort of green. Still walking down, he made a ring of both hands, put it to his mouth and shouted — hoy! — and a man who was standing stark naked in the middle of the pond that showed small below him, with both arms thrust into the water, lifted his face. Even at that distance one could see that when he made out Hikotarō he straightened up down there, stretched, slapped his back, and smiled up at him.
