About this work

A short story that begins from the inner life of Otaka, who finds herself examining her own skin in the mirror in embarrassingly close detail. A woman who has no ground to stand on but to say that it is all his fault, and the man beside her. Against an Edo night in which the lights of the whitebait boats blur in dots on the water, the relation between two people who cannot put what they feel into words slowly comes undone. The title — the cold bridge — fixes the temperature of the piece.

Summary

O-Kō is sometimes ashamed of herself, particularly when she is at the mirror. "Oh dear, how horrid, how horrid," she murmurs to herself, and blushes to herself, and looks minutely over the face in the glass with a kind of goaded feeling. She has grown altogether fuller; her skin looks transparent, and is soft and moist like the petal of some flower, so that when she strokes it it seems to cling to her fingers — the story begins from this vivid self-consciousness. ... The change she cannot manage herself, O-Kō can only lay to the man beside her. "It is all your fault." There was no other way to save her face. Upstream, toward Tsukudajima, the fires burning in the whitebait boats show hazily here and there, and voices come broken across the water. "Mother," O-Kō calls softly. Her father with his line in the water, her mother coming with a maid carrying tea and food and crouching beside him — such scenes of her childhood lie over the O-Kō of now. After many crossings and misunderstandings the man whispers at last in a low voice: "It is the last time; I shall never do such a thing again; you will see. ... I shall certainly make O-Kō happy." He covered his eyes with his arm, and a sob broke from his throat. Far off, the clappers of the fire watch sounded clear.

Characters

  • O-Kō　A woman who cannot manage the self she sees in the mirror
  • The man　The one who swears he will make O-Kō happy
