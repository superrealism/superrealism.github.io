What is Shinbun Shōsetsu (Newspaper Fiction)?

Category
Form / Genre

A novel serialized in the pages of a newspaper. Once daily newspapers spread in the Meiji era, papers competed to serialize fiction as a draw for readers, and many writers found their principal stage here. Ozaki Kōyō's "Konjiki Yasha" ("The Golden Demon," from 1897), serialized in the "Yomiuri Shimbun," and Tokutomi Roka's "Hototogisu" ("The Cuckoo," from 1898), serialized in the "Kokumin Shimbun" to unprecedented acclaim, are among its early representative examples. Natsume Sōseki joined the Asahi Shimbun company as a professional writer in 1907, and, beginning with "Gubijinsō" ("Poppy"), serialized all of his major novels in that paper, from "Sanshirō," "Sore Kara" ("And Then"), and "Mon" ("The Gate") through "Kokoro" to his unfinished final work "Meian" ("Light and Darkness"). With the flourishing of popular fiction, the newspaper novel grew still more vigorous: Nakazato Kaizan's sweeping epic "Daibosatsu Tōge" ("The Great Bodhisattva Pass," from 1913), serialized in the "Miyako Shimbun," and Yoshikawa Eiji's "Miyamoto Musashi," serialized in the "Asahi Shimbun" from 1935 and winning nationwide popularity, both belong here. Requiring devices to draw readers back day after day, newspaper fiction was the seedbed for domestic fiction and period fiction alike, and played a great role in spreading the modern novel widely among the general public.
