Biography

Born in 1878, the third daughter of a long-established confectionery shop in Sakai, Sakai Ward, Osaka Prefecture. She awakened to literature early through her brother's library, and, taking part in local poetry gatherings, met and fell in love with the tanka poet Yosano Tekkan, editor of the magazine "Myōjō." In 1901, following Tekkan to Tokyo, she published her first tanka collection, "Midaregami" ("Tangled Hair"), boldly singing of a woman's passion and sensuality, and became a central figure of Romantic literature. She married Tekkan that same year and, while bearing twelve children, continued a prolific creative career. In 1904, thinking of her younger brother sent to fight in the Russo-Japanese War, she published "Kimi Shinitamau Koto Nakare" ("Thou Shalt Not Die"), a poem of antiwar sentiment that stirred both praise and controversy. She was also active in the modern-Japanese translation of "The Tale of Genji" and in criticism, and in 1921 she and her husband founded Bunka Gakuin, a coeducational school. She died in 1942 at the age of 63.
