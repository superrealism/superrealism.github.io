About this work

A short story Higuchi Ichiyō serialized in the magazine Bungakukai from February 1894. The faint love of a young man and the orphaned cousin who grew up with him is torn apart by the ambition of his mother, who wants him to rise in the world. It draws the conflict between mother and son over a match with a good family, and the bitter resignation of a girl who withdraws and goes away. The title likens to a flower a girl's love, kept hidden and never allowed to open.

Summary

Segawa Yonosuke, who lives quietly with his mother in Hongō, is a young man who has finished law school and works at a publishing house. His cousin Oshin, who lives with them, lost both parents in childhood and has been raised by the Segawa family since she was eight; now eighteen, she looks up to Yonosuke as an elder brother and secretly dreams of one day becoming his wife. Yonosuke too has feelings for Oshin, but his mother Chika, past fifty, wishes for her son's advancement in the world, and believes that a match with the wealthy Tahara family — connected to a vice-minister — is the way to achieve it. The Tahara daughter had taken a fancy to Yonosuke at a New Year's card game, and Chika, resolved even to become a "demon aunt" if need be, determines to part the two young people and press this match forward. Yonosuke, unwilling to rise in the world through a wife's connections and wanting to make his own way by his own strength, clashes with his mother and declares that he wants neither Tahara's daughter nor Oshin. But his mother conspires with an old acquaintance, the moon-lute teacher Otatsu, to close off every avenue, and Yonosuke, outmaneuvered, finds himself unable to move. The two women go further still, arranging a position in service to send Oshin away. Made to choose her own destination, Oshin turns down a post in a noble household and instead asks to go to the countryside of Kai, to study under the painter Kurosawa who lives there in seclusion. To learn painting — so that, in her longing, she might draw Yonosuke's likeness and find some comfort for her heart — this was the wish behind her request. Yonosuke, overhearing it in secret, weeps alone. And so Oshin, on the very first morning train departing from Ueno, sets off entirely alone, her hidden feelings held close within her breast.

Characters

  • Segawa Yonosuke　A young man who has finished law school and works at a publishing house. Caught between his feelings for his cousin Oshin and his mother's ambitions for his advancement
  • Oshin　Yonosuke's cousin. An orphaned girl taken in by the Segawa family at eight, now eighteen. She loves Yonosuke but departs for Kai to study painting
  • Chika　Yonosuke's mother, past fifty. Wishing for her son's advancement, she pursues a match with the Tahara family and tries to part the two young people
  • Otatsu　A widowed moon-lute teacher living in Yanaka, an old friend of the Segawa family. She helps arrange the match with the Tahara family

The opening (original text)

Somewhere in Hongō — Maruyama, perhaps, or Katamachi — in a quiet spot along a hedge planted with willow and cherry in succession, stands a dwelling, not spacious but kept neat and clean. Its master is one Segawa Yonosuke, who last autumn graduated from a certain law school, and who now works — in the publishing division, or the editorial bureau, or some such department — drawing however much of a monthly salary it may be; a young man quietly biding his time for the dawn of his own rising fortune, living together with his mother Chika, past fifty.

The ending (original text)

Now that this hidden traffic between the two households had at last begun, there was no lack of moments when a chill of unease passed through Chika's breast, and she found herself thinking she wanted the girl sent off as soon as possible — and so, on some day chosen without any particular regard for the matter, Chika's will carried the day, and it was settled that Oshin would leave on the very first train departing Ueno the next morning. What could Oshin be thinking? A feeling left unspoken is one that others have no way of knowing, and yet even a single word, once it carried meaning, pierced Yonosuke's breast as though cut by a keen blade; and as he lay sleepless through the night, the failing lamp's shadow growing dim, he thought — before long the birds will cry, the temple bell too will sound its warning — and when the moment comes to step across the threshold, when the train's whistle sounds, when at last her shadow fades away into the smoke, what will become of her then? — so Yonosuke wondered, even as, far off, her painting hand would take the plovers of some rocky shore as her only companions, and sketch the sorrowful image of a love she carried within her. How pitiable, the heart hidden within Oshin.
