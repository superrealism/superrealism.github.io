About this work

Izumi Kyōka's first play, published in the magazine Engei Kurabu in March 1913. In a village suffering drought, it sets the legend of the dragon god said to live in Yashagaike against the fate of a couple who have kept the balance of the waters by going on ringing a bell. Human greed and superstition are set against an imagined other world, in his best-known play, staged many times since by the shinpa and shingeki companies.

Summary

Hagiwara Akira, who keeps the bell tower at Kotohikidani, a mountain hamlet in Echizen, lives quietly there with his wife Yuri. The bell is bound by an old compact: it must be struck three times a day so that Princess Shirayuki, the dragon deity who dwells in Yashagaike, shall not break her promise to men and drown the village. In the drought that has come, the villagers decide to sink Yuri into the pool as a sacrifice in a prayer for rain. Akira takes up the bell hammer to defend her against them all; but Shirayuki, learning that the hour for striking the bell has passed, breaks the seal at last for the sake of her own love, and lets the pool overflow. The flood sweeps down and swallows every one of the villagers, and Akira and Yuri sink with the bell to the bottom of the water — where, smiling, the two are joined; and Gakuen stands alone in the bell tower and mourns them under the moonlight.

Characters

  • Hagiwara Akira　The keeper of the bell tower at Shikami village, who stands against the villagers demanding a sacrifice in order to protect his wife Yuri
  • Yuri　Akira's wife, chosen as the sacrifice in the drought-stricken village, and the occasion of the dragon deity's anger being loosed
  • Princess Shirayuki　The dragon deity of Yashagaike, who has kept her promise to men and waited for the sound of the bell, until at last she can bear it no longer and breaks the seal
  • Yamazawa Gakuen　A Bachelor of Letters and Hagiwara Akira's close friend, who comes to the village in search of the vanished Akira and, as a kind of narrator, watches over the bell-keeper and his wife

The opening (original text)

Place: Kotohikidani, Shikami village, Ōno district, the province of Echizen Time: The present day. — High summer In the hamlet at the foot of Mount Mikuni, the bell of the sixth hour of evening is heard. — The curtain rises. Hagiwara Akira, made up at this point with white hair, stands on the bell tower gazing at the evening sun. Ivy twines about the pillars of the tower, moss covers the high stone steps, grass grows on the ridge of its roof. Presently Akira comes slowly down the steps and goes up behind O-Yuri, who is washing rice in the clear water. AKIRA: The water is beautiful. Whenever I look at it... beautiful. YURI: Yes.

The ending (original text)

SHIRAYUKI: This new Kanegafuchi shall be the dwelling of husband and wife. Come, all of you. I am going to Kengamine.... From now on we may come and go as we please. O-Yuri, O-Yuri, let us sing songs together. All at once it is dark again. And now the great bell lies in the water. Akira and O-Yuri are there together, Akira resting his cheek on his hand against the bell's crown, O-Yuri below him, her skirts trailing in the water, leaning back on her hands and looking up; the two gaze steadily at each other and smile. The light of the moon is brilliant. Gakuen stands alone, high in the bell tower; facing the water, he bows and puts his palms together. The moon grows brighter still. (— Curtain)
