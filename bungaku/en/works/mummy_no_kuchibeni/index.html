About this work

Tamura Toshiko's representative work, published in "Chūō Kōron" in 1913. Yoshio, a husband whose manuscripts will not sell, and his wife Minoru, who writes herself. In a two-story house looking out on the woods of Ueno, life goes on poorly, with bundles carried to the pawnshop through the rain — and then Minoru's story wins a competition and she is taken up by the literary world. The husband's jealousy, and the selfhood swelling inside the woman: the breathing of a married pair in whom love and hate are tangled together, drawn in a sensuous, clinging prose. The title comes from the strange dream Minoru has at the end.

Summary

At dusk in early January, Minoru was looking at the clouded sky from the upstairs window. Her husband Yoshio, who had gone out to look for work, would come home today too without having sold a manuscript. Sure enough he comes back carrying his wrapped bundle, presses his face into the matting and says, "No good. No good." The two have gone the whole day without eating. "I am a hopeless creature. I have no power at all to keep you." Minoru takes a kimono from the chest, makes up a bundle, and sets off through the rain for the pawnshop. When the light notes are in the pocket of her coat, the two of them get back for a while the faces of ordinary people in the world. But their want wears their nerves thin, and their love twines itself into the shapes of possessiveness and jealousy. Before long a story of Minoru's wins a competition, and she is invited to the house of Minomura, the man of letters who judged it. Surrounded by literary men, feeling her emotions dance brilliantly, she is kept there until late and comes home to find Yoshio receiving her in silence with a jealousy he has not shown before. That night Minoru has a strange dream: in a glass case a gray man and woman lie mummified one upon the other, and on the upturned face of the woman the lips alone are painted a vivid crimson.

Characters

  • Minoru　A young wife who writes. Out of a poor life she wins a competition and is taken up by the literary world
  • Yoshio　Minoru's husband. His manuscripts will not sell, and he feels jealousy at his wife's success
  • Minomura　The man of letters who judged the competition. He invites Minoru to his house and talks of the trouble of judging
  • Arino　A man of letters who happens to be at Minomura's house. "And yet, you know," is his constant phrase

The opening (original text)

1 A lonely wind came blowing, and the tip of a cypress that stood head and shoulders above the rest, like a crown, swayed thinly in it. The sky of an early-January dusk was clouded a muddy color with yellow in it, and between the bare treetops, drawn as though with a pen, appeared the celadon-colored roof of the five-story pagoda. Thinking of where her husband might be, who had gone out early that morning to look for work with no notion where to go, Minoru stood at the upstairs window with her hands in her sleeves, gazing at the sky.

The ending (original text)

"I had a dream like this." Minoru went over to Yoshio and told him about it. And saying, "It must be a sign of something," she went to her desk meaning to draw at least the shape of it. "I detest hearing about dreams." So said Yoshio, who was in the cold sunshine combing the body of the thin dog.
