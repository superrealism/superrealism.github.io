About this work

Naoki Sanjūgo's representative work, serialized in the "Tokyo Nichinichi Shimbun" and the "Osaka Mainichi Shimbun" from 1930 to the following year, which made him a fashionable writer. Taking as its subject the Oyura Affair that shook the Satsuma domain at the end of the shogunate, it depicts the covert struggle between the party backing Shimazu Nariakira and the faction supporting Hisamitsu, the son of a concubine. What rises from the very first page is the cursing rite performed by the military diviner Maki Chūtarō. Spells and assassins, senior retainers and low-ranking samurai, and the breath of young men such as Saigō and Ōkubo. A monumental work by which popular literature secured its place, and one that made immortal the name of the author from whom the Naoki Prize takes its own.

Summary

On a mountain path at dawn, Kajiki Genpakusai, military diviner to the Shimazu house, climbs with his senior pupil Wada Nijūrō. Gathering medicinal herbs along the way, they pass a hunter carrying a dog with its head cut off. Someone had been making a fire near the rocks the night before, the man says, and the dog that barked at them never came back. Reaching a rock that juts out over the valley, Genpakusai finds mulberry branches for firewood, the smell of storax and borneol, salt, and a snakeskin, and his face changes. "This is a rite to curse a man to death, worshipping the Wrathful Yamāntaka by the abhicāruka method. There is no one but Maki who knows this rite" — and Maki Chūtarō had gone up to Edo. The target of the curse is the heir of Shimazu Nariakira, who is to succeed to the lordship. The party backing Nariakira and the faction carrying Hisamitsu, the son of Lady Oyura. The covert struggle that split the seven hundred and seventy thousand koku of Satsuma in two draws in spells, assassins and intrigue, and sweeps at last into a great purge of the whole domain. In the midst of that whirlpool, young men of low rank such as Masumitsu Kyūnosuke set out for Edo to carry on Nariakira's will.

Characters

  • Kajiki Genpakusai　Military diviner to the Shimazu house. A practiced adept who reads the traces of the curse
  • Wada Nijūrō　Genpakusai's senior pupil
  • Maki Chūtarō　The military diviner who lays the curse on Nariakira's heir. He stands at the shadowed center of the story
  • Shimazu Nariakira　The able heir who is to succeed to the lordship. A party backs him and a faction carries Hisamitsu against him
  • Oyura　Shimazu Narioki's concubine. As Hisamitsu's mother she gave the affair its name
  • Masumitsu Kyūnosuke　A young retainer who sets out for Edo to carry on Nariakira's will

The opening (original text)

The Cursing The young leaves on the high treetops were rejoicing healthily in the faint breeze of early morning and the mild sunlight; but under the great gloomy trees, under the old trees, it was dim, and the thick undergrowth and the weeds were still wet. The narrow path, which even woodcutters and hunters used only now and then, showed nothing but a little red earth out of the grass, and from both sides the branches and the grass had grown out far enough to catch at a man's breast, at his head.

Chronicle of the Southern Domain by Naoki Sanjūgo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Those going went east, those seeing them off went back to the castle town. They were separated by a long distance, but their hearts were one. Hearts made new by Nariakira's death had heat and strength enough to carry out his will. Having passed the barrier, the four figures came into sight, small, on the road beside which they had fought Yamauchi in pursuit of Maki. Voices no longer carried, but they raised their hands, and raised them again — and drew away, and turned, and were parted.
