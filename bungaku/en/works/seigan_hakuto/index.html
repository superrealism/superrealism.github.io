About this work

A collection of aphorisms Saitō Ryokuu serialized in the Niroku Shinpō in 1900. He strings together short maxims that look coldly at the manners of the age, human feeling and the literary world. It is known for lines such as the one about the brush being single while the chopsticks are a pair, so that one should know the few cannot match the many — the light dexterity of Edo gesaku and a sharp satirical spirit compressed together. Held to show best the true ground of Ryokuu, at once novelist and cutting critic.

Summary

Since this work is a collection of short aphorisms and maxims strung one after another, it has no plot to summarize.

The opening (original text)

○ To speak of the life to come has become almost the habit of a certain party. On land there are railways, at sea there are steamships, and it must be because the convenience of a civilized age is now made the chief thing. Why, one need hardly say — it is because in the world to come we shall not have the grief of meeting nose to nose. For "grief" it would be better to write "fear."

Seigan hakutō by Saitō Ryokuu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

○ Consider: the pen is one, the chopsticks are two. Know that the few are no match for the many.

Seigan hakutō by Saitō Ryokuu (Aozora Bunko)
