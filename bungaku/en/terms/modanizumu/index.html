What is Modanizumu (Modernism)?

Category
Literary Movement

A general term for the avant-garde artistic current that arose in Western Europe after World War I, and the literature that developed in Japan under its influence from the late Taishō era into the early Shōwa era. The transformed city that emerged after the Great Kantō Earthquake, and new realities such as machinery, speed, and the crowd, could not be captured by existing methods of realism, and prompted an innovation in expression itself. In fiction, this includes the New Sensationist school of Yokomitsu Riichi and Kawabata Yasunari, which championed sensory metaphor and stylistic experiment; the Shinkō Geijutsuha (New Art School), which depicted urban manners; and the New Psychologist school of Itō Sei and Hori Tatsuo, who learned from Proust and Joyce to trace the stream of consciousness. In poetry, Haruyama Yukio and Nishiwaki Junzaburō, associated with the magazine "Shi to Shiron" ("Poetry and Poetics"), founded in 1928, introduced Surrealism and Intellectualism, leaving a lasting influence on Japanese modern poetry. Resonating with the urban culture of the early Shōwa era, when "modern" became a buzzword, it stood alongside proletarian literature, which sought political and ideological engagement in literature, dividing the literary establishment of the early Shōwa era in two, but as a current within fiction it was short-lived and dispersed amid suppression and the wartime system.
