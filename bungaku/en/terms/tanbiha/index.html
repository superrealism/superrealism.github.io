What is Tanbiha (the Aesthetic School)?

Category
Literary Movement

An anti-Naturalist current of the late Meiji and Taishō eras that placed supreme value on the enjoyment of sensual and decadent beauty over morality and practical use. It arose as a reaction against Naturalism's gloomy depiction of reality, and Nagai Kafū was its forerunner, depicting the pathos of the geisha quarters under the influence of French fin-de-siècle literature in works such as "Sumidagawa" ("The River Sumida"), and, as editor of Keio University's magazine "Mita Bungaku" (founded 1910), nurturing the next generation. Tanizaki Jun'ichirō emerged with "Shisei" ("The Tattooer," 1910), published in "Shinshichō," depicting an uncanny beauty dwelling in a woman's flesh, and went on to pursue sensuality and fantasy throughout his career. Satō Haruo, a disciple of Kafū's, established a decadent lyricism in "Den'en no Yūutsu" ("Rural Melancholy"), and Okamoto Kanoko, with her own distinctive sensual expression, is also counted within this lineage. The magazine "Subaru" was a stage for its activity as well. It stood in contrast to the idealism of the Shirakaba school, as an aesthetic sensibility standing side by side with it.
