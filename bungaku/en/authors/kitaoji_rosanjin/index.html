Biography

Born in 1883 in Kamigamo, Kyoto; his real name was Fusajirō. Starting out in calligraphy and seal engraving, he was an extraordinarily gifted artist who showed rare talent across ceramics, cuisine, and gastronomic criticism. As advisor and head chef of the members-only restaurant Hoshigaoka Saryō, he pursued the art of fine cuisine, and built his own Hoshigaoka kiln to make the vessels himself, producing numerous celebrated ceramic works. Guided by the belief that "tableware is the clothing of food," he treated cuisine and its vessels as a single art, and left many pointed, concrete essays on food. He died in 1959.
