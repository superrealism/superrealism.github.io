About this work

The second of Murō Saisei's three autobiographical novels, published in Chūō Kōron in 1919, and the sequel to Childhood: a novel of youth. It draws the wavering mind of the narrator, raised at a temple in Kanazawa, as adolescence brings an interest in the opposite sex and the ache of desire, and, at the same time, his turn toward writing poetry and the pure friendship he forms with a young poet in poor health. A fresh autobiographical work laying the waking of sexuality over the waking of art.

Summary

"I," brought up in the keeping of a temple, have come to the years between boyhood and youth. Bewildered by the face of a girl seen in the town and by the ache of sex waking within me, my heart swaying between guilt and longing, I am at a loss with a desire that has not yet taken shape. At the same time "I" throw myself deeply into the writing of poems. The one with whom I share most is Omote, an older friend in poetry who is often laid up with diseased lungs; the two of us show each other our work and keep a pure companionship. Omote has a girl, O-Tama, who secretly loves him. But the illness is cruel, and in time O-Tama too is taken by the same disease. Amid the crossing of the waking of sex and the waking of poetry, and the shadow of illness and death stealing over young lives, the growth of a sensitive boy into a writer is drawn in a delicate lyricism.

The opening (original text)

I lived freely with my father, who was near seventy, in the inner hall deep within the lonely temple grounds. By that time I had already turned seventeen.

Sei ni mezameru koro by Murō Saisei (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

On the way I felt that that dreadful disease had already begun to show itself in her. Suffering from the anxious nerves that asked whether the disease might not be in me too, and thinking of that small, girlish, pitiable body lying quietly at home, I felt that she, like Omote, would not last long. Gazing steadily at the fountain rising without a pause, I went down the slope of the park with a sunken heart. "Lately I feel that I am going to die." I felt that the words she had spoken the other day were now truly at work upon her.

Sei ni mezameru koro by Murō Saisei (Aozora Bunko)
