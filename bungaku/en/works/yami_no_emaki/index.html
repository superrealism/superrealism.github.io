About this work

A short story Kajii Motojirō published in Shi Genjitsu in 1930. The narrator, convalescing from tuberculosis at Yugashima in Izu, draws with acute senses, like a picture scroll, the fascination and the terror of a total darkness without electric light. The unease of taking one step into a blackness in which nothing can be seen, and the relief of a single lamp glimpsed at the bottom of it. A short piece of Kajii's mental-state writing, bringing up the tension of life and the sharpness of the senses through the contrast of dark and light.

Summary

"I," recuperating from tuberculosis at a hot-spring inn in Yugashima, Izu, find myself captivated by the allure of a total "darkness" unbroken by any electric light. A certain newspaper article — telling of a burglar who, even in the dark, could run any number of miles so long as he had a single stick — sends a thrilling, exhilarating shiver through "me." To take a single step into a deep blackness where nothing can be seen, where one cannot even know what is there, requires a passion for despair, like walking barefoot over thistles. "I" recall that road along the cedar forest by the ravine, walked night after night, dark even under the moon. As I walk, the darkness deepens, my unease rises, and at a certain extreme point, all at once, the roar of the rapids beneath my feet comes surging up. At the bottom of that darkness, where the heart feels as though it might be wrenched apart, a single electric light suddenly flashes into view, and the darkness ends — but on a foggy night, even that light lies distant, and no matter how long one walks, it cannot be reached. As "I" set down the memory of that dark landscape with a keen sensibility, like a picture scroll, I could not help but find the nights of the city, awash in electric light, all the more sordid by comparison. A short piece in which Kajii, with his finely honed sensibility, captures the tension between darkness and light.

Characters

The opening (original text)

According to what a certain notorious burglar, who had recently thrown all Tokyo into an uproar, said after his capture, he could run any number of miles even through a darkness where nothing at all could be seen, so long as he had a single stick. He would thrust that stick out before his body, again and again, and go running blindly on, through fields or wherever else lay in his path. When I read this article in the newspaper, I could not help but feel a thrilling, exhilarating shiver run irrepressibly through me.

Yami no emaki by Kajii Motojirō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The landscape of that darkness never changes, however often I see it in my mind. I walked that road more times than I can count. I repeated, always, the very same fancy. The impression has carved itself into my heart. The darkness of the highway, and the shape of the trees' darkness, deeper still than the darkness around it, remain before my eyes even now. And every time I call it to mind, I cannot help but find the nights of the city where I now live — awash, wherever I go, in the flow of electric light — a little sordid by comparison.

Yami no emaki by Kajii Motojirō (Aozora Bunko)
