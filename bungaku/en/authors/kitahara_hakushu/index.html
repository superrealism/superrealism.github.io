Biography

Raised as the eldest son of a sake-brewing family in Yanagawa, Fukuoka Prefecture, born in 1885; his real name was Ryūkichi. After dropping out of Waseda University he joined Yosano Tekkan's New Poetry Society and rose to prominence in the magazine "Myōjō." With Kinoshita Mokutarō, Ishii Hakutei, and others he founded the Pan no Kai, and with his first poetry collection, "Jashūmon" ("The Heretics," 1909), steeped in exoticism and sensual beauty, and "Omoide" ("Memories," 1911), which sang of memories of his hometown, he brought a fresh sensibility to modern Japanese poetry. With the tanka collection "Kiri no Hana" ("Paulownia Blossoms") and children's songs such as "Karatachi no Hana" ("The Trifoliate Orange Blossoms"), "Kono Michi" ("This Road"), and "Yurikago no Uta" ("The Cradle Song"), he became a leading figure across poetry, tanka, children's songs, and folk songs alike, leaving an enormous body of work over his lifetime. He continued to create even after losing his eyesight in his later years, and died in 1942 at the age of 57.
