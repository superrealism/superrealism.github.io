About this work

A short story Masamune Hakuchō published in Bunshō Sekai in 1916. It draws the painter Magoshi, who sells nothing, tormented by an unaccountable unease and self-disgust and driven toward ruin. On a cold, unseasonable day of rain, unable to bear a distraction like being haunted for two or three days by a vengeful spirit, he goes to see his sister. To that sister, plump and living without a care, he spits out that there is no art or anything else in pictures like his. Through a painter carrying an inner anguish no one understands and rolling about wrapped in bedding in an empty room, Hakuchō gouges out the emptiness and despair that lie at the bottom of a life.

Summary

Though it is the end of May, the cold is so out of season that one wants a brazier, and the rain has fallen all day. The painter Magoshi, who has lain down with the bedding over him trying to quiet his mind, finds that the more he keeps still and silent the less he can bear the violence in his head. Might it not lighten his head to smash whatever furniture is at hand, or to strike or kick somebody? — and driven by such impulses he is tormented by a frantic feeling whose true nature he cannot make out, as though "for two or three days now a vengeful spirit had been upon him." Unable to bear it, Magoshi goes out into the rain and calls at the house of his younger sister, who lives nearby. Her features are like her brother's, but she is plump and of a good color, and lives without a care; she is glad of a visit from a brother she has not seen for some time, and gossips about their relations. But Magoshi grinds his teeth at the way she criticizes her brother's pictures as though she were a connoisseur, and argues impudently in the fashionable language of art. "What art or anything else can there be in pictures like mine?" "The room is empty. And in that empty room I roll about with the bedding over me" — so he abuses himself as though flinging himself down. Cut off from the world and from his own family alike, carrying an anguish that reaches no one, Magoshi's mind grows more and more desolate. And in time he comes to the fixed thought that in order to escape the beggarly life of every day and stand on his own feet there is nothing for it but to destroy this worthless body of his, and is seized by a vision of hanging himself from the pine branch that reaches over from the neighbor's fence. A psychological novel in which Hakuchō looks without mercy at the emptiness and the despair that lie coiled within a human being.

The opening (original text)

Though it was already the end of May, the cold was so out of season that one wanted a brazier, and the rain had fallen all day besides. Magoshi, who since the afternoon had lain down with the bedding over him and tried to quiet his mind, found that the more he kept silent and still the less he could bear the violence in his head. It seemed to him that if he were to smash all the furniture about him one after another, or to strike or kick somebody, his head might grow a little lighter.

Kamen by Masamune Hakuchō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Suddenly it came to him that in order to escape the beggarly life of every day and stand on his own feet, there was nothing for it but to destroy this worthless body of his. The frantic feeling of these two or three days, whose true nature he could not make out, made him forget for a little while the dread of death. The pine branch reaching over from the neighbor's fence in the dim rainy night looked as though it would serve to hang his body from and put an end to his breath....
