What is Roman-shugi (Romanticism)?

Category
Literary Movement

A literary current that prized the liberation of individual emotion and the self, singing loudly of love, ideals, and the world of fancy. The importation of the European Romanticism that had arisen at the end of the eighteenth century began with Mori Ōgai's collection of translated poetry "Omokage" (1889) and his novel "Maihime" ("The Dancing Girl," 1890), and it began in earnest with Kitamura Tōkoku and Shimazaki Tōson, associated with the magazine "Bungakukai," founded in 1893. Tōkoku became the movement's ideological pillar, championing the nobility of love and the freedom of the inner life in essays such as "The Poet of World-Weariness and Woman" and "On Inner Life," and Tōson's poetry collection "Wakanashū" ("Seedlings") set the sentimentality of youth to fresh new cadences and established modern lyric poetry. It also spread into the tanka reforms of "Myōjō," beginning with Yosano Akiko's collection "Midaregami" ("Tangled Hair"), boldly singing of sensuality, into Izumi Kyōka's fantastical fiction, and into Takayama Chogyū's passionate criticism, reaching its peak in the 1900s after the First Sino-Japanese War. It declined after the Russo-Japanese War as Naturalism rose to prominence, but its spirit of prizing the self was carried on by the Aesthetic school and the Shirakaba school.
