About this work

A child the size of a finger, granted after prayers to the deity of Sumiyoshi. Rowing up the Yodo River in a rice bowl with a chopstick for an oar, Issunbōshi enters the service of a lord in Kyoto, stabs his way about inside an ogre's belly and comes away with the magic mallet — one of the best-loved of all Japanese folk tales, retold by Kusuyama Masao for his Treasury of Japanese Children's Tales. It keeps the older plot, in which it is Issunbōshi's own mischief that causes the princess to be turned out of the house.

Summary

A husband and wife living at Naniwa in the province of Settsu prayed to the deity of Sumiyoshi for a child — even a child no bigger than a finger would do — and a boy no bigger than a finger was really born to them. However old he grew he never got any taller, and the neighborhood children teased him when he went out; but Issun Bōshi only smiled and said nothing. In his sixteenth year he asked leave to go up to the capital and try his fortune. He got a sewing needle from his mother and made a hilt and scabbard for it out of barley straw; then, in a rice bowl for a boat with a chopstick for an oar, he rowed out from the beach at Sumiyoshi and reached Toba after a month. Slipping in at the grandest gate in Sanjō, he found a place in service, worked willingly and was liked by everyone, and came to attend the daughter of the house, a girl of thirteen. One day, to spite her after losing a quarrel, he smeared crumbs of sweets around her mouth while she was napping and then wept out in the garden; and the affair rolled in an unexpected direction, so that the young lady was turned out of the house. Feeling himself to blame, Issun Bōshi went with her aboard a boat; but a storm carried them off to a strange island, and there two demons attacked them. Swallowed at a gulp, he stabbed about inside the demon's belly, climbed up behind its eyes and drove the pair away. Waving the magic mallet the demons had dropped, he grew a foot taller, then three feet, and at the third stroke stood a fine man of nearly six feet.

Characters

  • Issun Bōshi　A man no bigger than a finger, granted by the deity of Sumiyoshi
  • The young lady　The daughter of the Councillor of Sanjō, turned out of the house by Issun Bōshi's trick
  • The Councillor of Sanjō　A minister of great standing, and Issun Bōshi's master
  • The two demons　Who attack the lady on the island and drop the magic mallet

The opening (original text)

Long ago, at a place called Naniwa in the province of Settsu, there lived a husband and wife. Because they had not a single child, they used to go and worship at the shrine of the deity of Sumiyoshi and pray with all their hearts: "Please grant us one child. Even a child no bigger than a finger would do."

Issunbōshi by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The emperor at once granted Issun Bōshi a rank and had him called the Lesser Captain of Horikawa. The Lesser Captain then asked leave of the Councillor of Sanjō afresh and took the young lady as his wife. And they sent for his father and mother from Naniwa in Settsu, and the whole household came together and passed their lives happily.

Issunbōshi by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)
