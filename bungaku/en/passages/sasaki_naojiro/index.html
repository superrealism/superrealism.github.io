Famous passages by Sasaki Naojirō

Well-known lines from the works of Sasaki Naojirō.

2 passages

The Black Cat

Who has not, a hundred times, found himself committing a vile or a silly action, for no other reason than because he knows he should not?

A passage in which the narrator discusses the spirit of perverseness as the cause of his own ruin. Pinning down the human impulse to do something precisely because it must not be done, it shows the heart of Poe's psychological horror.

Asshāke no hōkai

During the whole of a dull, dark, and soundless day in the autumn of the year, when the clouds hung oppressively low in the heavens, I had been passing alone, on horseback, through a singularly dreary tract of country; and at length found myself, as the shades of the evening drew on, within view of the melancholy House of Usher.

The opening of the story. Heaping up gloomy descriptions — oppressive low clouds, a dull autumn day, a dreary stretch of country — it fixes from the very first the atmosphere of a story moving toward collapse.
