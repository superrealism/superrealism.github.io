About this work

The best-loved folk tale in Japan: Momotarō, born from a great peach that came floating down the river, takes a dog, a monkey and a pheasant with him and subdues the demons of Demon Island. This is Kusuyama Masao's retelling for the anthology Nihon Dōwa Hōgyokushū, which keeps the sound-words and the refrains — the rolling, tumbling of the peach on the water — for reading aloud. The plot and much of the wording that come to mind today when one thinks of the Momotarō story are largely owed to this version.

Summary

Long, long ago, the old man went to the hills to cut brushwood, and the old woman went to the river to wash. One day a great peach came floating down from upstream, donburakokko, sukkokko. The peach the old woman carried home split neatly in two, and out jumped a healthy baby boy. Because he was born from a peach they named him Momotarō, and he grew so strong that there was no one in all the villages round about who could wrestle with him. When he was fifteen, Momotarō heard that far off at the end of the sea lay Ogre Island, where wicked ogres guarded treasure they had carried off from every province, and he made up his mind to subdue them. With the best millet dumplings in Japan, made by the old woman, hanging at his waist, he set out; and on the way he shared his dumplings with a dog, a monkey and a pheasant, and made the three of them his retainers. Crossing to Ogre Island in a boat, the party beat the ogres in no time: the pheasant pecked at their eyes from the sky, the monkey opened the castle gate from within, and the dog bit at their shins. The ogre chief, pinned down, wept and surrendered, and offered up mountains of treasure — the cloak of invisibility, the hat of invisibility, the lucky mallet, the wish-granting jewel and the rest. When Momotarō came home in triumph with the treasure loaded on a cart, the old man and the old woman welcomed him beside themselves with joy. The sky was clear and blue, and in the garden the cherry blossoms were in full bloom.

Characters

  • Momotarō　The boy born from a peach. Strong, and gentle in disposition
  • The old man and the old woman　The old couple who found Momotarō and brought him up with care
  • The dog, the monkey and the pheasant　The three who took his millet dumplings and became his retainers
  • The ogre chief　The head of the ogres who guard the treasure on Ogre Island

The opening (original text)

Long, long ago, in a certain place, there were an old man and an old woman. Every day the old man went to the hills to cut brushwood, and the old woman went to the river to wash. One day, while the old woman was busily washing at the riverside, one great peach came floating down from upstream: "Donburakokko, sukkokko. Donburakokko, sukkokko."

The ending (original text)

The dog stood up on his forelegs, barking bow-wow, bow-wow for joy. The monkey laughed kya, kya and bared his white teeth. The pheasant cried ken, ken and turned somersault after somersault in the air. The sky was clear and blue, and in the garden the cherry blossoms were in full bloom.
