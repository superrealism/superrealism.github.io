About this work

On a mountain road, out driving as a couple for the first time in three months, the husband begins deliberately to talk about what he saw on the day he went to play golf that spring — a short story Yamakawa Masao wrote for a motoring magazine. A left-hand-drive foreign car, a white Western-style hotel, a navy Toyopet. A greeting of hands touched through the window on the pretence of overtaking. A meticulously assembled plan of revenge is turned inside out, in a way nobody expects, by a single sheet of paper that arrives at the end.

Summary

"It really must be three months since we came out on a drive like this together." Sundays and holidays alike, her husband had never missed his golf. The imported five-dash they had strained to buy instead of a child costs a great deal in tax and in repairs. When his wife suggests trading it in, her husband answers in a serious voice: "Once a thing is mine I do not care to let go of it easily.... Unless the compensation is really advantageous, there is no pleasure in it." ... Before long her husband begins to talk. How this spring he was taken by a client to the golf course further along this road. How, the game being rained off, he came back by this same road and saw their own car standing in front of a white Western-style hotel. How he checked the number. How Moriya got out of it and went into the hotel. To the wife who cries "That is a lie," her husband goes on quietly. That since that day he has never once gone to play golf, but always waited at the hotel ahead of her. That her car is left-hand drive and Moriya's right-hand drive; so that she always draws alongside as if to overtake, and puts her hand out of the window and touches his. That this is their "good day" and their "good-bye." ... We shall soon be at the place where you always touch hands. Her husband put on the brake, pushed her forcibly into the driver's seat, got into the passenger seat, hid his body against the back of the seat and gave his order. When Moriya's car tries to pass on the inside, turn the wheel sharply to the left. On the left, where there was no guardrail, was a gorge with a torrent gnawing at the rocks. — In the mirror a dark blue Toyopet draws near. Desperately ignoring those words, the wife kept the car as close to the guardrail as she could and went on pressing the accelerator.

Characters

  • The husband　A man of fixed habits, who will not let go of anything once he has it
  • The wife　Married to him six years
  • Moriya　A man who was in the same tennis club as her husband

The opening (original text)

"...It really must be three months since we came out on a drive like this together." "About that," said his wife, leaning back in the seat with her eyes closed. Whether it was the thick green flowing past near the window, or her headache, her cheeks looked a little pale and drawn. "And all that time, on Sundays and holidays, you never once missed your golf.... How you never grew tired of it."

Aru doraibu by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"...No. It is not so. It is not so, I tell you." She murmured it without meaning to; but she no longer had the strength to explain, and she knew there was no chance of being believed. In the pain of her whole body and the new tears welling up, she noticed suddenly that she had begun to reckon up the small estate her husband had left.
