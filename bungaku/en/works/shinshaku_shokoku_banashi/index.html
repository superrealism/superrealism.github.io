About this work

A collection Dazai published in 1945. It consists of twelve stories in which he chose short pieces he liked from the work of Ihara Saikaku and, keeping the original as a frame, added freely from his own imagination. Exactly as he says in his own note — that these are not modern translations of the classics — it is a book of adaptations, borrowing Saikaku's ideas and breathing into them the psychology of a modern person along with Dazai's characteristic humour and feeling. Written under wartime and widely read as one of his central works from before and during the war.

Summary

"My Saikaku" — Dazai, who reveres Ihara Saikaku as the greatest writer in the world, selects short pieces from his complete works — from Buke Giri Monogatari, Seken Munazan'yō, Saikaku Shokoku Banashi and the rest — and, borrowing their ideas, freely rewrites them into new tales. A story that in the original runs to only a few pages of manuscript swells, in Dazai's hands, to ten times its length, and samurai living by duty and pride, townsmen tossed about by money, and unexpected touches of human feeling all come vividly back to life. Through twelve pieces mingling laughter and sorrow — among them "Hadakagawa," drawing the pride of a samurai house, and another telling of a man who renounces the world — Dazai breathes the rhythm of the modern novel into a retelling of the classics. A linked set of stories brimming with respect for Saikaku and with the sheer pleasure of storytelling.

The opening (original text)

I have given it the title Shinshaku Shokoku Banashi, feeling as though I would like to gloss it, so to speak, as "My Saikaku" — but this is by no means a modern translation of Saikaku. A modern translation of a classic is, on the whole, a meaningless thing, and no work for a writer to undertake. What I plan is to select some twenty short pieces I am fond of from among Saikaku's complete works, write out freely the fancies of my own that gather around them, and gather the whole into one volume for publication under the title Shinshaku Shokoku Banashi.

Shinshaku shokoku banashi by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

As for this truly reckless renouncing of the world, I beg you to take pity on me; and please do not forget the lottery ticket, the image of Kannon, and the colored paper, and give my regards to my old companions in pleasure. Come the warmth of spring, I shall be waiting, with all my heart, for the day you all come together to visit me at Yoshino. With deepest respect.

Shinshaku shokoku banashi by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)
