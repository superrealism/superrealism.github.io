About this work

A short story Nagai Kafū wrote after the war. In the middle of the great Tokyo air raid of March 1945, Satō, a hardware dealer serving in the civil defence corps at Fukagawa, is separated from his wife and child in the fire — a piece in which Kafū draws in a quiet hand the confusion under the raid and the feeling of ordinary people.

Summary

Satō, a hardware dealer serving on the civil defence corps of Furuishibachō in Fukagawa, barely manages to flee through the fire to near the Kasai Bridge in the great Tokyo air raid on the night of March 9, 1945. But in the confusion of flight he loses sight of his wife, who is carrying their child on her back. He calls out for her at the top of his voice, but no longer knows even where they became separated. Reaching the levee of the drainage canal and confirming that he has come through alive, Satō searches the crowd of refugees for his lost wife and child. The confusion under the raid, the scrambling of people burned out of their homes, and small kindnesses passed between strangers — Kafū draws the figures of ordinary people living through this devastation in a hand at once detached and warm. The story of the reunion with his wife and child arrives, in time, at an unexpected end. A piece of Kafū's late years, capturing the calamity of war not in a raised voice but plainly, as it was.

The opening (original text)

Satō, a hardware dealer who served on the civil defence corps of Furuishibachō in Fukagawa, barely managed to flee through the fire, in the air raid on the night of March 9, to near the Kasai Bridge, and blinking his reddened eyes between the folds of his hood, saw the color of the grass and the flow of the water at the levee of the drainage canal, and knew for the first time that he had come through alive.

"I thought I wouldn't know unless I saw it through to the end. It was all for your sake. They took seventy yen off me for a single cup of tea." Chiyoko, without even a flash of jealousy, set off the very next day to pay her thanks at Hokekyōji temple.
