What is Historical Kana Orthography?

Category
Style & Technique

A system of kana spelling that takes as its norm the usage found in texts from the mid-Heian period and earlier — writing, for example, "tefutefu" for "chōchō" (butterfly) or "kefu" for "kyō" (today). Also called "old kana orthography." In the medieval period, the kana orthography attributed to Fujiwara no Teika was widely followed, but in the mid-Edo period the priest and kokugaku scholar Keichū examined old texts such as the "Man'yōshū," corrected its errors, and set out the ancient kana usage in his 1695 work "Waji Shōranshō." The Meiji government adopted Keichū's kana orthography as the standard for official documents and school education, so that the great majority of modern literary works are written in this orthography. Since the 1946 cabinet notification "Gendai Kanazukai" (revised in 1986 as "Gendai Kanazukai"), which switched to spelling based on pronunciation, it has become knowledge needed chiefly for reading the classics and the original texts of modern literature. The "kana-orthography type" this app shows for each work indicates which orthography the Aozora Bunko source text follows: "new characters, new kana" denotes text revised into modern orthography, while "new characters, old kana" and "old characters, old kana" preserve the historical kana orthography — Higuchi Ichiyō's "Takekurabe" ("Growing Up") and Kamo no Chōmei's "Hōjōki" fall into the latter category.
