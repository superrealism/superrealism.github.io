About this work

The debut work with which Yumeno Kyūsaku entered the literary world, winning a prize competition in Shin Seinen in 1926. The word ayakashi comes from the figured red oak of which the drum's body is made, and at the same time carries the sense of an apparition in the nō theatre. A cursed drum that brings misfortune and death to one owner after another — and the narrator, in whose blood that karma runs, understands that he is the killer beyond escape, and resolves to smash the drum and die. Set down as a testament: a heterodox detective story full of the grotesque and the fantastic.

Summary

The "ayakashi drum" — an old hand drum, seemingly close to a hundred years old, carved from red oak with a strange, twisted grain. Unlike the bright, clear tone of an ordinary hand drum, it gives off a gloomy, toneless sound, without resonance — po... po... po... — and a terrible curse clings to it: that it brings misfortune and death, one after another, upon whoever owns it. "I," a descendant of the bloodline bound up with this drum, come, in tracing the strange chain of deaths that have occurred around me, to realize that I myself — however I look at it — am the inescapable "culprit." But who could possibly believe that a drum is the culprit? Thinking to myself what a strange thing the world is, I set about writing a long testament, recording the whole course of events. The grudge of my ancestor, Otomaru Kunō, has already been avenged. And so, to erase from this world, once and for all, this drum — the hollow husk of a curse — and the bloodline bound to it, "I" now mean to smash the drum to pieces and die by my own hand. Wondering, as though in a dream, whether I was born for no purpose but to leave behind this one fragment of a strange tale. A debut work of unconventional detective fiction, in which Kyūsaku, with his own peculiar, brooding touch, weaves together the eerie, the fantastical, and a strange tale of fated destiny.

Characters

  • I　A descendant of the bloodline bound to the curse of the "ayakashi drum." Realizing that he himself is the inescapable culprit, he writes a testament before smashing the drum and taking his own life
  • The ayakashi drum　A cursed hand drum carved from red oak, which brings death, one after another, upon its owners
  • Otomaru Kunō　An ancestor of "I." His grudge is the source of the fated curse bound up with the drum

About the app

The opening (original text)

　I am glad. For the time has come when I may write of the origin of the "ayakashi drum"... 　I sit now, in this one room, with the ayakashi drum set before my eyes. It is this drum that has made me what I am today. Once I have finished writing of the strange fate bound up with this drum, I mean to smash it to pieces, and then to die myself as well.

Ayakashi no tsuzumi by Yumeno Kyūsaku (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

　When I had finished reading this, I realized that, however I looked at it, I was the culprit, beyond all escape. But who would ever believe that a drum was the culprit? Thinking what a strange thing the world could be, I feel as though I were caught in some kind of dream. 　I mean now to smash this drum to pieces, and to die. So that the ayakashi drum, and the bloodline bound to it, may vanish from this world, this day, forever...

Ayakashi no tsuzumi by Yumeno Kyūsaku (Aozora Bunko)
