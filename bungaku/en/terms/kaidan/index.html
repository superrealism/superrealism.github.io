What is Kaidan (Ghost Stories)?

Category
Form / Genre

A general term for tales that take as their subject the uncanny and the otherworldly — ghosts, monsters, and the like. In the Edo period they were much told at kabuki performances, in yomihon fiction, and at gatherings for the game of hyakumonogatari ("a hundred tales"), and this tradition flowed on into modern literature. San'yūtei Enchō, an active rakugo performer from the late shogunate into the Meiji era, composed and performed on stage his own ghost stories, including "Kaidan Botan Dōrō" ("The Peony Lantern") and "Shinkei Kasanegafuchi" ("The Ghost of Kasane Swamp"), and the 1884 shorthand transcript of "Kaidan Botan Dōrō" is also remembered in literary history for how its faithful transcription of the spoken voice served as a reference point for Futabatei Shimei and others in shaping the genbun'itchi style. In 1904 Lafcadio Hearn (Koizumi Yakumo) retold, in English, legends of the uncanny handed down across Japan in the collection "Kwaidan," introducing tales such as "Yuki-Onna" ("The Snow Woman") and "Miminashi Hōichi no Hanashi" ("Hōichi the Earless") to the world. Through the Taishō and Shōwa eras as well, Izumi Kyōka's fantastical tales of the uncanny and Okamoto Kidō's ghost story collection "Seiadō Kidan" ("Tales of the Blue Frog Hall," 1926), refined with a modern narrative voice, continued to be written, sustaining a distinct lineage even as it resonated with the grotesque tastes of detective fiction.
