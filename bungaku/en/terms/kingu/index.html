What is King?

Category
Society / Group

A popular entertainment magazine founded in January 1925 by Noma Seiji, president of Kōdansha (then the Dai Nihon Yūbenkai Kōdansha). Under the banner "Japan's most entertaining and beneficial" magazine, its pages combined kōdan storytelling, fiction, and practical articles with lavish supplements, and it captured a broad readership from the cities to the countryside. The inaugural issue printed five hundred thousand copies, and, including reprints, sold through more than six hundred thousand; by 1927 its circulation had passed one million, making it the best-selling magazine in Japan through the prewar era, a magazine of truly national reach. Alongside the enpon boom flourishing around the same time, it symbolizes the mass popularization of publishing in the early Shōwa era. Many popular fiction writers were called on for its pages: Edogawa Ranpo contributed the popular detective serial "Ōgon Kamen" ("The Golden Mask," 1930), depicting a master of disguise, and Yamamoto Shūgorō contributed stories such as "Kuwa no Ki Monogatari" ("The Tale of the Mulberry Tree"). Its editorial policy, foregrounding entertainment, established a mode for the popular magazine standing in contrast to the serious literary journal, and became the prototype for the national entertainment magazines that followed. It went on hiatus in 1957.
