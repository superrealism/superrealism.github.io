What is Genbaku Bungaku (Atomic Bomb Literature)?

Category
Form / Genre

Literature on the theme of the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and their aftermath. Much of it is rooted in the personal experience of writers who were themselves exposed to the bombings, and it is characterized by an inseparable bond between the duty to record what happened and the question of how to put into words an experience that resists language. During the Occupation, the GHQ press code strictly limited what could be written about the atomic bomb, and publication was often obstructed. Hara Tamiki, exposed to the bombing in Hiroshima, published "Natsu no Hana" ("Summer Flowers") in the magazine "Mita Bungaku" in 1947 — retitled from a work originally called "Genshi Bakudan" ("Atomic Bomb") — engraving the horror in restrained, lucid prose and making it the representative work of atomic bomb literature. Tōge Sankichi's "Genbaku Shishū" ("Poems of the Atomic Bomb"), known for the line "Give me back my father, give me back my mother," was turned down by a Tokyo publisher in 1951 and put out in Hiroshima itself as a mimeographed private edition of five hundred copies. Ōta Yōko's "Shikabane no Machi" ("City of Corpses," 1948) and the physician Nagai Takashi's Nagasaki memoir "Nagasaki no Kane" ("The Bells of Nagasaki," 1949) are likewise early records, and this lineage continues on into Ibuse Masuji's "Kuroi Ame" ("Black Rain").
