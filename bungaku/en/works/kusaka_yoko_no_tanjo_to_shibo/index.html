About this work

An extraordinary autobiography in which the author set down the three and a half years from the birth of the pen name Kusaka Yōko into the world to its burial. The month after writing it, at twenty-one, she took her own life at Rokkō station on the Hankyū line. Written as a rite for burying that other self which is a pen name, the piece became, exactly as it stands, a funeral oration for the author herself.

Summary

"Just about three and a half years ago now, before the summer of 1949, Kusaka Yōko began to exist in this world" — from its opening line, this piece speaks of its author in a strange third person. The birth of a human being, it says, occurs by chance and unconsciously, yet this name was given by her own deliberate act, and its birth was thereby forced into being. A rainy day when she was taken by a friend from her girls'-school days to visit Shimao Toshio's house in Rokkō. Being given a haiku pen-name by her father and attending haiku gatherings. The three months, submitting poems under her own name, during which magazines folded or she grew tired of them, and manuscripts piled up unused. And then, one by one, the days recalled since joining a coterie magazine and beginning to publish work under the name "Kusaka Yōko." Being named a candidate for the Akutagawa Prize; the expectation and curiosity of those around her; drink and love; and impulses she could not control even in herself. The name "Kusaka Yōko" had, before she knew it, slipped free of her own hands and begun to walk on its own. In time the author finds she can no longer write. She, who had never once in her life crumpled up an unfinished manuscript, cannot write for five days running. Why must I go on burying characters in manuscript paper, suffering so, her head asks of her hand. And so she resolves: let us bury Kusaka Yōko. Let us make a small box of plain wood, cover it in white cloth, pack it full — of course — with every scrap of paper bearing that name, and burn it. Let us light incense. Let us play Brahms's Fourth, and see to it that she is never revived again — such was her resolve. Three and a half years, that had been the life of Kusaka Yōko... I will prepare a death notice for Kusaka Yōko, and then hold her funeral. Let me read the eulogy. You really are a fool, it will say.

The opening (original text)

Just about three and a half years ago now, before the summer of 1949, Kusaka Yōko began to exist in this world. The birth of a human being, I think, occurs by chance, unconsciously, and yet solemnly all the same. But this name was given by my own deliberate act, and its birth was thereby forced into being.

Kusaka yōko no tanjō to shibō by Kusaka Yōko (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

I made up my mind. Let us bury Kusaka Yōko. I will make a small box of plain wood, cover it in white cloth, and pack inside it, of course, every scrap of paper bearing the name Kusaka Yōko. And then let us burn it. Let us light incense. Let us play Brahms's Fourth, and resolve that she shall never be revived again — that was my resolve. Three and a half years — that had been the life of Kusaka Yōko. ...I will prepare Kusaka Yōko's death notice, and then hold her funeral. Let me read the eulogy. You really are a fool, it will say.

Kusaka yōko no tanjō to shibō by Kusaka Yōko (Aozora Bunko)
