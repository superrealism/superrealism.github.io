What is Akutagawa Prize?

Category
Literary Award

Formally the Akutagawa Ryūnosuke Prize. Established in 1935 by Kikuchi Kan, who ran the magazine "Bungei Shunjū," in memory of his friend Akutagawa Ryūnosuke, who had died young; it was created together with the Naoki Prize. It is administered by the Nihon Bungaku Shinkōkai (Society for the Promotion of Japanese Literature), a public-interest foundation Kikuchi established in 1937, in what amounts to a joint operation with the Bungei Shunjū publishing company. The prize is awarded twice a year, for the first and second halves of the year, to unpublished or emerging writers for serious literary novellas and short stories published in newspapers and magazines, including coterie magazines. The first prize (first half of 1935) went to Ishikawa Tatsuzō's "Sōbō" ("The People"). Often spoken of together with the Naoki Prize, which honors popular fiction, it has become widely known as the gateway to the literary establishment.
