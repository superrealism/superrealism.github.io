About this work

A long novel Ranpo serialized in two parts in Bungei Kurabu in 1930. Aoki Ainosuke, a young man of property in Nagoya at a loose end with his boredom and his taste for the bizarre, seeks ever stronger stimulation and steps into terrible crime. Written as a warning to lovers of the bizarre — of how frightful a thing the end of that taste is — it is a long grotesque detective novel of Ranpo's.

Summary

"He had, altogether, been far too bored, and far too much a lover of the bizarre" — the protagonist of this story is Aoki Ainosuke, second son of a wealthy family in Nagoya, a young man at the time not quite thirty. Out of sheer boredom, sinking himself into a taste for the bizarre as the last stimulation left to him in this world, he is dragged, as if by a bloody fascination, ever deeper into a terrifying world. A certain detective novelist once feared that, as his taste for the bizarre grew and grew, he might at last commit an actual murder — and it is precisely that fear that Ainosuke makes into reality. The mystery surrounding his beautiful, beloved wife Yoshie; the appearance of a woman who is her very double; and one strange incident after another. Ainosuke's fate rolls, tumbling, toward the ultimate end of a taste for the bizarre. At the story's close, another beautiful woman, identical to Yoshie and yet with an entirely different face, smiles at him, enchanting and alluring. "Lovers of the bizarre — you must never be too much lovers of the bizarre" — Ranpo's own warning, set at the head of this long, uncanny detective novel.

Characters

The opening (original text)

He had, altogether, been far too bored, and far too much a lover of the bizarre. A certain detective novelist, so the story goes, once feared that, going on from one bloody crime to another in his fiction, he might no longer be satisfied with mere novels and end up actually committing a real crime himself — and the protagonist of this story went and did precisely what that detective writer had feared. His taste for the bizarre grew and grew, until at last he committed a terrible crime.

Ryōki no hate by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The woman turned slowly toward him and came quietly into the room. Her figure from behind was Yoshie's very image, but her face was entirely different. She bore no resemblance at all to Ainosuke's beloved wife — and yet she too was, in her own right, a beautiful woman. The woman drew close, right up to him. And, bowing modestly and gracefully, she showed an endearing dimple, and, with well-shaped, rouged lips, smiled at him, enchanting and alluring.

Ryōki no hate by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)
