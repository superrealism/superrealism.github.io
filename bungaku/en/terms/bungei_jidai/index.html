What is Bungei Jidai?

Category
Society / Group

A coterie magazine founded in October 1924 by up-and-coming writers who had gathered around "Bungei Shunjū" — Kawabata Yasunari, Yokomitsu Riichi, Kataoka Teppei, Kon Tōkō, and others. The title was chosen by Kawabata. The critic Chiba Kameo's description of the bold figurative language in Yokomitsu's short story "Atama Narabi ni Hara" ("Heads and Bellies"), carried in the inaugural issue, as "the birth of the New Sensationists" (Shinkankakuha) made the magazine a stronghold of that school, and it stirred a fresh wind through the literary establishment. Kawabata's "Izu no Odoriko" ("The Izu Dancer," 1926) and many other experimental works by coterie members dissatisfied with established realism were published here. The magazine ceased publication in May 1927 after 32 issues in all, but it remains in literary history as the magazine that embodied the ferment for new modes of expression among young writers of the late Taishō era.
