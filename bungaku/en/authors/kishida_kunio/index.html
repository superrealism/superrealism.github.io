Biography

Born in Tokyo in 1890. After graduating from the Army Academy and serving in the military, he resigned his commission to pursue theater, studying French literature at Tokyo Imperial University before going to France, where he studied under Jacques Copeau at the Théâtre du Vieux-Colombier. On his return he established a new kind of playwriting with works such as "Kamifūsen" ("Paper Balloon") and "Ushiyama Hoteru" ("The Ushiyama Hotel") that portrayed human beings through the rhythm of conversation rather than plot, transforming modern Japanese drama. He also took part in founding the Bungakuza theater company and trained many playwrights. His name survives in the Kishida Kunio Drama Award, Japan's leading prize for playwriting. He collapsed during a rehearsal and died in 1954 at the age of 63.
