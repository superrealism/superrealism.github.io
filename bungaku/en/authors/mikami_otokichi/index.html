Biography

Born in 1891 in Sakurai Village, Saitama Prefecture (now Kasukabe City). He studied English literature at Waseda University, won popularity with newspaper fiction, and became a leading writer of fashionable fiction in the early Shōwa era. He brought into popular fiction a psychological description inherited from Western novels, and his prolific output earned him the epithet "the Balzac of Japan." "Yukinojō Henge" ("An Actor's Revenge"), serialized in the Asahi Shimbun from 1934, was taken up again and again for film and stage and became his representative work. He also translated Sherlock Holmes. His common-law wife was Hasegawa Shigure, who presided over the magazine "Nyonin Geijutsu." He died in 1944 at the age of 52.
