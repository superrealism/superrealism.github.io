Biography

Born in Edo in 1861, the son of a samurai of the Takasaki domain. He was baptized under the influence of Clark at Sapporo Agricultural College, then went to the United States to study at Amherst College. On returning to Japan he refused, as a teacher at the First Higher Middle School, to bow to the Imperial Rescript on Education, and became the target of fierce public condemnation in what came to be called the "Lèse-majesté Incident." Thereafter he championed a faith belonging to no church, "Mukyōkai-shugi" (Non-church Christianity), and continued independent evangelism through the journal "Seisho no Kenkyū" ("Bible Study"). When war broke out with Russia he preached pacifism, joining Kōtoku Shūsui, Sakai Toshihiko, and others in leading the opposition. His idea of the "Two J's," recorded in the words "I, for Japan; Japan, for the World," and his lecture "Go-sei e no Saidai Ibutsu" ("The Greatest Legacy to Posterity"), continue to encourage many readers today. He died in 1930 at the age of 69.
