Biography

Born in 1863 into a raw-silk trading family in Yokohama; his real name was Kakuzō. He learned English from childhood and, studying under Fenollosa at Tokyo University, came to recognize the value of Japanese art. He worked to establish the Tokyo School of Fine Arts (now Tokyo University of the Arts) and became its principal, later founding the Japan Art Institute. He trained painters such as Yokoyama Taikan and Shimomura Kanzan, and set the course of modern Japanese art. In his later years he served as head of the Chinese and Japanese Art Department of the Boston Museum of Fine Arts, active abroad, where he wrote "The Ideals of the East," "The Awakening of Japan," and "The Book of Tea" in English. "The Book of Tea" in particular, a celebrated work explaining East Asian aesthetics and a philosophy of life to the West through the way of tea, is still read around the world today. He died in 1913 at the age of 50.
