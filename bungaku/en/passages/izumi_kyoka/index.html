Famous passages by Izumi Kyōka

Well-known lines from the works of Izumi Kyōka.

4 passages

The Surgery Room

“Does it hurt?” “No — because it is you, because it is you.”

A single exchange on the operating table between a countess who has refused anesthesia and the surgeon operating on her. This brief exchange reveals a relationship built on a single instant of love, hidden for nine years.

The Holy Man of Mount Kōya

I told myself there was no need to unfold the General Staff Office map again, but the road was so wearisome that even to touch anything felt stifling; still, I lifted the sleeve of my traveling robe and drew out the folding map in its cover.

The opening in which a traveling priest begins to tell of crossing the mountains of Hida. It opens a representative work of Kyōka's fantastical literature, colored by uncanny wonders deep in the mountains and the vision of a beautiful woman.

A blood-sucking creature, shaped like a warty cucumber, with smooth, murky black skin banded in brown — this here, is a leech.

A scene in which the traveling priest, deep in the mountains of Hida, finds his whole body covered in leeches. The clinging quality of the description heightens the dread of the uncanny events still to come.

The Lantern Song

The stout shrine pillars, thick as Miyashige daikon radishes, that the god of Atsuta, boiled soft as furofuki, watches over — the seven-league crossing rich with waves, and in the joy of arriving safely by ferry at Kuwana...

An opening that begins with a voice humming a passage from “Shanks' Mare.” Set in Kuwana, it opens a representative work of Kyōka's mature period, depicting the karma bound up with noh and the arts.
