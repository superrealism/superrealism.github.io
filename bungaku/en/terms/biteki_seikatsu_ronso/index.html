What is Biteki Seikatsu Debate?

Category
Other

A debate that arose in 1901 over Takayama Chogyū's essay "Biteki Seikatsu o Ronzu" ("On the Aesthetic Life"). In this essay, published in the August issue of the magazine "Taiyō," Chogyū argued that morality and knowledge are no more than means serving the satisfaction of human instinct, and that true happiness lies in fulfilling the original demands of human nature, that is, in the "aesthetic life." That a critic who had once championed Japanism and placed great weight on the state should now assign supreme value to the satisfaction of the individual's instincts caused a great stir. Beginning with a critique Hasegawa Tenkei contributed to the "Yomiuri Shimbun" that same August, Tsubouchi Shōyō also serialized "Bakotsu Jingen" in the same paper, sharply criticizing the thought of Nietzsche that lay behind Chogyū's argument. Against them, Tobari Chikufū, known as an introducer of Nietzsche, took up the defense in the magazine "Teikoku Bungaku," and Chogyū himself replied repeatedly, so that the debate gradually shifted into a dispute over the merits of Nietzsche's thought and is also called the "Nietzsche debate." It also took the shape of a confrontation between Chogyū and Chikufū, both educated at Tokyo Imperial University, and the Waseda literary men centered on Shōyō. Chogyū died of illness in December of the following year, 1902, at the age of 31, but the debate is remembered as one that reflected the current of thought in the Meiji 30s (around 1897–1906), as interest shifted from the state toward the inner life of the individual.
