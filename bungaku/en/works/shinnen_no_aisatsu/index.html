About this work

Near midday on New Year's Day, hearing her husband set down the receiver, she looks at her watch: exactly thirty minutes, again this year — a very short piece Yamakawa Masao wrote for Shinkan News. For seven years her husband has taken a New Year's call from a woman he met once at a marriage interview. He calls it a nuisance and gives her thirty minutes all the same, and his wife no longer blames him. She too watches the clock, and begins to dial a certain number.

Summary

"The customary New Year's greeting?" "Yes. ... Her." Having been told at some length that her child is going to school, the husband frowns: "Every year, punctual as a stamp, this telephone call on the morning of New Year's Day." "It is a nuisance." But she was remembering that last year, and the year before, and the year before that, they had had exactly the same exchange. She has long known who the woman is who telephones her husband once a year, always near noon on New Year's Day. It is the woman with whom, just after he left the university, he was made to have one single formal interview with marriage in view. The woman with a large mole on her cheek, who greeted him in an exaggerated voice as they passed in the Ginza. ... They have been married four years, and the calls have not been missed in seven. At first she grew angry and wept, and once she waited for it, took up the receiver and cried, "Please stop this. I beg you. You are making trouble in this house!" But the bell rang again at once, and the woman was laughing at the other end of the line. "Forgive me — for making your wife angry." That call too lasted exactly thirty minutes. Her husband says, "She must still be single. She wants somebody. Once she marries, I shall certainly be excused." — But she knew. On the way back from a class reunion the year before, she had seen that woman walking through the streets with a man who must have been her husband and leading a little girl by the hand. She had heard the voice too: "It is the seventh year of our marriage now." "Your papa is a fool, isn't he," she says quietly to herself as she plays with her three-year-old child. Anyone at all, though he have a lover and a husband and children, is lonely for all that.

Characters

  • She　A wife of four years
  • Her husband　A man honest to the point of being too scrupulous
  • The woman　The one with whom her husband once had a single formal interview

The opening (original text)

When her husband put down the receiver she looked at her wristwatch. "Exactly thirty minutes; this year too." The light of the New Year's noon comes flowing into the room through the glass door. For a while her husband did not speak. "The customary New Year's greeting?" "Yes. ... Her."

Shinnen no aisatsu by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The light of New Year's noon now fills the room transparently. She took up the receiver and began to dial the house of the boy in her class who, in her high-school days, had first taken her hand inside a cinema. A harmless chatter, unconnected, so to speak, with her present self, with a distant person who lives in her heart — a kind of New Year's card sent once a year at her own pleasure.... It was a custom she had tried on New Year's Day about three years ago and had now grown addicted to, an annual event of thirty minutes that she could no longer do without.
