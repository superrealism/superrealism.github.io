What is Shinchō?

Category
Society / Group

A literary magazine founded in May 1904 by Satō Giryō, founder of the Shinchōsha publishing company. Continuing publication for more than a century since, it is Japan's longest-running literary magazine still in existence. It became one of the central venues for the literary establishment from the Taishō era into the Shōwa era, publishing a great many landmark works, including Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's "Mikan" ("Tangerines") and "Yabu no Naka" ("In a Grove"), and Dazai Osamu's "Hashire Merosu" ("Run, Melos!"). It became a leading stage in particular for the Buraiha writers just after the war: Sakaguchi Ango's affirmative reckoning with the collapse of established morality, "Darakuron" ("On Decadence," 1946), and Dazai Osamu's "Shayō" ("The Setting Sun," 1947), depicting a fallen aristocratic household, were both published in this magazine's pages, each a monument marking the start of postwar literature.
