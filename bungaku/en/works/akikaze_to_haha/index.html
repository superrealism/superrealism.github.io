About this work

A short story Ozaki Shirō published in Shinshōsetsu in 1926. It draws quietly one day in which the narrator tries to see his aged mother, in the grip of an attack of her chronic illness, through to the end. The doctor is out and does not come; his wasted mother writhes with pain on the bedding. Doing everything he can with his wife and sister, he tastes the coming presentiment of death and a bottomless anxiety at losing her. The chill of the autumn night wind in the coppice is laid over the shivery sensation of the boundary between life and death: a fine piece full of the author's feeling for his own family.

Summary

A little past noon my old mother's condition suddenly changed. When my wife came to call me and I hurried down to the lower house, my mother was crouching on the small quilt that is left spread for her, her body bent like a prawn, groaning and twisting her lower body violently against the pain. It is her usual attack. My wife rubs her back and my sister runs to the neighborhood for the doctor; but the doctor is unluckily out on his rounds and will not be back until four. There is another doctor if one goes out to the main street, but it is uneasy work showing a complicated illness to a new doctor, and we can only wait beside her for the attack to quiet. Watching my wasted mother in her pain, my breast is gripped by my own helplessness and by the foreboding of death. In time the attack subsides for the moment, but how long her life will hold no one knows. Leaving the sickroom, I go back to my own room and think of her. "I wonder whether my mother is alive" — murmuring it as though to myself, I gave a start. For I suddenly felt the cold of the night wind sounding in the tops of the coppice outside the window. The dim light of the lower house soaks into the frosted glass, and from my mother's room some cold current of air seems to be flowing. The forlornness of a son about to watch his old mother die, and the fine feeling of life and death touched in the night air of autumn, are set down by Ozaki Shirō with a deep love for his own flesh and blood, in a few plain words.

The opening (original text)

A little past noon my mother's condition suddenly changed. When my wife came to call me and I hurried down to the lower house, my mother was crouching on the small quilt that is left spread for her, her body bent like a prawn, groaning continually as though straining, and twisting her lower body violently to resist the pain. Her usual attack had come on.
