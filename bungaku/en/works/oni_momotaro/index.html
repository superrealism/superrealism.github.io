About this work

A short story for children published by Ozaki Kōyō in 1891, one of the Books for the Young issued by Hakubunkan. It takes the Momotarō everyone knows and turns it round to the demons' side, telling in a grandiose Chinese-flavoured style the fortunes of Kumomotarō, a demon child who means to avenge the defeat of Demon Island. A parody written in fun.

Summary

Ogre Island, invaded by Momotarō and stripped of its treasure, is crushed by a disgrace that will last to the end of its line. The ogre king proclaims that whoever kills Momotarō shall be the new king, but no one comes forward. An ogre and his wife, who had guarded the castle gate and were stripped of their office for failing to keep Momotarō out, pray to the Yasha god for a child who shall surpass Momotarō; and from a great bitter peach that comes floating down the river an ogre child is born, whom they name Nigamomotarō. At the royal castle Nigamomotarō asks leave to subdue Momotarō, is given provisions of skulls in the guise of millet dumplings and the arms of the ogres, and sets out for Japan. On the way he meets a golden poison dragon that bears a grudge because Momotarō's retainer the pheasant devoured its kin, and they swear the bond of lord and vassal; and through the dragon's introduction he takes a great ape and a great wolf into his service as well, and rides a cloud toward Japan. But the art of steering the cloud does not go well, and while they overshoot and turn back and overshoot again, the dragon's magic gives out, and the great ape and the great wolf fall from the cloud and are lost in the sea. In rage at losing his retainers Nigamomotarō seizes the dragon, and after a grappling match tears it into four; but with that the spell of the cloud is broken too, and Nigamomotarō himself comes down head first from the far heavens into the wide ocean.

Characters

  • Nigamomotarō　The ogre child born from a bitter peach. Hoped to surpass Momotarō in valor, he invades Japan to redeem the honor of Ogre Island. The hero of the work
  • The ogre and his wife　Who once guarded the gate of Ogre Island, were stripped of office for failing to keep Momotarō out, and are granted the birth of Nigamomotarō
  • The poison dragon　The dragon king of Mount Maō, who takes Nigamomotarō's side out of a grudge because Momotarō's retainer the pheasant devoured its kin

The opening (original text)

Long, long ago the old man went to the hills to cut brushwood and the old woman to the river to wash, and out of the peach they found there was born Momotarō; and that he came attacking this Ogre Island at the head of a monkey, a pheasant and a dog, and carried off the treasures of generations, and went home boasting of his victory — that is a disgrace to this island until the end of its line.

Oni momotarō by Ozaki Kōyō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Nigamomotarō lost his footing in the far-off void and came whirling straight down like a pebble, and into the boundless ocean — splash!

Oni momotarō by Ozaki Kōyō (Aozora Bunko)
