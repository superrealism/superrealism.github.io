Biography

Born in 1876 in Kitakanbara District, Niigata Prefecture (now Shibata City). He worked his way through Tōhoku Gakuin in Sendai, where during his student years he became close with Shimazaki Tōson, who had come there as a teacher. He was baptized as a Christian and later went to the United States to study at the University of California. On his return he served as a professor at Tōhoku Gakuin, and alongside that work took up the great task of his life, a complete translation of Dante's "Divine Comedy," translating all three canticles in dignified classical prose, from "Inferno" in 1914 to "Paradiso" in 1922. His translation is celebrated as the foundation of Dante's proper introduction to Japan, and after the war it was collected into Iwanami Bunko and continues to be widely read. He died in 1947 at the age of 71.
