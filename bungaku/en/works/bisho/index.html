About this work

A short story published posthumously in the magazine Ningen in 1948, as Yokomitsu Riichi's last work, and held to be a masterpiece of his final years. Late in the war, with defeat close, the mathematical prodigy Sukata, recruited by the navy, appears before the writer Kaji. Around the unearthly, beautiful smile of a young man said to be near completing an invention that will overturn the war at a stroke, Yokomitsu stares at genius and madness, truth and illusion, and the human heart living in the middle of a war.

Summary

It is the season of new greenery, just before the air raids begin, and the tide of war has turned wholly against Japan. The haiku poet Takada, an acquaintance of the writer Kaji, brings him a request from a young man who wants Kaji to write out a piece of calligraphy for him. The young man's name is Sumikata — a twenty-one-year-old student at Imperial University, and yet a mathematical genius of extraordinary gifts, now drawn into the navy at Yokosuka as a research student. What is more, he seems to be on the verge of completing the invention of a formidable ray weapon, one said to guarantee absolute victory once perfected. In an age when everyone starved for good news, Kaji too finds his heart irresistibly stirred by the appearance of this "giant" who might yet save his homeland from ruin. When Sumikata at last appears before Kaji, he proves a mischievous young man who still bears traces of boyhood. The smile that blooms across his face — sudden as a flower bursting open, innocent as a newborn's first laugh, fleeting and gone in an instant — is a "smile" of such matchless beauty that before it, even the question of whether his invention is real or mere delusion comes to seem beside the point. Is Sumikata's invention genuine, or only the mad delusion of a nation on the verge of defeat? Drifting along the boundary between truth and illusion, Kaji comes to think of that beautiful smile as something like "a ray that kills the human heart." A late work, and Yokomitsu's last, in which he fixes his clear, undimmed gaze upon genius and madness, and upon the state of the human heart amid war.

Characters

  • Kaji　A writer. The narrator, who meets Sumikata, a brilliant young man drawn into the navy, and whose heart is moved by his invention and his beautiful smile
  • Sumikata　A twenty-one-year-old student at Imperial University and a mathematical genius. As a research student for the navy, he is said to be developing a ray weapon that could overturn the course of the war. He carries an unearthly, innocent smile
  • Takada　A haiku poet. Sumikata's mentor, who introduces him to Kaji

The opening (original text)

Next Sunday, let's go to Kai. The new greenery there is so beautiful. Such a scrap of conversation drifted, unbidden, from among the voices passing by. That's right, Kaji thought — the new leaves must already be out by now. To forget the season entirely was not something that had happened to him, these past several months.

Bishō by Yokomitsu Riichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"But still, that smile of Sumikata's — that alone was beautiful. Meet with that, and any one of us would be done for. That alone—" In saying this, Kaji meant to suggest, too, that a smile was itself a kind of ray that could kill a person's heart. And yet, whenever he called to mind that most beautiful of all things, Sumikata's smile, like the first light of spring, he found it strange how his own heart settled of its own accord into a readiness to wait for whatever might fall from the sky he now looked up at. It was a hope resembling the one clear verdict that everyone, in this present age, longed for above all else.

Bishō by Yokomitsu Riichi (Aozora Bunko)
