About this work

The long novel with which Kyōka made his literary debut, serialized in the Yomiuri Shimbun in November 1894. It draws the twisted, doomed love of Taki no Shiraito, a travelling water-conjuror, and Murakoshi Kin'ya, a poor coachman whose studies she pays for and who rises in the world. Published at the urging of his teacher Ozaki Kōyō, it is his first work, later staged and filmed many times under the title Taki no Shiraito, and the starting point of everything he wrote.

Summary

On the highway at Takaoka in Etchū, Taki no Shiraito, a traveling performer of water tricks, meets Murakoshi Kin'ya, a coachman who is poor and yet means to study. Struck by his purpose, Shiraito offers to pay out of her own earnings for Kin'ya's studies in Tokyo, and the two pledge themselves to each other. One night, on her way to borrow the promised money from a lender, Shiraito is set upon by robbers and stripped of what little she has. At her wits' end she steals into a mansion she is passing; but she is seen by the household and, in the struggle, kills the master and his wife and escapes. Three years later Shiraito stands in the court of the Kanazawa district tribunal on a charge of murder, and finds that the acting prosecutor who is to try her is Kin'ya himself, now risen in the world. Killing his private feeling, Kin'ya has indicted his benefactor; he asks for the death penalty and obtains a conviction — and that night, grieving that he is now divided from his benefactor by the border of the other world, he takes his own life.

Characters

  • Taki no Shiraito (Mizushima Tomo)　A traveling performer of water tricks. The heroine, who pays for the coachman Kin'ya's studies and so brings on the tragedy that follows
  • Murakoshi Kin'ya　A young man risen from a poor coachman to acting prosecutor, tormented by the fate that makes him judge his benefactor
  • The master of the mansion　The head of the house into which Shiraito breaks in search of money. He is stabbed on the instant with a kitchen knife and dies
  • The mistress　The master's wife. In the shock of her husband's death she grapples with Shiraito, and is killed in the struggle

The opening (original text)

From Takaoka in Etchū to Isurugi, the posting station below Kurikara, a distance of four ri and eight chō, there ran an omnibus at fixed hours. Because the fare was cheap, most travelers gave up the rickshaws and relied on it. The rickshaw men blamed the coach company for their bad times, and the friction between men and horses grew steadily worse; and though the mediation of the head men barely kept up an appearance of not interfering with one another's trade, disturbances arose again and again as occasion offered.

The ending (original text)

After this Shiraito confessed her grave crime as well, without any ado. The presiding judge broke off the questioning at once and ended that day's session there and then. Murakoshi Kin'ya, the acting prosecutor, had covered the eye of private feeling and examined Shiraito's crimes in every detail, and had indicted as a murderer the greatest of benefactors, who had heaped kindness upon kindness. Thus the preliminary inquiry ended and the trial was opened, and the presiding judge, holding the acting prosecutor's demand to be just, pronounced upon her the sentence of death. Murakoshi Kin'ya, who had sworn that they should never be strangers as long as they lived, grieved that he was now divided from his benefactor by the border of life and death and could never see her again, and on the evening of the sentence killed himself on the upper floor of his lodging.
