About this work

A novel written to order and published by Sakura Shobō in October 1909. Drawing on the diary of a real young teacher, Kobayashi Hidezō, it follows Hayashi Seizō, who wanted literature and further study but became an elementary-school teacher in the Saitama countryside because his family was poor, falls ill without ever getting where he meant to go, and dies at twenty-one in a corner of a country celebrating victory over Russia. Known for its opening — that the four leagues of road were long — and a central work of Japanese naturalism.

Summary

Hayashi Seizō finishes middle school, and while his friends go on to higher schools he becomes, for want of money at home, a substitute teacher at the Miroku higher primary school near Hanyū. "The road of four ri was long" — and yet, going out to his post, he still had hope of a new life in his breast. His ambition in literature, his dream of the music school, his friendship with the priest of the Jōganji and with old schoolfellows, and a faint love. But the pay is small, his family sinks further, and his hopes of study collapse one by one. Every time he hears that a classmate has made his way in the world, Seizō tastes the bitterness of being left behind in the country. For a time he nearly ruins himself in dissipation out of despair; then he takes hold of himself, throws himself into teaching and becomes a master his pupils love — but his body is already eaten away by tuberculosis. The war with Russia begins, and in the autumn of the thirty-seventh year of Meiji, while the country is loud with the news of the fall of Liaoyang, Seizō quietly ends the short life in which he had "seen to the very bottom of the bottom of despair." He was twenty-one. Flowers were never absent from his rough stone grave; and some years later, they say, a young woman teacher who said she had once been his pupil came and wept there where no one could see her.

Characters

  • Hayashi Seizō　A young man with ambitions in literature and study, who ends his life as a substitute teacher in the country
  • Katō Ikuji　Seizō's close friend, a companion in literature since middle school
  • Ogyū　A friend who looks out for Seizō
  • Mihoko　The girl for whom Seizō has feelings

The opening (original text)

The road of four ri was long. Along it lay the town of Hanyū, where the market for aojima cloth is held. Milk vetch was in flower in the paddies, and double cherry blossoms scattered down over the hedges of the rich men's houses. Now and then a country girl passed with a red underskirt showing. From Hanyū he took a rickshaw. He wore the three-crested cotton haori his mother had sat up all night to sew for him and a new muslin sash; the puller laid a faded blanket over his lap and lifted the shafts. Somehow his heart was beating high.

The ending (original text)

Toward the end of autumn the wind off Mount Akagi always blew across, and the wood behind the temple roared like a tide. And past that wood the trains of the Tōbu railway, which runs as far as Ashikaga, went by morning and evening with a tremendous noise.

Famous passages

The four-league road was long.

A line from the scene of a young man, fresh out of middle school, walking to the village where he is to take up his post. This short sentence, stating only a distance walked, hints at the length of the years still to come — a celebrated opening of Naturalist literature.
