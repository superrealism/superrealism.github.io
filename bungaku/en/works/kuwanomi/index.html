About this work

A novel Suzuki Miekichi serialized in the haiku magazine Hototogisu in 1913, standing in the line of the sketch prose he learned from his teacher Natsume Sōseki: a work rich in atmosphere. Okumi is a kind-hearted young woman with no family, dependent on the proprietress of a small café. No great event occurs; her days looking after the people who come and go in the shop and the small children are copied down carefully, with a clear eye for observation and a gentle hand. A central work of Miekichi's, drawing with great affection the feeling of the modest life of ordinary people.

Summary

Okumi, who has no family of her own, has taken shelter with the mistress who single-handedly runs a small cafe. Since there is no other establishment like it nearby, the shop seemed to get by well enough, and yet it was a frugal life all the same — one in which the mistress would wear a gloomy face whenever the end of the month came round. Mindful of her position as a dependent, Okumi works readily and willingly, and above all looks after the mistress's young children — ten-year-old Akiko and a small boy — teaching them the beginnings of needlework, and passing her days in careful, attentive fashion. Nothing especially dramatic ever happens. The customers who come and go at the cafe, the children's innocent words, the passing of the seasons, and the poor yet warm scenes of a household of women are all rendered, one by one, with a clear eye for observation and a gentle touch. One day Okumi takes Akiko by the hand and goes out into town to buy lining and batting for a quilt — and with this small scene of ordinary daily life, tinged with a tender fondness for the life it depicts, the story quietly closes. This is Miekichi's representative work, in which he depicts, with deep affection, the quiet charm of ordinary people's modest lives.

Characters

  • Okumi　A kind-hearted young woman with no family of her own, who has taken shelter with the mistress of a small cafe. Looks after the children and works attentively about the house
  • The mistress　Runs the cafe single-handedly. Raises her young children amid a frugal life
  • Akiko　The mistress's ten-year-old daughter. Grows attached to Okumi

The opening (original text)

One The cafe where Okumi has taken shelter is a small shop that the mistress runs with her own inexperienced hands, but since there is nothing else like it nearby, deliveries come now and then, and customers drop in steadily enough, so that to outward appearances it seemed to be getting along tolerably well — yet once one stepped inside, there was this and that going on, and when the end of the month came round, the mistress would often wear a gloomy face, and, pen and ledger still in hand, sit staring blankly at one spot.

Kuwanomi by Suzuki Miekichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"That's a public telephone. Come now, let's hurry along," she says, taking the child's hand and leading her on. Okumi did not say so aloud, but she was going to buy the lining and the batting for the quilt she meant to make today out of the sash's outer cloth.
