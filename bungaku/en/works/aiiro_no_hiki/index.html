About this work

The posthumous collection of Ōte Takuji, who died without publishing a single book of poems in his lifetime. Here is the dense, sensuous world of language of a poet reckoned the equal of Hagiwara Sakutarō and Murō Saisei and yet unknown to the world. Uncanny, beautiful images full of colour and scent follow one another in poems such as "The Indigo Toad" and "The Porcelain Crow."

Summary

"In the bedchamber of the forest's treasure-house / the indigo toad breathes out a yellow breath / and draws a single painted pattern within a dark, damp hearth" — the title poem opens with lines such as these. A boy, frail as though he were the sun's hidden child, with eyes like grapes, sets off in soft, knitted kangaroo shoes, "Onward, onward, bravely." In the following poem, "The Porcelain Crow," a blue crow comes swooping in, enfolding smooth vowel sounds, and is called to: "Eat up this stillness of fine weather." In "The Withered Ship," a sensation of being drawn into a sea held in the palm of a hand is sung; in "Golden Darkness," a head like fermented sake, and lips as fearful as red rubber. What runs consistently through Takuji's poetry is a somehow diseased sensuousness in which colour, scent, and touch dissolve into one. Toad, crow, snake, rose, spice — these obsessively repeated motifs convey the density of a world shut up inside a poet who spent his whole life as an unmarried company employee. In the sequence of rose poems placed in the latter half of the collection, short numbered fragments — "a rose flower hidden at the bottom of a blue sea," "an ill-starred rose flower, wrapping the heart deeper and deeper within a fortress of smiles" — line up one after another, and the variations continue, as though trying to give a single flower every form it could possibly take. This is the first volume of a poet who never held a book of his own poems in his lifetime.

The opening (original text)

The Indigo Toad In the bedchamber of the forest's treasure-house the indigo toad breathes out a yellow breath and draws a single painted pattern within a dark, damp hearth. A boy, frail as though he were the sun's hidden child, with beautiful eyes like grapes, sets off, onward, onward, bravely, a hunter of fancy, in soft, knitted kangaroo shoes.

Aiiro no hiki by Ōte Takuji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

A rose flower, hidden at the bottom of the blue sea, thorny and yet soft, a rose flower ringing out a bell of fragrance. A rose flower, in the blue sky, blindfolded with a cloth of golden colour. An ill-starred rose flower, wrapping the heart deeper and deeper within a fortress of smiles.

Aiiro no hiki by Ōte Takuji (Aozora Bunko)
