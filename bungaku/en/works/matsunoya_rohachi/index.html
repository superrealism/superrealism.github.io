About this work

A long novel Yoshikawa Eiji serialized in Sunday Mainichi in 1934. At the end of the shogunate, Dohi Shōjirō, a serious swordsman in the service of the Hitotsubashi house, loses everything with the collapse of the shogunate and comes in time to live as Matsunoya Rohachi, a jester in the Yoshiwara. Modelled on the real Matsunoya Rohachi, it is an unusual work following as far as the Meiji era the joys and sorrows of a man who, tossed about by the upheaval of his age, lived as a clown without losing the pride and the feeling of a former shogunal retainer.

Summary

In the first year of Bunkyū, at the twilight of the shogunate, there lived, at the Irie fencing school attached to the Hitotsubashi house (that of Tokugawa Yoshinobu), a dull but earnest swordsman named Dohi Shōjirō. The eldest son of the head of the personal attendants, a huge man with acorn-round eyes who knew nothing of dissipation, this plain, unworldly fellow sat at the same practice hall as the man who would later become Shibusawa Eiichi. But the age was hurtling toward the great upheavals of the shogunate's final years. With the shogunate's collapse, the life of Shōjirō, a retainer of the fallen regime, was overturned root and branch, and he tumbled, with nowhere to turn, toward the very bottom of the world. Carrying within him a samurai's pride and earnestness he could no longer put to any use, and drifting from one misfortune to the next, Shōjirō at last arrived at the Yoshiwara quarter, fragrant with the powder of its courtesans. There he took the name "Matsunoya Rohachi," and, as a taikomochi — a professional entertainer and jester — lived out his days playing the clown at drinking parties, all the while keeping the pride of a former shogunate retainer buried deep in his breast. Watching, as the Meiji era unfolded around him, the great old houses of the past swallowed up and ruined by the new zaibatsu conglomerates, the aging Rohachi found, among the fading images of the women who had passed through his life, the one pure thing that remained, in the end, from his own ravaged existence.

Characters

  • Dohi Shōjirō (Matsunoya Rohachi)　An earnest swordsman who served the Hitotsubashi house. After the shogunate's collapse, he becomes a taikomochi entertainer in Yoshiwara — the protagonist
  • Shibusawa Eiichi　A young man of standing who studied at the same school as Shōjirō. Later a great businessman
  • Irie Tatsusaburō　The fencing master of the Hitotsubashi house
  • O-Kiyo　A woman whose pure image remains with Rohachi for the whole of his life

The opening (original text)

"Who will earn his first certificate in this winter's campaign?" "In the summer campaign, I'll see to it I'm promoted to keeper of the roll." So the students would say, honing themselves toward that day. The Irie school held its award ceremonies twice a year — on the twenty-fifth of December, at year's end, and on the tenth of July, at Bon — and the students called these two half-year terms of training the "winter campaign" and the "summer campaign," vying with one another over nothing so much as the speed with which they earned their certificates.

Matsunoya rohachi by Yoshikawa Eiji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Good — that's good! ...One pure thing, at least, remains to me from my whole life now, thanks to this. From those three sisters, after all, I've gathered up something worth the gathering." Lamps were lit in the teahouses and boathouses. The night river of the great Sumida, dyed with countless rainbows, rippled anew. — The sound of drums, of shamisen, of street performers doing their impersonations; the autumn night still had a long way yet to go.
