Biography

Born in 1884 into a printing-business family in Ginza, Tokyo. His father's sudden death brought the family to ruin, and he spent his boyhood moving between relatives. He studied under Tsubouchi Shōyō and Shimamura Hōgetsu in the English literature department at Waseda University, and after graduating worked at Waseda Bungakusha and the Yomiuri Shimbun before joining the publisher Fuzanbō. While involved with the Bungei Kyōkai and Geijutsu-za theater groups as a drama critic and translator, he devoted much of his energy to editing for children's literature. His retellings of Japanese folk tales such as "Momotarō," "Hanasaka Jiji" ("The Old Man Who Made the Flowers Bloom"), and "Shitakiri Suzume" ("The Tongue-Cut Sparrow"), written in plain language that children could read aloud just as it stood, culminated in the "Nihon Dōwa Hōgyokushū" ("Treasury of Japanese Fairy Tales") and became the widely followed "standard text" for folk tales thereafter. He died in 1950 at the age of 66.
