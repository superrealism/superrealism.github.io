About this work

A long novel Takahama Kyoshi serialized in the Kokumin Shimbun in 1908. Heiwa Sanzō, a young man determined to make his way as a haiku poet, comes up to Tokyo from Matsuyama and takes his place in the haiku world through poverty and setback. Kyoshi's own youth and the poets of Masaoka Shiki's circle are drawn from the life, and it is valuable too as a record of the inner workings of the Meiji haiku world around Hototogisu. His central work of fiction, taking the step from sketch prose into the novel, and the one in which Sōseki acknowledged Kyoshi the novelist.

Summary

In the twenty-fourth year of Meiji, Heiwa Sanzō graduates from the Iyo Ordinary Middle School and makes up his mind to become neither a doctor nor a politician but a man of letters. Having put his hand to haiku, Sanzō throws himself into making verses under the guidance of older poets from his own province, and in time goes up to Tokyo with his purpose in him. But the road of letters is not easy. His studies do not continue; he moves from lodging to lodging; he lives in as a student of German and passes his days like a hanger-on. When he moves to Kyoto, a faint intimacy grows up with Tsuruko, the daughter of the house where he lodges, and with the maid O-Tsune, but neither comes to settling his life. Through poetry meetings with his companions, the editing of haiku magazines and his dealings with poets in the provinces, Sanzō little by little takes a place in the world of haiku. But the rootlessness of making a living by haiku does not change. Suigetsu, a fellow poet who had once seemed a philosopher of an extraordinary cast, has become at some point an ordinary man; in fear of earthquakes he says calmly, "I think I shall kill myself," and before long shoots himself with a pistol. With that death in his breast, Sanzō goes on with his days as a master of haikai.

Characters

  • Heiwa Sanzō　A young man who means to make his way as a master of haikai. A projection of the author himself
  • Suigetsu　Sanzō's fellow poet, a man of a philosophical cast, who later kills himself
  • Tsuruko　The daughter of the house where he lodges in Kyoto
  • O-Tsune　The maid of the same house, who is fond of Sanzō

The opening (original text)

I In March of the twenty-fourth year of Meiji, Heiwa Sanzō graduated from the Iyo Ordinary Middle School. Until his fourth year Sanzō had been a faithful student of the school subjects, and his one hope had been to take first place in the examinations. But for some reason, from about a year before this, he gave up studying before the examinations altogether. Before this final examination he read straight through Chikamatsu's domestic plays. He handed in his answers earlier than anyone, and spent the time left in the waiting room reading over and over, closely, the "no-ideals" debate in Waseda Bungaku and Shigarami Zōshi.

Haikaishi by Takahama Kyoshi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Suigetsu killed himself in the autumn of that year. For three or four years he had almost broken off his dealings as a poet with the others, and Sanzō, meeting him for the first time in a long while soon after coming back from Kyoto, was astonished at the extraordinary change in his bearing and his speech. The man who had once seemed at a glance a philosopher of an extraordinary cast had become an entirely mild and ordinary person. When Sanzō asked, "And how is the haiku these days?" he said, "I have made two or three lately," and recited them as he remembered them. Sanzō was astonished at how utterly commonplace they were. And what astonished him most was his saying, "I think I shall kill myself." But since his manner was perfectly calm, and his tone such that it did not seem a great matter at all, Sanzō, though startled at first, did not think much about it.
