About this work

A historical story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Gendai in 1939. The samurai Hyōma, entrusted with a secret mission and setting out on a dangerous journey, and Obusa, who sees him off in anxiety. A piece drawing in concise prose the tension of a duty and the feeling at a parting.

Summary

"Hey, Makino, won't you get up?" — on the road, Hyōma struggles to rouse a companion too exhausted to move. With help from his comrade Obusa, the two press on along a road burdened with a heavy charge. The story follows, at its center, the samurai Hyōma, entrusted with a secret mission, and the human feeling exchanged among the men amid the tension of their circumstances. In time Hyōma swears he will "deliver it without fail," the precious thing entrusted to him, and sets off alone on the dangerous journey. "Wait for good news — farewell." Hyōma raises his whip and spurs his horse on. Obusa stands rooted to the spot for a long while, listening to the sound of hooves growing distant on the night wind. A piece of Yamamoto's drawing, in concise and richly resonant prose, the resolve of a man living for his mission and the feeling at a parting.

Characters

  • Hyōma　A samurai who sets out bearing a secret mission
  • Obusa　The one who sees Hyōma off
  • Makino　A companion on the road

The opening (original text)

"Hey, Makino, won't you get up?" "Have mercy on me, I really can't go on." "...There's no help for it." Unable to rouse him, Hyōma turned around. "Lend me a hand a moment, Obusa — he really doesn't look like he can move."
