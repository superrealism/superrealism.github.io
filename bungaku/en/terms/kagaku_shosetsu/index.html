What is Kagaku Shōsetsu (Science Fiction)?

Category
Form / Genre

Fiction that, grounded in scientific knowledge and imagination, depicts future societies, outer space, inventions, and extraterrestrials — what is today called "SF" (science fiction). Oshikawa Shunrō's adventure tale of a submarine warship, "Kaitei Gunkan" ("The Undersea Battleship," 1900), stands as its Meiji-era forerunner. The genre became fully established as such in the early Shōwa era: Unno Jūza, who also worked as an engineer for the Ministry of Communications, debuted in the detective magazine "Shinseinen" with "Denkiburo no Kaishi Jiken" ("The Mysterious Death in the Electric Bath," 1928), and went on to publish one work of scientific imagination after another, including "Jūhachiji no Ongakuyoku" ("The Eighteen O'Clock Music Bath"), depicting a future city where people are controlled by artificial music, and the boys' story "Kasei Heidan" ("The Martian Legion"), depicting a war with Martians, earning him the title "the father of Japanese SF." Miyazawa Kenji's children's story "Gusukō Budori no Denki" ("The Life of Gusukō Budori"), depicting a volcano and self-sacrifice, and Edogawa Ranpo's boys' fiction belong to this lineage as well. Unno's work nurtured the first generation of postwar SF writers, including Tezuka Osamu and Komatsu Sakyō, and became the rich soil of Japanese SF.
