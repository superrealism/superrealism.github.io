About this work

A novella Hori Tatsuo published in Chūō Kōron in 1941, and with The Wind Has Risen one of his central works. Beginning with the diary a dead mother left for her daughter — "The House of Elms" — it draws the wavering inner life of Naoko, who falls ill in the chest amid an unsatisfying marriage and enters a sanatorium in Shinshū. The paths of two women's hearts across two generations, traced in a clear lyricism and fine psychological description: the high point of Hori's psychological fiction.

Summary

The story opens with a diary — "The House of Elms" — that Naoko's late mother, Mrs. Mimura, left behind for her daughter. Widowed early, Mrs. Mimura had taken her two children — her son Yukio and her strong-willed daughter Naoko — and shut herself away to live quietly in a mountain cottage with an elm tree, in the run-down post town of O Village at the foot of Mount Asama. In her diary she sets down her feelings toward Naoko, who as she comes of age grows ever more intellectual and ceases to open her heart to her mother. In time, after her mother's death, Naoko marries into a certain household, but the marriage, in which her heart and her husband's never truly meet, leaves her unfulfilled. Naoko, who has fallen ill in the chest, enters a sanatorium in the mountains of Shinshū, and spends her days of convalescence surrounded by the shadow of death. In her heart, too, sways an unspoken feeling for Tsuzuki Akira, the young man she has known since childhood. One day, Naoko slips secretly out of the sanatorium, descends the mountain, and spends a night alone at a small hotel. In the helplessness of being entirely alone, she suddenly feels that the various facets of life, invisible to her as long as she remained fixed in one place, now seem, however faintly, to point the way toward a new way of living. A representative work in which Hori Tatsuo traces, in clear lyricism and delicate psychological portraiture, the emotional course of two generations of women, mother and daughter.

Characters

  • Naoko　Mrs. Mimura's daughter. Falls ill in the chest amid an unfulfilling marriage and convalesces at a sanatorium in Shinshū. Carries a hidden feeling for her childhood friend Tsuzuki Akira
  • Mrs. Mimura　Naoko's mother. Widowed, she raised her two children at the House of Elms at the foot of Mount Asama. Leaves behind a diary for her daughter
  • Tsuzuki Akira　Naoko's childhood friend. Continues to think of Naoko
  • Yukio　Naoko's elder brother. Becomes a doctor

The opening (original text)

The House of Elms Part One Naoko, I mean to write this diary so that you may read it someday. Some years after I have died, a time will surely come when even you, who these days will hardly speak a word to me, will think that you wish I had spoken to you more openly. It is for that time that I want to leave this diary written for you.

Naoko by Hori Tatsuo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Lost in such thoughts, she went on gazing, without any particular purpose, at the scene outside, where already nothing could be seen but a hazy whiteness. Pressing her face against the cold glass in this way had begun, little by little, to feel pleasant to her. The hall was warm enough that her face had begun to flush. Even in this pleasant warmth, she could not help thinking of the clinging cold of the mountain sanatorium she would have to return to tomorrow. …… A waiter came to tell her that the meal was ready. She nodded silently, and, suddenly feeling hungry, without going back to her own room, set off instead toward the inner dining hall, from which the quiet clink of dishes had already begun to sound.

Naoko by Hori Tatsuo (Aozora Bunko)
