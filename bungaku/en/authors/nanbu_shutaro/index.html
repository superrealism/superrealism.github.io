Biography

Born in 1892 in Sendai, Miyagi Prefecture. He entered the finance department of Keio University before switching to the literature department, and under the guidance of Nagai Kafū took part in editing "Mita Bungaku." He won recognition with "Shūdōin no Aki" ("Autumn at the Monastery") and became a writer, publishing sophisticated, urbane short stories while keeping close company with literary friends such as Akutagawa Ryūnosuke and Kikuchi Kan. He is also known as one of those who introduced the game of mahjong, learned on a trip to China, to fellow writers in Japan, and his essay "Majan o Kataru" ("Speaking of Mahjong") vividly conveys the literary customs of the early Shōwa era. He died suddenly in 1936 at the age of 43.
