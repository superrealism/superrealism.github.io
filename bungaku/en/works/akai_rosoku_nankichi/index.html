About this work

A very short piece by Niimi Nankichi. A monkey brings back a single red candle from the village, and the animals of the mountain become convinced it is a firework. The comedy of the drawing of lots, by which everyone's wish to see it and unwillingness to be the one to light it are worked out, and the deflating ending of a candle that simply burns quietly, leave a flavour hard to describe.

Summary

A monkey who had gone down from the mountain to the village to amuse himself picked up a red candle. Red candles being rare things, the monkey took it into his head that it was a firework. He carried it carefully back up the mountain, and there was a great commotion. The deer and the boar and the hare and the tortoise and the weasel and the badger and the fox had none of them ever seen a firework. To all of them jostling and pushing to peer at it, the monkey said, "Careful, careful. You must not come so close. It will explode" — and he told them what a great noise a firework makes as it flies up and how beautifully it spreads across the sky. If it is as beautiful as all that they must see it, they said — and it was settled that it should be set off that night on the summit. In the evening they all gathered at the summit with beating hearts, and the monkey was already waiting with the red candle tied to the branch of a tree. But then a difficulty arose. No one would go and light it. They all liked seeing fireworks; they did not like going to light them. So they drew lots, and the tortoise, who drew first, came as far as the candle and then his head went in and would not come out. The weasel, who was next, was near-sighted and only looked about him at the foot of the candle. At last the boar rushed forward and lit it, boldly. Everyone was startled and dived into the grass and stopped their ears hard, and stopped their eyes as well. But the candle did not go off with any bang at all; it only went on burning quietly.

Characters

  • The monkey　Who takes the red candle for a firework and carries it home
  • The tortoise, the weasel and the boar　The beasts on whom the lot to light it falls

The opening (original text)

A monkey who had gone down from the mountain to the village to amuse himself picked up a red candle. Red candles are not things there are many of. And so the monkey took it into his head that the red candle was a firework. The monkey carried the red candle he had found carefully back up the mountain. On the mountain there was a great commotion. For a firework was a thing that the deer, and the boar, and the hare, and the tortoise, and the weasel, and the badger, and the fox had none of them ever seen even once.

Akai rōsoku by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

At last the boar rushed forward. The boar was truly a brave beast. He really went and lit it. Everyone was startled and dived into the grass and stopped their ears hard. And not only their ears — they stopped their eyes as well. But the candle did not go off with any bang at all; it only went on burning quietly.
