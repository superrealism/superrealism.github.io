About this work

Written in 1952 and found after Kusaka Yōko's death: a very short piece following one day in which a woman calling herself Anan goes about delivering five identical letters, in short sentences that explain no feeling at all. What is happening is not disclosed until the last few lines, and even then a riddle remains. The protagonist of the novel The Brilliant Moment, written the same year, is also called Anan — the self-portrait that possessed the author in her last year shows itself here too.

Summary

The woman wrote five letters and put them into white square envelopes. The contents were all of them the same. She sealed them, wrote her own name on the back, thought for a while, and then began impatiently to write five different addresses.

On a spring morning, dressed in a black figured silk scattered with the character for long life, the woman leaves the house at ten. At a house by the river she gives the maid nothing but the name "Anan" and goes away, and the first letter goes into the postbox at that gate. At a shack in the poor quarter she has the envelope slipped into the lattice door; at an antique dealer's she buys a Chinese knife and leaves a message for a white-haired customer. At an elementary school she leaves a letter addressed to the mother of a boy for whom she stands surety and pays the school fees. At a beauty parlor she hands a girl the letter for a baroness.

Passing through the darkening streets, the woman goes in at a black gate. On the veranda was her husband. To his voice asking whether she has come back, the woman answers, "But I shall go away again at once," and steps over the low bamboo and goes out. When she returns to the house she left that morning, the paper door of her own room is shut, and from within a voice says, "Wait." Through the door the woman reports her day — the aunt, the woman like a fox, the self-styled lover of art, the child she bore and gave away, the baroness, and last of all the husband she visited. "If I do not go away, my feeling will kill that man." When she opened the door, there was no one inside.

Characters

  • The woman (Anan)　Who goes about delivering five letters of the same content
  • The husband　The man who was waiting for the woman at the house with the black gate
  • The boy　The child for whom the woman stands surety and pays the school fees
  • The voice behind the paper door　The one who answers from within the woman's room

The opening (original text)

The woman wrote five letters and put each of them carefully into a white square envelope. The contents were all of them the same. When she had sealed them, the woman wrote her own name on the back. Then she thought for a while about the five addresses, and presently, with the sound of her pen, began impatiently to write five different names. The dusk made the whiteness of the narcissus in the woman's room stand out. The woman put the five letters into a black leather handbag. The spring morning came to the woman's bedside bringing a sweet scent and bright colors.

Onna by Kusaka Yōko (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Not long afterward the woman ended her life with a razor. Four women and one man read the letter. "I am a woman called Anan. I have taken away the person you love most. I called that person my husband and lived with him for ten years. Until today. But I have been called by the invitation of the devil. I have promised to wait upon the devil. I give your husband back to you." That man went to no one. And he answered the invitation of the devil.
