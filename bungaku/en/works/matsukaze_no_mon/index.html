About this work

A historical story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Gendai in 1940. A young samurai shuts himself away in the Cave of the White Fox, far from any village, and disciplines himself for a certain resolve. A fine early short story drawing in still prose the sad karma around his friend Hachirobei and a heart that mourns the dead friend at a temple gate through which the wind in the pines blows.

Summary

On a cliff overlooking a ravine, in a cave the villagers call the "White Fox Grotto" and dare not approach, a young samurai arrives one day at the start of autumn. Carrying a swollen travel pack and a bundle wrapped in scarlet felt, he mutters, "Here, at least, no one will disturb me," and unpacks his belongings in the cave. From the next morning he sits in quiet meditation near the entrance, and amid the roar and spray of the great waterfall, fixes his eyes on the rock wall and begins to discipline his body and mind — all for the sake of fulfilling a certain resolve. The story then traces the sad bond between this young samurai and his friend Hachirobei. Time passes, and two men, Munetoshi and Daigaku, ride on horseback toward Gidarin-ji, a temple north of the castle. Dismounting on the approach lined on either side with pine trees, they walk on in silence as the pine wind sighs mournfully around them. Climbing the stone steps and passing beneath the tall temple gate, they find the temple grounds, too, filled with the sound of the pine wind, like the roar of the tide. "Hachirobei — I have come to see you," Munetoshi murmurs, as though calling out to the very sound of the wind in the pines, and only then does he find the tears welling up as though a dam had broken. Beside the temple gate, a young woman with her hair bound low stood watching them go, her tear-swollen eyes lifted, gazing after them in silence. A fine early short story by Yamamoto, entrusting to a gate swept by the pine wind his mourning for a dead friend.

Characters

  • Munetoshi　A warrior who mourns his dead friend
  • Hachirobei　Munetoshi's late friend

The opening (original text)

That cave stood atop a cliff overlooking a ravine. The valley was deep, and on both its banks grew a forest whose trees, never yet touched by an axe, spread their branches thick and close together, so that only for the briefest moment at midday did the sunlight slip weakly through in faint, hesitant stripes. ...The villagers called it the "White Fox Grotto." And it was said that unlooked-for misfortune would befall anyone who went near it.

Matsukaze no mon by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Hachirobei — I have come to see you." Munetoshi murmured this in his throat, as though calling out to the sound of the pine wind. And only then, for the first time, did the tears come welling up as though a dam had broken. The two of them walked on through the pine wind. And so they never noticed that, beside the temple gate, a young woman in samurai dress, her hair bound low, knelt upon the ground, her tear-swollen eyes lifted, watching them go in silence.

Matsukaze no mon by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
