About this work

An essay on taste by Kitaōji Rosanjin on the subject of tofu simmered in hot water. What decides a good pot of it is above all the quality of the tofu itself, and that quality hangs on good water — so he puts the tofu of Kyoto, the city of clear water, first. Weaving in his experience of being given the secret of the house by a celebrated shop at Nawate, and boasting of the good water that sprang up in his own grounds, he sets out concretely the practical points as well: the earthenware pot, cedar chopsticks, kelp for the stock, and the condiments.

Summary

Rosanjin states that however carefully one chooses the condiments and soy sauce, hot-water-simmered tofu cannot succeed if the tofu itself is poor, and declares flatly that if you want good tofu, the place is Kyoto, without question. This, he says, is because Kyoto has long been famed for its clear water and blessed with good water, and is a place that prizes inexpensive delicacies such as vegetarian cuisine. He laments that even Kyoto is today losing the taste of former times to piped water, machine manufacture, and poor-quality soybeans, but goes on to tell how he was taught the secret method handed down in a certain shop just north of Shijō on Nawate, and, drawing on the good water that springs up on his own grounds, has come to make tofu no worse than that of the shop itself. Good tofu, he says, is delicious whether eaten raw with fresh soy sauce, grilled, fried, or made into hiryōzu; and in the latter half he gives concrete instruction on the equipment and method for hot-water tofu — the earthenware pot, cedar chopsticks, kelp stock, condiments and soy sauce.

The opening (original text)

If you want to eat good hot-water-simmered tofu, the most important thing, above all else, is to choose good tofu. However carefully you choose your condiments and soy sauce, it is no use if the tofu itself is poor. So then, where should one go looking for good tofu? Kyoto, without question. Kyoto, being a place long famed for its clear water, is rich in good water, and so good tofu is made there. The people of Kyoto, moreover, are first-rate at seeking out inexpensive delicacies such as vegetarian cuisine. For these reasons, the tofu of Kyoto is good.

Umai tōfu no hanashi by Kitaōji Rosanjin (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

I might add that the people of Tokyo, being to begin with no great connoisseurs of fine food, include very few who enjoy their meals in close detail. Even out in the provinces there are cities, towns and villages blessed with fine food. Those with the inclination would do well to take the delicacies of the various regions as their reference, and enjoy them in close detail.

Umai tōfu no hanashi by Kitaōji Rosanjin (Aozora Bunko)
