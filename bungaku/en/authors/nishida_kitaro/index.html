Biography

Born in 1870 in Kahoku District, Ishikawa Prefecture. While teaching at the Fourth Higher School he practiced Zen meditation alongside sustained philosophical reflection, and in 1911 published his first book, "Zen no Kenkyū" ("An Inquiry into the Good"). Positing "pure experience," prior to the split between subject and object, as the fundamental ground of reality, he built an original philosophy and is known as the thinker who first genuinely absorbed and made his own Western philosophy in Japan. As a professor at Kyoto Imperial University he trained many gifted students, and his school of thought came to be called the "Kyoto School." "Nishida Philosophy" is regarded as a cornerstone of modern Japanese philosophy. He died in 1945.
