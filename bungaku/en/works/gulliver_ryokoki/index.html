About this work

Swift's satire (1726) on the lands of the little people, the giants, the flying island and the horses, retold in four parts by Hara Tamiki, the author of "Natsu no Hana" ("Summer Flowers"), so that children might read it. It was the last work finished by a writer who had gone on writing about Hiroshima after the atomic bombing, just before he took his own life, and was published in March 1951. The bitter criticism of humanity in the original — the party quarrels of the little people, the hideousness of the Yahoos, beasts exactly like men — is kept as it stands, and at the end are added Hara's own record of the bombing and his "Song of Gulliver."

Summary

Lemuel Gulliver, a ship's surgeon, studies navigation and medicine out of a longing to go abroad, and takes to the sea. The first wreck carries him to Lilliput, a land of little people one twelfth his own height. Bound, carried off, and at last trusted by the emperor, he does them the service of towing away the enemy's fleet by ropes; but he is caught up in the court's party quarrels over the height of a shoe heel and the end at which an egg should be broken, and is hunted as a traitor. In Brobdingnag, where he is next cast ashore, the positions are reversed, and he lives as a little man on show, terrified of cats and wasps and monkeys, telling the king about the government of his own country and being laughed at for its barbarity. On his third voyage he visits the flying island of Laputa, and sees scholars so drowned in music and mathematics that they cannot see what is at their feet, and an academy given over to useless research. And at last he comes to a country governed by the Houyhnhnms, horses who are reason itself, where beasts exactly like men, the Yahoos, are kept as livestock. Shown the baseness of mankind to the bone, Gulliver asks to stay in that country; but it is not permitted, and he goes back to the world of the Yahoos.

Characters

  • Lemuel Gulliver　A ship's surgeon who goes to sea out of a longing for foreign countries
  • The Emperor of Lilliput　The sovereign of the little people, who favors Gulliver and then declares him a traitor
  • The King of Brobdingnag　The sovereign of the giants, astonished at Gulliver's account of the government of his own country
  • The Houyhnhnms　Horses endowed with reason, who receive Gulliver as a guest
  • The Yahoos　Beasts exactly like human beings, kept as livestock in the land of the horses

The opening (original text)

Part One: Lilliput 1. A Great Commotion I am one who has traveled through many strange countries and seen a great many curious things. My name is Lemuel Gulliver. From my childhood I wanted to go abroad by ship, and so I studied navigation and mathematics and medicine. I was very good at foreign languages too. In May 1699 I sailed from an English port aboard the Antelope. From about the time the ship turned toward the East Indies the sea grew rough, and the crew were much worn down. It was the fifth of November. The ship was going forward through a heavy fog. Because of that fog, we did not notice the great rock until it was right in front of us. In an instant we struck, and the ship broke in two.

The ending (original text)

I told him my story very briefly, and he looked as though he were listening to a dream. But he was a shrewd man, and little by little he came to understand me. I promised him too that I would not try to run away again. The voyage went well. On the fifth of November, 1715, the ship reached Lisbon. On the twenty-fourth of November I left Lisbon in an English vessel, and on the fifth of December I arrived at the Downs. My wife and children, who had been quite certain I was dead, welcomed me with great joy. As soon as I entered the house my wife took me in her arms and kissed me; but since for so many years I had not been touched by a human being, I fell into a swoon for almost an hour.
