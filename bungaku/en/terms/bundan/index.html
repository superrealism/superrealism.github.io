What is Bundan (the Literary Establishment)?

Category
Other

The society formed by the connections among writers, critics, and magazine editors — everyone engaged in literature. Its formation is generally traced back to the Ken'yūsha, bound together under Ozaki Kōyō in the 1890s, since it was around Kōyō that disciples gathered and a mechanism took shape by which newcomers made their debut through a coterie journal or magazine; Kōyō's late memoir, dictated near the end of his life, "The History of the Ken'yūsha," is a first-rate historical source for how it came about. Thereafter literary magazines, the literary columns of newspapers, and coterie magazines became the stage of the bundan, and gatherings such as Natsume Sōseki's Thursday salon or the Ryūdokai, where Naturalist writers met, connected people to one another. Having a work published in a coterie magazine recognized by a literary journal was called "emerging into the bundan," and the Akutagawa and Naoki Prizes, established by Kikuchi Kan in 1935, became its gateway. From the Taishō through the Shōwa era, when the I-novel was the mainstream, a writer's life and friendships became matters of reader interest in their own right, giving rise to the term "bundan gossip." After the war, the large-scale expansion of publishing and the growth of mass media thinned out its substance as a closed circle, and today the word is used mostly as a historical term.
