About this work

Aizawa Kin, a former geisha of fifty-six. She takes a telephone call from Tabe, once her lover, saying he would like to call after a year, and waits for him behind a carefully applied make-up that hides her age. Set in postwar Tokyo, it draws coldly, and exactly to the psychology of a heroine who means to be a woman still, the embers of an affair, the disillusion and the manoeuvring over money. Winner of the third Women Writers' Prize, and a central work of Hayashi Fumiko's last years.

Summary

"I'll come by around five this evening" — with this call from Tabe, a man from her past, Kin, a former geisha, sets about preparing meticulously for their reunion after a year apart. She checks her skin in the bath, massages her face with ice, and takes a sip of cold sake to bring color to her cheeks. For Kin, who at fifty-six still preserves, through "years of practice," the beauty that once made her the subject of postcards in her youth, a life without a man is unthinkable, and each love affair is a work of art in itself. But the Tabe who appears, having come through the war, has been reduced to nothing more than a vulgar middle-aged man, his business floundering, come only to beg money from Kin. A man who wants to retrace the memory of their old love, and a woman who wants to keep that memory alone beautiful. As the drink flows, the gap between their two hearts only widens, until at last something dangerous even flickers in the man's eyes. The author renders, in prose stripped of all sentimentality, the irony by which this one night of reunion turns instead into a time meant "for each to reject the other." Rare is the novel that portrays an aging woman's pride and solitude so mercilessly, and yet so voluptuously — a work also known through Naruse Mikio's film adaptation.

Characters

  • Aizawa Kin　A fifty-six-year-old former geisha. Possessed of a beauty that once appeared even on postcards in her youth, she refuses, even in old age, to let go of being a woman
  • Tabe　Kin's former lover. Returned from the war, he now comes to beg money from Kin, his business having floundered
  • Kinu　A mute maid who serves in Kin's household

The opening (original text)

There had been a telephone call saying he would come by around five that evening, and Kin, with the feeling of "ah, so it's been a year already, is that how it is," left the telephone and looked at the clock — there were still nearly two hours until five. Before anything else, in that time, she would have to go to the bath, she thought, and, having the maid start dinner early, hurried off to bathe. She had to look younger than she had at their parting. To let herself feel her own age even for a moment would be defeat, she told herself, and soaked slowly in the hot water; the moment she came out, she took ice from the refrigerator, crushed it fine, wrapped it in a folded square of gauze, and, before the mirror, massaged her face evenly with the ice for a good ten minutes. Her face grew flushed and numb, until she could no longer feel her own skin.

The ending (original text)

Kin took a pellet of Philopon from the tea cupboard and swallowed it quickly. A third of the whiskey still remained. She would make him drink it all, put him to sleep like the dead, and send him packing tomorrow. She herself could not afford to sleep. Onto the blue flame of the well-tended brazier she fed a photograph of Tabe from his younger days. Smoke rose up in thick billows. The smell of something burning hung heavy all around. The maid Kinu peered in through the quietly opened sliding door. Laughing, Kin gestured to her by hand to go lay out the bedding in the guest room. To mask the smell of burning paper, Kin fed a thin slice of cheese into the fire as well. "Whoa, what are you burning?" Tabe, back from the privy, called through the door, his hand resting on the maid's ample shoulder as he peered in. "I wondered what it would taste like to grill some cheese, and when I picked it up with the fire tongs I dropped it right into the fire." Amid the white smoke, a straight thread of black smoke rose up. The round glass shade of the electric light looked like the moon floating among clouds. The smell of burning fat caught in her nose. Choking on the smoke, Kin went roughly around the room, throwing open the shoji and fusuma on every side.
