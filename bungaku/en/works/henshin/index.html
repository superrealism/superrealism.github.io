About this work

One morning he woke to find himself turned into a gigantic vermin — Harada Yoshito's translation of the novella by Franz Kafka ("Die Verwandlung," 1915) with the most famous opening sentence in twentieth-century literature. A transformation that has happened without reason and without explanation is carried on not as an outcry nor as a fantasy, but with the everyday texture of worrying about being late for work. The course by which the family are astonished, then find him more than they can manage, and at last come to wish him got rid of, is drawn quite flatly, and the book has been read the world over as the byword for the literature of the absurd.

Summary

Gregor Samsa, a traveling salesman, wakes one morning from uneasy dreams to find that he has been turned into a gigantic vermin. A back hard as armor, a multitude of thin legs. But what he thinks of first is that he has missed the five o'clock train, that the chief clerk will take him to task for being late, and that he is working off his father's debt. His voice turns into something that cannot be made out, and outside the door stand his mother, his sister and his father, and the chief clerk who has come to see what is the matter. When with great effort he opens the door, the chief clerk flees at the sight of him and his father drives him back into the room with a stick. After that Gregor is shut in, and only his sister Grete brings him food and cleans the room. On the day when he resists his mother and sister as they try to carry out the furniture and clings to a picture on the wall, an apple thrown by his father sinks into his back, and the wound never heals. The family take in lodgers and go out to work, and Gregor, covered in dust, is forgotten. One evening, drawn out of his room by the sound of his sister's violin, he causes the lodgers to leave, and his sister says at last that they must get rid of it. His body, after he dies before daybreak, is cleared away by the charwoman, and the family go out by tram into the country for the first time in a long while.

Characters

  • Gregor Samsa　A traveling salesman who wakes one morning changed into a gigantic vermin
  • Grete　His sister. At first she nurses him devotedly, and at last proposes getting rid of him
  • Father　He goes out to work in his son's place. He throws the apple that wounds Gregor's back
  • Mother　She worries about her son, and yet cannot bear to look at him
  • The chief clerk　He comes to ask about the lateness, and flees at the sight of the transformed Gregor

The opening (original text)

When Gregor Samsa woke one morning from uneasy dreams, he found himself changed in his bed into a gigantic vermin. He lay on his back, which was hard as armor, and when he lifted his head a little he could see his brown, arched belly, divided into stiff curved sections, on top of which the bedclothes, ready to slide off altogether, could barely hold their place. His many legs, pitifully thin compared with the rest of him, flickered helplessly before his eyes. "What has happened to me?" he thought. It was no dream. His room, a proper room for a human being, only rather too small, lay quiet between its four familiar walls.

The Metamorphosis by Harada Yoshito (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

While they talked in this way, it struck Mr. and Mrs. Samsa almost at the same moment, as they looked at their daughter growing ever more lively, that for all the trouble of these last days, which had made her cheeks so pale, she had lately blossomed into a beautiful and shapely girl. Growing quieter, and understanding one another almost unconsciously by a look, they thought that it would soon be time to find a good husband for her. And it was like a confirmation of their new dreams and good intentions when, at the end of their journey, their daughter rose first and stretched her young body.

The Metamorphosis by Harada Yoshito (Aozora Bunko)
