About this work

Tachihara Michizō's second collection, published in 1937, and, like his first, issued as one of the elegant Hyacinth Books he designed himself. Held throughout to the fourteen-line sonnet, it weaves motifs of wind, autumn, evening and sleep into an air that seems to fade away, in soft colloquial Japanese and much use of spacing. One of the two books the poet published before his death at twenty-four, its delicate lyricism marks one limit of modern Japanese verse.

Summary

Beginning with "To a Certain Wind," which calls out "I was full of you, west wind," the collection follows the turning of the seasons and the hours. A rainy noon, the faint light at a shut window, the lonely thoughts one bites down on — all of it has turned, before one knows, into "things unknown to me." In "Presently, Autumn..." the evening speaks familiarly, and the trees throw their bared shadows into the night like the gestures of an old man, and autumn comes back "rather like a shallow, quiet sigh." But that autumn too will pass away without a keepsake, leaving the keepsake of what it did not forget. The poems ask their questions again and again and get no answer, and the words vanish into the blanks set in place of punctuation. In the last piece, lamenting a youth that passes, he says, "I am not waiting for you" — and closes quietly with the shadow of someone sitting on the edge of the bed and gazing at something, as though he had learned and stolen the secret of last night's sleep.

The opening (original text)

To a Certain Wind I was full of you, west wind, at a rainy noon where a slack song would not stop singing, in the faint light at the shut window, biting down on lonely thoughts — the trembling and the shivering that I remembered, those are things unknown to me....

Akatsuki to yūbe no uta by Tachihara Michizō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Ah — who is there? Emptily, emptily, my youth passes! I am not waiting for you and yet, sitting quite still on the edge of that bed, who is it there, gazing? As though he had learned and stolen the secret of last night's sleep
