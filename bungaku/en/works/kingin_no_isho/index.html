About this work

A children's story by Yumeno Kyūsaku. With a poor mother and daughter lives Otama, a girl as beautiful as a jewel. One day a silkworm flies in and weaves a beautiful garment from cocoons shining gold and silver — a story of the happiness that comes to a poor but good-hearted mother and daughter.

Summary

Once upon a time, in a certain place, there lived a poor mother and daughter. The father had died young, and the mother barely made a living on the wages from washing and needlework. The daughter, Otama, was as beautiful as a jewel and as pure as the moon, and the people of the neighbourhood would say, teasing, that a jewel had sprung up out of the gravel. The mother grieved that she could dress so beautiful a daughter in nothing but filthy rags. But in the spring Otama turned seven, a single moth flew in and laid its eggs. The mother, taking this for a gift from heaven, stole mulberry leaves from the house next door and raised the silkworms. In time the silkworms spun their cocoons — and those cocoons, astonishingly, shone gold and silver. Otama, dressed in a beautiful garment woven from the gold and silver cocoons, caught the eye of the crown prince with her beauty, and in time came to hold the rank of empress of that land. The joy of mother and daughter reunited, the mother's delight in her daughter's rise, the crown prince who had won a beautiful empress — the whole world was filled with joy and wonder. A children's story of Yumeno Kyūsaku's, drawing the happiness that comes to a poor but beautiful-hearted mother and daughter.

The opening (original text)

Once upon a time, in a certain place, there lived a poor mother and daughter. The father had died young, and now it was only the mother and her daughter Otama, the two of them together; but being so poor, they had nothing to eat from one day to the next, and it was only by the mother going out every single day to do other people's washing and needlework for a little pay that they kept even the thinnest wisp of smoke rising from their kitchen.

Kingin no ishō by Yumeno Kyūsaku (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The years passed like an arrow, and when Otama turned seventeen she at last came to hold the rank of empress of that land. The lords and nobles gathered from every province were struck with wonder at the splendour of the ceremony and could only marvel at it; and among them all, not a single one could so much as raise his head and look straight upon the beauty of Otama in her garment of gold and silver.
