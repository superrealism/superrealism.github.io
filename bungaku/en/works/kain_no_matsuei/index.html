About this work

A novella published in Shin Shōsetsu in 1917. A poor, violent tenant farmer drifts onto a great farm in Hokkaido and is beaten down, for all his struggle against a harsh nature and the landlord system. The author, who pursued humanist ideals within the Shirakaba group, cut the wildness and loneliness of a man at the bottom in strong lines here, and the work is placed in literary history as a forerunner of Japanese peasant literature.

Summary

Leading a lean horse, with his wife following behind carrying a baby on her back, Hirooka Nin'emon drifts into the great plain of Hokkaidō. He settles as a tenant on a corner of the Matsukawa farm, within sight of the Ezo Fuji — a coarse, violent man of wild instincts who cares nothing for law or for kindness. While he throws himself against a harsh nature to break new ground, he also takes to gambling and clashes again and again with the other tenants and with the farm office. His scheme to put his horse to use as a stud comes to nothing; then the horse he counted on dies, and his infant dies too, and his life is driven into a corner. Meeting the order of a land ruled by its owner with nothing but his fists and his defiance, he is unable in the end to put down roots in that soil. Like the descendants of Cain, the first murderer, driven out by God, Nin'emon leaves the farm again with his wife, a wanderer with no place to settle, and is swallowed up beyond a dark wood of fir. Through one man who is beaten by the land and by the system and yet will not bend, Arishima drew the primitive vitality of a human being and the loneliness of his fate.

Characters

  • Hirooka Nin'emon　A tenant farmer who drifts onto a farm in Hokkaidō. Coarse and violent, a man of wild instincts bound by neither law nor feeling
  • Nin'emon's wife　Limping, carrying the baby on her back, she follows her husband and endures poverty and his violence
  • The people of the Matsukawa farm　The office that manages the farm and the tenants around him, with whom Nin'emon clashes again and again

The opening (original text)

Trailing a long shadow on the ground, holding the halter of a lean horse, he walked in complete silence. His wife, with a great dirty bundle and a baby on her back whose head was all out of proportion, like an octopus, limped a little as she trudged after him, three or four yards behind. The winter of Hokkaidō was pressing down as far as the sky. Across the great plain of Iburi, which runs out from the foot of Makkarinupuri, the mountain they call the Ezo Fuji, the west wind blowing from the Japan Sea toward Uchiura Bay swept over it, wave upon wave, like breakers rolling in. It was a cold wind.

The ending (original text)

The man and the woman moved little by little toward Kutchan, suffering under their loads. A belt of fir showed ahead of them. Among all the trees stripped bare, these alone had not changed their gloomy dark green. Straight trunks pierced the sky as far as one could see, holding within them a sound of wind like breaking surf. Small as ants, the two drew near that wood, and before long were swallowed up in it.

Famous passages

Trailing a long shadow across the ground, leading his gaunt horse by the reins, he walked on in dogged silence.

An opening that depicts a family drifting to a tenant farm in Hokkaido through nothing more than a long shadow and a gaunt horse. It begins the rough-hewn life of a man who can settle into neither the land nor its people.
