About this work

A long novel Takami Jun serialized in Bungakukai late in life and completed in 1963. Through the life of Sanma Kōichi, an anarchist possessed by terrorism, it draws a Shōwa Japan pushing on from the Great Kantō Earthquake through the Manchurian Incident and into the mire of the war with China. It renders in a brazen, vivid first person the inner life of a man drifting between the underworld, the labour movement, the right and the left, and sinking into violence. In the repeated bad feeling of the title, felt by the protagonist as a bodily state, the ominousness of the age itself rises: one of the high points of Takami's work.

Summary

The story of the anarchist Sunama Kōichi — "I" — opens with buying a woman at a den of vice on the city's outskirts. Against the chaotic backdrop of the years around the Great Kanto earthquake, I, risen from the very bottom of poverty, scrape together money, women, and violence through plunder, living out desolate, day-to-day existence. In time I come into contact with anarchist thought, and my hatred of authority and inclination toward terrorism only deepen. Labor disputes, a murky entanglement with right-wing groups, solidarity and betrayal among comrades — I drift between left and right, growing ever more possessed by destruction and violence themselves. As the era rushes on from the Manchurian Incident into the Sino-Japanese War, and society as a whole avalanches into the madness of war, my own inner violent impulses swell too, with no outlet left to them. The words "an unpleasant feeling" that escape my lips from time to time are not merely some personal physiological reflex, but also an expression of the ominous mood worn by an era itself hurtling toward catastrophe. Condensing nearly half a century of Japan's dark underside into the life of a single scoundrel, the work drives on toward a catastrophic, gruesome close, thick with the premonition of ruin.

The opening (original text)

We stopped the taxi short of a dark railway crossing. "Having yourself a good time tonight, sir," the young driver said, smirking as he handed over the change. "What's it to you," Sunama Kōichi said, snatching the small coins from his hand. Across the road, the railway tracks cut through. An old-fashioned locomotive stood square in the middle of the road, wheezing out white breath like some decrepit asthmatic. Out past the city limits — this is the very bottom of the slums. Stalls lined the edge of the pavement, packed together in a row like lice clinging to a seam. The stalls were hung round with patched, ragged cloth. Smoke from pig's-belly fat being grilled rose up in billows from between the cloths. Sunama and I turned down the alley to the right. This here's prime real estate, Sunama said — meaning the choicest women were to be had here. We had money on us that day. Money we'd wrung out through plunder.

Iyana kanji by Takami Jun (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

(An unpleasant feeling!) A strange laughter came welling up from somewhere inside me. "Ha, ha, ha, ha." The laughter went on and on, unstoppable. Still laughing that mad laugh, I rolled down into the pit. I thrust my hand into my own pocket. The little beanbag had somehow torn open, and the red beans had spilled out, scattered loose. Gripping those beans, mixed now with metal snap-fasteners, in my blood-slicked hand, I went on, "Ha, ha, ha, ha." And down onto my head, still laughing at the bottom of the pit, came falling the body of Kō, his neck lolling limp from side to side.
