About this work

A short story published in the Osaka Mainichi Shimbun in 1920. A historical piece drawn from the Edo anecdote collection Jōzan Kidan, and often carried in school readers. Nakamura Shinbei, a captain feared in the home provinces of the warring age as the Spear Nakamura, lends the scarlet surcoat and Chinese-crown helmet that were the emblems of his valour to a young warrior — and meets an unlooked-for fate. Was it his ability that men feared, or the outward form? A sharp question about the strange power of appearances.

Summary

Nakamura Shinbei, captain of the guard to Matsuyama Shinsuke, lord of half the province of Settsu, was a warrior of great renown throughout the five home provinces and the western country. In his scarlet surcoat, blazing like fire, and his helmet of the Chinese-crown type with its gold tassels, his figure on the battlefield had a brilliance that dazzled; and the enemy foot soldiers would cry "Ah, the scarlet! the Chinese crown!" and shrink from his spear point. One day a young samurai whom he had long favored asks, before his first battle, to borrow the scarlet and the crowned helmet so that he may make a name. Shinbei agrees readily and goes out himself in a plain suit of black-laced armor and an unfamiliar helmet. In the next day's battle the young man in the borrowed scarlet mows the enemy down by the mere authority of that appearance and wins a striking success. But before Shinbei, who has put off his usual dress, the enemy are not frightened in the least. On the contrary, the foot soldiers rage at the chance to pay off their old grudge against "Spear Nakamura," and come at him with all the strength they have. Shinbei fights desperately, drawing twice his ordinary strength, and even so is nearly beaten down. And just as a feeling of regret at having lent his helmet and his scarlet so lightly crossed his mind, an enemy spear had gone through the lacing and into his side. Was it his own strength that men feared, or the "form"? It is a fine short story in which Kikuchi asks, in a tightly written hand, about the strange power of outward form.

The opening (original text)

Nakamura Shinbei, captain of the guard to Matsuyama Shinsuke, who was lord of half the province of Settsu, was a warrior of great fame throughout the five home provinces and the western country. Among the men of the great and lesser lords who then divided the home provinces between them — the Tsutsui, the Matsunaga, the Araki, the Wada, the Bessho and the rest — there was probably not one who did not know "Spear Nakamura." So often had Shinbei piled up the honors of leading the van and of holding the rear with the point of his great spear on its shaft three ken long. And what is more, his figure in armor showed a striking brilliance on the field. In his surcoat of scarlet like fire, and his helmet of the Chinese-crown type with its gold tassels, he had a dazzling clarity in the eyes of friend and enemy alike.

The ending (original text)

Shinbei noticed that things were not as they usually were. Usually the enemy had the fear of a sheep facing a tiger; it was nothing at all to spear them down while they panicked and lost their heads. Today they were roused as men are when they fight. Every foot soldier of them brought out his full strength against Shinbei. It was no easy thing even to spear down two or three. Enemy spear points grazed his body again and again. Shinbei brought out his utmost strength; he brought out twice his ordinary strength. And still he was on the point of being beaten down. It was just as something like regret at having lent his helmet and his scarlet so lightly crossed his mind that the spear an enemy thrust out went through the lacing of his armor and into his side.
