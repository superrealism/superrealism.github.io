About this work

Ouida's story (1872), set in a country town in Belgium, of the bond between Nello, a boy who dreams of painting, and the old dog Patrasche. It came out in Kikuchi Kan's translation as a volume of the "Complete Library for Primary School Children." In Japan it has a national fame through the television animation, and the ending in which boy and dog die before the Rubens in Antwerp Cathedral is still spoken of. It is known as a work more loved in Japan than in the Europe and America where it was published.

Summary

On the edge of the village of Antwerp in Belgium lived old Jehan Daas and his grandson Nello. One day the two of them save a great dog left collapsed by the roadside after being worked to death by an ironmonger, name him Patrasche, and take him into the family. From then on Patrasche draws the milk cart and keeps the old man and the boy alive. Nello loves to draw, and longs for a single sight of the Rubens hanging in Antwerp Cathedral; but money is needed to see it. He and Aloise, the miller's daughter, are fond of each other, but her father Cogez keeps the poor boy away. Then the grandfather dies, and when the miller's windmill burns, Nello is suspected of setting it. The villagers avoid them, and the milk round is lost. Rejected too in the drawing competition that was his one hope, and turned out of the house for unpaid rent on Christmas Eve, the boy finds in the snow the purse Cogez has dropped, and, instead of taking a reward, entrusts Patrasche to them and sets out for Antwerp. The dog follows and finds the boy inside the cathedral. The moonlight draws the curtains aside, and the Rubens he had always wanted to see stands before the two of them.

Characters

  • Nello　A boy who dreams of painting
  • Patrasche　The great dog rescued after being abandoned
  • Jehan Daas　Nello's grandfather, who makes a living carrying milk
  • Aloise　Daughter of Cogez the miller, and Nello's friend
  • Cogez　The master of the mill, who keeps the poor Nello from his daughter

The opening (original text)

Nello and Patrasche were left all alone in the world. They were friends in a friendship closer than brotherhood. Nello was a little Ardennois; Patrasche was a big Fleming. They were both of the same age by length of years, yet one was still young and the other was already old. They had dwelt together almost all their days; both were orphaned and destitute, and owed their lives to the same hand. It had been the beginning of the tie between them, their first bond of sympathy; and it had strengthened day by day, and had grown with their growth, firm and indissoluble, until they loved one another very greatly.

The ending (original text)

What the two of them had sought so hard for while they lived, the people of Antwerp gave them only now, when they were dead and had need of nothing. These two, who could not be parted while there was life in them, were not parted in death either. The boy's arms were clasped about the dog so fast that they could not be loosened. And the people of the village, ashamed and full of remorse, prayed that God might show them some particular mercy, and buried them in a single grave, master and servant still in each other's arms. — Forever. (The End)
