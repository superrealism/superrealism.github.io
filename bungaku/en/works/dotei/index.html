About this work

Takamura Kōtarō's best-known poem, published in 1914 and placed at the end of his first collection, which took its title from it. The original ran to a hundred and two lines, looking to nature as a father and, trusting that guidance, singing the strong will of a young poet cutting a path into the unknown, and the praise of life. The few lines to which the ending was later shortened — that there is no road before him, and that a road forms behind him — are famous beyond quoting. One of the central poems of modern Japanese verse.

Summary

"I am not walking on some great road that leads somewhere / Before me there is no road / Behind me a road comes to be" — the poet sings that he does not walk a road prepared beforehand, but makes his road with his own feet as he goes. Looking back, there is the road that all but perished in dissipation, the road shut in by despair, the road crushed by a childish anguish — his own road, so incoherent and so wretched that it is worth a shudder. But it was "nature" that showed him plainly, out of that seemingly worthless road, the "meaning of life." The poet looks up to nature as "the love of a stern father," and filled with that father's spirit is pierced by the voice that says: walk. There is no hand left to lean on now; he has only to believe in the father who fills the universe and throw his whole body into it. A hymn of a young soul stepping out into a vast landscape, feeling in his whole body his mission as a child of nature, and closing: "Ah, father / Father who made me stand alone (...) for the sake of this long way." A monument of modern Japanese poetry.

The opening (original text)

I am not walking on some great road that leads somewhere Before me there is no road Behind me a road comes to be The road is the footprints I have trodden down And so I am always standing at the road's furthest end What a twisting, what a wandering, lost road it is

The ending (original text)

Ah The way of mankind is long And there is no great road for it The children of nature must open it with the strength of their whole bodies Walk, walk Whatever comes out, climb over it and walk Step on into this shining landscape Before me there is no road Behind me a road comes to be Ah, father Father who made me stand alone Do not take your eyes from me, but watch over me Fill me always with the father's spirit For the sake of this long way

Famous passages

Before me there is no road Behind me a road is made

The opening of the title poem of the collection. Declaring that the very footprints one leaves behind become the road, it is Kōtarō's representative work of self-encouragement.
