What is Mono no Aware?

Category
Other

The fundamental aesthetic principle running through Heian court literature, above all "The Tale of Genji." It describes the deeply felt emotion or sentiment that quietly wells up in the heart upon encountering things — objects, nature, or the affairs of human life. It was the mid-Edo kokugaku scholar Motoori Norinaga who gave this term its profound meaning as a literary principle. In works such as "Genji Monogatari Tama no Ogushi," Norinaga argued that the essence of "The Tale of Genji" lay not in Confucian or Buddhist moral instruction but in "knowing mono no aware," rejecting the standpoint that judges literature by the morality of rewarding good and punishing evil, and recasting the monogatari as an artistic endeavor independent of morality and practical use. This view — that the value of literature lies in the movement of the heart that deeply feels the flavor of things, be it joy or sorrow — resonates as well with the elegant, delicate waka and diaries of the court, and, as a principle underlying the Japanese aesthetic sensibility, it exerted a decisive influence on later views of literature.
