About this work

Published in 1946, the last work Shimaki Kensaku left. A little before his illness turned grave, "I" went to Shuzenji to convalesce, was given a wretched room, lost his temper over it, and spent his days walking out of doors. Sitting down on a rock by the Katsura River, he finds a single red frog that throws itself into the rapids, is swept away, comes back to the same place, and does it all again. Just upstream is a quiet pool it could cross without trouble — so why does this frog choose the torrent? A day spent staring at a small creature whose struggle may be futility or may be will, set down in a quiet hand.

Summary

A little before he took to his bed for good, "I" went to Shuzenji to rest. The room he was shown to was at the end of the third floor, where the sun never reached all day, and it stank of the privy; his requests to be moved were brushed aside. A guest traveling alone is the one an inn of that sort likes least. Unable to manage his anger and ill humor, he took to spending his days outside except at mealtimes. One day, on the way back from walking along the Katsura River, he sat down on a large rock overlooking the water and noticed a single red frog crouched on the sandbar. Presently the frog set off toward the current and plunged head first into the strongest of the water. In a moment it was swept away, swallowed in the waves, and barely crawled out at the tip of the sandbar. Then it went back to the same place, crouched down, and after a while did exactly the same thing again. Just upstream lies a quiet pool where a great willow trails its branches; crossing there would be nothing at all. "What a fool," he laughs, and throws stones to make it notice — but the red frog will not stop doing what it means to do. Before long "I" can no longer think of it as anything but the working of a will, and an obstinate one. The sun goes down, the frog's movements take on the look of desperation, and the intervals between attempts grow shorter and shorter.

Characters

  • I　The narrator, come to Shuzenji to rest a weakened body. Still nursing his grievance against the inn, he meets the red frog at the Katsura River
  • The red frog　A single frog that throws itself again and again from the sandbar into the rapids and is swept away

The opening (original text)

A little before I took to my bed for good, I went to Shuzenji. I was already much weakened by then, though I did not think so myself. I meant to rest the body a little and soon be well again. Hot springs are in fact bad for a passive illness like mine, so that all I wanted was to be alone in quiet surroundings.

The ending (original text)

I went back to the inn in a mood altogether different from the one I had gone out in that afternoon. The stinking, dark, cold room, the unkind people there — none of it troubled me now. For a little while at least I had been able to forget society and human life and their vulgarity. The next day I left the place, without having read the one book I had brought with me, keeping only the impression of that red frog at the bottom of my memory. Even after my illness had kept me long in bed, I met the red frog in my dreams. I am a man who almost never sees color in a dream. Yet the yellow belly and the crimson markings of the red frog, in the instant it sank between the waves, were vivid to the point of witchery.
