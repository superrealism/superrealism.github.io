About this work

A short story published in Sunday Mainichi in 1924: a bold satirical rewriting of the folk tale everyone knows. Akutagawa's Momotarō is no hero of justice. Born from a great peach tree that has stood deep in the mountains since the beginning of the world, he hates work, and out of a whim for treasure he invades the island of blameless demons as a conqueror. The demons are peaceful and kindly, and are the victims — their children and women killed in a sudden attack, their treasure carried off. A piece full of scathing irony, exposing the underside of the hero's tale and the fraud of invasion and conquest.

Summary

Long ago, deep in the mountains, there stood a great peach tree that had been there since the parting of heaven and earth. Once in ten thousand years it bears fruit, and in each of its fruits an infant is lodged. One morning an eight-span crow pecks one of the fruits loose, and it goes down the valley stream and drifts into the world of men — and so Momotarō is born. But Akutagawa's Momotarō, brought up on the old man's and old woman's coddling, comes to hate the work of the mountains and the river, and announces that he would rather go to Ogre Island and subdue the ogres. The old couple, glad to be rid of a nuisance, cheerfully send him off with millet dumplings. On the road the dog, the monkey and the pheasant become his retainers reluctantly, discontented with Momotarō's arrogance and with his stingy dumplings. But the Ogre Island they are to attack turns out to be a beautiful everlasting land of palm trees, and the ogres a peaceful, blameless race who chant poems and play the koto. Momotarō's party kills them without mercy, carries off their treasure, takes an ogre child as hostage, and returns in triumph. Seen from the side of the subdued, Momotarō is nothing but an unreasoning invader. The young ogres left alive on the island begin packing bombs into coconuts, in the hope of independence. A masterpiece of Akutagawa's satire, turning the hero's tale inside out and drawing vividly the arrogance of the conqueror and the sorrow of the conquered.

Characters

  • Momotarō　The boy born from a peach, who invades Ogre Island
  • The dog, the monkey and the pheasant　The retainers he takes along reluctantly
  • The ogres　The race attacked, living peacefully in the everlasting land

The opening (original text)

Long, long ago, in the very oldest of times, there was one great peach tree deep in a certain mountain. To call it great may not be saying enough. The branches of this peach spread out above the clouds, and its roots reached down to the land of Yomi at the bottom of the earth.

Momotarō by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Deep in a mountain that men do not know, the peach tree that broke through the clouds and mist bears today, as of old, its numberless fruits hanging in clusters. The fruit that held Momotarō, of course, was long ago carried away down the valley stream. But the geniuses of the future still sleep, no one knows how many of them, inside those fruits. When will that great eight-span crow show itself once more upon the top of this tree? Ah, the geniuses of the future still sleep, no one knows how many of them, inside those fruits....

Momotarō by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)
