About this work

One of Akutagawa's stories about Japan's Christians, written in 1918. In the age of Oda Nobunaga, it frames the tale of a devil as told by the southern-barbarian missionary Organtino, and sets down, as an old manuscript is said to record it, how the devils of hell, invisible to human eyes, lurk in the streets of Kyoto and go about tempting people. The central episode is of a devil said to have sat cross-legged on the palanquin of a certain lady. A fantastical small piece drawn from a foreign point of view, closing with a strange pity for a devil who keeps human company: an intellectual short story characteristic of Akutagawa.

Summary

The blue eyes of the foreign missionary Urugan, it was said, could see even things invisible to ordinary people. Above all, the form of the devils of hell who came to tempt mankind, it was said, appeared vividly clear to him — and among the faithful who gathered at the Southern Barbarian church, none doubted this for a moment. According to an old manuscript, Urugan once told before Oda Nobunaga of the devils he had seen in the streets of Kyoto. These were strange little creatures, possessed of human faces, bat's wings, and goat's legs, who would clap their hands and dance atop the finial rings of pagodas, or cling to the backs of monks, or hang from the hair of women. Among the many tales of devils, the most curious of all is the episode concerning one devil said to have sat cross-legged atop a certain princess's palanquin. The author of the manuscript interprets this as an allegory Urugan told in order to admonish Nobunaga — that Nobunaga, having fallen in love with the princess and sought to force his will upon her, was being warned against his violence by Urugan, under the guise of a devil. But whether that interpretation is correct or not can no longer be determined today. Urugan, pity us, together with the devil — for we too carry within us a sorrow just like his. The tale closes quietly, stirring in the reader a strange sympathy for the devil.

Characters

  • Urugan　A missionary from the Southern Barbarian lands
  • Oda Nobunaga　The ruler of the realm, who listens to Urugan's tale

The opening (original text)

The eyes of the padre Urugan, it was said, could see even things invisible to other people. Above all, the form of the devils of hell who came to tempt mankind, it is said, appeared vividly clear to him — those who had once looked into Urugan's blue eyes all seemed to believe as much. At the very least, among the faithful who worshipped the Deus of the Southern Barbarian church, it was said to be a fact beyond all doubt.

Akuma by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The legend of the old manuscript does not tell in detail what became of this devil afterward. But what concern is that of ours? All we need feel, on reading it, is the wish to call out, only this — …… Urugan. Pity us, together with the devil. For we too carry within us a sorrow just like his.
