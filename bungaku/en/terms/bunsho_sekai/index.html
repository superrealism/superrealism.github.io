What is Bunshō Sekai?

Category
Society / Group

A literary magazine founded by the publisher Hakubunkan in March 1906. It began as a magazine of practical writing instruction and reader submissions aimed at teaching young people useful composition, but bringing in the Naturalist writer Tayama Katai as its first editor-in-chief broadened it to welcome fiction and poetry submissions as well, and its pages gradually shifted toward creative writing. Through Katai's connections, Naturalist writers such as Kunikida Doppo and Shimazaki Tōson contributed, and Katai and Tōson, along with Masamune Hakuchō, Tokuda Shūsei, and Kitahara Hakushū, served as judges for reader submissions, so that by the end of the Meiji era it was regarded as a great stronghold of Naturalist literature. Shimamura Hōgetsu's essay on the nature of sketch-based prose, "Ima no Shaseibun" ("Sketch-Prose Today," 1907), and Masamune Hakuchō's "Kamen" ("The Mask") were published in these pages, and its submissions column served as a gateway for newcomers, launching many tanka poets and writers into the world. Its momentum declined once Tayama Katai stepped down as editor, and it ceased publication in December 1920 after 204 issues in all. It stands in literary history as a leading Naturalist magazine alongside "Waseda Bungaku."
