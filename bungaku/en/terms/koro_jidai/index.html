What is Kōro Jidai (The Kōro Era)?

Category
Historical Period

A literary-historical term for the period of the 1890s (roughly 1887–1896), when the literary establishment was led jointly by the two great writers Ozaki Kōyō and Kōda Rohan. It takes one character from each of their names — Kō and Ro — to form the name "Kōro Jidai." Kōyō, leading the Ken'yūsha, won broad popularity with human-interest fiction of manners such as "Konjiki Yasha" ("The Golden Demon"), written in an ornate, realistic style, while Rohan, idealistic and rich in Eastern learning, stood opposite him with the dignified classical prose of "Gojū no Tō" ("The Five-Storied Pagoda") and "Fūryūbutsu" ("The Elegant Buddha"). After Tsubouchi Shōyō and Futabatei Shimei had laid, through theory and practice, the foundations of the modern novel, the appearance of these two writers brought Meiji literature to its first genuine flowering, leaving great influence as well on the subsequent Ken'yūsha-school writers and the current of pseudo-classicism. Adding Shōyō and Mori Ōgai, the period is sometimes called the "Kōro Shōō Jidai."
