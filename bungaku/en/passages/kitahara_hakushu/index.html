Famous passages by Kitahara Hakushū

Well-known lines from the works of Kitahara Hakushū.

4 passages

Heretical Faith

I think of it — the heresy of these latter days, the sorcery of the Kirishitan Deus. The captains of the black ships, the mysterious lands of the red-haired men, the crimson glass, the sharp-scented ambergris, the striped cloth of the southern barbarians, and arrack, and rare wines besides.

The opening of the “Secret Song of the Heretical Faith.” Stringing together goods brought from foreign lands like an incantation, this gorgeous early poem of Hakushū's is steeped in the exotic taste and the scent of transgression of the Southern Barbarians.

In the sky, the color of crimson clouds. In the glass, the color of crimson wine. Why should I be sad? In the sky, the color of crimson clouds.

The whole of a short poem from Heretics. Layering the red of the sky and the red of the wine, it brings sorrow to the surface precisely by denying it: “Why should I be sad?”

Paulownia Blossoms

Sing not, birds of spring, sing not — an evening when the sun sinks red, red on the grasses outside.

A poem placed near the opening of his first tanka collection. The archaic cadence of “sing not” and the red of the setting sun together set off the pathos of youth.

On the paving stones of the morning I send you home, crunch, crunch — O snow, fall like the scent of apples.

A poem from Paulownia Flowers. On the morning he sees his lover home, he calls on the snow falling on the paving stones to fall like the scent of apples. A representative tanka of Hakushū, in which sensuality and freshness live together.
