What is Gunki Monogatari (War Tales)?

Category
Form / Genre

Narrative literature that arose in the medieval period, depicting warfare and the rise and fall of warrior clans. It begins with "Hōgen Monogatari" and "Heiji Monogatari," recounting the Hōgen and Heiji Disturbances, and reached its peak with "The Tale of the Heike" (compiled in the thirteenth century), depicting the wars between the Taira and Minamoto clans. "The Tale of the Heike" opens with the famous preface — "The sound of the bell of Gion Shōja echoes the impermanence of all things" — and, under a Buddhist sense of impermanence, tells of battle and human fate in an epic mixed Sino-Japanese prose. Its author is unknown, but it spread across the country as "heikyōku," chanted with musical accompaniment by blind biwa-playing priests, and, as literature meant to be heard, it was beloved among the common people too. The lineage continues into "The Taiheiki," depicting the upheaval of the Nanboku-chō period, and this narrative tradition also flowed into early-modern jōruri and kōdan storytelling. In the modern era, too, Yoshikawa Eiji recast "The Tale of the Heike" into the sweeping epic "Shin Heike Monogatari" ("New Tale of the Heike," from 1950), reimagining Taira no Kiyomori as a hero who opened a new age, and it won nationwide popularity.
