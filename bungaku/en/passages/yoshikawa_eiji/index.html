Famous passages by Yoshikawa Eiji

Well-known lines from the works of Yoshikawa Eiji.

3 passages

Musashi

In what I do, I shall have no regrets.

The vow Musashi writes in his travel diary. The scene shows him revising it — first writing “I shall not repent,” then, finding that too weak, changing it to “I shall have no regrets.” It is drawn from an article of the Dokkōdō, the precepts the historical Musashi is said to have written late in life.

Rather than fretting to become this or to become that, make yourself into something that does not move — silent, like Mount Fuji. If you do not court the world's favor, and the world comes to look up to you, people will settle your worth for you of their own accord.

Musashi's words of admonition to his disciple Iori, who says he wants to become like the Yagyū. The teaching — rather than fretting to rise in the world, make yourself silent and unmoving — is widely quoted as one of the great lines of the novel.

The Three Kingdoms

Once more I say it to heaven: we three who stand here do not ask to have been born in the same year, the same month, on the same day; but we pray that we may die in the same year, the same month, on the same day!

The oath of Liu Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei as they become sworn brothers in the peach garden. The line “may we die in the same year, the same month, on the same day,” shouted by Zhang Fei as he raises his cup, is known as the Oath of the Peach Garden, the symbol of the three men's bond.
