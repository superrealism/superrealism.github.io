About this work

Taken by the turtle he saved to the dragon palace, and turned into a white-haired old man on opening the jewelled box — one of the best-known folk tales in Japan, retold by Kusuyama Masao. Memorable for the ending, in which Urashima himself says what was inside the box that the sea princess had filled with what she called the most precious treasure a human being has.

Summary

Long ago, at the bay of Mizunoe in the province of Tango, there was a fisherman named Urashima Tarō. One day, unable to bear the sight of children tormenting a small turtle, he paid money for it and let it go into the sea. Two or three days later, while he was fishing offshore, a voice called, "Urashima-san, Urashima-san," and that turtle came up alongside the boat. Told that it would take him to the Dragon Palace in return for his kindness, Urashima climbed onto its back and went down to the bottom of the sea. At the Dragon Palace the beautiful Princess Otohime received him. In the rooms on the four sides were the scenes of spring, summer, autumn and winter, and among the dancing of the sea bream and the flounder he forgot time and spent three years there. But his father and mother at home weighed on his mind, and when he took his leave the princess gave him a jeweled box, saying: you must never open this box. When he came back to the shore, it was a place he did not know at all. His house was gone and his parents were gone, and when he asked, people only told him that the tale of a fisherman named Urashima Tarō was a thing of seven hundred years ago. In his grief Urashima forgot the princess's charge and took the lid off the box. A purple cloud rose billowing out and covered his face; and when it cleared he had become an old man with a face full of wrinkles and hair and beard gone white. "Ah, so what the princess said she was putting in — the most precious treasure of a man — was the span of a man's life" — Urashima murmured it with regret. The spring sea stretched hazily away as far as one could see.

Characters

  • Urashima Tarō　A fisherman of the bay of Mizunoe in the province of Tango
  • The turtle　Rescued from the children who were tormenting it
  • Princess Otohime　The mistress of the Dragon Palace, who gives him the jeweled box

The opening (original text)

Long, long ago, at the bay of Mizunoe in the province of Tango, there was a fisherman named Urashima Tarō. Every day Urashima Tarō would shoulder his rod and go out to the sea, and catch sea bream and bonito and other fish, and so keep his father and mother. One day Urashima went out to the sea as usual, and fished the whole day, and came home.

Urashima tarō by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Urashima looked into the empty box and murmured, with regret, "Ah, so what the princess said she was putting in — the most precious treasure of a man — was the span of a man's life." The spring sea stretched hazily away as far as one could see. From somewhere the sound of a boat song, sung in a fine voice, came again to his ears. Urashima stood there vacantly, remembering old times.
