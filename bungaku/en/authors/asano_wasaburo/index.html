Biography

Born in 1874 in Naka District, Ibaraki Prefecture. After graduating from the English literature department of Tokyo Imperial University, he taught English at the Naval Engineering School and was also known for his translations of Poe and Lafcadio Hearn. When his child fell gravely ill he converted to the Ōmoto faith and moved to Ayabe to edit the sect's journal, but he later left the organization and founded the Society for Psychic Science Research, becoming a pioneer of psychical research in Japan. He left records of communications received through his wife Takako acting as medium, published as "The Tale of Princess Kozakura" and "Messages from the New Trees," among others. He died in 1937 at the age of 62.
