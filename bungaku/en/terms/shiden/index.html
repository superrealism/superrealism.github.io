What is Shiden (Historical Biography)?

Category
Form / Genre

Biographical literature that narrates the lives and deeds of real historical figures on the basis of the critical study of historical sources. In contrast to historical fiction, which mixes in invention to portray the inner lives of people, it values letting the sources themselves speak. In modern literature the term refers above all to the works of Mori Ōgai's last years. Beginning with "Okitsu Yagoemon no Isho" ("The Last Testament of Okitsu Yagoemon," 1912), Ōgai went on writing historical fiction such as "Abe Ichizoku" ("The Abe Family"), and as he confronted the weight of historical fact, as seen in his essay "Rekishi Sonomama to Rekishibanare" ("History as It Is and History Ignored," 1915), he eventually advanced to the shiden, in which the process of research itself becomes the work. While collecting bukan (directories of Edo-period daimyo and shogunal retainers), he repeatedly came across the ownership seal "Hirosaki Ikan Shibue-shi Zōshoki" ("from the library of Shibue, physician of Hirosaki"), and the work in which he brought to light the life of this physician of the Hirosaki domain in the late Edo period is "Shibue Chūsai" (1916), serialized in the "Tōkyō Nichinichi Shimbun" and the "Ōsaka Mainichi Shimbun." Together with the following "Isawa Ranken" (1916–17) and "Hōjō Katei," continued in "Teikoku Bungaku" and "Araragi" after its newspaper serialization was broken off, it is called his trilogy of historical biographies. Kōda Rohan's "Unmei" ("Fate," 1919, in the first issue of "Kaizō"), depicting the Jingnan campaign of the Ming dynasty, is also regarded as the pinnacle of the shiden, and Nagai Kafū, who revered Ōgai, wrote under his influence "Shitaya Sōwa" (first published in 1924, in book form in 1926), portraying his maternal grandfather, the Confucian scholar Washizu Kidō, and others.
