What is Kakekotoba (Pivot Words)?

Category
Style & Technique

A rhetorical technique, centered in waka, that layers two meanings sharing the same sound into a single word. By pivoting "matsu" ("pine") on "matsu" ("to wait"), "aki" ("autumn") on "aki" ("weariness, satiety"), or "nagame" ("long rains") on "nagame" ("gazing pensively"), a poet folds several layers of meaning into a limited number of syllables, generating rich resonance. It was refined in the age of the "Kokin Wakashū" and became an indispensable technique in court waka. In "The Tale of Genji," with its many embedded poems, characters' feelings are often entrusted to kakekotoba, and Ki no Tsurayuki's "Tosa Nikki" ("The Tosa Diary") likewise scatters poems that pivot the thought of a dead child onto place names and scenery. By letting two worlds of differing meaning coexist within a single word, the kakekotoba suggests a subtle feeling that could not be stated directly. Together with engo, which lets related words resonate with one another, it is a technique that makes the fullest use of Japanese homophony — the very essence, one might say, of court waka.
