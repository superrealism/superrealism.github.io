What is Myōjō?

Category
Society / Group

A monthly literary magazine centered on poetry, founded in April 1900 as the organ of the Shinshisha (New Poetry Society), led by Yosano Tekkan. It became a stronghold of Romanticism, following the "Bungakukai" founded in 1893, and brought a fashion for passionate Romantic tanka known as the "Myōjō style." Tekkan's tanka collection "Murasaki" and Yosano Akiko's "Midaregami" ("Tangled Hair," 1901), which boldly sang of sensuality and shocked the tanka world, are its representative works, and Akiko later married Tekkan and became the magazine's central figure. It trained many new tanka poets and other poets, including Takamura Kōtarō, Ishikawa Takuboku, Kitahara Hakushū, and Yoshii Isamu, but internal discord and the rise of Naturalist literature brought it to an end in November 1908 after 100 issues. Its spirit was carried on into the successor magazine "Subaru," founded with the backing of Mori Ōgai and others.
