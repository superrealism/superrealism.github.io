About this work

A novel Itō Sachio published in Hototogisu in 1908, a rural love story following The Grave of the Chrysanthemum. It draws the forbidden feeling between Otoyo, a young wife newly married into the house next door, and Shōsaku, a young man due to be adopted into another family as a husband. The life and the seasons of a farming village in Bōsō are caught exactly in the sketching manner, and against them rises the grief of two people whom family and morality will not let come together. The line that morality is an instrument for strangling human beings holds a quiet protest against the feudal family system.

Summary

To the house of Shōsaku, who is roused from his morning sleep by his sister's scolding, a young wife has come next door. Her name is O-Toyo. Meeting in the fields and in the comings and goings of the neighborhood, Shōsaku and O-Toyo come to understand each other's hearts. Honest and warm-hearted, O-Toyo feels herself saved by Shōsaku's young sincerity in the constrained life of the house she has married into; and Shōsaku cannot stop his feeling for the wife next door from growing day by day. But there is talk of Shōsaku's being adopted into another family, and O-Toyo is another man's wife. Rice-planting, wheat-cutting, the festival — as the round of the farming year turns, the deeper their feeling grows the less place it has to go. When at last the two open the bottom of their hearts to each other, still they cannot go against the way of a world that will not have it. If morality means being drawn together artificially by the reasoning that one must marry, and then having to be like machines, then morality is an instrument for strangling human beings — lamenting so, the two promise to tie together the ropes of their tears. Two days later O-Toyo went back to her own people, and never returned to the house next door. And Shōsaku's adoption too was broken off because of the talk.

Characters

  • Shōsaku　A young man due to be adopted as a son-in-law, whose heart goes out to the wife next door
  • O-Toyo　The young wife who has married into the house next door
  • Manzō　A manservant of Shōsaku's house
  • The sister　Shōsaku's difficult elder sister, who shouts at him

The opening (original text)

I "Manzō, Manzō! Shōsaku, Shōsaku! It's broad daylight outside. Come, get up, get up. Across the way and next door they've done a morning's work already. What do you mean by sleeping in on a fine day like this? Shōsaku, Shōsaku! Come now!" So shouted the difficult sister, as usual, rattling open the rain shutters of the front room.

Tonari no yome by Itō Sachio (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The two took each other's hands and twisted together the threads of their tears, and promised that if hereafter anything should come to save them by the grace of God, they would tie together the ropes of tears that each of them held. Two days after this, O-Toyo went back to her own people. And she never came back to the house next door. Shōsaku too went once to the family that was to adopt him, but the match was broken off in the end, perhaps because of the talk about O-Toyo.
