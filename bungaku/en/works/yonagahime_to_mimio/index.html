About this work

A short story published in Shinchō in 1952. Mimio, a young craftsman who has succeeded to the place of the finest master in Hida, must carve a buddha for the beautiful, cruel princess Yonagahime. The girl's innocent cruelty and the obsession of the man she plays with grind against each other in a story weird and vivid in Sakaguchi's own way. A central work, drawing the paradoxes of love and hatred, beauty and cruelty, with the force of myth.

Summary

The master craftsman celebrated as the finest in Hida is summoned by the rich man of Yonaga when age and illness have brought his death near. In his place the master recommends "me" — Mimio, a young man of twenty. Set to compete in skill with his elder fellow pupils Aogasa and Furukama, Mimio comes face to face with the rich man's daughter, Princess Yonaga: a girl beautiful and innocently cruel. Told to carve a Buddha for the princess, Mimio, out of rivalry with his seniors and out of his complicated feeling for her, carves not a Miroku but a hideous monster, hanging snakes from the ceiling and looking at them as he works. The girl's cruel innocence, and Mimio thrown about by it yet burning with obsession — the strange relation between the two moves at last to a shocking end. "What you love you must curse, or kill, or fight with. That is why your Miroku is no good, and why your monster is splendid" — so Princess Yonaga whispers when Mimio has stabbed her, and closes her eyes smiling. And "I" faint away and fall, still holding her. A representative short story of Ango's, drawing the paradoxes of love and hate, beauty and cruelty with mythic force.

Characters

  • Mimio ("I")　The young craftsman set to carve a Buddha
  • Princess Yonaga　The rich man's daughter, beautiful and cruel
  • Aogasa and Furukama　Mimio's elder fellow pupils

The opening (original text)

My master was a craftsman celebrated as the finest in Hida, but it was when age and illness had brought his death near that he was summoned by the rich man of Yonaga. My master recommended me in his place. "This is a young fellow of only twenty, but he has grown up at my knee since he was a small brat, and though I have not trained him particularly, he is one who has got the bones of my method without any great fault. ... He does work with strength in it."

Yonagahime to mimio by Sakaguchi Ango (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Then the princess took my hand and whispered, smiling. "What you love you must curse, or kill, or fight with. That is why your Miroku is no good, and that is why your monster is splendid. Always hang snakes from the ceiling, and do work as fine as the way you have just killed me...." The princess's eyes smiled, and closed. Still holding her, I fainted away and fell.

Yonagahime to mimio by Sakaguchi Ango (Aozora Bunko)
