About this work

A very short piece Ranpo published in Shin Seinen in 1925: a mystery in dialogue form, in which the whole story proceeds through the conversation of two men who once shared a railway carriage and now meet again. Around a ring that went missing on that train, their exchange leads to an unexpected truth. A technically neat little piece from Ranpo's early work, with a brilliant reversal set inside a few pages.

Summary

"Excuse me, but haven't we met before, on a train?" — two men begin exchanging words like this. One of them, A, brings up a "misfortune" that once happened when the two of them shared a train. That time, a man who came to sit beside him carrying a bag of mandarin oranges — B — had been oddly familiar; and as the reminiscence unfolds, it gradually traces the outline of an incident. A ring had gone missing on that train. A presses B for the truth of it. Eventually B, laughing, lets the secret out: he had, in fact, slipped the ring into the bottom of A's own tobacco pouch and neatly taken it back again from his wide-open companion. The whole thing proceeds by dialogue alone, and turns completely around with the very last line. A witty little mystery of Ranpo's, an experiment in dialogue form.

The opening (original text)

A: Excuse me, but haven't we met before, on a train? B: Forgive me, I didn't recognize you. Now that you mention it, I remember too — it was this same line, wasn't it. A: You had quite a misfortune that time. B: Oh, I'm much obliged for the thought. I didn't know what to do with myself at the time either.

Yubiwa by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

B: It'd gall me to have you think I'm lying, so I'll tell you. Don't get angry, now. The truth is, I slipped it quietly into the bottom of the tobacco pouch you had hanging at your waist. Though I must say, you were wide open the whole time. Ha ha ha ha ha. Eh, when did I get the ring back, you ask? No need to say — it was when you went rushing out through the ticket gate in a great hurry to pick up your dropped mandarin oranges.

Yubiwa by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)
