About this work

The programme for a new government that Sakamoto Ryōma wrote out in his own hand in November 1867, just before his assassination. Known as the eight-article programme for a new government, it sets out in eight clauses the basic principles of the government to come: the employment of able men, the appointment of capable lords, and the renewal of diplomacy, law, assembly, military system and finance. At the end he deliberately left the name of the one who should be its head as blank circles. Famous as a document conveying the character of a man who sought nothing for himself.

Summary

The Eight-Article Programme for a New Government sets out the framework the new government should have, arranged in eight articles. First, that men of renown throughout the realm should be summoned and made advisers; second, that capable lords should be chosen and given rank and office at court, and that offices now held in name only should be abolished; third, that relations with foreign countries should be settled by deliberation; fourth, that laws should be drawn up and a new great code established, which the lords should all uphold, and so on down through an upper and lower deliberative assembly, a bureau for the army and navy, an imperial guard, and the stabilizing of gold, silver and prices with foreign countries. At the end it states that, having first deliberated with a few clear-sighted men, on the day the lords assemble in league, "○○○ shall himself become their leader," and this shall be reported to the court and proclaimed to all the people of the realm; and it closes by declaring that any who oppose this public counsel, be they of the most powerful houses or noblest lineage, shall be resolutely put down. This passage, leaving blank the name of the one who should be leader, is known as showing that Ryōma sought no position for himself; the document is signed at the end, "Sakamoto Naonari, the eleventh month of Keiō 3 [1867]."

The opening (original text)

Article One Men of renown throughout the realm shall be summoned and made advisers. Article Two Capable lords shall be chosen and appointed, granted rank and office at court, and offices now held in name only shall be abolished. Article Three Relations with foreign countries shall be settled by deliberation. Article Four Laws shall be drawn up and a new, enduring great code established; once the laws are set, the lords shall all uphold them and lead their retainers accordingly.

Shinseifu kōryō hassaku by Sakamoto Ryōma (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The above, having first been deliberated with two or three clear-sighted men, shall await the day the lords assemble in league, whereupon — ○○○ shall himself become their leader, this shall be reported to the court, and thereupon proclaimed for the first time to all the people of the realm. Any who defiantly resist and violate this public counsel shall be resolutely put down, and no allowance shall be made even for the most powerful houses or noblest lineage. Sakamoto Naonari, the eleventh month of Keiō 3.
