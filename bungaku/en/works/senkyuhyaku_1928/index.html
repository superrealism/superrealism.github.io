About this work

A novella Kobayashi Takiji published in the November and December 1928 numbers of the magazine Senki. With The Crab Cannery Ship it is his central work and one of the central works of Japanese proletarian literature. Drawing on the March 15 Incident, in which members of the Japanese Communist Party and the labour movement were arrested all over the country on 15 March that year, it describes exactly how the activists seized in Takiji's native Otaru were tortured at the police station. Its indictment of the brutality of state suppression foreshadows the fate of Takiji himself, who would later die under torture by the special higher police: a monument of the literature of denunciation.

Summary

On the fifteenth of March, in the third year of Shōwa, the hand of mass arrest reaches even the town of Otaru. Wataru (Ryūkichi), a labor-union activist, and his wife O-Megu, along with Kudō, Suzumoto, Saitō, and others involved in the labor movement, are seized one after another by the police. What awaited the men thrown into the holding cells was torture beyond imagining. Beaten with bamboo swords and ropes, strung up by their feet, driven past the point of sanity — the Special Higher Police employ every atrocity at their disposal to fabricate "facts" along the lines of their own predetermined script. Some hold firm against it; others merely endure their terror — each man's response is drawn in turn. In time, those whose interrogations are complete are transported under guard to the courthouse in Sapporo, and the station abruptly empties out. On the walls of the now-vacant holding cells remain words that the prisoners, as though by some silent agreement, had carved there: "March 15, 1928!" "Workers of the world, unite!" A landmark of accusatory literature, in which Takiji, with burning anger, depicts both the outrages of power and the unbroken spirit of those who resisted it.

Characters

  • Wataru (Ryūkichi)　A labor-movement activist in Otaru. Arrested in the March 15 Incident and subjected to brutal interrogation
  • O-Megu　Ryūkichi's wife. Never quite grows used to her husband's involvement in the movement, and is always left frightened by it
  • Kudō, Suzumoto, Saitō, and others　Activists arrested together with Ryūkichi, who endure horrific torture at the hands of the police
  • The Special Higher Police and the chief inspector　The interrogators, who repeat atrocious torture to extract a confession that fits their predetermined script

The opening (original text)

For O-Megu, it was something she could never quite manage to grow used to, however many times it happened. No matter how many times — however many times it came around — O-Megu was startled anew each time, as though for the first time, and flinched, and lost her composure. And each time, her husband Ryūkichi would say something to her about it. But for a woman, it was, in any case, a blow too heavy to bear.

The Fifteenth of March, 1928 by Kobayashi Takiji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

By the ××th of April, everyone who had been held at the ×× police station had been transported under guard to Sapporo. The station grew abruptly empty. Only the graffiti on the walls stood out now, in the rooms with no one left in them. On the walls where they had all been held, as though by silent agreement, the following words had been carved, almost without exception, and with great care: 　　March 15, 1928! 　　Long live the Labour-Farmer Party! 　　Workers of the world, unite!
