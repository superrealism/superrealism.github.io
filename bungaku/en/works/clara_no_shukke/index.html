About this work

A short story Arishima Takeo published in Taiyō in 1917: the story of how Clare of Assisi, the saint of thirteenth-century Italy, took the veil. Raised in every comfort as the daughter of a wealthy noble house and with suitors of her own, Clare is struck deeply by Saint Francis's teaching of holy poverty and throws away wealth, love and honour alike for the religious life. Arishima draws in a solemn hand how a young woman, through the conflict between the sweet temptation of worldly love and her vow to God, makes the leap into faith.

Summary

Thirteenth-century Italy, Assisi. Clara, born into a wealthy noble family, was a beautiful and noble-hearted girl. She had suitors who sought her hand, chief among them Paolo of the illustrious Montorsoli family. But at sixteen, Clara had vowed to live out her life as a handmaiden of Christ, the Son of God, and the teaching of poverty and devotion preached by Saint Francis had taken ever deeper hold of her heart, refusing to let her go. In dreams, whenever the hand of her suitor Paolo touched her throat and she felt herself drawn toward the sweet love of this world, Clara was seized with terror at the thought of falling into a dark abyss — the terror of falling into hell as punishment for forgetting her vow to God — and prayed with her whole heart to the Holy Mother. Wealth, rank, love, every happiness this world could offer — Clara longed, with a burning wish, to cast them all aside and set out upon the pure path of the convent, and at last, one night, driven by that wish, she slipped secretly from her home. Along the dark night road she made her way toward the little chapel of the Porziuncola, where Saint Francis and his brethren awaited her, and, clinging weeping to the men who bore torches to light her way, pleaded her wish to renounce the world. Breaking the silence of the night over the plain, from the Porziuncola far below came the sound of the brethren's voices, raised in chorus to welcome their new sister, drifting up quietly, faintly, solemnly. A piece in which Arishima, in a solemn and stately style, depicts the drama of a maiden's soul as she casts aside the happiness of this world and leaps toward faith.

Characters

  • Clara　A noble maiden of Assisi. Vows at sixteen to serve Christ. Rejects the temptations of worldly love and casts everything aside to enter the convent (Saint Clare of Assisi)
  • Paolo　A young man of the illustrious Montorsoli family who loves Clara. In her dreams, he presses upon her as the temptation of worldly love
  • Saint Francis　The saint who preaches poverty and devotion. Clara is guided by his teaching onto the path of the convent

The opening (original text)

Seized once more by the dream, Clara woke in the darkness. Her younger sister Agnes, in the same bed, lay nestled against her breast, sleeping on quietly and peacefully. It was the morning of March eighteenth, in the year twelve hundred and twelve — Palm Sunday, the sabbath commemorating the Savior's entry into Jerusalem.

Kurara no shukke by Arishima Takeo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Clara pleaded with the four men who bore the torches, sobbing as she spoke. The four of them crouched down in silence, with Clara at their center. Breaking the peaceful silence of the night over the plain, from the Porziuncola far below came the sound of the brethren's voices, singing together with all their hearts to welcome their new sister, drifting up quietly, faintly, solemnly.
