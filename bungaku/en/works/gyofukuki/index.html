About this work

An early short story Dazai published in the coterie magazine Kaihyō in 1933, collected in his first volume The Final Years. With Suwa, a girl living with her charcoal-burner father by a waterfall on Mount Umahage in the mountains of Tsugaru, for its protagonist, it draws in a transparent style the confusion of a girl waking to sexuality within a closed natural world, and the fantastical transformation at the end of what passes between her and her father. A fine piece, suggested by a Chinese tale, compressing Dazai's lyricism and his darkness.

Summary

On Mount Umahage, at the northern tip of Honshū. At a teahouse by a waterfall, the girl Suwa lives alone with her charcoal-burner father. In summer she waits on visitors who come to see the falls, growing up surrounded by snakes and fish and trees through the mountain's four seasons. One year she hears talk of a student who fell into the pool below the falls and died, and the shadow of death passes over Suwa's heart as well. In time she grows uneasy at her own changing, from girl into woman. In the snowbound season when even visitors down the mountain have ceased to come, on a night of storm, as she spends the time with her father in their hut, an unnameable feeling rises in Suwa's breast. The next morning she throws herself into the pool below the falls. Sunk beneath the water, Suwa becomes a crucian carp, drifts for a while along the bank and among the rocks, and is then drawn once more into the whirlpool at the foot of the falls.

Characters

The opening (original text)

The mountain range at the northern tip of Honshū is called the Bonju range. Since it is nothing more than hills rising and falling to at most three or four hundred meters, it does not appear on ordinary maps. Long ago, they say, this whole area was a broad open sea, and Yoshitsune, fleeing northward and northward with his retainers, is said to have passed through here by boat on his way to cross over to the far land of Ezo.

Gyofukuki by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

After that the carp went perfectly still, only its pectoral fins stirring faintly now and then. It seemed to be thinking of something. It stayed like that for a while. Then, twisting its body, it went straight toward the pool below the falls. In an instant, spinning round and round like a leaf, it was drawn in.
