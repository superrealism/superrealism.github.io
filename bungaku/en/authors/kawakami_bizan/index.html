Biography

Born in 1869 in Osaka; his real name was Ryō. At the preparatory school for Tokyo Imperial University he mixed with Ozaki Kōyō and Yamada Bimyō and took part in founding the literary group Ken'yūsha, publishing comic pieces and stories in its journal "Garakuta Bunko" and becoming one of its popular contributors. Known for his flowing, elegant prose and skillful pen, he became a fashionable writer alongside Izumi Kyōka with works of the "ideas novel," which took as their theme the contradictions of society and the workings of human thought, beginning with "Shokikan" ("The Clerk"), published in 1895, and continuing with "Ōsakazuki" ("The Great Sake Cup") and "Uraomote" ("Front and Back"). He later distanced himself from the Ken'yūsha and published ambitious works such as "Kannon Iwa" ("Kannon Rock"), but, tormented by declining fame and creative deadlock, he cut his own throat in 1908 and died at the age of forty.
