What is Shinkoku Shōsetsu (Serious Novel)?

Category
Form / Genre

A term for a body of fiction in the second half of the 1890s, around the same time as the kannen shōsetsu (ideas novel), that stripped away idealization to depict the darkness of human society and the miserable reality of the lower classes with unflinching candor. Also called "higeki shōsetsu" ("tragic novel"), the two terms are often used synonymously or as a pair. Hirotsu Ryūrō, a writer of the Ken'yūsha school, is its representative figure, and he left a work standing at the peak of the serious novel in "Imado Shinjū" ("Love Suicide at Imado," July 1896, in "Bungei Kurabu"), depicting the doomed love and death of a courtesan in the Yoshiwara pleasure quarters through realist prose. It overlapped with the current of Izumi Kyōka and Kawakami Bizan's ideas novel; the writers of the Ken'yūsha school shared the feature of stepping beyond the popular, tastefully mixed classical-colloquial style of romance to hint, against the backdrop of social unease after the First Sino-Japanese War, at sympathy for the weak and criticism of society. This stance of stripping away idealization to stare straight at reality is also positioned as one of the forerunning elements of the Naturalist literature that followed.
