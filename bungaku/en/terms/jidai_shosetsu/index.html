What is Jidai Shōsetsu (Period Fiction)?

Category
Form / Genre

A general term for entertaining reading matter set in a past era, chiefly the Edo period, that freely develops fictional characters and stories. It stands in contrast to historical fiction, which places its weight on the interpretation of historical fact, and it has been at the core of popular fiction. Nakazato Kaizan's "Daibosatsu Tōge" ("The Great Bodhisattva Pass"), which began serialization in the "Miyako Shimbun" in 1913, is regarded as its forerunner, and in 1917 Okamoto Kidō serialized the tales of an Edo-period informant's investigations, "Hanshichi Torimonochō," in "Bungei Kurabu," opening a distinctively Japanese form, the "torimonochō" (detective casebook), that fused mystery-solving with the flavor of old Edo. This form was carried on by Nomura Kodō's "Zenigata Heiji Torimonokae," begun in 1931, which won nationwide popularity. Yoshikawa Eiji's "Miyamoto Musashi," serialized in the "Asahi Shimbun" from 1935, became a representative work of period fiction with its portrait of a swordsman on a spiritual quest, while Yamamoto Shūgorō refined the genre of human-interest period fiction by depicting the joys and sorrows of ordinary townspeople. This lineage leads directly into the flourishing of postwar period fiction.
