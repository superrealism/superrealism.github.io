What is Buraiha?

Category
Literary Movement

Writers who, immediately after Japan's defeat in the Pacific War, lived out in their own persons a rebellion against established morality and the authority of the literary establishment. Also called the neo-gesaku school. Dazai Osamu left behind "No Longer Human" (1948), a stark portrait of a self-destructive life, and soon after drowned himself; Sakaguchi Ango shocked postwar literature with the essay "Darakuron" ("On Decadence," 1946), which affirmatively argued for the collapse of existing morality, and with the novel "Sakura no Mori no Mankai no Shita" ("Beneath the Full Cherry Blossoms"), which rendered legendary beauty in grotesque form. Oda Sakunosuke, too, known for "Fūfu Zenzai" ("Meoto Zenzai"), which portrayed the life of ordinary Osaka people in a light, buoyant style, is counted in this line. Amid the ruin of war and the collapse of values, their stance — mocking established order and hypocrisy, and seeking the truth of human beings precisely amid decadence and self-mockery — is also seen as having pushed the confessional I-novel tradition, dating from Naturalism, to its furthest extreme.
