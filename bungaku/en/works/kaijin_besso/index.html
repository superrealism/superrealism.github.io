About this work

A play Izumi Kyōka published in Chūō Kōron in 1914, counted with The Demon Pond and The Castle Tower among his three great fantasy plays. Set in the Rōkan palace on the sea floor, it draws a beautiful woman offered as a human sacrifice in exchange for treasure, coming as a bride to the young prince who is lord of the sea. Beauty and love not of this world, and the space between human beings and the other world, sung in gorgeous diction: a one-act piece that is the furthest point of Kyōka's fantastic beauty.

Summary

Deep beneath the sea, in the solemn, indigo-blue light of the Rōkan Palace. Its ruler is a beautiful yet fierce young lord — master of the sea — his black hair swept back over his shoulders. Tonight, a beautiful woman is to be brought to him as his bride. Her father, coveting the treasures of the sea god, had sworn to offer up his daughter, and having received immense treasure from the young lord as her "bride-price," had, true to his vow, sunk her beneath the waves and sent her down to the sea floor. Attended by the aged Bishop of the Offing and her waiting-women, the beautiful woman, her hair dressed in a shimada coiffure and wearing a long-sleeved kimono, comes face to face with the young lord. The woman believes herself dead, drowned in the sea, but the lord tells her: you have not died — you have become the consort of this Rōkan Palace. It is a world of otherworldly beauty and love, beyond all measure by the reasoning of those who live on land. When, before long, the woman wishes to show her glorious new form to the people of the land above, the lord reveals to her that, to human eyes, she appears as nothing but a monstrous great serpent, and makes her understand that the two of them can never return to the human world. "In the paradise where men go, there are no women. In the paradise where women go, no man may enter" — and as the curtain falls, the young lord embraces the beautiful woman, his dragon claws laid upon her. A one-act play in which Kyōka sings out, in gorgeous, fantastical verse, the splendor of beauty and love blooming in the borderland between the human world and the world beyond.

Characters

  • The Young Lord　The beautiful yet fierce master of the sea, ruler of the undersea Rōkan Palace. Takes as his consort the beautiful woman sunk as a human sacrifice
  • The Beautiful Woman　A girl sunk beneath the waves by her father in exchange for the treasures of the sea. Believes herself dead, but becomes the young lord's consort
  • The Bishop of the Offing　An aged sea-monk. Attends the young lord and oversees the arrangements for the bride's arrival

The opening (original text)

Time. The present day. Place. The Rōkan Palace, on the sea floor. Persons. The Young Lord. The Bishop of the Offing. (An aged sea-monk) The Beautiful Woman. The Doctor. The Matron. Waiting-women. (Seven) Knights of the Black Current. (Many) Within the solemn, indigo-blue Rōkan Palace. A dark shadow — the Bishop of the Offing. Bishop: Ladies of the chamber. Waiting-Woman One: (Emerging from the door, dressed in pale Western clothing) Yes, yes. Why, if it isn't the reverend monk. Bishop: Ah — how striking, how beautiful, and how strange your attire is today. Waiting-Woman One: You flatter me. Whether it is beautiful or not I cannot say, but it is certainly a strange sort of dress. Young Master's long-cherished wish is at last to be fulfilled — tonight the bride is to be brought in.

The ending (original text)

The Young Lord: Ha, ha! As if I could bear to be lumped in with such a place. Listen — in the paradise where women go, no man may enter. (Beginning to loosen the cords of his armor, slowly, in time with the music, standing at a slight angle, he lays his dragon claws upon the beautiful woman's back. Her snow-white sleeves show faintly at the edge of his purple scales) In the paradise where men go, there are no women. — Curtain —
