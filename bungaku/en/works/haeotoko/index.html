About this work

A long detective novel Unno Jūza serialized in Kōdan Zasshi in 1937. A central work featuring the detective Homura Sōroku, it is set in Osaka and draws a run of murders by a villain called the Fly Man, who leaves behind the corpses of flies with their legs and wings pulled off, and the secret of bodily reconstruction hidden behind them. With machine guns and detachable artificial limbs flying about, it is a wildly inventive piece characteristic of a pioneer of the scientific detective story.

Summary

At the mansion of the eccentric Dr. Kamoshita, in Osaka's Sumiyoshi Ward, a half-burned corpse is found in the fireplace flue. The young detective Homura Sōroku, joining the investigation, is shot and wounded inside the mansion by someone's machine gun. Before long, a threatening letter arrives for Tamaya Sōichirō, a wealthy man who made his fortune manufacturing radio receivers — enclosed with it, the corpse of a fly with its limbs torn off and its belly cut open, signed "the Fly Man." Tamaya barricades himself in but is killed all the same, and next the former chief prosecutor, Shioda, likewise loses his life. From the testimony of Kaoru, Dr. Kamoshita's orphaned daughter, Homura sees through the truth: that the man calling himself "Dr. Kamoshita" is none other than the disguised Fly Man, and that the body burned in the fireplace was the true doctor himself. The diary left behind by the real doctor reveals a truth to make one's blood run cold — the Fly Man's true identity is Norimoto Senshirō, a man once condemned to death for a murder committed in the South Seas. The doctor, seeking to confirm his own theory that removing unneeded limbs and organs would leave a surplus of brain power sufficient to create a genius, had revived Norimoto's executed corpse, and, cutting down his limbs and viscera, remade him into a "miniaturized human." Endowed with a superhuman brain and detachable prosthetic arms and legs, the Fly Man set out on a course of revenge against Kamoshita, who had made him what he was; against Tamaya, who had brought about his death sentence; and against Shioda, who had cut off his hope of a life sentence. Rampaging with planted bombs and more, the Fly Man is pursued alone by Homura, who, together with his mistress Oryū, is trapped in a deadly snare at the sand bath of Tennōji — but he turns the tables at the last moment, and seizes the Fly Man, dragging him down into a pool of boiling water. The Fly Man bites through his own tongue and dies. After the case, Homura marries Itoko, Tamaya's daughter, whom he came to know through the affair, and sets off for Tokyo.

Characters

  • Homura Sōroku　A young detective. Pursues the Fly Man with wit and courage, the author's representative sleuth
  • Fly Man　A monstrous figure who leaves behind the corpses of flies with their limbs torn off. His true identity is Norimoto Senshirō, a former death-row convict remade into a miniaturized human
  • Dr. Kamoshita　An eccentric scientist of Sumiyoshi Ward. Creates the Fly Man through his experiments in remodeling the human body, and is killed by his own creation's hand
  • Tamaya Sōichirō　A wealthy man who made his fortune in radio manufacturing. Killed after receiving the Fly Man's threats
  • Itoko　Tamaya Sōichirō's daughter. Comes to be united with Homura through the case
  • Oryū　The Fly Man's mistress. Disguises herself as a household helper and others to aid the Fly Man
  • Prosecutor Muramatsu and Police Chief Masaki　Members of the Osaka investigation team who cooperate with Homura

The opening (original text)

The mysterious figure known as the "Fly Man" had, it turned out, been breathing the very same air as the rest of us for some time before the matter ever came to light. It was only that none of us had ever dreamed, even in our wildest fancies, that so grotesque a creature could be living so close at hand. We flatter ourselves that our eyes and ears are sharp enough to daily life that there is nothing we do not know — not one back alley of the city's hidden lanes, not even the corpse of a rat that has lain three days beneath a gutter board — and yet the world is wide, and deep, and no end of astonishing things lie quietly buried within it, known to no one at all.

Haeotoko by Unno Jūza (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The bell fell suddenly silent. The train was about to depart at last. Those who had come to see them off raised both hands, as though by some unspoken agreement, and cried out banzai for the new life the two of them were setting out upon. "Detective Homura, banzai!" "The bride, Itoko-san, banzai!" Catching, just for an instant, the eye of Homura Sōroku, now her husband, the new bride Itoko flushed red with a shy blush. Seeing it, the well-wishers broke, once more, into a great burst of lively applause. The train, laden with a happiness beyond all measure, began, little by little, to move eastward.

Haeotoko by Unno Jūza (Aozora Bunko)
