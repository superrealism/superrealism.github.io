What is Ukiyo-zōshi?

Category
Form / Genre

Entertaining fiction depicting the manners and people of its own time, produced in the Kamigata region from the early to the mid-Edo period. It began in 1682, when the haikai poet Ihara Saikaku published "Kōshoku Ichidai Otoko" ("The Life of an Amorous Man"), depicting the life of a man devoted to the way of love, and it remained popular for roughly the next eighty years. "Ukiyo" means "the present age," and its name reflects a stance devoted to depicting the world as it is now lived, and the people who live in it. What was new was its frank depiction, freed from the moralizing of kanazōshi, of the daily life, money, and love of townspeople, and, beside the amorous ukiyo-zōshi set in the pleasure quarters, Saikaku also left samurai stories treating the pride of the warrior and townsman stories depicting a talent for making money, "Nihon Eitaigura" and "Seken Munazan'yō." After Saikaku's death, the Kyoto publisher Hachimonjiya took the lead, and works by writers such as Ejima Kiseki, sold under the publisher's name as "Hachimonjiya-bon," won popularity, but they drifted into formulaic repetition and declined. Before long the center of publishing shifted to Edo, and the age of the yomihon and gesaku began. Saikaku's spirit of realism, observing human desire with detachment, was rediscovered by writers of pseudo-classicism such as Ozaki Kōyō in the 1890s, and, together with the mixed classical-colloquial style, was carried into the modern novel.
