About this work

The short story that made Yamamoto Shūgorō's name in literary circles, published in Bungei Shunjū in 1926; Kikuchi Kan's notice of it opened his way as a writer. Seizō, a young man wounded in spirit, goes to stay with a friend at Suma, forms a faint attachment to Yasuko, his friend's sister-in-law, and falls in those few days into a still deeper sense of loss. A novel of youth full of lyricism and sentiment, quite unlike the historical novelist he later became, in which the autumn scenery around Sumadera answers the young man's inner state.

Summary

Seizō, who has taken a severe blow in his spirits, comes to Suma at the invitation of his friend Aoki. Aoki was keeping the house of his elder brother, who had been posted to the branch office in America, together with the brother's wife. In the autumn at Suma, Seizō tries to find comfort in the shade of his friend's quiet life. In the clear air of a place held between the sea and the hills, the vermilion gate of the Sumadera, the peak of Mount Tekkai, Seizō comes upon the secret shadow in Yasuko, his friend's sister-in-law. Having sent her husband beyond the sea and keeping the house alone while she is still young, Yasuko is by turns cold to Seizō and mysteriously familiar. Her question under the vermilion gate — "Do you know what the purpose of living is?" — pierces the breast of a man who has no answer. In the very place to which he came for healing, Seizō is made to know his own emptiness instead; and on a rainy morning, looking from the upstairs window at the hazy hills, he sheds tears he cannot hold back.

Characters

  • Seizō　A young man who has been hurt in his spirits and comes to Suma to lean on a friend
  • Aoki　Seizō's friend, who keeps his brother's house with his brother's wife
  • Yasuko　Aoki's sister-in-law, who has sent her husband to America and keeps the house while still young

The opening (original text)

I Seizō came to Suma at Aoki's invitation. Aoki was keeping the house of his elder brother, who was away on a posting to the branch office in America, and lived near the Sumadera with the brother's wife; and Seizō, who had taken a rather severe blow in his spirits, sought comfort in the shade of that quiet life of his friend's. Suma was in autumn.

Sumadera fukin by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Feeling that he wanted to breathe the cold clean air deep into his chest, Seizō went upstairs and opened the north window; the Sumadera and the peak of Mount Tekkai were coldly hazy in the rain. "Do you know what the purpose of living is?" Seizō remembered Yasuko's words under the vermilion gate. He saw too the alluring figure of the woman as she looked at him sidelong while opening her umbrella. Before Seizō's eyes the hills and the woods faded away. Tears ran down his cheeks one after another.
