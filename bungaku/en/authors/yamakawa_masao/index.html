Biography

Born in Shitaya, Tokyo, in 1930. He graduated from the French literature department of Keio University and, while editing "Mita Bungaku," helped launch the careers of writers such as Sono Ayako and Etō Jun. He then turned to writing himself, publishing short stories that stared unflinchingly at the absurdity of death and the postwar generation's youth, and was known as a masterful writer of tightly crafted short-short fiction. He left many stories, including "Natsu no Sōretsu" ("The Summer Funeral Procession") and "Yokan" ("Premonition"), that vividly captured, in spare prose, the irony and dread lurking beneath everyday life. "Natsu no Sōretsu" has long been included in Japanese-language school textbooks. He died suddenly in 1965, struck by a truck on the national highway in Ninomiya, at the age of 34.
