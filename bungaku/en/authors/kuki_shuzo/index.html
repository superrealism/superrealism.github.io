Biography

Born in Tokyo in 1888, the fourth son of Baron Kuki Ryūichi. His mother, Hatsuko, had been close to Okakura Tenshin. After studying philosophy at Tokyo Imperial University, he spent eight years in Europe, where he became close with Rickert, Bergson, Heidegger, and Sartre. On his return he became a professor at Kyoto Imperial University. In "'Iki' no Kōzō" ("The Structure of Iki," 1930), conceived during his years abroad, he gave a phenomenological analysis of the aesthetic of "iki" cultivated in the pleasure quarters of Edo, breaking it down into the three elements of coquetry, pride, and resignation, and the work became a classic of Japanese cultural theory. He also wrote "Gūzensei no Mondai" ("The Problem of Contingency"), among other works. He died in 1941 at the age of 53.
