About this work

A Boy Detectives story Ranpo serialized in the magazine Shōnen in 1951. Around a strange affair in which an invisible villain troubles the public, the great detective Akechi Kogorō, young Kobayashi and the Boy Detectives face their old enemy the Fiend with Twenty Faces. An entry in the series in which the fiend is captured, leading on to The Fiend with Forty Faces.

Summary

On a Sunday at the start of spring, two sixth-graders, Shimada and Kinoshita, lose their way on the road home from their teacher's house and wander into a strange, unfamiliar town, where they meet with the most frightening experience of their young lives — and so begins the strange affair surrounding the invisible "transparent phantom." Beginning with an uncanny incident involving a wax figure, a phantom who appears and vanishes like an invisible man stirs up the whole city. His true identity proves to be none other than the great detective Akechi Kogorō's archenemy, the master of disguise and illusion, the Fiend with Twenty Faces. Akechi, young Kobayashi, and the Boy Detectives Club see through the transparent phantom's trickery and close in on him step by step. In the story's final scene, ten people — the section chief, the deputy chief, detectives, young Kobayashi, and members of the Boy Detectives Club — converge upon the "false Akechi" bound to a chair, whose true identity has now been exposed as the Fiend. Even so consummate a magician can do nothing once cornered like this, and lies there like a dead man, his face gone pale, greasy sweat streaming down. And so, at last, the great affair of the transparent phantom, which had so stirred up the whole world, comes to its close. An entry in the series in which the captured Fiend with Twenty Faces later reappears under the new name of the "Forty-Faced Fiend."

The opening (original text)

Never, in all their lives, had the two boys had so frightening an experience. It was a Sunday at the start of spring. Shimada and Kinoshita, both sixth-graders, had gone to visit their teacher's house to play, and after hearing all sorts of interesting stories, at last left the teacher's house as evening came on. What follows happened on their way home.

Tōmei kaijin by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

And so, at last, the curtain fell on the great affair of the transparent phantom, which had so stirred up the whole world. Needless to say, this only raised higher still the reputation of the great detective Akechi Kogorō and his fine assistant, young Kobayashi. For a good while afterward, wherever one went, all anyone could talk about was the tale of the two of them's great feat.
