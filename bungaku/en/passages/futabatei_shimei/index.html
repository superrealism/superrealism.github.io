Famous passages by Futabatei Shimei

Well-known lines from the works of Futabatei Shimei.

3 passages

Drifting Clouds

It was around three in the afternoon of the twenty-eighth, with the tenth month of the old calendar down to its last two days, when out from Kanda Mitsuke there came swarming and seething, like ants smeared over a surface or a scattering brood of baby spiders, a crowd of gentlemen all mindful of their whiskers.

Although this novel is regarded as the pioneer of genbun'itchi prose, its opening is in the celebrated mixed classical-colloquial cadence inherited from Edo gesaku. Likening office workers leaving at the end of the day to ants or a brood of baby spiders, it paints a vivid picture of Meiji Tokyo.

The Tryst

I was sitting in a birch-wood in autumn, about the middle of September.

The opening of Futabatei Shimei's translation of a story from Turgenev's A Sportsman's Sketches. Rendering the shifting moods of nature in fine detail in the new colloquial style, the translation had a great influence on the nature writing of Meiji authors, beginning with Kunikida Doppo's Musashino.

Heibon

I turn thirty-nine this year. If fifty is the going rate for a human life, I do not expect to be in my grave tomorrow or the day after, but even so, the future, however long it looks, is short.

A colloquial opening that begins to tell the story of an ordinary half-life. It conveys the free, easy narrative voice that Shimei, who staked his career on genbun'itchi prose, arrived at in his later years.
