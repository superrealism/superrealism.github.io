Romeo and Juliet

O Romeo, Romeo! wherefore art thou Romeo? Deny thy father and refuse thy name; Or, if thou wilt not, be but sworn my love, And I'll no longer be a Capulet.

Juliet's speech in Act Two, the balcony scene, as she reveals her heart alone. The famous lines that distill the tragedy of love between enemy houses into a single point — a name — can be savored here in Tsubouchi Shōyō's old-fashioned translation.

What's in a name? that which we call a rose By any other name would smell as sweet;

The continuation of the same scene. Declaring that a name does not change the nature of a thing, Juliet expresses, through the memorable image of the rose, her wish to be united with Romeo even if he must cast off his family's name.
