What is Shinshinri-shugi (New Psychologist School)?

Category
Literary Movement

A current of Modernist literature in the early Shōwa era that, under the influence of Western avant-garde writers such as France's Proust and Ireland's Joyce, sought to depict with precision the human interior and the stream of consciousness. The critic and writer Itō Sei was its theoretical leader, translating Joyce's "Ulysses" while setting out this position in the essay "Shinshinri-shugi Bungaku" ("New Psychologist Literature," 1932). Hori Tatsuo, who had learned from French psychological fiction as well as from Rilke and Radiguet, left a representative achievement of this current, depicting life and death, love and loss, from the interior in a transparent style — "Seikazoku" ("The Holy Family"), depicting the collapse of a family; "Utsukushii Mura" ("The Beautiful Village"), set in Karuizawa; and "Kaze Tachinu" ("The Wind Has Risen"), contemplating his fiancée's death. Yokomitsu Riichi, who had started out with the New Sensationist school, also drew close to this current in "Kikai" ("Machine," 1930), an experimental work of psychological depiction incorporating the stream of consciousness. Where the New Sensationist school championed an innovation in sensory expression, the New Psychologist school is distinguished by turning toward a precise analysis of human consciousness and psychology.
