Biography

Born in 1885 in Marugame, Kagawa Prefecture, the eldest son of an army officer. Expelled from the Nagoya Army Local Cadet School, he went to Tokyo and studied French at the Tokyo School of Foreign Languages. Drawn to socialism under the influence of Kōtoku Shūsui and Sakai Toshihiko, he was repeatedly imprisoned while, through the magazine "Kindai Shisō" ("Modern Thought") and others, he expounded a distinctive brand of anarchism grounded in individual freedom and the liberation of the self. Known also for his gift for languages, largely self-taught in several tongues, he left a translation of Fabre's "Souvenirs Entomologiques" ("The Insect World") along with writings such as "Jijoden" ("Autobiography") and "Dorei Konjōron" ("On the Slave Mentality"). In 1923, amid the chaos of the Great Kantō Earthquake, he was taken away by military police and murdered together with Itō Noe, at the age of 38.
