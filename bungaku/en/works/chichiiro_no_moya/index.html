About this work

A short story published in Shinchō in 1926. In the worst heat in forty years, a man on the run after striking a great landowner with an oak bar slips onto a train for Shimonoseki. He is a hardened lone wolf, able to disguise himself and to spot a tail at will. But in the same third-class carriage he comes across a boy of seventeen or eighteen sleeping in a sash slung from the luggage rack, and cannot make the least impression on him. The boy is in the middle of a labour dispute, and has boarded with his fellows knowing that each of them has a detective attached. A piece in which organized young people appear, before a man who knows only private revenge, as a light he cannot understand at all.

Summary

It was a summer the weather bureau declared the hottest in forty years. 'He' sat in the third-class waiting room of a station, sizing up the plainclothes detectives one by one — reading them by their shoes, their spectacles, the cloth of their trousers. In a single instant, lost in the crowd at the ticket gate, he could become someone else — pulling on a straw hat, fixing on a false mustache, throwing on a summer haori. Disguise and flight were as much a part of his trade as anything else. Every newspaper called him the 'cudgel bandit.' It was 'he' who had smashed in the skull of Honda Tomijirō — the shrewdest operator in the whole region, a great landowner who had sent one leading member of the tenant farmers' union after another off to prison — with an oak door-bolt. A search that mobilized both the young men's association and the fire brigade had turned up not so much as a footprint of evidence. But then, coming back to his seat after drinking a beer in the dining car, he found that the basket he had left behind had been stepped on by a boy's foot. 'Ah — is this yours?' The boy, seventeen or eighteen, spoke to him as though they were of an age, forty-year-old man though he was, and told him without hesitation that there were twenty spies riding this very car — one assigned to each of them. He was on his way, he said, to a labor dispute, and 'he' simply could not read the light in that boy's eyes, try as he might. At the station in the dead of night, forty or fifty police officers waited. Without so much as a rope to bind them, a crowd of thirty or forty were shoved, cursing, toward the parcel exit. In the very middle of that churning crowd, the boy leapt up onto the delivery platform and shouted: 'Comrades! Break through...' Not run, but break through. As the train pulled away, 'his' mind was no longer on his own next job, but filled entirely with thoughts of that boy.

Characters

  • He　A forty-year-old man on the run after attacking a great landowner. Has taken his revenge on the authorities single-handed, relying on disguise and instinct
  • The boy　A laborer of seventeen or eighteen, found sleeping in the third-class car. On his way to a labor dispute, shadowed by police along with his comrades
  • Honda Tomijirō　A great landowner and power broker in prefectural politics. After crushing the tenant farmers' union, he is attacked in his sleep with an oak door-bolt

The opening (original text)

It was the hottest summer in forty years, the Central Meteorological Observatory announced. Through that once-in-forty-years heat, the great luminaries of the political world scurried this way and that. In Spain, and in Italy, politics turned to face Napoleon and marched in retreat. Atop Mount Akaishi, a stomach-shaped mass of fat had been hoisted up, bed and all, still lying flat. The times were lively. The newspapers laid it all out, dazzlingly, one item after another.

The ending (original text)

(Incomprehensible kids, that's what they are. They've got eyes different from ours, those boys. Uncanny, unnervingly precocious kids. And yet they carry on so openly about it, as if they were doing something good. I hate to say it, but I just don't understand them. Well — try not to get hurt.) The train went on running. He had forgotten the plan so peculiarly his own — the instinctive, professional scheme to make use of the massed police for his own escape — and before he knew it, his mind had been entirely carried away by that boy.
