About this work

The short story that made Yamakawa Masao's name most widely known, a standard text in middle-school Japanese readers. Stopping on the way back from a business trip at a seaside town, a man is overtaken by memories of the summer he spent there as an evacuated child. A sweet-potato field, a carrier plane, a white dress — and when two funeral processions, more than a decade apart, come together, the past he had been trying to forget stands before him as an inescapable present. The weight of the wound war leaves in one person, compressed into a dozen-odd pages.

Summary

A salaried man on his way back from a business trip, he uses the few hours before returning to Tokyo to get off at the station of a small town on the coast. It is the town where he lived a full three months as an evacuated child at the end of the war. At midday in summer, walking along the railway line, he sees a small funeral procession moving beyond a green field of sweet potatoes, and stops as though turned to stone. Ten and more years ago, in that same place, he had stood watching a funeral procession beside Hiroko, an older girl who had also been evacuated. Hearing that children who went would be given buns, he shouted that it was a race and dashed into the potato field — and at that moment carrier planes came in. Hiroko, in a pure white dress, ran toward him to help him where he stood frozen with terror. Conspicuous in her white dress, he pushed her away with all his strength. Ever since, out of a single wish not to touch that wound, he has avoided this town. Today he had meant to seal that memory into the past at last and go home lightened. But when he asks some children about the procession in front of him, he is given an answer he never expected. In the coffin lies a mother who lost her only daughter to the strafing in this field, and had been out of her mind ever since. The two deaths, he understands, can be buried nowhere but in himself. And without following the procession he turns back along the road to the station.

Characters

  • He　A salaried man who spent a summer in a seaside town as an evacuated child
  • Hiroko　A girl two years his senior, evacuated with him. She wore a white dress
  • The woman　The mother borne in the funeral procession, who lost her daughter to the strafing

The opening (original text)

Getting off at the station of a small town on the coast, he stood for a while looking about him with curiosity. The scene in front of the station had changed completely. There was a bright market-like street with an arcade over it, and the road too had been hard-paved. He suddenly remembered vividly the boy he had been, made to run to school barefoot along this road full of gravel. That had been at the end of the war.

The ending (original text)

Presently he turned slowly toward the station. The wind stirred, and there was the smell of potato leaves. The clear sky was blue, and the sun was as dazzling as ever. The sound of the sea came back into his ears. A train ran along the line with the monotonous noise of its wheels. It came to him suddenly that in some other time than this, in some other summer in the future perhaps, he would be looking at this same scene and hearing these same sounds again. Thinking it, he was walking along the road beneath the arcade. The knowledge that there was no longer anywhere to run to made his step remarkably firm.
