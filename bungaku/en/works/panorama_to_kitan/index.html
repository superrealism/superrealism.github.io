About this work

A novel serialized in the magazine Shin Seinen from 1926 into the following year. Hitomi Hirosuke, a failed literary young man, seizes on the sudden death of the millionaire Komoda Genzaburō, who is his exact double, to stage a return from the grave and take his place and his fortune. With that money behind him he builds on an island offshore the gorgeous, uncanny paradise of his own imagining, Panorama Island. An abnormal fixation on beauty, and beneath it the fear that the crime supporting it will come out, drawn in Ranpo's own strange and splendid imagery. Aestheticism, the grotesque and detective interest run together in one of his central romances.

Summary

Hitomi Hirosuke, a poor literary young man whom the world has no use for, remembers that Komoda Genzaburō, a schoolmate of his student days and heir to a great fortune, is his exact double. When he learns that Genzaburō has died suddenly of a seizure, Hirosuke puts a daring plan into action: he makes it appear that he himself has died, disappears, opens the grave in which Genzaburō has been buried, and, pretending to have revived from a trance, takes his place as head of the Komoda house. Having stepped so neatly into a millionaire's life, Hirosuke deceives the eyes of Chiyoko, the wife who wonders at the change in her husband, and pours the money he now has without limit into the dream he has carried for years — to build on an island offshore an artificial paradise of a splendor and strangeness not of this world: Panorama Island. A tunnel beneath the sea, hills buried in flowers, a hot pool where naked women gather, a great hall that might be mistaken for the sky. But in the shadow of that half-mad paradise the secret of the imposture begins to come apart. As the detective Kitami Kogorō closes in on the truth, Hirosuke, driven into a corner, sends a huge firework up into the sky of the country he has made, and in its light destroys himself. His flesh and blood come down like rain over every corner of the paradise he had dreamed. Madness for beauty and crime cross in what is the high point of Ranpo's fantastic writing.

Characters

  • Hitomi Hirosuke　A poor literary young man who passes himself off as a millionaire
  • Komoda Genzaburō　The young millionaire who is Hirosuke's exact double
  • Chiyoko　Genzaburō's wife
  • Kitami Kogorō　The detective who follows the truth

The opening (original text)

Even among those who live in the same M Prefecture, most may never have noticed it. At the southern tip of S District, where I Bay opens toward the Pacific, set well apart from the other islands, there floats a small island, less than two ri across, that looks exactly like a green dumpling laid face down. It is as good as uninhabited now; fishermen from nearby land on it now and then out of whim, and hardly anyone else gives it a thought.

The ending (original text)

Kitami Kogorō, wondering at the naked women who somehow did not cry out in the midst of such a bloody scene, did not move either, but rested his head against the edge of the pool and stared vacantly at the bright red cut of the wrist that floated near his breast, opened like a flower. And so it was that the body of Hitomi Hirosuke was broken to fragments along with the firework, and came down as a rain of blood and flesh over every corner of every scene of the Panorama country he had created.
