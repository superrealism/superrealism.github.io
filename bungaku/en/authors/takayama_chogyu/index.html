Biography

Born in 1871 in Tsuruoka, Shōnai, Dewa Province (now Tsuruoka, Yamagata Prefecture); his real name was Rinjirō. He went from the Second Higher School to study philosophy at Tokyo Imperial University, and while still a student anonymously submitted the historical novel "Takiguchi Nyūdō," drawn from the "Yokobue" episode of "The Tale of the Heike," to a Yomiuri Shimbun fiction contest, winning first prize and instant literary fame. After graduating he wrote vigorously as chief editor of the magazine "Taiyō" ("The Sun"), first championing Japanism and becoming a leading voice of the nationalism that followed the First Sino-Japanese War, but he went on to show a sharp critical eye, among other things praising Higuchi Ichiyō's "Takekurabe" ahead of most others, and his thought deepened increasingly toward the individual's inner life. In his later years he fell deeply under the spell of Nietzsche, and his essay "Biteki Seikatsu o Ronzu" ("On the Aesthetic Life"), which placed supreme value on the satisfaction of instinct, sparked a public debate on the aesthetic life with Mori Ōgai and others, before he turned finally to devotion to Nichiren. Celebrated as a critic and thinker representing Meiji Romanticism, he exerted great influence, but died of pulmonary tuberculosis in 1902 at the young age of 31. He was a lecturer at Tokyo University and held a doctorate in literature.
