About this work

A prose-poem-like short piece by Akutagawa on the subject of the old tale of Kachi-kachi Mountain. In the half-light of the age of nursery tales, an old man and a hare mourn the murdered old woman and set out for revenge on the badger — the familiar vendetta drawn in a fantastical hand, as a symbol of a struggle in which beast destroys beast.

Summary

In the half-light of the age of fairy tales, an old man and a hare, listening to the faint sound of the tongue-cut sparrow's wings, quietly lament the death of the old man's wife. Far off is the sound of the waves of the dream sea that leads to Ogre Island, waves that will never break down. Over the earth in which the wife's body is buried, a cherry tree without blossoms stretches thin branches into the sky. Before long the hare, comforting the old man, points to the two boats, one white and one black, tied up at the shore — the boats of that revenge, in which the badger is set aboard the boat of mud and sunk in the middle of the lake. When the vengeance is accomplished, on the flowerless cherry tree blossoms like shells come out, and a pale golden sun climbs into the sky. "It is meant, perhaps, to be a symbol of man rejoicing at the dawn of the age of fairy tales — rejoicing in the struggle in which brutality destroys brutality." A short piece of Akutagawa's, sublimating the cruelty of an old tale into a fantastic symbolist poem.

The opening (original text)

In the half-light of the age of fairy tales, an old man and a hare, listening to the faint sound of the tongue-cut sparrow's wings, are quietly lamenting the death of the old man's wife. What is making that languid sound far off must be the waves of the dream sea that leads to Ogre Island, waves that will never break down. Over the earth in which the body of the old man's wife is buried, a cherry tree without blossoms stretches thin bronze branches out finely into the sky.

Kachikachiyama by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Behold. And with it, on the cherry tree without blossoms, flowers like shells came out. And in the sky, brimming with the half-transparent light of dawn, a pale golden sun climbed up. It is meant, perhaps, to be a symbol of man rejoicing at the dawn of the age of fairy tales — rejoicing in the struggle in which brutality destroys brutality: the sun, and beneath it the cherry blossoms like inlaid work.

Kachikachiyama by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)
