Biography

Born in 1879 to a well-to-do family in Honami Village, Wake District, Okayama Prefecture (now Bizen). He went to Tokyo and studied at Tokyo Semmon Gakkō (now Waseda University), where he was baptized as a Christian for a time under the influence of Uchimura Kanzō and Uemura Masahisa. After joining the Yomiuri Shimbun, where he wrote vigorously on theater and art, he was recognized as a leading Naturalist writer with the 1908 novel "Doko e" ("Whither?"). Marked by a cold, unflinching view of life as futility and ennui, his work depicted the bleak reality of provincial families and human existence in works such as "Irie no Hotori" ("By the Inlet") and "Doroningyō" ("The Clay Doll"). He was a novelist and, at the same time, the foremost literary critic of his day, celebrated for sharp, unsparing criticism, and he kept writing vigorously until his final years. He died in 1962 at the age of 83.
