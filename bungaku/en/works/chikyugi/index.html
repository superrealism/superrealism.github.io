About this work

An early short story by Makino Shin'ichi, published in "Bungei Shunjū" in 1923. Returning to the family house in Odawara for the seventeenth-anniversary memorial service for his grandfather, "I" finds in a corner of the closet a tall old box holding a globe, which his mother treats as so much clutter. It was bought long ago by the grandfather to show the family where his eldest son — "my" father — was living, after he crossed to America and never came back. A father still sunk in dissipation, a mother resolved to carry the household alone, and an "I" who knows his own uselessness and hollow bravado and can do nothing about either: the tilting of a house over three generations, reflected in one worn globe.

Summary

Summoned by a letter from his mother saying he must come home for the seventeenth-anniversary service for his grandfather, "I" returns to the house in Odawara for the first time in two months. When he asks after his father, his mother, tidying the closet, says only that he "gets worse and worse." The father's dissipation has not stopped; he has left the house with orders that "I" is not to be given another sen. Sneering about it changes nothing — it only makes his own uselessness and hollow cheer feel uglier. His mother points to the bulky box in the corner of the closet, some three feet long, that holds the globe, and complains that it is in the way. Seeing it, "I" remembers that not long ago he had begun a story with that globe for material. — Almost every evening the grandfather would gather the family and set the globe before his knees. Japan was easy to find, being red. The place his mother's finger came to rest on was "Hayerhaven," the town where the father whose face "I" knew only from a photograph was living. "Say what you like, I cannot believe this world of ours is round," the grandfather would laugh. Alone where no one could see, the young "I" would spin the ball, muttering "turn faster, turn faster," until before he knew it the words had become "come home soon." At the memorial service he is seated in the lowest place as his father's proxy and stammers through a greeting; through the sliding doors he hears the relatives calling him a bully at home, a coward. The next day too his mother finds the globe box in the way, frowning with impatience each time she sees it. "I" nearly says that Eiichi may make a toy of it one day, and swallows the words. Eiichi is his eldest son, born the previous spring.

Characters

  • I (Jun'ichi)　The narrator. Eldest son of the main house in Odawara, without steady work or backbone, who shows his mother nothing but a hollow cheerfulness
  • Mother　Resolved to carry the household alone. She goes on finding the globe in the closet a nuisance
  • Father　He quarreled with the grandfather and crossed to America, and even after coming home has not given up his dissipation. He never appears in the story
  • Grandfather　The head of the house, dead these seventeen years. He bought the globe to show the family where his eldest son was
  • Eiichi　The narrator's eldest son, born last spring

The opening (original text)

A letter from my mother — you must come home, there is to be the seventeenth-anniversary service for your grandfather — brought me back to the house in Odawara for the first time in something like two months. "And how is he these days?" I was asking about my father. "He only gets worse and worse…." My mother said it while tidying the closet. Then, as if to shake the mood off, she went on muttering, "This house is really so cramped that at a time like this one is quite at a loss. To begin with, I have no idea at all what has been put away where."

The Globe by Makino Shin'ichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"But a thing like this, what good does it do standing there? We might as well…" My mother went on grumbling with a frown. — One of these days Eiichi may make a toy of it — I was within a breath of saying so, but that "fairy tale" came back to me all at once, and with the feeling of tickling myself I swallowed the words where they were. Eiichi is my eldest son, born last spring.
