About this work

A story of Japan's Christians, and a piece of thinking about the remaking power of the country itself. Before Organtino, the padre of the southern-barbarian church, appears the spirit of an old man embodying the ancient gods of Japan, who foretells that the imported teaching of Deus too will in time be absorbed and altered by the soil of this land. Japanese culture's power to remake whatever comes in from outside, drawn as "the smile of the gods," and much noticed as a piece of Akutagawa's writing on civilization and culture.

Summary

Padre Organtino of the Nanbanji, who has come to Japan to preach, walks in the garden of his church on a spring evening, sunk in homesickness and in a sense that nothing he does takes hold. The Japanese are converted easily enough, and yet their faith is somehow not what he wants. As he prays in the garden, in a state between dream and waking, a spirit embodying the old gods of Japan appears to him in the shape of an old man. This country, the old man tells him, is full of "our power": Confucianism and Buddhism alike came over from the continent, and all of them were taken into this soil and made over into something Japanese. Nor will the teaching of Deus be an exception; the power of the gods of this country will change its shape in time. "We are in the trees as well. We are in the shallow streams.... We are everywhere, and always. Be on your guard" — and with that the old man vanishes into the dusk. From the tower the bell rings the Ave Maria; but which will win, the foreign faith or the gods of the soil, is not yet decided.

Characters

  • Organtino　A padre of the Nanbanji, sunk in homesickness and in the sense that his preaching takes no hold
  • The old man (the spirit of the gods of Japan)　Who embodies the power of this soil to absorb, and foretells that the religions brought here will be made over in time
