About this work

A short story published in Shin Seinen in October 1928. In Vladivostok just after the revolution, a former Red Army soldier known as the mad gentleman stops a Japanese officer and begins a strange confession — the truth about his beautiful comrade Lyatnikov, and a story of love after death bound up with a jewel. The unbroken monologue and the fantastical conceit, tied to the tragedy of the Romanovs, make it one of Yumeno Kyūsaku's central stories.

Summary

On the main street of Vladivostok, a drifter whom people call the mad gentleman stops a Japanese officer, takes him into a restaurant and begins to tell him his history. Raised in Moscow and of noble blood, he says, he became a soldier of the Red Army during the revolution and grew close to a beautiful comrade named Lyatnikov. One night Lyatnikov told him the whole story of the shooting of the Emperor Nicholas II and his family, as though he had been present at it, and showed him a handful of jewels. A few days later, on a field where the unit had been wiped out, he found Lyatnikov's body. And it was a woman's — and might it not have been one of the Romanov princesses herself, escaped from the execution? And might not the jeweled earrings set in the ears of the corpse have been a confession of love offered to him after death? The man holds out to the officer the jewels that are the proof of that love beyond the grave; but the officer does not believe him and gets up to go. "W-wait, please" — and the man's cry breaks off with the words: "Her Imperial Highness the Grand Duchess Anastasia...."

Characters

  • I (the mad gentleman)　A former Red Army soldier wandering Vladivostok. The narrator, possessed by a love beyond the grave
  • Lyatnikov　The narrator's comrade in arms, a beautiful soldier who tells him the secret of the Romanovs
