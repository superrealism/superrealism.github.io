About this work

Shimomura Kojin, the author of The Story of Jirō, put the whole of the Analects into plain modern Japanese and added his own notes at the important points. It is arranged so that a reader without any training in classical Chinese can come directly at the living voices of Confucius and his disciples, and the phrase with which each passage opens — the Master said — is easy to take to. A long introduction explaining how the Analects came to be is placed at the head.

Summary

"To know the East, one must know Confucianism. To know Confucianism, one must know Confucius. And to know Confucius, one must know the Analects" — in the opening piece, "For the Reader of the Analects," Shimomura Kojin sets out the intent of this book. How the Analects came to be compiled, how it survived the calamity of the burning of the books, and how it was handed down in the three textual traditions of Qi, Lu, and the old text; the differences between the Neo-Confucian and the Ancient Learning interpretations; and how a reader today ought to read this book — all of this he explains carefully, before the translation of the full twenty books begins. The translated text is unified throughout under the narration "The Master said —," rendered into natural, readily comprehensible Japanese while keeping the concision of the original. From "To learn and, at due times, to practise it" in "Xue Er," through "To know a thing is to know it" in "Wei Zheng," "If in the morning one hears the Way, one may die content in the evening" in "Li Ren," all the way to the final book, "Yao Yue." After each chapter, a note by the translator, marked with a circle, is attached, filling in places where the interpretation of a phrase is divided, or the background to one of Confucius's sayings. The closing chapter reads: "Without knowing the mandate of Heaven, one has no qualification to be a gentleman. Without knowing propriety, one cannot stand firm in the world. Without knowing words, one cannot know other people." A note follows this that draws, in a manner characteristic of a translator who spent long years in the field of education, on the practical application of the teaching.

Characters

  • Confucius (the Master)　The central figure spoken of throughout this book
  • His disciples　Yan Yuan, Zilu, Zigong, Zengzi, and others

The opening (original text)

For the Reader of the Analects To know the East, one must know Confucianism. To know Confucianism, one must know Confucius. And to know Confucius, one must know the Analects. The Analects is, indeed, one of the most precious keys there is to knowing Confucius, and thus Confucianism, and thus the East.
