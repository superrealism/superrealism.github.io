About this work

A fine piece among the group of Akutagawa's stories about Japan's Christians, on the subject of martyrdom. Told in an archaic style suggesting the religious books translated around 1600, it follows the tragedy of Lorenzo, a beautiful boy taken in by a church in Nagasaki. Stripped of everything by one misunderstanding, he makes the truth blaze out in his last instant — and in him Akutagawa's view of art and of life is concentrated: that the worth of human life comes down to an instant of feeling that nothing can replace.

Summary

At the church called Santa Lucia in Nagasaki there was raised an orphan boy of great beauty, Lorenzo. With a face as clean as a jewel, he was loved by everyone, and above all by Simeon, a believer he looked up to as an elder brother; but before long it was rumored that he was on intimate terms with the daughter of an umbrella maker. When the girl was found to be with child, Lorenzo was named as the father; he only denied it stubbornly, offered no defense, and was at last driven from the church, to wander the town as a beggar. Some years later a great fire broke out in Nagasaki, and Lorenzo threw himself into the flames to save the girl's baby, who had been left behind. He brought the child out and took mortal injuries in doing it — and at that moment, beneath the burnt and torn clothing, it appeared that Lorenzo had in fact been a woman. Weeping, the girl confessed that her testimony had been false. While the people looked on, Lorenzo died with a peaceful smile left on the lips. No one had known that life; and yet, says the narrator quietly, it is in such a moment of feeling that the preciousness of human life is complete.

Characters

  • Lorenzo　A beautiful boy raised at the church in Nagasaki. In fact a woman, who dies a martyr without ever clearing her name
  • Simeon　A powerfully built believer who loves Lorenzo like a younger brother
  • The umbrella maker's daughter　Who loves Lorenzo in vain, and ruins him by naming him as the father of her child

The opening (original text)

In times gone by there was, at the ecclesia called Santa Lucia in Nagasaki in Japan, a boy of this country named Lorenzo.

Hōkyōnin no shi by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The preciousness of all human life comes to its height in a moment of feeling that nothing can replace. In the sky of the passions, which one might liken to a night sea, to raise one wave and catch in its spray the light of a moon not yet risen — that, surely, is what makes a life worth living. And so, may not one who knows the last hour of Lorenzo be one who knows the whole life of Lorenzo?

Hōkyōnin no shi by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

Famous passages

All the worth of human life lies, in the end, in a moment of deep emotion that nothing can replace.

The narrator's comment after it is revealed that Lorenzo was in fact a woman. Written in an archaic style modeled on the Christian books printed in early Japan, it voices Akutagawa's view of art: that the value of a life lies in a single moment of emotion.
