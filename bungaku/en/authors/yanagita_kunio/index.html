Biography

Born in Hyōgo Prefecture in 1875; his original surname was Matsuoka. After graduating from the Faculty of Law at Tokyo Imperial University, he traveled the country's farming and mountain villages as a bureaucrat in the Ministry of Agriculture and Commerce, and after leaving government service worked as an editorial writer for the Asahi Shimbun. In 1910 he self-published "Tōno Monogatari" ("The Legends of Tōno"), recording the local legends he heard from Sasaki Kizen of Tōno, Iwate Prefecture, and it became the starting point for folklore studies in Japan. He went on to meticulously survey and record the living culture found in dialects, festivals, and household deities across the country, and through writings such as "Kyōdo Seikatsu no Kenkyūhō" ("Methods for the Study of Local Life") he established the theory and methodology of Japanese folklore studies. From 1935 he led the Minkan Denshō no Kai (later the Folklore Society of Japan), training many younger scholars. He received the Order of Culture in 1951. He died in 1962 at the age of 87. As the recorder of the lives and beliefs of the ordinary people (jōmin) being lost amid modernization, his achievements remain a cornerstone of folklore studies today.
