About this work

A historical novella Yamamoto Shūgorō published in a special number of Sunday Mainichi in 1954, known as the source of Kurosawa Akira's film Sanjuro of 1962. Sugata Heiya, a masterless samurai so hungry that he goes as far as play-acting a suicide, falls in with the young samurai Isaka Jūrōta, who means to put right the corruption of the domain's government, and by an unhurried cunning brings off the rescue of the chief retainer brilliantly. With its easy humour and a protagonist who is a vulgar man with an eye to advancement and who nevertheless shows a conscience at the last, a fine piece representing Shūgorō's brighter side.

Summary

Isaka Jūrōta, a young samurai on the road back to Edo, is hailed by a rōnin going through the motions of seppuku at the roadside. The man, who gives his name as Sugata Hirano, is in truth so famished he is only trying to wheedle the price of a meal out of a passer-by. Learning that Jūrōta is turning back because he has left his purse at his uncle's house, Sugata declares that, having come this far, he may as well see it through, and asks to come along. Along the way he learns that Jūrōta — nephew of the castle keeper Rikuta Seibei — has been driven out to Edo for plotting to purge the corrupt officials, led by the vice-councillor Kurofuji Gendayū, who have seized control of the domain's government, and Sugata tries to talk him into using the chance to win himself a position. But on reaching the castle town they find that the corrupt faction has struck first, abducting Castle Keeper Rikuta and preparing to pin the blame on him. In the crisis, Sugata — belly empty, wits as sharp as ever — volunteers to serve as strategist. Gathering allies, he lures the enemy's search party away to a temple with a false tip-off, then slips into Kurofuji's mansion himself disguised as a beggar, locates where the castle keeper is being held, and sets a fire as a signal — carrying his bloodless scheme through to a brilliant success. But the instant it succeeds, Sugata, ashamed of his own self-serving motives, tries to slip quietly away. Held back by his empty stomach and by Jūrōta, who comes running after him, he covers his embarrassment by claiming he did it all "for the peace of the domain," and, unable to hold back his laughter, bows his head.

Characters

  • Sugata Hirano　A rōnin from Hokuetsu. Down-at-heel in appearance but sharp of wit even while starving. The protagonist
  • Isaka Jūrōta　Nephew of the castle keeper Rikuta Seibei. A young samurai sought as an adopted son-in-law, bent on purging the domain's corrupt officials
  • Rikuta Seibei　The castle keeper. A magnanimous man whose habitual phrase is "peace, day by day." He is abducted by the corrupt faction
  • Chidori　Rikuta's only daughter. Open-hearted and unaffected
  • Kurofuji Gendayū　The vice-councillor who has seized control of the domain's government. Ringleader of the corrupt faction
  • Terada Bunji, Kawahara Gennai, Yasukawa Einosuke, and others　Young samurai, comrades of Jūrōta

The opening (original text)

Isaka Jūrōta was angry. He walked on with his indignation still unspent, so that he did not so much as notice the man's figure, nor hear the voice calling to him at once. It was only at the third call that he became aware of it, and stopped to turn around.

The ending (original text)

"Will you come back with me?" "I suppose there's no help for it," said Sugata Hirano. "—Very well, for the peace of the domain, I'll come back." And before he knew it he had bowed, and looking at Jūrōta, unable to hold back any longer, he said with a laugh, "Much obliged."
