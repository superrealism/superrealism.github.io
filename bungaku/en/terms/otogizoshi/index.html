What is Otogizōshi?

Category
Form / Genre

A general term for anonymous short tales composed from the Muromachi period into the early Edo period. Enjoyed as illustrated manuscripts and woodblock-printed books, they number some four hundred in all. Well-known examples include "Issun Bōshi" ("The One-Inch Boy"), "Urashima Tarō," "Monokusa Tarō," "Hachikazuki," and "Bunshō Sōshi"; unlike the court tales, which took aristocrats as their protagonists, these often feature commoners, animals, and other non-human beings, and many are stories of rising in the world or finding happiness. The name derives from the fact that around the Kyōhō era, the Osaka bookseller Shibukawa Seiemon published twenty-three of these tales as the "Otogi Bunko" ("Companion Library"); the term later came to designate the whole genre of similar tales. Their form, read together with pictures, also connects to the kusazōshi of later Edo. Though sometimes dismissed as outlandish, they candidly record what the common people of the medieval period wished for and feared, and stand at a point linking setsuwa literature to the kanazōshi and ukiyo-zōshi of the early modern period. Many of these tales continue to be read today through modern retellings, such as the "Issun Bōshi" and "Urashima Tarō" Kusuyama Masao rewrote for children in 1921.
