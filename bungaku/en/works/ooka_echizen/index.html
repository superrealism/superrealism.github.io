About this work

Ōoka Tadasuke, handed down as the model magistrate, written by Yoshikawa Eiji shortly after the war. It opens with the lament that he envies dogs, and wonders why he was ever born a man, and asks, against the tangled poverty, greed and obligation of ordinary Edo life, how heavy a thing it is for one person to judge another. The line placed in the judgment scene — that what only a god could rightly judge is shown instead by a human being — states the subject of the whole work.

Summary

"I envy the dogs. Ah, why was I born a human being?" — the story begins from an age in which such words had come to be heard even in jest. The scene is Edo in the Kyōhō era. In the streets are rōnin at the end of their means, drifters, men who buy and sell women, and beneath them all poverty and greed and obligation tangled together. The lawless set called the three men; a man who opens the sliding door and comes in; Ichijūrō, sunk in drink — the days of the people of the streets are drawn one by one with a fine brush. ... Across all this run the judgments of Ōoka Tadasuke, newly come to the office of city magistrate of Edo. What is brought before him is always a suit in which there is reason on both sides and which the law alone cannot divide. "It is the will of the gods. What none but a god should be able to judge, a human being judges in his place. When one thinks of it, it is hard.... We are too human," says Echizen; and someone answers, "And yet, to judge human beings, it may be that only a human being can judge them well." His judgments are talked of as famous judgments; and every time, Echizen himself is brought face to face with the bottomless nature of one man's judging another. At the end of the story a man goes off beyond a crossroads, in what may be the color of the moon or the color of the mist, with the tapping of a stick that holds no sentiment at all.

Characters

  • Ōoka Echizen-no-kami Tadasuke　The city magistrate of Edo, who faces the weight of judging men
  • Tetsuun　The man who goes off from the crossroads leaning on a stick
  • O-Sode　A woman living among the common people

The opening (original text)

Chapter one The three men "I envy the dogs. Ah, why was I born a human being?" It has become no rarity in these years to hear such words, even in jest, among our fellow men.

Ōoka echizen by Yoshikawa Eiji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Wild geese. There, a flock of wild geese has gone over, calling.... Well, I shall take my leave of you here. When I come up from Uji I shall call on you again. And each time, let us come here together and see how the seedlings are growing." And Tetsuun, dragging his lame leg, went off beyond a crossroads in what might be the color of the moon or the color of the mist, with the tapping of a stick that held no sentiment at all.

Ōoka echizen by Yoshikawa Eiji (Aozora Bunko)
