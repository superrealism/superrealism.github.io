What is Miyako Shimbun?

Category
Society / Group

A daily newspaper founded in Tokyo on September 25, 1884, as "Konnichi Shimbun," its predecessor. It set out as Japan's first true evening paper, and, after passing through the name "Miyako Shimbun" in a different orthography, was retitled "Miyako Shimbun." Unlike the great newspapers that traded on political commentary, it devoted its energy to articles on the "arts" — theater, variety-hall performance, and the geisha quarters — building close ties to the everyday life of Tokyo's old downtown and a distinct readership of its own. It also had much to offer in serialized fiction: Nakazato Kaizan's sweeping epic "Daibosatsu Tōge" ("The Great Bodhisattva Pass"), which he kept writing from September 1913, is the paper's representative serial. Tokuda Shūsei's late work "Shukuzu" ("Miniature," 1941), which calmly depicted the life of a geisha, was also serialized in this paper, but it was interrupted after 80 installments under pressure from the wartime Information Bureau, and it remained, unfinished, Shūsei's last novel. In 1942, under the wartime consolidation of newspapers, it merged with "Kokumin Shimbun" to become "Tokyo Shimbun," and its title disappeared.
