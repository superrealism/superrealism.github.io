About this work

A humorous romance Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shin Yomimono in 1948. Following "A Record of Bewitchment," it continues the story of Yaosuke, the jewel-maker's boy from Hida, and is set in Sakai, the one free city in Japan, where Yaosuke uses his ninja arts to give three villains a thorough drubbing. A piece full of laughter and invention.

Summary

The city of Sagai, in the province of Izumi, was the sole free city of medieval Japan. Boasting immense wealth and governed under republican self-rule, it was praised as being "like Venice of southern Europe" — yet even this city would in time be struck down by Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi, reduced to scorched earth in the Siege of Osaka, and end its days as nothing more than the seat of a Tokugawa magistrate's office. Rice rationed to a single gō a day, sake, sweets, and tobacco forbidden — against this bleak backdrop of Sagai, Yaosuke, the boy bead-carver from Hida familiar from "Enyōki," is active once again. Though burdened with a threefold disability — lame, one-eyed, and hunchbacked — Yaosuke, having mastered the deepest mysteries of ninjutsu, delightfully punishes, one after another, the three villains infesting the city. At the story's close, Yaosuke casts an illusion upon the villain Dekiroku, setting him dancing in a frenzy, and pins to his chest, beneath a stone, a paper listing out his crimes. "Dance all you like — your fellow prisoners in the jailhouse will surely applaud you for it." And then: "Well then, let us go home — to our mountains, to the dear, familiar refuge of Fukiya village." At that very moment a genuine inspector has arrived at the magistrate's office and is finishing the job Yaosuke began — while the three villains are still running, running for all they are worth, kicking up clouds of dust. A comic, fantastical tale by Yamamoto, brimming with laughter and whimsical invention.

Characters

  • Yaosuke　A boy with a threefold disability who has mastered the art of ninjutsu
  • Dekiroku　One of the villains who is punished

The opening (original text)

The city of Sagai, in the province of Izumi, was the sole free city of medieval Japan. Father Vilela, who came to Sagai in the fourth year of Eiroku to preach Christianity, records: "—While other cities suffer terrible ruin from the wars, Sagai alone preserves peace, independence, privilege, and freedom, and under a republican government lives in the utmost peace and prosperity. Its condition is much like that of Venice."

San'akunin monogatari by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Dance all you like — your fellow prisoners in the jailhouse will surely applaud you for it," Yaosuke said, turning to go. "Well then, let us go home — to our mountains, to the dear, familiar refuge of Fukiya village." One more detail, for good measure: at that very moment, a genuine inspector had already arrived at the magistrate's office, and, taking over where Yaosuke had left off, was busy finishing the job — while the three villains were still running, running desperately, kicking up clouds of dust... it seemed, indeed, that they meant to go on running forever.
