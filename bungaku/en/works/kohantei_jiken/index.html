About this work

A long novel Ranpo serialized in Sunday Mainichi in 1926. A strange murder in which the body vanished, at a hot-spring inn by a lake in the mountains. It is told in the guise of the narrator, the only person who knows, disclosing the truth of that lakeside case, still unsolved after five years. An early long mystery in which Ranpo gave the narrator a taste for peeping.

Summary

Beside Lake A, deep in the mountains of H, an uncannily strange murder once took place. The case — which the newspapers of the capital sensationalized with headlines like "The Uncanny Incident at Lake A" and "The Missing Corpse" — remains unsolved even now, five years later. Not only the culprit but, strangely enough, even the victim was never clearly identified; the police long ago gave up the case, and the villagers have all but forgotten it. But in the whole world, only two people know the truth of the matter — and "I" am one of them. Possessed of a taste for peeping, "I" had been secretly watching other guests' rooms with the help of a mirror at the hot-spring inn by the lake, the "Lakeside Inn," when I unexpectedly witnessed the entire course of a murder. The death of a man named Sanzō, the vanished corpse, suspicions of counterfeit banknotes, and a pair of lovers, Kōno and Chōkichi — bound by these tangled, complicated circumstances, "I" have long kept my silence. But now that everyone involved has gone their separate ways, the time has finally come when I can tell the truth. And yet, at the very end of this telling, a grave doubt casts its shadow over my own heart: if my own surmise is correct, have I not, without cause, let a detestable murderer go free? A long early mystery by Ranpo built around the conceit of peeping.

Characters

  • I　The narrator, who has a taste for peeping
  • Kōno　A young man involved in the incident

The opening (original text)

Dear readers, do you perhaps remember the uncannily strange murder that occurred some years ago beside Lake A, deep in the mountains of H? Though it took place in a remote mountain village, it was so extraordinary an affair that it was even reported in the newspapers of the capital. One paper ran it under a headline like "The Uncanny Incident at Lake A"; another, with a headline meant to pique curiosity, "The Missing Corpse"; and both gave the case a fairly prominent write-up.

Kohantei jiken by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

If my own surmise is correct, then I have, without cause, let a detestable murderer go free. But even now, this is not yet the time to speak that suspicion openly. Kōno is alive. What is more, he is working abroad in service to his country. Why, for the sake of that foolish man Sanzō, dead these several years now, should I go out of my way to make another victim of him at this late date?

Kohantei jiken by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)
