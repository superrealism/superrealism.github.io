About this work

A detective story for boys that Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shōnen Shōjo Tankai in 1930. Haruta Ryūsuke, a boy detective who is good at baseball, takes up the challenge of a gang of villains calling themselves the Black Cravat Gang. A bracing entry in the series, drawing the wit and courage of Ryūsuke, who has already distinguished himself in the submarine affair.

Summary

The August afternoon sun beat down on the baseball ground of Prefectural Middle School No. X. On the field, the third-year team was in the middle of fierce practice. From the stands, Kuramochi, the teacher in charge of the third-years, asked a colleague, a chemistry teacher, "That catcher has a good arm, and plays such beautiful ball — what's his name?" "Him? Ah, that's Haruta Ryūsuke — the one who won such credit last month in the C.C.D. Submarine Incident." To this Ryūsuke — a fine baseball player and a boy detective besides — comes a challenge in a black envelope, sent by an evil gang calling itself the "Black Cravat Gang." Led by a mixed-race boy with a birthmark on his forehead, the evil hand of the Black Cravat Gang reaches out toward him. Ryūsuke, with his native wit and courage, rises to meet their scheme head-on. At the story's close, with the case resolved, both his father and Viscount Wakabayashi can only let out sighs of admiration. But Ryūsuke, sounding regretful, adds: "Still, it's a shame we let that mixed-race boy get away, Father — he's sure to come back someday to get even..." And then, picking up his uniform, he sets off cheerfully for the baseball ground, to train hard for the coming autumn matches. A rousing episode from the series depicting the exploits of boy detective Ryūsuke.

Characters

  • Kuramochi　The teacher in charge of Ryūsuke's class

The ending (original text)

His father and Viscount Wakabayashi could do nothing but let out sighs of admiration. Before long, Ryūsuke added, sounding regretful: "Still, it's a shame we let that mixed-race boy get away, Father — he's sure to come back again someday to get even..."
