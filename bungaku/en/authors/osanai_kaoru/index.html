Biography

Born in Hiroshima in 1881, the second son of an army surgeon; he lost his father in early childhood and was raised in Tokyo. He was drawn to writing from his student days in the English literature department of Tokyo Imperial University, and in 1909 he founded the Free Theater with Ichikawa Sadanji II, setting out to introduce modern drama to Japan. In 1924 he founded the Tsukiji Little Theater with Hijikata Yoshi, taking charge of directing, translation, and the training of actors alike, and led the Shingeki movement, earning the name "father of the new theater." He also left his mark on fiction, on criticism, and on the film industry in its early days. He died suddenly in 1928 at the age of 47.
