What is Kaizō?

Category
Society / Group

A general-interest magazine founded by Yamamoto Sanehiko's Kaizōsha publishing company in April 1919. Against the backdrop of the post–World War I mood for social reconstruction, it grew rapidly by boldly featuring commentary on labor issues and socialism, becoming, alongside "Chūō Kōron," one of the two great general-interest magazines. It was also known for its resourceful programming, including bringing Einstein and Bertrand Russell to Japan. It also placed weight on its literary pages, serializing Shiga Naoya's "An'ya Kōro" ("A Dark Night's Passing") intermittently, and it was the venue for important works of the late Taishō and early Shōwa era: Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's late work "Kappa" (1927), Yokomitsu Riichi's experimental "Kikai" ("Machine," 1930), which incorporated the stream of consciousness, and Hori Tatsuo's "Kaze Tachinu" ("The Wind Has Risen," 1936). Kaizōsha also touched off the enpon boom with its "Gendai Nihon Bungaku Zenshū" ("Complete Collection of Modern Japanese Literature," begun 1926). The magazine was forced to cease publication under military pressure in 1944, during the war, and although it was revived after the war, it ceased publication for good in 1955.
