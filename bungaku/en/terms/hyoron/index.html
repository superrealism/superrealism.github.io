What is Hyōron (Criticism and Commentary)?

Category
Form / Genre

Discursive prose in which a writer sets out and explains a reasoned view on literature, art, society, or human life. A major field of prose alongside fiction and poetry, in modern Japan it developed richly in both the enlightenment and intellectual sphere and in literary criticism. Fukuzawa Yukichi's "Gakumon no Susume" ("An Encouragement of Learning," from 1872) led the age with its plain exposition of practical learning and self-reliant independence, and Kitamura Tōkoku's "On Inner Life" and Takayama Chogyū's "Biteki Seikatsu o Ronzu" ("On the Aesthetic Life") posed young readers questions about life's ideals. In literary criticism, Masaoka Shiki's "A Letter to Practitioners of Tanka" lit the fuse for the reform of tanka, and Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's late "Bungeiteki na, Amari ni Bungeiteki na" ("Literary, All Too Literary"), which defended "stories without much of a story," argued a central point of contention in the literary establishment. Meanwhile masterpieces were also born that crystallized philosophical thought into plain prose, such as Nishida Kitarō's "Zen no Kenkyū" ("An Inquiry into the Good"), and after the war Sakaguchi Ango's "Darakuron" ("On Decadence") struck postwar thought with a sharp, affirmative view of the collapse of old morality. The appeal of criticism lies in moving the reader not through fiction, but through the power of thought itself.
