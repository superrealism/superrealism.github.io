About this work

In a café in the bright afternoon he notices a woman in kimono at a table five or six metres away, watching him steadily — a very short piece Yamakawa Masao published in Hōseki. It is Yoriko, who kept the lending library where he lodged as a middle-school boy in the years of the burnt ruins. A small butterfly-shaped mark on a girl's bare shoulder calls back a distant night. In the last few lines the meanings of light and shadow quietly change places.

Summary

In a coffee shop where he is waiting for a friend, he notices a pair of eyes turned upon him. A refined woman of nearly forty in a kimono is looking at him with a smile that has nothing held back in it. It is Yoriko, he realizes. In the days when the marks of the war were still to be seen everywhere, she was the mistress of a small lending library called the Kōbunsha, which he, a middle-school boy, had gone into for the pleasure of being able to read books while earning a little; and she used sometimes to ask him to a meal, and it was rumored that she was the daughter of a family of the old nobility. ... In the autumn of that year, in the middle of a night when the power was off, Yoriko appeared suddenly in his two-mat room under the roof. When he woke she was gone, and the memory of that first experience was too vivid; driven by a terror he could not explain, he left the shop that same day. A year later the shop and the mansion had passed into other hands, and Yoriko had disappeared. — But all that is now no more than a memory like an old dream. He is confident that he can greet her calmly and part from her calmly. Thinking so, he glances at the girl in a pink dress at the same table, and suddenly cannot move: on her bare shoulder is a birthmark shaped like a small butterfly. On his own right shoulder is a mark of exactly the same shape. Thirteen full years since then. He walks over to the table almost staggering and asks in a hoarse voice, "...Your daughter?" "Yes," answers Yoriko. "She is just ten." When he looks at the shoulder again, there is no mark there. On the white table small shadows of the summer sunlight lay scattered. He began to laugh, and Yoriko was laughing too. A few minutes later, as he left the shop with his friend, the woman saw him off with a quiet smile.

The opening (original text)

In a coffee shop in the bright early afternoon he had arranged to meet a friend. The friend was late. White round tables without customers stand in a row. At that hour in summer the customers were few, and perhaps for that reason the empty shop looked all the wider. Suddenly he noticed a pair of eyes fixed upon him. Reaching slowly for his coffee, which had gone lukewarm, he looked at the woman at a table by the same window, five or six meters away.

The ending (original text)

"But Mother," said the girl, who until then had gone on keeping a shy silence, in a voice full of grievance. "Mother, how could you? I am twelve now. Why did you get it wrong?" Without answering, the woman turned her eyes with the same mild smile toward the door. Just then the glass door opened and a middle-aged man came in with a parcel from a department store in his arms. "There, Father has come," said the woman. "Father!" cried the girl. The man, with the paper parcel filling his arms, broke into a beaming smile and walked over to the table. He was dragging his left leg a little.
