About this work

A short story Yokomitsu Riichi published in Bungei Jidai in 1926. The standard-bearer of the New Sensationalists lays the ringworm spreading over the emperor Napoleon's belly over the history of his conquests and his fall, in a bizarre and humorous fable. The gradual extension of the ringworm's dominion across Napoleon's belly is interleaved with descriptions of marching armies and campaigns, in acute figures and a novel sensibility. An unusual work overflowing with the experimental spirit of the school.

Summary

Upon the robust, jutting belly of the Emperor Napoleon Bonaparte, a single ringworm had made its home. While Napoleon, on the observation terrace of the Tuileries, faces the rainbow in all his magnificence, upon his belly the ringworm is steadily extending its dominion. The tale proceeds by skillfully interweaving Napoleon's history of conquest as he sweeps across Europe with the "domain" the ringworm spreads across his belly. The ringworm's advance — eating into the skin, throwing up purple trenches, gnawing through forests of fine hair — mirrors precisely the campaigns in which Napoleon's great armies trample nation after nation underfoot. But just as soldiers, gorged on victory after victory and having seen too much blood, begin to lose their minds at the very peak of conquest's glory, so too does the ringworm's domain turn, in time, toward its own downfall. As Napoleon watches his several hundred thousand troops advancing toward Russia from a hill on the riverbank, upon his very belly the ringworm's empire, too, carries within it the scent of ruin. A bizarre and playful fable by Yokomitsu, the standard-bearer of the Shinkankaku-ha, in which the rise and fall of an emperor is overlaid upon the waxing and waning of the ringworm on his belly.

Characters

  • Napoleon Bonaparte　The emperor who sweeps across Europe. A ringworm has taken up residence on his robust belly, and the history of his conquest and downfall is overlaid upon the rise and fall of that ringworm's domain
  • The ringworm　A skin disease that spreads across Napoleon's belly. It extends its "domain" ever further, only to move at last toward its own downfall

The opening (original text)

One Napoleon Bonaparte's belly, upon the observation terrace of the Tuileries, strained and swelled as though contending with the rainbow that had just then appeared. At the very summit of that robust belly, a button of Corsican agate, distorting half the view of Paris within it, lay faintly clouded by the fingerprint of the Empress.

Napoleon and the Ringworm by Yokomitsu Riichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Napoleon watched those troops from atop a hill on the riverbank. Cavalry, infantry, artillery — a great army of several hundred thousand madmen, resplendent in the brilliance of their uniforms, advanced from the woods in layered ranks, like clouds of three colors. The unbroken ruts left by the gun-carriages seemed like some riverbed set ringing with sound. The crests of sabers and muskets, flashing in the morning sun, scattered rainbows through the air. The chestnut-horsed plain, undulating beneath its burden of madmen, formed a black horizon, and overflowed, like a rising tide, into ruin.
