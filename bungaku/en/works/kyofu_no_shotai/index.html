About this work

"That is why I said corpses do not frighten me" — a small, thin man begins to talk, in a piece from Yamakawa Masao's series "Close Friends." The man, a police forensic officer, thrusts photographs of the dead at him one after another purely to enjoy his expression of fear. But what he actually feared was not corpses; it was the life in living people. The last line discloses the narrator's own occupation, and the whole outline of the story changes.

Summary

A thin young man with a jutting Adam's apple begins to speak with the eyes of a beaten dog. — Wanting to sell an old ring and necklace that were in the house, he consulted a woman who had been at elementary school with him. It was her husband, said to know about dealers in old things, who was the trouble. On the evening he was asked to dinner, while the wife was in the kitchen, the man quietly held out a photograph of a naked woman. To "me," gazing at it and taking it for a dirty picture, the man says in a low voice, "That's the long way; turn it sideways, like this," and explains with delight that it is a photograph of a body laid on the mortuary table, and that from the end they run in order: strangling, suicide by gas. When "I" guessed at a gourd-shaped piece of flesh with "an enlarged photograph of an amoeba?" the man said, breaking up each word: "This, you see, is the tongue of a woman who was strangled." When I came to myself I was lying on my back on the mats. An explosion of laughter rang through my whole body. — From that day the man showed "me" his photographs of bodies at every opportunity, indeed made opportunities for it, and came to taste my look of terror each time like a sweet pleasure. A photograph of a butchered corpse is slipped into the family album; at a street corner a photograph is thrust out of his pocket. Again and again "I" protested that it was not the bodies I feared, but that it was the unreasoning life of living human beings that was uncanny; but the man would not understand a word of it. ... And today the ring and the necklace at last fetched seven thousand three hundred yen. He hands over the fee, pays the twenty percent, they drink beer to it, and now everything is over — and at that moment the man took another photograph out of his bag.

Characters

  • I　A small, thin man with a jutting Adam's apple
  • He　A member of the police forensic section, third dan at judo
  • The wife　The forensic man's wife, who was at elementary school with "me"

The opening (original text)

— That is why I said I am not afraid of bodies. It is human life that is uncanny to me. There is no telling what it will do, and it is dreadful and cruel and strange and hideous. That is what I am afraid of.... From the very beginning I have gone on saying nothing but that. The thin young man with the jutting Adam's apple said it and drank off a glass of water at a draft. He was small, with the timid eyes of a beaten dog. But those eyes suddenly shone with a haughty light, and he began to talk rapidly....

Kyōfu no shōtai by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

— Good. That will do for the statement.... By the way, what is your occupation? — I am a city employee. I work at the crematorium. — The crematorium? Indeed. Then you are an onbō? — That is what people call it, the man answered, working his Adam's apple in jerks. — It is my hereditary trade, from my father's time; but nowadays the official name is corpse incineration worker. His eyes had turned back again to those of a timid, beaten dog.
