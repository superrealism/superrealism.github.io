About this work

An unusual work by Miyazawa Kenji, drawing the love of two signal posts of unequal rank — a fine signal on the main line and a modest little signal on the light railway. The gossip of the telegraph poles, the roof of a warehouse acting as go-between, and a promise exchanged under the stars. It is rare to find so fresh a love story with inanimate things for its protagonists.

Summary

"Clattety-clack, clattety-clack, shoo-fuff-fuff" — as the light railway's first morning train came singing in and drew to a stop, Signaless, the signal post belonging to the light railway, raised her white signal arm. Close beside her stood Signal, the fine, imposing signal post of the main line. One morning Signal spoke to Signaless, and little by little the two of them fell into the habit of exchanging words. But a main-line Signal and a light-railway Signaless were of very different standing. The telegraph poles attached to the light railway found the pairing distasteful and gossiped about it endlessly, and the head telegraph pole attached to the main line, too, made a sour face at the thought of 'that fellow, taking up with something like a light railway.' Even so, the two of them found their hearts drawn together, and at last Signal spoke up: 'Let's make a promise to each other.' It was the black roof of the nearby warehouse that took on the role of go-between. Having witnessed the two of them exchange their vow, the roof gave its word, saying, 'I'll be your witness.' That night, the two of them, out in the snow, dreamed the very same dream: a sky where neither ground nor sea could be seen, only the misty fires burning among the stars — 'Our wish has come true, hasn't it,' 'The earth seems so far away, doesn't it.' As they went on exchanging words in a world all their own, a deep voice suddenly spoke up: 'Now, isn't that kind of you.' Before they knew it, the mist had cleared, and the whole sky was thick with stars, blinking busily blue and orange, while over on the far side the pitch-black warehouse roof stood laughing. And the two of them let out another small, relieved breath.

The opening (original text)

"Clattety-clack, clattety-clack, shoo-fuff-fuff, just when the scorpion's red eye came into view, at four o'clock, this morning too, I came along. The Tōno basin lies in utter darkness, with nothing but the voice of the cold water." The light railway's first morning train from the east came in, a little as though flustered, singing this song, and drew to a stop.

The ending (original text)

"Now, isn't that kind of you" — a deep voice suddenly spoke up. And when they came to themselves, ah, the two of them had, all along, been dreaming together.
