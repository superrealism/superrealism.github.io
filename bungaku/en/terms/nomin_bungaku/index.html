What is Nōmin Bungaku (Peasant Literature)?

Category
Form / Genre

Literature depicting, just as it is, the labor and daily life of people living in farming villages, together with their joys and sorrows. In modern Japanese literature, the Naturalist tanka poet Nagatsuka Takashi's full-length novel "Tsuchi" ("The Soil," 1910, in the "Tokyo Asahi Shimbun"), depicting the life of a poor tenant-farming family in Ibaraki through meticulous sketch-based realism, is regarded as its landmark forerunner, and Natsume Sōseki praised it highly as a precious book he wished young readers to read without fail. From the end of the Taishō era into the Shōwa era, a peasant-literature movement, echoing proletarian literature's depiction of the urban working class, gathered momentum in an organized way, taking as its theme the exhaustion of farming villages and the liberation of peasants. Miyazawa Kenji, who farmed the land himself in the villages around Hanamaki and also taught at an agricultural school, engraved his own distinctive earth-rooted worldview in the children's story "Gusukō Budori no Denki" ("The Life of Gusukō Budori"), depicting a young man who sacrifices himself to save others from a volcano, and in "Nōmin Geijutsu Gairon Kōyō" ("An Outline of the Principles of Peasant Art"), setting out his ideal of art rooted in farming village life. This gaze turned upon the nameless people who lived by the soil adds real depth to modern literature.
