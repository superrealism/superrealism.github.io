What is Imayō?

Category
Form / Genre

A form of song popular from the middle to the end of the Heian period. "Imayō" means "of the present style," a new, fashionable song as against the ancient gagaku court music and kagura sacred music. Built basically of four lines alternating seven and five syllables, it is especially distinguished by having been sung across every level of society, from aristocrats to courtesans, puppeteers, and shirabyoshi dancers, and even farmers. The Retired Emperor Go-Shirakawa, who loved imayō deeply, was so devoted that he studied directly under courtesans, and, regretting that it might be lost, compiled the song anthology "Ryōjin Hishō" (around the Jishō era, circa 1180). Its most famous verse runs, "Were we not born to play and to frolic? To hear the voices of children at play makes even my own body sway." "The Tale of the Heike," too, in the appearances of the shirabyoshi Giō and Hotoke Gozen, suggests how imayō and shirabyoshi dance colored the seats of the powerful. Ranging widely from songs preaching the Buddha's teaching to songs of love and songs of children's play, it conveys to us today the voice of the common people that waka poetry did not capture. It stands as one great wellspring of the flow of song that continues into the heikyōku and yōkyoku of the medieval period and the kouta and hauta of the early modern era.
