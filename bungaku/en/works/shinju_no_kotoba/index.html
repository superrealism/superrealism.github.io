About this work

A short story set in Kōfu, written for the 1939 collection On Love and Beauty published by Takemura Shobō. A young man who was nursed by the same woman turns up unexpectedly at the house where the narrator has moved, in a town in the basin. Through a brother and sister who look up to him straightforwardly, with nothing to go on but the tie left by their nurse Tsuru, it draws a clean bond that is not one of blood. A bright piece from the period just before his marriage.

Summary

One day, an unexpected visitor comes calling on "I," living in lodgings in Kōfu. The young man, with regular, well-formed features, says he is Kōkichi, son of Tsuru, who had once been my wet nurse. To me, handed over to a wet nurse right after birth, Tsuru was a figure in my memory even before my own mother. A fair-skinned boy I had met only once, at the Obon festival, when I was in primary school — that had been him. Kōkichi, who lost both his parents young and now lives with his younger sister, running a small business, looked up to me as though I were a true elder brother. Not a bond of blood, but a bond of milk. I grew drunk, sang a lullaby, and rattled off complaints in a way that was almost shameful to hear. Then, on the night of the second day after, a clanging fire bell rings out.

Characters

  • I　A writer living in lodgings in Kōfu
  • Kōkichi　Son of Tsuru, my former wet nurse. A young man who is my foster brother
  • Sister　Kōkichi's younger sister. A girl whose sharp way of speaking is just like Tsuru's
  • Tsuru　The wet nurse who raised me. Born in a fishing village on the Tsugaru peninsula

The opening (original text)

Kōfu is a basin. On every side, all around, mountains. As a primary school student, when I first came across the word "basin" in geography class, my teacher explained it to me in various ways, but I could never for the life of me picture what it actually looked like. Only when I came to Kōfu did I understand, ah, so that's what it means. Drain some enormous, enormous marsh, and on the bottom of that marsh, make fields and build houses — that is a basin.

Shinju no kotoba by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The roaring of some beast could be heard, without a break. "What on earth is that?" I had been puzzled for a while now. "There's the zoo, right behind us, at the park," his sister told me. "It'd be awful if a lion got loose or something." She laughed, without a care in the world. You two are happy. A great victory. And you can go on becoming happier still. I crossed my arms tightly, and even so, trembling all the while, secretly flexed my muscles.

Shinju no kotoba by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)
