About this work

A strange noise, like grating radish, coming from the next room in a hospital. The narrator, who has gone on wondering what it can be through his stay, learns the answer only as he is about to be discharged — a small piece Sōseki wrote from his own long hospitalization for a gastric ulcer. Beneath the form of a small puzzle lies a quiet reflection on life and death.

Summary

Half-asleep, I wake to find a strange sound coming from the next room. It must be someone grating a radish or something with a wasabi-grater — but this is a hospital. Cooking, and even sweets, are forbidden in the sickroom, so there is no reason anyone should be making grated radish at this hour. Even once I understand that it must be some other sound I am mistaking for that one, once it has caught in my ear, that sound haunts my nerves and will not let me forget it. In a stillness so deep that not even the soft slap of a nurse's slippers can be heard in the corridor, only that grinding, scraping sound, like something being worn away, keeps drawing my attention. Before long, as my condition improves, "my" interest shifts to the patient in the next room — the groans heard now and then, the exchanges with the nurse, and the fact that the sound of my own razor being stropped on leather must be leaking through to their side as well. On the day of my discharge, in conversation with the nurse, the truth behind both sounds is finally revealed. The patient next door, it turns out, had mistaken the sound of my strop for the sound of some morning exercise machine, and had been saying, again and again, how he envied me. And that sound of grated radish had been the nurse grating cucumber for a patient who complained that his feet burned with fever. "So what exactly was wrong with Mr. So-and-so?" "Cancer of the rectum." He died, the nurse says, not long after being discharged from here. I return to my room in silence, and turn over in my mind the difference between the man who died, tormenting others with the sound of a cucumber, and the man who recovered, making others envy him with the sound of a strop.

Characters

  • I (Myself)　The narrator, a hospital patient
  • Mr. So-and-so　The patient in the next room
  • The nurse　Reveals the truth behind the sounds

The opening (original text)

No sooner had I thought myself dozing off than I woke. And there was a strange sound coming from the next room. At first I could make out no clear sense of what the sound was, or where it came from, but as I went on listening, a settled notion gradually formed itself in my ear. It must be, I decided, someone grating a radish or something of the sort with a wasabi-grater. I was quite convinced that this was so.

Henna oto by Natsume Sōseki (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Then it was quite hopeless, was it?" "Yes, hopeless indeed. It was right after he was discharged from here that he passed away." I returned to my own room in silence. And there I turned over in my mind the difference between the man who died, tormenting others with the sound of a cucumber, and the man who recovered, making others envy him with the sound of a strop.
