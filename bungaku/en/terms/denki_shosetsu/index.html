What is Denki Shōsetsu (Romance of the Fantastic)?

Category
Form / Genre

Fiction depicting the marvelous, the fantastical, remote and mysterious lands, and superhuman figures beyond the bounds of reality. The term originates in Tang-dynasty Chinese short fiction — the name of the collection "Chuanqi," compiled by the late-Tang writer Pei Xing, became a general term for Tang fiction. In Japan, the yomihon tradition beginning with Ueda Akinari's "Ugetsu Monogatari" ("Tales of Moonlight and Rain") inherited this imaginative power, and modern writers have repeatedly returned to the same vein, as when Akutagawa Ryūnosuke drew on the Tang tale "The Story of Du Zichun" for his own "Toshishun" (1920). From the late Taishō era into the early Shōwa era, romances of the fantastic in period settings flourished as a branch of popular fiction: Yoshikawa Eiji made his breakthrough with "Naruto Hichō" ("The Secret Scroll of Naruto," from 1926), a tale of a plot to overthrow the shogunate set in the closed-off domain of Awa. Tanizaki Jun'ichirō's "Bushūkō Hiwa" ("A Secret Tale of Lord Bushū," 1931), which frames a warlord's perverse desires as a scholarly examination of fictitious documents, belongs to this lineage as well. Kunieda Shirō's "Shinshū Kōketsujō" ("The Purple-Stained Castle") attracted little notice when serialized, but its 1968 reissue, praised extravagantly by Mishima Yukio, sparked a reappraisal that helped usher in the later flourishing of the genre.
