Wikipedia

Biography

A court official of the early Nara period; his birth year is unknown. He served the imperial court as a bureaucrat under the ritsuryō system. At the order of Empress Genmei, he transcribed the "Teiki" and "Kyūji" that Hieda no Are had committed to memory and presented Japan's oldest history, the three-volume "Kojiki," in 712. The "Kojiki," a grand narrative that begins with the origin of heaven and earth and the birth of the gods and traces the deeds of successive emperors from Jinmu to Suiko through myth, legend, and song, stands as the wellspring of Japanese mythology and literature. In 1979 his epitaph was discovered in Nara City, confirming that he died in 723.
