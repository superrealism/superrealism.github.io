Hirakō Goichi: Life and Major Works

Life
1887–1967

Biography

Born in November 1887, an anatomist. In 1921 he was appointed the founding professor of the second chair of anatomy at the Hokkaido Imperial University School of Medicine, where alongside histological research on the liver and nervous system he also carried out anthropological surveys of the Ainu. He later moved to the Kyushu Imperial University School of Medicine, where he served as chief professor of anatomy. In 1945, when American prisoners of war were subjected to medical experimentation in vivisections carried out at that faculty, he was implicated for having lent the dissection room and offering a single piece of advice, and was tried as a war criminal, receiving a sentence of twenty-five years' hard labor and serving time at Sugamo Prison at the age of sixty. Encountering American medicine in prison prompted him to study medicine anew, and after his release he worked at a clinic devoted to cancer research and treatment. In 1957, prompted by the publication of Endō Shūsaku's novel "The Sea and Poison," he published a memoir, "Facing the Disgrace of War Medicine," written from his own position as one of those involved, setting down the course of the incident and his misgivings about the war crimes tribunal. He died on May 12, 1967.
