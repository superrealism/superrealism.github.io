What is Jojōshi (Lyric Poetry)?

Category
Form / Genre

Poetry in which the poet subjectively expresses his or her own feelings and emotions. It is one of the three traditional divisions of poetry, alongside epic poetry, which tells a story, and dramatic poetry, which unfolds through dialogue and action; the English word "lyric" derives from songs sung to the lyre in ancient Greece. It forms the mainstream of modern poetry, and Japan too has a long tradition of verse that sings of the movements of the heart, such as waka and imayō. In the modern period, Shimazaki Tōson's "Wakanashū" ("Seedlings," 1897), which poured youthful love and the longings of adolescence into new-style verse in seven-five meter, became the monument announcing the birth of modern lyric poetry. In the Taishō era Hagiwara Sakutarō opened up a new lyricism in colloquial free verse with "Tsuki ni Hoeru" ("Howling at the Moon," 1917), and Murō Saisei, in "Jojō Shōkyokushū" ("Lyrical Sketches," 1918), which includes "Shōkei Ijō" with its famous line "Home is a place to think of from afar," sang freshly of boyhood sentiment and longing for his native place. In the Shōwa era Miyoshi Tatsuji's "Sokuryōsen" ("The Surveying Ship," 1930) fused traditional lyricism with the intellect of Western poetry, and together with the poets gathered around the magazine "Shiki" he led a revival of lyric poetry. Nakahara Chūya's posthumous collection "Arishi Hi no Uta" ("Poems of Days Past," 1938) is another book representative of the lyricism of that age. The word is also written with a different first character (叙情詩), a spelling that has become widespread since the postwar simplification of kanji usage.
