About this work

The story of the priest Shunkan on the island of Kikai, famous from The Tale of the Heike, rewritten as a play in three acts and published in Shinshōsetsu in December 1919. Naritsune and Yasuyori, exiled with him, alone are pardoned and leave by ship, and Shunkan is left on the island — following the same plot as the classic, Kurata draws in it the process by which hunger and loneliness eat away friendship, and one man who refuses rescue and curses the world itself. A dark, violent drama of negation, the exact reverse of the forgiveness that fills The Priest and His Disciples, and a glimpse of the author's other face.

Summary

Their plot against the Taira having been found out, Shunkan, Naritsune and Yasuyori are banished to the island of Kikaigashima. On a desolate island where the smoke of sulphur rises, Naritsune stands every day on the shore watching for ships in the offing, and Yasuyori prays to the god of Kumano and goes on setting a thousand wooden stupas afloat on the sea. Two years pass, and hunger eats into the hearts of all three, until at last Shunkan and Naritsune take up bows against each other over a single small bird. Then a ship of pardon arrives from the capital. But the letter of pardon names only Naritsune and Yasuyori; the name of Shunkan is nowhere in it. Shunkan clings to the ship and pleads, and the ship goes off into the offing without mercy. Seven years more. Left alone on the island, Shunkan is so fallen that he steals a fisherman's catch and is beaten for it. In time Ariō, once his household boy, crosses the sea to find his master and tells him of the deaths of the wife and child he left in the capital. His hope of revenge gone, Shunkan gives up food, and still cannot die. To Ariō, who counsels him to end his life quietly, Shunkan will not let go of a resentment that pierces to the marrow. On a night of storm, Shunkan clambers up onto the rock, staggering, and stands glaring at the moon.

Characters

  • Shunkan　Superintendent of the Hosshōji, left alone on Kikaigashima, who ends cursing the world itself
  • Naritsune　Junior captain of Tanba, who stands on the shore every day watching for ships
  • Yasuyori　Judge of the Taira, who prays to the god of Kumano and sets a thousand stupas afloat
  • Ariō　Shunkan's household boy of old, who crosses the sea to find his master and is with him at the end
  • Tanzaemon-no-jō Motoyasu　Kiyomori's envoy, who takes only the two named in the pardon

The opening (original text)

Persons Shunkan, superintendent of the Hosshōji Naritsune, junior captain of Tanba Yasuyori, judge of the Taira Ariō (Shunkan's household boy of old) Fishermen (men, women and children, several) Tanzaemon-no-jō Motoyasu (Kiyomori's envoy) His attendants, several Boatmen, several Time The height of the power of the Taira Place The island of Kikaigashima Act One The shore of Kikaigashima. A desolate sandy beach. Here and there sparse reeds and sedge. Beyond it the boundless sea of Satsuma. Far to the left, across a bay, the peak of Iō rises against the sky. Smoke pours from its summit. Waves break and scatter on the rocks here and there. Autumn. Naritsune stands on the shore looking out over the sea. Yasuyori sits on a rock making a wooden stupa out of a piece of wood. NARITSUNE: Ah, it is out of sight at last. It must be a ship bound for Kyushu. Or it may be a ship going up to the capital. Toward my own country. YASUYORI: There is no ship that will put in at such an outlying island in any case. What is the good of standing on the shore every day watching a ship that passes far off?

The ending (original text)

SHUNKAN: (Strikes his head against the edge of the rock. Falls.) A pause. ARIŌ: (Entering.) Master. (Searching about.) Ah, where has he gone? Ah, I know. I know. I know what you have thought of! Ah, it is dreadful. Master. (Finds the traces of blood on the sand.) Oh. (Follows the blood, climbs the rock, and finds Shunkan's body.) Oh, master. (Raises Shunkan up. Knows that he is already dead. Falls to the ground. Presently rises and holds Shunkan close. Weeps aloud. Silence. Presently, holding the body:) Ah, my poor master. It was a hard, hard life. Why you had to bear such torment I do not know either. Ah, but your life like a bad dream is over. May a quiet, peaceful world to come be waiting for you! (Looks long into Shunkan's face.) What a dreadful face in death. Ah, master, did you die cursing? Bearing a grudge against heaven, hating the world, cursing your enemies, holding those dreadful, dreadful thoughts to the very moment of death! Ah, I dread what is to come to you. Ah, what I have seen these ten years has been a truly dreadful face of human life. (Silence. Presently he rises as though resolved. To the body:) Ariō will go with you, wherever, wherever it may be. (Takes Shunkan's body on his back.) Ah, Buddha. I am weary of this world. I wish to escape from this dreadful world one moment sooner. I shall die with my master. I understand nothing. Even if what I do now is wrong, forgive me, I pray. Ah, save my master's life to come. Save my master's soul out of hell and grant him everlasting peace. (Choosing a place to die.) Now I give my soul into your hands. (With Shunkan's body on his back, throws himself from the rock into the sea.) The sound of the storm. The sound of the waves. The moonlight lies at will over the shore. — Curtain —
