What is Shintaishi (New-Style Verse)?

Category
Form / Genre

Poetry in a new form modeled on Western poetry, beginning with the 1882 anthology "Shintaishi Shō," edited by Toyama Masakazu, Yatabe Ryōkichi, and Inoue Tetsujirō. Its name means "poetry of a new form," as against the classical kanshi and waka, and this volume — gathering fourteen translated poems from writers such as Shakespeare and Tennyson alongside five original poems by the editors — opened a path for expressing modern thought and feeling in a longer verse form, departing from the short forms of waka and haikai. Built basically on the classical 7-5 meter, it flowered in the 1890s in combination with Romanticism, and Shimazaki Tōson's "Wakanashū" ("Seedlings," 1897), setting the sentimentality of youth to fresh new cadences, brought new-style verse to its literary peak and established modern lyric poetry. Once colloquial free verse arose at the end of the Meiji era, the name "shintaishi" gradually faded and the form came simply to be called "poetry," but its significance in literary history, as the starting point of modern Japanese poetry, is considerable.
