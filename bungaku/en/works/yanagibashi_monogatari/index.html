About this work

A long novel Yamamoto Shūgorō began with the first part in the opening number of his own magazine Tsubaki in 1946, continuing with the middle and last parts; the completed form now current appeared in Shin Seinen in 1949. Set around Yanagibashi in Edo, it draws the strange and pitiful life of Osen, a girl loved by two carpenters who grew up with her. Having promised Shōkichi, leaving for the Kansai, that she will wait, Osen is played with by a great fire, a flood and baseless rumour, and, losing everything, comes at last to where real love is. A fine piece representing Shūgorō's writing about ordinary people.

Summary

Osen, a girl who lives in Edo's Kayachō with her grandfather Genroku, a knife sharpener, is loved by two childhood friends, the carpenters Shōkichi and Kōta. When Shōkichi is sent to Kamigata to earn his living, he tells Osen, "Wait for me — three years, five years, however long it takes," and she vows, almost in a daze, to do so. Kōta, the other childhood friend, now adopted into the Sugita-ya household, goes on quietly loving Osen despite his gruff manner, but she keeps faith with Shōkichi alone, turning away both Kōta's proposal and the cruel rumors spread by Gonjirō, a young man from the courier shop. Then a great fire and flood that strike Edo change everything. Osen loses her grandfather, and pulls a stranger's infant from the flames, naming him Kōtarō and beginning to raise him as her own. But on the night of the fire, Kōta, who had risked his life to save Osen, never returns. When Shōkichi comes back from Kamigata, Gonjirō's slander and the sight of Osen nursing an infant convince him the child is proof of her unfaithfulness, and he tells her he will stay only if she gives up the boy. Unable to abandon Kōtarō, Osen lets Shōkichi go. When she later learns that Shōkichi has married another woman and settled as her family's adopted son-in-law, the old derangement that has haunted her since the fire threatens to return, but she is pulled back from it by the devoted care of her old friend Omon, who has herself fallen on hard times. Standing at last on the Yanagi riverbank, Osen understands that it was Kōta who truly loved her and who died to save her life. She resolves to raise Kōtarō as "the child of Kōta and herself," and has her own name entered in vermilion beside his on his memorial tablet. On the day of the celebration for the newly rebuilt Yanagibashi bridge, when Shōkichi comes to make his apology, Osen quietly but firmly shows him that tablet.

Characters

  • Osen　Granddaughter of the knife sharpener Genroku. A gentle but strong-willed girl determined to keep her promise to Shōkichi. The protagonist
  • Shōkichi　Osen's childhood friend, a carpenter. He leaves for Kamigata and comes to doubt Osen after his return
  • Kōta　Osen's childhood friend, a carpenter, adopted into the Sugita-ya household. Gruff but devoted to Osen above all others; he loses his life in the great fire
  • Genroku　Osen's grandfather, a skilled knife sharpener. He dies in the great fire
  • Kōtarō　An abandoned infant Osen rescues on the night of the great fire and raises as her own
  • Omon　Osen's old friend. Though she has fallen on hard times herself, she nurses the ailing Osen with devotion
  • Gonjirō　A young man from the courier shop who spreads cruel rumors
  • Matsuzō　The elder brother of Kōtarō's birth mother, Otsune. A farmer from Koga, gruff but he helps Osen

The opening (original text)

It was a fine, lively autumn horse mackerel, its blue-tinged skin still gleaming with an iridescent shimmer. She filleted it into three, firmed the flesh with salt, sliced it thin, and arranged it in a bowl, scattering shredded ginger over the top before pouring on the vinegar. ...Even as she watched, the flesh seemed to curl and shrink before her eyes; her heart lifted with a kind of gladness. For the garnish, should she shred some green shiso, or make a knotweed vinegar instead? She was turning these questions over with a feeling almost like singing, when voices reached her from the front of the shop.

Yanagibashi monogatari by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"That's Kōta's memorial tablet. Read the name entered in vermilion beside his — it says Osen, doesn't it? If his posthumous name alone leaves you in doubt, look on the back: his lay name, Kōta, is written there as well." Shōkichi said nothing. He bowed his head and went out, his shoulders drawn in. Left alone, Osen gazed steadily at the tablet and murmured, in a small, low voice, "This is right, isn't it, Kōta? Aunt Ochō wouldn't think ill of it either — now, at last, I feel as though I've truly become your wife. You feel that way too, don't you, Kōta?"
