About this work

Tolstoy's parable (1886), written in the form of a folk tale, translated by Kikuchi Kan for the "Complete Library for Primary School Children." Simon the soldier, Taras the stout, and Ivan the fool — the devils try to set the three brothers against one another, but no stratagem will work on Ivan, who knows nothing but how to work with his hands. The ending, in which force and money alike prove powerless and only those with horny hands may come to table, has been read on as a story that puts the thought of Tolstoy's last years directly into narrative.

Summary

A certain peasant had three sons — Simon the soldier, Taras the stout, and Ivan the fool — and a daughter, Martha, who was deaf and dumb. Simon wins rank and lands in the wars and Taras becomes a merchant in town and builds a fortune, but both depend upon their father's property. Ivan alone stays at home with his sister, working until his back is bent. To set the three against one another and ruin them, the old devil sends three imps. One brings Simon to defeat by stirring up his troops, and another brings Taras to bankruptcy by making him trust too far in the power of money; and the third goes at Ivan — but however hard he makes the field, however heavy the trees, Ivan only keeps driving the plow and swinging the axe in silence, and never gives in. Caught at last, the imp offers, to save its life, the art of making soldiers and the art of turning leaves into gold coins. Ivan uses those powers to give his brothers troops and money, and goes on tilling the soil himself as before. In the kingdom over which Ivan comes to rule, only those who work gather, and the custom grows up that those without horny hands eat what the others leave. Then the old devil himself appears in the shape of a gentleman and argues that one may live by one's head without using one's hands —

Characters

  • Ivan　The youngest of the three brothers. Called a fool, he goes on working in silence
  • Simon　The eldest brother, who wins rank and lands as a soldier
  • Taras　The second brother, who becomes a merchant in town and builds a fortune
  • Martha　The brothers' sister, who can tell whose hands are horny
  • The old devil　He sends the imps to set the three brothers against one another

The opening (original text)

1 Once upon a time, in a certain kingdom, there lived a rich peasant. And the rich peasant had three sons — Simon the soldier, Taras the stout, and Ivan the fool — and a daughter, Martha, who was deaf and dumb. Simon the soldier went off to the wars to serve the Tsar; Taras the stout went to town and became a merchant; and Ivan the fool stayed at home with his sister and worked until their backs were bent. Simon the soldier won high rank and broad lands, and married the Tsar's daughter. His pay was large and his lands brought in a great deal; but he could not keep it in order. Worse still, whatever the husband got, the wife squandered, so that they were always in want.

Ivan the Fool by Kikuchi Kan (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Devils again. Nothing but big talk — that one must be their father." So said Ivan. And Ivan is living still. People crowd into that kingdom of his. His two brothers came to him as well, meaning to be kept, and Ivan kept them. To anyone who comes and says, "Give us food," Ivan says: "Very well, very well. Come and live with us. We have plenty of everything." Only there is one particular custom in Ivan's kingdom. Whoever has horny hands may come to table; anyone else, whoever he may be, must eat what the others leave.
