What is Seitō (Bluestocking)?

Category
Society / Group

Japan's first literary magazine written and edited entirely by women, founded in September 1911 by the Seitōsha, whose five founders included Hiratsuka Raichō. The title is a translation of "bluestocking," after the eighteenth-century London gathering of learned women. The founding declaration Raichō placed at the head of the inaugural issue, "In the beginning, woman was truly the sun," and a line from the opening poem contributed by the supporting member Yosano Akiko, "Sozorogoto" ("Idle Words") — "The day the mountain moves has come" — are celebrated as monumental words of the women's liberation movement. Though it began as a literary magazine, it steadily took on more of the character of a critical journal questioning the very way women lived, and its members came to be called the "New Women," drawing both public attention and censure. The discussion in its pages of Nora, the heroine of Ibsen's "A Doll's House," is emblematic of this. In its later years Itō Noe took over its editorship, and it ceased publication with its February 1916 issue.
