About this work

Unno Jūza's central science adventure story for boys, serialized in the Dai-Mai and Tō-Nichi elementary school papers in 1939 and 1940. It draws the struggle between the Martian host that means to conquer the earth and the human race that meets it, around the exploits of the boy Senji. Invisible men, secret bases underground, weapons that cross the great spaces — the most advanced fancies of the science fiction of its day are packed in, and the imagination of a writer called the father of Japanese science fiction runs at full stretch.

Summary

"The Martian Corps" — an uncanny rumor begins to go about the town. The old scientist Doctor Arita warns that this is no joke but the sign that the human race is to be destroyed to the last man; and no one takes him seriously. Before long strange incidents come one after another. People attacked by something no one can see, objects that vanish on the instant, an unearthly signal that reaches them from under the ground. The boy Senji is drawn by chance into the middle of it, and while following the mystery with Doctor Arita he establishes that beings come from Mars are steadily preparing to conquer the earth. An advance force of Martians who command a technique of invisibility, a secret base built underground, and a huge heavenly body drawing near the earth. The fate of mankind hangs by a thread. Senji and the others turn to the counterattack with all the wits and courage they can gather, and at last the earth escapes the danger of collision — but that is not the end. "From now on it will be a race against the Martians" — shaking his fist, Doctor Arita urges the boys to study hard.

Characters

  • Senji　The boy who is drawn into the affair and fights the Martian Corps
  • Doctor Arita　The old scientist who warns early of the Martian attack
  • The Martians　The invaders who plot the conquest of the earth and command a technique of invisibility

The opening (original text)

1. An uncanny rumor Have you not by now heard the rumor of the "Martian Corps"? Hm — you say that this morning, in the underground train, you caught a little of it from the side as the passengers were talking? — No, indeed, this is no joke, I assure you. It is not that we scientists want to frighten people for half the fun of it. We of the human race on earth must now, in earnest, and in the greatest haste, make ready for battle.

Kasei heidan by Unno Jūza (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Doctor Arita's forecast had been almost right, but where the influence of the moon came in he had been a little out. Still, that the earth escaped a dangerous pass can only be called the protection of God. "...The earth has been saved from collision. But from now on it will be a race against the Martians, so we of the human race on earth must study harder than we ever have, or the Martians will take from us the place of leader in the great universe. Study! Study hard!" So said Doctor Arita, shaking his fist. Senji and the teacher both nodded firmly.

Kasei heidan by Unno Jūza (Aozora Bunko)
