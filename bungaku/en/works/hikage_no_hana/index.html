About this work

A central late work of Nagai Kafū's fiction of the pleasure quarters, published in Chūō Kōron in August 1934. With Tsuyu no Atosaki and A Strange Tale from East of the River, it draws a woman living in the places downtown Tokyo where the sun does not reach — the shade of the title — and the man she keeps. Ochiyo, a woman of thirty who was once a geisha and a bar girl, and Jūkichi, the lover she supports while he passes his days gambling. Kafū copies down the tedium and the affection of a couple living pressed together in a rented upper room off the main streets of the world, at a distance and yet with something like pity. From the frankness of its descriptions of manners, it was forced into many blanks and cuts on publication: a piece of Kafū's maturity.

Summary

Ochiyo, a few years past thirty, lives from day to day in a rented upper room off the main streets. Wrapped in his bedding and reading the newspaper is Jūkichi, her lover, a man with no fixed occupation who is supported by her while he devotes himself to gambling. Ochiyo, who has known plenty of hardship as a geisha and a bar hostess in her time, still secretly sells herself even now, drifting through her days with this unreliable man. In a life kept off the main streets of the world — the "shade" of the title — the two of them, weary of each other though they are, cannot bring themselves to part. Then, in the latter half of the story, it emerges that Ochiyo has a daughter she once parted from for reasons of her own, and that this daughter too has sunk into the same shaded world as her mother. Following the thread of human connection, Ochiyo comes to meet her daughter again — Kafū draws the way of life of these people living at the margins of society, a streetwalker and her kept man, with a cool eye set apart from any moral yardstick, and yet not without a certain warmth. The story closes quietly with a single letter from the daughter, who has come to stay with her mother.

Characters

  • Ochiyo　A woman past thirty, living in a rented upper room off the main streets. Once a geisha and bar hostess, still sells herself while supporting her lover — the protagonist of this piece
  • Jūkichi　Ochiyo's lover, supported by her while he gives himself over to gambling
  • Tamiko　The daughter who parted from Ochiyo for reasons of her own, and lives in the same shaded world as her mother

The opening (original text)

Outside the glass window of the upper room the two of them rented lay the house's drying platform. It must have been getting on toward noon that day. From somewhere the smell of sardines being grilled drifted in, and on the drying platform, for some time now, the shadow of a woman who rented the front room on the same floor had been moving across the frosted glass, the hem of her nightclothes hitched up as she hung out one thing after another.

Hikage no hana by Nagai Kafū (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

I have decided to stay with my mother for the time being. If anything else changes I will let you know. And so, goodbye.
