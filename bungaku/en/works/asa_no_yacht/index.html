About this work

"The sea is not a place two people go to enjoy. It is what a person goes out to meet alone" — a very short piece Yamakawa Masao wrote for Bijutsu Techō. Having said so, the boy took a Snipe-class yacht out to sea and did not come back. The next morning, a single gull flies on and on above the girl who watches the search, never leaving her. A thirst for solitude and a fear of loving, crystallized in a few pages.

Summary

The boy got aboard his Snipe-class yacht and called to the girl as he cast off. "I am going." "Please, take me too." "I told you no." The boy says it in an angry voice. "The sea is not a place two people go to enjoy together. It is something a man goes out to meet entirely alone." When the girl asks whether he loves the sea more than her, he frowned in irritation. "When I am with you I sometimes feel that another self of mine has been left behind somewhere very far away. I go to the sea to get that other self back.... Because the sea is the place that makes a man truly alone." Why do you want to be alone? "A woman would not understand." And then, suddenly showing his white teeth in a laugh, he said, "I love you." ... Seeing the yacht slide away over the ultramarine afternoon sea with its white sail slanting, tears came into the girl's eyes. But she went on waving with a smile, feeling the rapidly widening distance between them as a wound that tore her breast and widened. — The boy vanished into the sea. The answers from every point along the coast and from the islands were all "Not arrived." A sudden change of weather, a squall and a small waterspout seemed to tell the reason. The next morning, while several yachts ran searching for the boy's Snipe, the girl goes on asking the sea: Why? Why did you have to go and be alone? The sea had already fallen calm, as if it were all a lie.

Characters

  • The boy　Who went out to sea in a Snipe-class yacht and never came back
  • The girl　His sweetheart, who saw him off from the shore
  • The gull　The one bird that goes on flying without leaving the yacht

The opening (original text)

The color of dawn faintly stained the eastern sky, and before long the first light of that day's sun made the far surface of the sea shine like tinfoil. Out on the water it was still dim and the border of sky and sea was not clear; but at all events, morning had come to the sea. One gull was slowly beating its wings above the yacht. As though it had made up its mind never to leave, the gull kept its direction and its speed one with the yacht's and went on flying through the considerable wind that moved across the morning sky.

Asa no yotto by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

But however the gull strained its throat, the cry came out as nothing but a monotonous mewing like a cat's. ... And before long the gull forgot the meaning of its own flight, and forgot too the freshness of solitude, and the terror and the humiliation and the joy of loving, and went on repeating a cat's cry above the girl's yacht in the transparent morning wind that washed its whole body, keeping up the slow rise and fall of its wings without a thought.
