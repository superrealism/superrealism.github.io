About this work

A short story Hori Tatsuo published in Bungei Shunjū in 1932. The narrator, just up from middle school to higher school, recalls his boyhood in the dormitory with a fresh and delicate sensibility. Temptation from an older boy, a longing for the beautiful and sickly classmate Saegusa, and a waking sensuality and hesitation — the burning cheeks of adolescence, drawn in Hori's acute new-psychologist hand. A central early work.

Summary

Now seventeen, "I" have just moved from middle school to higher school, and, taken from my parents' hands, have been placed in a dormitory. That environment, with more than ten of us crammed into small, honeycomb-like rooms, hastens my molting away from boyhood in a way that feels almost unsettling. Uozumi, an upper-classman and discus-throw athlete idolized by the younger boys, calls me one day into the botany laboratory, and, letting me peer through the microscope, breathes hot breath upon me with a faint, girlish smile. Then, in May, a classmate named Saegusa moves into my room. Thin, with beautiful skin through which the veins seemed almost to show, and rose-colored cheeks, he was a beautiful boy regarded by the upper-classmen with a kind of "boyish" fascination. I come to adore his anemic beauty, stealing glances at his slender neck from behind my textbook, and dreaming, in various ways, of his sleeping face in the dark. One night, I find, in the dormitory, the figure of Uozumi, wrapped in a cloak, sitting down at Saegusa's pillow. An early masterpiece in which Hori captures, with the keen sensibility of the new psychologism, the "burning cheeks" of adolescence, swayed between awakening sensuality and hesitation, longing and jealousy.

Characters

  • I　A seventeen-year-old higher-school student spending his boyhood in a dormitory. Amid awakening sensuality and hesitation, comes to adore the beautiful boy Saegusa
  • Saegusa　A classmate who moves into "my" room. Thin, with beautiful skin and rose-colored cheeks, a beautiful boy regarded by the upper-classmen with a kind of "boyish" fascination
  • Uozumi　An upper-classman and discus-throw athlete idolized by the younger boys. Shows seductive behavior toward "me," and also approaches Saegusa

The opening (original text)

I turned seventeen. It was just the time when I had moved on from middle school into higher school. My parents, fearing that I would grow up too high-strung under their own care, placed me in the dormitory there. That change of environment could not help but leave a marked effect on my character. Through it, my molting away from boyhood was being hastened, to an almost unsettling degree.

Moyuru hō by Hori Tatsuo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

One morning, I found myself suddenly rising from bed, and, fearfully, wanting to walk alone as far as the window. It was that pleasant a morning. From the window of my own sickroom, I then caught sight, on the veranda opposite, of that boy, sunbathing stark naked without even his shorts on. Leaning slightly forward, he was gazing intently at some part of his own body. He seemed to believe that no one was watching him. My heart pounded violently. And straining my near-sighted eyes to see it better, narrowing them, I made out, upon his jet-black back as well, what seemed to be the same peculiar protrusion that Saegusa had. Feeling a sudden dizziness, I made my way back to bed with great difficulty, and lay face down upon it. The boy was discharged some days later, entirely unaware of the great blow he had dealt me.

Moyuru hō by Hori Tatsuo (Aozora Bunko)
