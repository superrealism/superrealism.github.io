About this work

The dog Shiro barking "dig here," gold pieces overflowing from the mortar, and ashes that make a dead tree bloom — one of the five great folk tales of Japan, retold by Kusuyama Masao in prose meant to be read aloud. Told through the contrast between an honest old man and a greedy one who copies him and fails: a representative tale of virtue rewarded.

Summary

Long ago an honest old man and old woman, having no children, doted on their dog Shiro as though he were their own child. One day, out in the field, Shiro took hold of the old man's skirt and barked, "Dig here, bow-wow!" They dug, and gold coins came pouring out. The greedy old man next door, seeing it, forced them to lend him Shiro, but all he dug up was rubbish; and in his rage he killed the dog. The honest old man grieved, and from the tree that grew where Shiro was buried he made a mortar; and when he pounded rice cakes in it, the cakes swelled and came pouring out. When the greedy old man copied him, nothing but filth came out of the mortar, and in his anger he burned it. On his way home with the ashes, the honest old man saw them blown by the wind onto a withered tree, and at once it burst into blossom. "The old man who makes flowers bloom, the finest in Japan at making flowers bloom — I'll make the withered trees blossom!" — and before a lord who happened to pass by, he made the cherry blossom splendidly, and was given many rewards. The greedy old man, envying him, tried the same thing; but no flowers came, and the ash flew into the eyes and noses of the lord and his retainers and raised a great commotion, and in the end he was dragged off to prison.

Characters

  • The honest old man　A good-hearted old man who dotes on his dog Shiro
  • Shiro　The dog who shows where the gold coins are
  • The greedy old man　The old man next door, who does nothing but copy him
  • The lord　Who rewards the old man for making the flowers bloom

The opening (original text)

Long, long ago, in a certain place, there were an old man and an old woman. They were both honest, good-natured people; but having no children, they doted on their dog Shiro as though he were a real child of their own. And Shiro too was very much attached to the old man and the old woman. Now, next door there were also an old man and an old woman. These were bad, greedy people.

Hanasaka jijii by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Just then a great wind came blowing up, and the ash scattered without ceremony in every direction, all over the place, and got into the eyes and noses of the lord and his retainers. Here and there they were rubbing their eyes, and sneezing, and beating the ash out of their hair, and there was a terrible commotion. The lord was greatly angered. "This must be a false flower-blooming old man. An outrageous fellow!" And so saying, he had the greedy old man bound.
