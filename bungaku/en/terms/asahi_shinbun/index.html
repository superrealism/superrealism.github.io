What is Asahi Shimbun?

Category
Society / Group

One of the great newspapers of modern Japan, founded in Osaka in 1879 (as the Osaka Asahi Shimbun) and expanding to Tokyo in 1888 with the Tokyo Asahi Shimbun. Devoted to literature, it hired Natsume Sōseki away from his university professorship in 1907 to be the flagship of its literary pages, and sent Futabatei Shimei abroad as a correspondent, greatly raising the standard of the newspaper novel. Sōseki serialized all his major novels in the paper, from "Gubijinsō" ("Poppy") through to his unfinished final work "Meian" ("Light and Darkness"), and it was on his recommendation that Nagatsuka Takashi's "Tsuchi" ("The Soil") and Naka Kansuke's "Gin no Saji" ("The Silver Spoon") were also published there. Futabatei Shimei's "Heibon" ("Mediocrity"), Tokuda Shūsei's "Kabi" ("Mold"), Shimazaki Tōson's "Shinsei" ("New Life"), Tanizaki Jun'ichirō's "Chijin no Ai" ("Naomi"), Nagai Kafū's "Bokutō Kitan" ("A Strange Tale from East of the River"), and Yoshikawa Eiji's "Miyamoto Musashi" are among the many masterworks of modern literature published in its pages from the Meiji era through the Shōwa era, and the Asahi Shimbun remained one of the most important venues for modern literature.
