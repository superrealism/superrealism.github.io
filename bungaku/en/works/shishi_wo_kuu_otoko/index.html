About this work

A grotesque short story published in the detective magazine Shin Seinen in 1927, unusual among the works of Hayama Yoshiki, known as a writer of labour literature. In the dormitory of a middle school in the mountains, Yasuoka and Fukaya are fifth-year boys sharing a room with only the two of them. On the nights when there is a fresh grave of a drowned baseball player, Fukaya listens for his roommate's breathing and slips out. Some things are better not known — having said so at the start, it tells straight through to what becomes of the one who did look.

Summary

The middle school stood deep in the mountains. On one side of the athletic field lay a shrine, on another a Kokubun-ji temple, and on the third a lake with a custom of claiming one drowned student every year. On the lake's north shore stood a slaughterhouse, on its south shore a graveyard. With seven new middle schools recently opened nearby, the dormitory had grown hollow and empty, and in a twenty-mat room only two graduating students remained: Yasuoka and Fukaya. At the end of October, one of the baseball team dove into the lake and never surfaced. "Seko-chan," the drowned boy, was buried in the graveyard overlooking the lake, and from then on Yasuoka's nerves grew steadily thinner with the loneliness of that too-large room. Fukaya, on the other hand — pale, thin, and seemingly the most timid of the two — would fall into a light snore the moment he lay down on his cot. On the seventh night, Yasuoka, drifting toward sleep, felt a person's warmth close to his face. Fukaya had been checking whether he was breathing in sleep, then turned on the light and left the room. He did not come back for two hours, three hours. The following night, Yasuoka kept up the pretense of sleep and followed Fukaya, who slipped out through a corridor window to the schoolyard like a gust of wind. Passing along the highway, crossing the winding road that followed the lake, he arrived at last at the graveyard. Peering in from outside the hedge on his stomach, Yasuoka's eyes fell on a sight too terrible for pen or paper to render. The next morning, Fukaya said quietly: "Around your mouth — it's all muddy, as if you'd eaten a corpse or something."

Characters

  • Yasuoka　A fifth-year student in the dormitory. His nerves wear thin with the loneliness of the empty room, and he follows his roommate on his nighttime excursion
  • Fukaya　A fifth-year student who shares Yasuoka's room. Pale, thin, and seemingly frail, yet he slips out of the room every night without a sound
  • Seko-chan　A baseball team member who dove into the lake and drowned. Buried in the graveyard

The opening (original text)

There must be many times, for you as well, when you feel there are things better left unknown. Your eye happens to fall on the newspaper's "today's fortune" column. If you have no idea whether you're a Seven Red or an Eight White, you have no complaint at all — but if you know you're a Two Black, and it says travel or money matters are inadvisable, well, it makes no real difference, you go ahead and do them anyway, but somewhere in a corner of your mind, that little thing clings on, stubborn and persistent.

Shishi o kū otoko by Hayama Yoshiki (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

So saying, in halting, broken words, he told of the scene he had witnessed — of eating the corpse. And with that he bade farewell to this hateful world. At that very same hour, just as Yasuoka breathed his last, Fukaya vanished without a trace while traveling far away. Some days later, Fukaya's body washed up on a beach. It was translucent, like marble.
