About this work

Kubota Mantarō's representative work, serialized in the "Osaka Asahi Shimbun" in 1928. In the Tokyo of after the Great Kantō Earthquake, walking from Asakusa to Mukōjima and from Tabata to Dōkanyama, the actor Tashiro and the haiku poets Ogura and Miura carry the whole book on their conversation alone. The embankment where the Ushijima Shrine has vanished, a river that is said to have burned, talk of friends who died in the earthquake. There is no plot to speak of, and the talk slides easily from gossip about people to landscapes of long ago — and through it seeps the atmosphere of the old downtown and the author's deep regret for what is being lost.

Summary

Coming up from the Mukōjima ferry, Tashiro the actor is stopped by two young women. Asked by Ogura who they were, Miura says bluntly, "Isn't that Chibisaburō's wife?" If she is an actor's wife, Miura grumbles to himself, she might at least greet him as one; and Ogura walks on turning over a verse about the bareness of winter. Along an embankment planted with nothing but young cherry trees, Tashiro suddenly stops. The Ushi no Gozen — the Ushijima Shrine — is gone without a trace. In the earthquake the whole place became a sea of fire, and even the water they counted on burned and ran, Miura says, so that those who jumped into the river were the ones who lost their lives. There is talk of Azuma, who died here, and gossip about why a man who ought to have been in Azabu should have died in such a way. Tashiro himself, when he was the second son of an oil dealer in Shōten-chō, ruined himself eloping with the adopted daughter of a Kiyomoto teacher. The three men's feet carry them from Mukōjima to Asakusa and from Tabata to Dōkanyama, where roofs to the horizon and a forest of chimneys tell the story of the "new Tokyo."

Characters

  • Tashiro　Once the second son of an oil dealer in Shōten-chō, who became an actor after eloping
  • Ogura　A haiku poet who walks turning verses over in his mind. He knows the old Tokyo well
  • Miura (Miura Kōbei)　A man who speaks his poison bluntly about everything. He saw the ruin of the earthquake with his own eyes

The opening (original text)

Mukōjima 1 …Coming up from the ferry, Tashiro was stopped by two young women. — Ogura and Miura walked on ahead toward Kototoi without waiting. "Who was that?" Ogura asked Tashiro when he caught them up a moment later. "That was — " Tashiro began, and then: "Kei-chan, you must know her?"

The ending (original text)

The great sky that spread above that scene. — And on that sky, clear as water, the sunset cloud that trailed its shadow…. Ogura looked at it and said nothing. — Loneliness rises and floats, a far spring cloud: quietly he was turning over some such verse in his mind. ———————————— …The three of them took the train from Tabata, got off at Ueno, and there took the underground railway to Asakusa. — They were making for the Kiku no Ya again, as they had done on the way back from Mukōjima that other time.
