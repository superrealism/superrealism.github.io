About this work

Seven young kids left to keep house, and a wolf who changes his voice and whitens his feet and knocks at the door. Kusuyama Masao's version for children of the Grimm tale in which three detections and three disguises — the voice, the feet, and at last the belly — are piled one upon another. It carries the original through to the end exactly as it runs, with only the youngest kid, hidden in the clock case, saved, and the mother goat cutting open the belly of the sleeping wolf and filling it with stones. In Japan too it has long been a standby of picture books and paper theater.

Summary

The mother goat, going out into the forest to fetch food, tells her seven young kids never to let the wolf in: "You will know him at once by his rough voice and his black feet." Before long someone knocks and says, "It is your mother"; but the voice is rough, and the kids will not open. The wolf eats chalk to make his voice thin, frightens the miller into whitening his forepaws with flour, and on the third attempt gets the door opened. The kids run to hide under the table, in the bed, in the stove; but all except the youngest, who hides in the case of the clock, are swallowed one after another. The mother goat comes home and learns what has happened from the wrecked house and from the youngest, trembling inside the clock. Finding the wolf asleep and snoring in the meadow outside, she cuts open his belly with her scissors, brings out the six of them alive, fills it with great stones instead, and sews it up. When the wolf wakes and goes to the well to drink, the weight of the stones in his belly drags him down into the water.

Characters

  • The mother goat　She warns her children to be careful before she goes into the forest
  • The seven young kids　The brothers left to keep house. Only the youngest, hidden in the clock, is saved
  • The wolf　He changes his voice with chalk, whitens his feet with flour, and at the third try gets the door opened
  • The miller　Frightened by the wolf into flouring his forepaws

The opening (original text)

1 There was once an old mother goat who had seven young kids, and she loved them as a mother loves her children — not in the least differently. One day the mother goat was going out into the forest to fetch food for her children, and she called all seven of them to her and said: "Dear children, I am going out into the forest. Be on your guard while I am gone, and never let the wolf into the house. If he gets in he will eat every one of you, skin and hair and all."

The Wolf and the Seven Young Kids by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"What rumbles and tumbles about in my belly? I thought it was six little kids, but it is nothing but cobblestones." So the wolf sang. And when he had struggled as far as the well and stooped over the water to drink, the weight of the stones in his belly dragged him down, and willy-nilly, wailing as he went, the wolf fell into the water. The seven kids, watching from a distance, came running up, crying, "The wolf is dead! The wolf is dead!" and danced round and round the well for joy, hand in hand with their mother.
