About this work

A historical novel Sakaguchi Ango published in the magazine Gendai Bungaku in January 1944 under the title "Kuroda Josui," and issued as a book in 1947 with new material added. Beside the great stars of the age, Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu, it draws the life of Kuroda Josui — Kanbei — an incomparable strategist who nevertheless never quite became ruler of the realm. From the Odawara campaign through the invasion of Korea to Sekigahara, it renders his ambition and his prudence and the futility of his dream, in a relaxed storyteller's voice mixed with humour: a central work among Ango's historical novels.

Summary

In the eighteenth year of Tenshō, in the encampment before the siege of Odawara, Tokugawa Ieyasu and Kuroda Josui meet face to face for the first time. Even as he supports Hideyoshi's bid for the realm as a strategist without peer in his age, Josui remains, always, a "second-rate man." For all his outstanding cunning and ambition, Hideyoshi keeps a wary eye on him, and at every crucial turn Josui is made to yield the first rank to another, never quite able to become a ruler of the realm himself. Tossed about by Hideyoshi's invasion of Korea, Josui, even in old age, cannot let go of his dream of carving out a domain of his own in Kyushu. Seizing the chance offered by the Battle of Sekigahara, though already retired from active service, he singlehandedly subdues Ishida's forces in Kyushu in an unexpected feat of arms — yet for this service Ieyasu, while richly rewarding Josui's son Nagamasa, gives Josui himself not so much as a single word of recognition. Ieyasu only laughs coldly, asking just whose benefit that schemer's exploits in Kyushu were really meant to serve. Later, Ieyasu invites Josui to Osaka and, with flattering words pleasant to the ear, offers him rank and land — but Josui, sensing the emptiness behind the offer, calmly declines. "The chaos that has run on since the Ōnin War has come to its end, and my own part in it is finished" — so Josui murmurs quietly to himself, never once realizing that his own life, too, had come to as empty and farcical a close as that very chaos. Ango, with his characteristic humor and a historian's eye for civilization and its critique, depicts the tragicomedy of a hero caught between the titans of his age, never able to become first-rate himself.

Characters

  • Kuroda Josui (Kanbei)　A strategist without peer in his age. Possessed of both the ambition to seek the realm and the good sense to restrain it, yet ultimately a "second-rate man" who never reaches the first rank
  • Tokugawa Ieyasu　The ruler of the realm, who sees through Josui's true capacity and treats his service coldly
  • Toyotomi Hideyoshi　The ruler of the realm, who employs Josui while keeping a wary eye on him. Tosses Josui about with his invasion of Korea
  • Kuroda Nagamasa　Josui's son. Richly rewarded by Ieyasu for his service at Sekigahara

The opening (original text)

Chapter One: At Odawara One It was the eighteenth year of Tenshō, at the very height of midsummer. Odawara lay under the siege of the Hōjō, with Hideyoshi's great army of two hundred and sixty thousand men laying siege on every side — across the mountains of Hakone and Ashigara, over the plains of Sagami, and along the whole expanse of the sea. In the Tokugawa encampment, Ieyasu and Kuroda Josui sat down together in conference. This day marked the first time the two men had ever set eyes on each other. One might say that on this day a single wedge was driven into the eventual fall of the house of Toyotomi — though Hideyoshi, diverting himself with Lady Yodo at Ishigakiyama, knew nothing at all of it.

Niryū no hito by Sakaguchi Ango (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The chaos that had run on unbroken since the Ōnin War has come to its end; my own part in it is finished — with this thought, Josui's heart cleared, plain and untroubled. The chaos was over. He gave no thought at all to the farcical, fleeting truth that his own life, too, had come to its end together with that chaos.

Niryū no hito by Sakaguchi Ango (Aozora Bunko)
