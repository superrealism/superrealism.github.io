Biography

Born in 1858 into a Shin Buddhist temple family in Ura Village, Mishima District, Echigo Province (now Nagaoka, Niigata Prefecture). He studied philosophy at Tokyo University and in 1887 founded the Philosophy Academy (now Tōyō University), opening the way for philosophical education broadly accessible to the general public. He toured the country tirelessly giving lectures, preaching the dismantling of superstition, while at the same time gathering and classifying accounts of the strange and uncanny in order to lay bare the reality behind superstition and give them psychological explanations — the discipline he called "yōkaigaku," or "monsterology." He was affectionately known as "Dr. Monster" or "Dr. Ghost." His writings include "Lectures on Monsterology," "Explaining Superstitions," "The True Nature of Ghosts," and "Popular Lectures on the Immortality of the Soul," and he also left many records of his inspection tours of the West and Asia. He died in 1919 in Dalian, China, while on a lecture tour.
