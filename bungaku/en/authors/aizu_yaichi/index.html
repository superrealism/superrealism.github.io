Biography

Born in 1881 in Niigata City. He wrote under the pen names Shūsōdojin and Konsai. From his middle-school days he immersed himself in the Man'yōshū and the poetry of Ryōkan, and graduated from the English literature department of Waseda University in 1906. After some years teaching in his home province, he was invited to Tokyo by Tsubouchi Shōyō in 1910 and later taught the history of East Asian art as a professor at Waseda. A devoted student of Nara's old temples and Buddhist art, he earned a doctorate in literature for this research in 1934. As a poet he developed a distinctive style of tanka in the manner of the Man'yōshū, written in hiragana with word breaks but no punctuation, and left the collections "Nankyō Shinshō" ("New Songs of the Southern Capital," i.e. Nara) and "Rokumeishū." He was also an accomplished calligrapher, a man of many talents who left his mark in poetry, scholarship, and calligraphy alike. In his later years he returned to his native Niigata and in 1951 was named an honorary citizen of Niigata City. He died in 1956.
