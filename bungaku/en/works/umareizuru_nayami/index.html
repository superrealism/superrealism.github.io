About this work

A central work by Arishima Takeo of the Shirakaba group, serialized in the Osaka Mainichi and Tokyo Nichinichi in March and April 1918 and published the same year with a newly written ending. It follows a young man who wanted to be a painter and had to throw himself instead into the hard life of a fisherman in the north to keep his family, watched and encouraged by the narrator, a writer. Modelled on the real painter Kida Kinjirō, it draws with force — against the rough sea and nature of Hokkaido — the anguish of a young man torn between an irrepressible longing for art and the facts of making a living. Still read by young people as a send-off for anyone trying to live and to make something.

Summary

One winter day, stuck in his own writing in Tokyo, the narrator remembers a young man who called on him once, some years before. He had come then with pictures he had painted, and confessed his urgent wish to live as an artist. But having to support a poor family, he gave up the way of painting and went home to Hokkaidō to live as a fisherman. The narrator imagines how the young man has been living since, out on the northern sea, and writes it out: the fishing that risks a man's life on the winter Sea of Japan, the brutal work against rock and wave, the body and the spirit honed in the violence of nature. And in the depths of the young man there smolders, like fire, a longing for the brush that he cannot cut away. He gave up art for the sake of living; and yet every time he meets the beauty of the mountains or the sea, an urge to paint rises in him and becomes "the pain of being born." The narrator calls to him not to be crushed by the real world but to guard and feed that inner fire; and, believing in the spring that must follow winter, he prays with his whole heart that a strong spring may come to the young man's soul.

Characters

  • The young man (Kimoto)　A youth who lives as a fisherman in Hokkaidō to support his poor family while holding to the dream of becoming a painter. He is modeled on the painter Kida Kinjirō
  • I　The writer and narrator, who watches the young man's gift and his suffering and urges him to guard and feed the fire of art within him

The opening (original text)

I was trying to make my work into something sacred. Whipping my own heart when it tried to twist itself out of shape, I was struggling to come out into as free and straight and bright a world as I could, and to build there the palace of my art. How joyful a thing that was for me. And at the same time, how painful a thing.

Umareizuru nayami by Arishima Takeo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

My friend! Winter is over in Tokyo too now, and the plum and the camellia are in bloom. The earth throws its breast open and drinks in the light of compassion the sun brings forth. Spring is coming. My friend, spring is coming. After winter comes spring. May an everlasting spring smile upon you too, surely, rightly, and with strength.... That is all I pray, with my whole heart.

Umareizuru nayami by Arishima Takeo (Aozora Bunko)

Famous passages

My friend, spring is coming. After winter, spring comes.

The ending. Words the author addresses to a young man in a Hokkaido fishing village, torn between his passion for painting and the hardships of making a living. A prayer for the spring that surely comes after suffering.
