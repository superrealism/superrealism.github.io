About this work

An enormous novel following the lives of countless people wandering through Japan in the upheaval from the end of the shogunate into the Meiji era, with the nihilist swordsman Tsukue Ryūnosuke — who cut down an old pilgrim for no reason at the Daibosatsu pass in Kai — as its pivot. Serialization began in the Miyako Shimbun in 1913 and continued for twenty-eight years across changing papers and magazines, left unfinished by the author's sudden death. Praised as a pioneer of popular literature by Kikuchi Kan, Tanizaki Jun'ichirō and others of its time, it influenced later period fiction beyond measure. All forty-one volumes are carried here separately.
