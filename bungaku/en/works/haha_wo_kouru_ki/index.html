About this work

A short story Tanizaki serialized in the Osaka Mainichi and Tokyo Nichinichi in 1919, raising into a dreamlike story his inexhaustible longing for the mother he loved as a child. A dream in which the narrator, in the shape of a small boy, wanders a road by the sea in bright moonlight searching for his mother — and the beautiful young woman he meets at last is his mother as she was in her youth. When he wakes, she is no longer in this world. A fine piece of Tanizaki's writing on longing for a mother, set down in a sweet and fantastical style.

Summary

On a moonlit night, "I" wander alone along a white highway beside some unknown sea, searching for my mother. Along a road lined with pine trees, the sound of a shamisen drifts from somewhere, and a child's crying voice can be heard: "I want fried tempura, I want fried tempura." "I" am a timid child of seven or eight, my heart filled with a helpless sorrow — my family's once-lively house in Nihonbashi has fallen into ruin, we have moved to some remote countryside, and the wet nurse who once doted on me is gone. Counting the telegraph poles, one, two, as I press on along the lonely night road, I come at last upon a beautiful young woman. That elegant figure is none other than my mother, in the days of her youth. …… Before long, "I" wake from the dream. It seems I had truly been weeping in my sleep, for my pillow is damp with tears. I am now thirty-four years old, and my mother passed from this world in the summer of two years before. At this thought, fresh tears fall once more upon the pillow. The sound of that shamisen still echoes, faint and far, at the bottom of my ear, like a visitation from the world beyond. A masterpiece of Tanizaki's "mother-yearning" tales, in which he renders, in a sweet and fantastical style, an inexhaustible longing for a lost mother.

Characters

  • I　A child (the "I" within the dream) wandering alone along a moonlit seaside road in search of my mother. My family's house has fallen to ruin, my wet nurse is gone, and my heart is filled with an unaccountable sorrow. On waking I am thirty-four, and my mother passed away two years before
  • Mother　Appears, young and beautiful, before "me" in the dream — the lingering image of my late mother

The opening (original text)

Is it a bird that yearns for days long past, that cries and wings its way across, above Mitsui, past the yuzuriha's leaves? 　　— The Man'yōshū — ……The sky hangs dull and overcast, and the moon lies swallowed deep within the clouds, and yet some light must be leaking through from somewhere, for the world outside glows faintly, whitely bright. That brightness, thought about, seems bright enough — bright enough that even the pebbles at the roadside show up clear — and yet, somehow, a haze hangs mistily before the eyes, so that to stare hard into the distance leaves the eyes feeling strangely ticklish: a strange, dreamlike brightness, unlike any other.

Haha o kōru ki by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

I awoke with a start. It seemed I had truly been weeping in my dream, for my pillow was damp with tears. This year I turn thirty-four. And my mother has been gone from this world since the summer of two years before. —— At this thought, another fresh tear fell, drop by drop, upon the pillow. "I want fried tempura, I want fried tempura. ………" That sound of the shamisen still echoed, far and faint, at the bottom of my ear, like a visitation from the world beyond.
