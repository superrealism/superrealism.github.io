What is Dōyō (Children's Songs)?

Category
Form / Genre

Artistic songs created for children, which arose on the pages of the children's magazine "Akai Tori," founded in 1918. Born as one wing of the movement, championed by Suzuki Miekichi, that respected the child's own heart — in reaction against the moralistic, textbook-style school songs (shōka) of the Ministry of Education, remote from any child's real feelings — poets competed to write beautiful Japanese songs attuned to children's hearts. Kitahara Hakushū, who selected children's songs for "Akai Tori," left many masterpieces, including "Karatachi no Hana" ("The Trifoliate Orange Blossoms") and "Kono Michi" ("This Road"), and became the genre's leading figure; Saijō Yaso's "Kanaria" ("The Canary"), published in "Akai Tori," set to music by Narita Tamezō, became the first widely sung children's song. Together with Noguchi Ujō, known for "Nanatsu no Ko" ("Seven Baby Crows") and "Jūgoya Otsukisan" ("The Full Moon of the Fifteenth Night"), Hakushū, Yaso, and Ujō came to be called the three great children's-song poets. The movement continued into the early Shōwa era, and children's songs came to be sung by children across the country, utterly transforming the landscape of Japanese children's songs.
