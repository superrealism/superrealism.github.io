About this work

A play written in 1916 and staged by Ichikawa Sadanji II, a central work of the new kabuki. It boldly recasts the famous ghost story of the plate mansion as a human tragedy with no ghost in it. The single-minded love between the hatamoto Aoyama Harima and the maid Okiku, and the fierce pride of a samurai whose sincerity has been doubted, drive toward tragedy around the famous scene of the breaking of the plates.

Summary

Edo, at the beginning of the Meireki era. Aoyama Harima, a bannerman who belongs to the Shiratsuka gang and spends his days in brawls, is urged by his aunt Mayumi to settle down, and is cool about the match she proposes. Harima has a love of his own — O-Kiku, a maid in his household. But O-Kiku is tormented by the rumor that a wife is to be brought to Harima from another house, and to test whether his love is true she deliberately breaks one of the Korean plates that have been handed down in the Aoyama family, for which the penalty is death by the master's own hand. Harima at first passes it over as an accident; but learning that she broke it on purpose, and that she did it to test his heart, he is enraged. It is not the plate he minds: what he cannot bear is that the single-hearted love he had given without a shadow of falsehood should be doubted. He smashes the remaining plates himself to show what the plates are worth to him. And even so his bitterness at having his true love doubted is not spent; he has the pleading O-Kiku dragged out into the garden, cuts her down with a single stroke and has her thrown into the well. His family treasure shattered and the love of his life destroyed, Harima takes up his spear and runs out into the night streets, ready to be disowned by his aunt and to ruin himself in the trade of brawling.

Characters

  • Aoyama Harima　A bannerman of the Shiratsuka gang. He spends his days in brawls, and loves the maid O-Kiku single-heartedly
  • O-Kiku　A maid of the Aoyama house and Harima's love, who doubts the rumor of a match and breaks a plate to test him
  • Shibata Jūdayū　The steward of the Aoyama house, a scrupulous man who examines the matter of the plates
  • Gonji and Gonroku　Harima's menservants, who beg him to spare O-Kiku's life
  • Mayumi　Harima's aunt, the widow of Shibukawa, who reproves his brawling and presses the match on him
  • Hanarekoma Shirobei　A town rowdy of Banzuiin Chōbei's following, who comes to blows with Harima

The opening (original text)

HARIMA: The cherries have come out well. GONJI: They might be artificial flowers, my lord. GONROKU: One might think somebody had torn up the red paper streamers of Tanabata and blown them all over the place at once. HARIMA: Well, well — what a fellow you are for complicated speeches. Say it in a word: they are like the eaves decked out for a festival. Ha ha ha ha ha.

The ending (original text)

HARIMA: The treasure of my house is shattered. The love of Harima's life is destroyed. GONROKU: If I may report, my lord: Mizuno Jūrōzaemon was on his way here when the town rowdies blocked his road; there are a great many of them, and with one thing and another it looks as though the flower of a quarrel will bloom. GONJI: Hm — then those same fellows from a while ago must still be hanging about there. HARIMA: Good. Harima will go at once and scatter the rowdies for them. JŪDAYŪ: My lord. Another brawl — HARIMA: Do you tell me to stop? Having lost the love of my life... what is a man to live for from now on? Let my aunt disown me if she will. I shall rampage through the eight hundred and eight blocks of this city, and make a trade of quarreling day and night. And to begin with — fellows! Come along! (Harima runs out in his tabi with no sandals. Gonji and Gonroku set themselves straight and follow after. Jūdayū looks after them.) — Curtain —
