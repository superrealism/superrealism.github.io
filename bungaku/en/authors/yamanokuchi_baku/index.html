Biography

Born in 1903 in Naha, Okinawa Prefecture; his real name was Jūzaburō. He dropped out of the prefectural First Middle School and went to Tokyo, where for roughly the next twenty years he lived in extreme poverty with no steady job or fixed address. Sleeping rough and taking day-labor jobs, he kept writing poetry, selling manuscripts to buy rice, until he was recognized by Satō Haruo, Kaneko Mitsuharu, and others and published the poetry collections "Shiben no Sono" ("The Garden of Poetic Argument") and "Yamanokuchi Baku Shishū" ("The Poems of Yamanokuchi Baku"). In plain colloquial language shot through with humor and melancholy, he sang of poverty, marriage, and his native Okinawa, and after the war left works imbued with deep feeling for Okinawa. He died in 1963 of stomach cancer at the age of 59.
