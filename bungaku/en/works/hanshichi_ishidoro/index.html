About this work

The second story of the Hanshichi series, serialized in Bungei Kurabu from 1917 — the first genuine Japanese detective fiction in Edo dress, in which the old former constable Hanshichi tells the tales of his own successes. Here Okiku, a haberdasher's daughter, vanishes without trace, and afterwards appears and disappears like a ghost; from the footprints at a single stone lantern Hanshichi draws in the truth. The origin of the whole detective-in-Edo genre, and rich in the feeling of the old city.

Summary

What old Hanshichi tells as "the beginning of my reputation" is his first success, when he was still nineteen and an assistant to a constable. At the end of the twelfth year of Tenpō, O-Kiku, the only daughter of the Kikumura, a haberdasher's in Nihonbashi, goes to worship the Kannon at Asakusa, becomes separated from the maid who went with her, and disappears. And then the next day O-Kiku appears at the shop barefoot, having left her clogs behind, walks into the back of the house before the eyes of the household, and vanishes without a trace. No one saw her come or go, and the gate had been shut all the while; the people of the shop are terrified at what seems the work of a ghost. Following the trick of it, young Hanshichi notices faint marks of toes in the moss of the stone lantern by the high wall of the garden. Only someone very light of body could go up and down that wall so nimbly — an acrobat. Reasoning so, he comes to Harukaze Koyanagi, a woman acrobat of doubtful reputation who appears at the booths in Ryōgoku. Koyanagi had joined with a younger man, Kinji, to deceive O-Kiku and sell her far away. Cornered at last, Koyanagi turns like a swallow from the middle of the Ryōgoku bridge and throws herself into the Ōkawa. O-Kiku is recovered safely, and Hanshichi's name rises throughout Edo — a fine piece in which the atmosphere of Edo and a clean solution melt together.

Characters

  • Hanshichi　A capable constable of Edo, who tells the tales of his old successes as an old man. This is his first success, at nineteen, when he sees the truth from the marks on a stone lantern
  • O-Kiku　The only daughter of the haberdasher Kikumura, who disappears on her way to worship and is deceived and sold away by the acrobats
  • Harukaze Koyanagi　A woman acrobat at the booths in Ryōgoku, who deceives O-Kiku with Kinji and, cornered, throws herself into the Ōkawa

The opening (original text)

1 Old Hanshichi once told me in detail about what his position had been in the old days. For the convenience of those who read detective stories of the Edo period, I should like to pass a little of it on here.

The ending (original text)

"You ask whether I have any more interesting stories? Of my own successes I have any number. Ha ha ha ha. Come and see me again one of these days." "I shall certainly come to have another from you." And having made my promise with old Hanshichi, I took my leave.
