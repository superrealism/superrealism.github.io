About this work

A short story published in the magazine Ryōki in 1928. A small brother and sister, cast ashore from a wreck, grow up alone together on a desert island; their story is given in three letters. Found sealed in three beer bottles, the letters are deliberately presented out of sequence, so that what begins as an idyll of paradise turns, as one reads on, into a story of forbidden love and ruin. Still highly regarded for the shock of its ending and the ingenuity of its construction, compressed into very few pages.

Summary

On an isolated island in the southern seas, three beer bottles are found washed ashore. Inside were letters asking for help, from a brother, Tarō, and his sister, Ayako, who as small children had drifted alone onto the island from a wreck. The first letter read contains the anguished words in which the two ask God to forgive them for holding each other fast and throwing themselves into the sea, though a rescue ship has appeared at last. The remaining two set down the sturdy survival of the small brother and sister — making fire, getting nuts and fish — and their bewilderment as, growing up, their feeling for each other turns into something troubling and joyful and lonely. Suffering under a feeling that goes against God's teaching, the paradise that was theirs alone turns by degrees into a hell from which there is no going back. In what order the three letters were really written is never disclosed to the reader, and there the story closes.

Characters

  • Tarō　The brother, cast ashore on an uninhabited island as a small child after a wreck. He survives there with his sister Ayako
  • Ayako　Tarō's sister. Raised on the lonely island with her brother, she comes to feel something complicated as she grows

The opening (original text)

With respect. I trust that you are in the best of health at this season, and offer my congratulations. To the matter: as previously notified, I had given general instructions to the islanders that any beer bottle sealed with red wax, apparently for the study of currents, should be reported and delivered as soon as it was picked up; and it is now reported that three beer bottles sealed with resin, such as the enclosed parcel, were discovered washed ashore on the south coast of this island.

The ending (original text)

FATHER. MOTHER. WE BROTHER AND SISTER ARE LIVING ON THIS ISLAND TOGETHER AND IN GOOD HEALTH. PLEASE COME AND SAVE US SOON. Ichikawa Tarō ICHIKAWA AYAKO

Famous passages

Ah... At last a ship has come to rescue us from this lonely island.

The opening of the letter in the first bottle. The letters in the three bottles that drifted ashore are arranged in reverse chronological order, and from this line of joy at the sight of a rescue ship, the hidden hell of a brother and sister is revealed backward in time.
