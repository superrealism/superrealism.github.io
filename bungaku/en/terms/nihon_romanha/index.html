What is Nihon Roman-ha (Japan Romantic School)?

Category
Literary Movement

The literary thought of the coterie magazine "Nihon Roman-ha," founded in March 1935, and of the writers and critics affiliated with it. It was led by Yasuda Yojūrō, with Kamei Katsuichirō, Jinbō Kōtarō, Nakatani Takao, and Ogata Takashi among its founding members. It is distinguished by championing a return to Japan's own classical beauty and a critique of the modern, and, wielding "irony" as learned from German Romanticism, sharply rejecting the realist literature descended from Naturalism, which depicted the mundane just as it was. It became a vessel filling the ideological vacuum left after the crushing of the proletarian literature movement under suppression, and its coterie numbered more than fifty by the time it ceased publication. In 1935, Dazai Osamu, drawing on his own experience as a survivor of a failed love suicide, published in its pages "Dōke no Hana" ("The Flowers of Buffoonery"), a story built on the double structure of the fiction itself and the author's self-consciousness in writing it; Dan Kazuo, Itō Shizuo, and Haga Mayumi joined as well. The magazine ceased publication in 1938. Taking on a strongly nationalist coloring under wartime conditions, it drew severe criticism after the war, but its stance of pressing modern skepticism to its furthest point also cast a shadow over the writers of later generations.
