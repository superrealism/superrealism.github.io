About this work

A documentary essay based on old records preserved on Mount Kōya, published in the magazine Kotōtaman in September 1931. Kakukai Shōnin, who served in the Kamakura period as the thirty-seventh administrator of Mount Kōya, is said to have died believing that he would be reborn after death as a tengu in the demon realm. Tanizaki copied out himself the sermons of Nanshōbō and the life of Kakukai preserved at the Zōfukuin, and traces the origin of that belief, quoting the story of the priest's former life as the great master told it to him.

Summary

"If one turns the ordinary mind, then the very body bound by karma is itself the Pure Land of true recompense, the ground one depends on and dwells in" — the priest who left behind this saying of Nanshōbō was Kakukai Shōnin, who lived at Mount Kōya in the Kamakura period. I visited the Zōfukuin, which preserves his surviving writings, and, through the kindness of its head priest, copied out the old documents in their entirety, every one of them. According to the Kii Zoku Fudoki, tengu, though a kind of demon spirit, are divided into good tengu and evil tengu, and are not necessarily enemies of the Buddhist Law. The holy man, on one occasion, prayed with tears of blood that his former life be shown to him, and received a manifestation of the great master. He was told that he had been born a small clam in the southern sea off Settsu, drifted a thousand years upon the waves, and become a horse ridden on pilgrimage to Kumano — and the holy man to whom this was revealed had, it is said, foreknowledge of his own life to come as well.

Characters

  • Kakukai Shōnin (Nanshōbō)　A high priest of Mount Kōya in the Kamakura period. Believed he would be reborn in the demon realm after death
  • Kōbō Daishi　Manifests his true form in answer to the holy man's prayer, and tells him of his former life
  • Master Jūhō　Head priest of Zōfukuin. Allows Tanizaki to copy out the old documents

The opening (original text)

It is said in the sayings of Nanshōbō: "Nan says: because I hold to no attachment among the Ten Realms, I wander at play among the nine realms, and according to the changes of each thought I take on a body to depend upon; being so, I am free to dwell or not dwell in any of the Ten Realms." This priest called Nanshōbō is none other than Kakukai Shōnin, who is said to have been raised, in the fifth year of Kenpō under Emperor Juntoku, to the position of Hokkyō and thirty-seventh administrator and overseer of Mount Kōya — a man, that is, of some seven hundred years ago as reckoned from today, of the Kamakura period, in the time of Sanetomo.

Kakukai shōnin tengu ni naru koto by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The holy man's mausoleum stands at Henjōgaoka in the mountains, though one account says he was buried instead by the pond within the grounds of Kaōin, and other traces of him remain even now in the garden of Zōfukuin. The eulogy at the end of the Kakukai biography closes: "If one so much as takes a dry branch or a fallen leaf from the cave at Henjōgaoka, a stern curse is visited upon him; his power and his divinity are to be believed, and to be feared. Whether the attainment he achieved was of the highest, middling, or lowest order, was he, in every respect, a Buddha in this very body? How strange indeed, this samadhi of play."

Kakukai shōnin tengu ni naru koto by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)
