What is Shajitsu-shugi (Realism)?

Category
Literary Movement

The founding principle of modern Japanese literature, rejecting the moralistic "reward good, punish evil" convention of Edo gesaku and championing the depiction of human interiority and social conditions just as they are. Tsubouchi Shōyō lit the theoretical fuse in the critical work "Shōsetsu Shinzui" ("The Essence of the Novel," 1885–86), declaring "the essence of the novel is human feeling," and attempted to put this into practice in his own "Tōsei Shosei Katagi," though he could not fully break free of the old style. Futabatei Shimei, Shōyō's disciple, influenced by Russian realist literature, published "Ukigumo" ("Drifting Clouds," 1887–89) in genbun'itchi prose, and, equipped with modern psychological depiction, this work brought realism into true being for the first time. This principle went on to be carried forward by the pseudo-classicism that sought beauty in the classics of Edo literature and by the writers of the Kōro era, and became the wellspring of the modern novel leading into the Romanticism that followed the First Sino-Japanese War and the Naturalism of the late Meiji era.
