About this work

"Moon, how old are you? Thirteen and seven" — a short piece in which Kitahara Hakushū gathers the children's songs handed down all over the country, sets out how they are sung differently from region to region, and speaks gently to the reader. Cherishing the child's mind that sees the moon as a young and beautiful aunt, he shows the variants from Tokyo, Ise, Echigo, Kii, Owari and Rikuchū. A piece conveying the deep affection for traditional children's song of a poet who gave his life to the nursery-song movement.

Summary

"Moon, how old are you? / Thirteen and seven. / Still so young a thing" — the piece begins with this song handed down in Tokyo. It goes on, "I bore this child, I bore that child, who shall hold her, let Oman hold her..." and continues, without any real connective sense, at a good clip, from a story about spilled oil to a dog's drum — a nursery song of just this shape. Hakushū writes that as children they too used to go out and clap their hands, singing to worship the red, round moon, and introduces the many different, corrupted forms this children's song has taken across the country. In Ise it is "Moon, dear moon, how old are you"; in Echigo, "How old, dear lady moon"; in Kii, "How old, big sister." All these variants share the common feature of picturing the moon as a young and beautiful woman. "That's exactly how it feels, isn't it," Hakushū agrees. He goes on to cite a song (from Owari) in which the moon grows angry at being called "peach-coloured," and a spirited song of an Edo child (from Tokyo): "Our precious moon, hidden by the clouds — if you must hide her, hide her behind a gold-leaf screen." "Truly, nothing but a gold-leaf screen would suit that young lady moon, would it," he comments. He closes with "Rabbit, rabbit, what do you see, leaping so? Seeing the moon of the fifteenth night, leaping, hop, hop" — telling of the rabbit's joy on a moonlit night, and closing with the thought that inside that round, round moon, surely, a rabbit must always be pounding rice cakes with a mallet.

The opening (original text)

Moon, how old are you? Thirteen and seven. Still so young a thing. I bore this child, I bore that child, who shall hold her? Let Oman hold her. Where has Oman gone? To buy oil, to buy tea. (Tokyo)

Otsukisama ikutsu by Kitahara Hakushū (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

You must know the song "Rabbit, Rabbit" too, I should think. Rabbit, rabbit, what do you see, leaping so? Seeing the moon of the fifteenth night, leaping — hop, hop. Truly, there is nothing quite like the joy of a rabbit on a moonlit night. ...And come to think of it, inside that round, round moon, surely, a rabbit must always be pounding rice cakes with a mallet.

Otsukisama ikutsu by Kitahara Hakushū (Aozora Bunko)
