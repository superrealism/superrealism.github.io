About this work

In 1852, two young men in travelling clothes make for the climbing route up Fuji — a long novel by Yamamoto Shūgorō set against the first stirrings at the end of the shogunate. Young men wavering between expelling the barbarians and opening the country, between study and action, begin to move as each of them believes right. The final scene, in which a woman holds up a hand candle amid blood and shouting without flinching in the least, drawn as though the sounds of it were being carved into the soul, stays with one.

Summary

On a day at the beginning of the fifth month of the fifth year of Kaei, two young men in traveling dress set out from before the Sengen shrine in the village of Ōmiya in Fuji district in the province of Suruga toward the climbing path of Fuji. The old shrine keeper sweeping the approach called after them and asked whether they were going up the mountain — and from there the story begins. On the eve of the coming of the black ships, the world had begun quietly to creak. ... What the young men throw themselves into is a world where learning and the affairs of the country cross. "To strike the barbarians... what your teacher says... that is the doctrine of ignorance." "I think... hold your tongue." The arguments heard from far off are split over expelling the foreigners or opening the country. Hidenoshin and Daisuke face the times each from his own position, and go, one to Kiso, another to a shop in Osaka, another to the foundry in the Bishū domain, to see things for themselves. The question "who is to take the command?" is thrown out, and the resolve of the young men is tried. In time matters go past the point where they can be avoided. At the close, Hidenoshin opens the rain shutters. The light of the hand candle streams out into the front garden, and two or three figures leap into the darkness like flung stones. "Daisuke, the clouds are breaking; tomorrow will be fine," he says, and walks off into the dark; and a scream and a roar rise, and the sound of feet kicking the ground tears the night air. Daisuke sinks down with a groan of pain, and Fujio, holding up the hand candle, biting her lip until the blood nearly comes and yet with no trace of flinching, listened intently to the sounds outside.

Characters

  • Hidenoshin　A young man who chooses to act in the current of the times
  • Daisuke　The friend who goes with Hidenoshin
  • Fujio　The woman who holds up the hand candle and does not flinch

And Fujio — she, holding up the hand candle, biting her lip until the blood nearly came, and yet with an expression that did not flinch in the least, listened intently to the sounds outside as they died away. As though she were carving those sounds into her very soul.
