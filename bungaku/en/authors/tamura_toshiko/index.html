Biography

Born in Asakusa, Tokyo, in 1884; her real name was Satō Toshi. She left Japan Women's University without graduating to study under Kōda Rohan, and entered the literary world in 1911 when "Akiramé" ("Resignation") won a prize in the Osaka Asahi Shimbun's fiction competition. With "Miira no Kuchibeni" ("The Mummy's Lipstick") and "Hōraku no Kei" ("Death by Roasting"), which boldly depicted a woman's sensuality and the ache of her selfhood, she became a representative woman writer of the Taishō era. She later moved to Canada and then to China, and died abroad in Shanghai in 1945 while publishing a women's magazine there. After her death the Tamura Toshiko Prize was established in her name.
