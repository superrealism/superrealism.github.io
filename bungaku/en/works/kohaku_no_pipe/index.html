About this work

The work that made Kōga Saburō's name, published in "Shinseinen" in 1924. The setting is the high ground of Shibuya after the Great Kantō Earthquake, where the vigilante bands have turned into night-watch bands. On a night of storm, "I," a clerk in the Ministry of ——, is on watch with a retired army colonel and a newspaper reporter. Soon after the changeover comes the cry of fire. In the inner room of the burnt-out house lies a single body, sunk in a pool of blood. Who, and how — and as one after another of the watchmen becomes a suspect, the case rolls off in an unexpected direction. A piece worked out to the last detail, showing the author's pride in the "orthodox" school that prized logic.

Summary

A little over a month after the Great Kantō Earthquake. On the high ground of Shibuya the vigilante band goes on under the new name of night watch. "I" am a clerk of forty-odd in the Ministry of ——, and once a week I must go out in the middle of the night beating the wooden clappers. On the night a typhoon is forecast to strike the capital, I come out for the watch beginning at twelve and find waiting for me Aoki Shinya, a retired army colonel, head of the night watch and an advocate of expanding the armed forces, and Matsumoto Junzō, a newspaper reporter who argues for abolishing the night watch. The two fight out their argument at a pitch to match the storm, and in the end Aoki curses a neighbor named Fukushima who will not take his turn on watch: "A house like his ought to be burnt to the ground." At one thirty-five the two go out on the last round. Aoki says he will look in at his own house on the way and leaves the others; Matsumoto comes back first, and Aoki returns at two. Just as the watch changes and I am starting for home, a cry of fire comes out of the storm. It is Fukushima's house that is burning. In the inner room of the ruin, in blood that has blackened the mats, lies a body, savagely killed. Aoki, or Matsumoto, or — . What rises to the surface at last is a jewel robbery from before the earthquake, and a single cipher-like sign.

Characters

  • I　A clerk in the Ministry of ——. Forty-odd years old, he takes his turn on the night watch unwillingly — the narrator
  • Aoki Shinya　A retired army colonel. Head of the night watch and an advocate of expanding the armed forces
  • Matsumoto Junzō　A young man who calls himself a newspaper reporter. He argues for abolishing the watch and for cutting the armed forces
  • Fukushima　Master of the newly built house that backs onto Aoki's. He will not take his turn on watch and is the target of abuse
  • Iwami　A man caught up in the affair of the jewels

The opening (original text)

Even now the scene of that night makes me shudder to remember it. It was not long after the great earthquake in Tokyo. After ten that evening the look of the sky did indeed turn threatening, and with the sound of the typhoon great drops of rain began to fall one by one. That morning I had seen in the paper that a typhoon would strike the capital in the night, so that I had worried about it all day at the office; and unluckily the observatory's forecast proved perfectly correct.

The Amber Pipe by Kōga Saburō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

So as not to startle you, and partly to prove my own skill and partly as a keepsake of me forever, I have put that amber pipe into your inside pocket. It is nothing suspicious; pray use it with an easy mind.
