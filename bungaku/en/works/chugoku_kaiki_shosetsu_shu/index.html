About this work

One of Okamoto Kidō's central labours: two hundred and twenty strange tales selected and translated from Chinese fiction and miscellanies between the Six Dynasties and the Qing. It takes the form of a ghost-story gathering — people assembled at the Blue Frog Hall in Koishikawa introduce the books of the strange in turn, period by period — and from famous works such as Soushen ji, Youyang zazu, Yijian zhi, Jiandeng xinhua, Zi buyu and Yuewei caotang biji come tales of women whose heads fly off, the peony lantern, the nine-tailed fox and the human-faced sore. It is also an excellent door to seeing how deeply Chinese fiction has fallen across Japanese tales, rakugo, and the work of Bakin and Enchō. Published in 1935. The general note, the opening address and fifteen talks are carried here separately.
