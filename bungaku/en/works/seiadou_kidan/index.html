About this work

Okamoto Kidō's central collection of ghost stories. On the third of March, twelve guests invited to the house of the master of the Blue Frog Hall on the heights of Azabu tell in turn, through one snowy night, the uncanny things they have seen — in the form of the old game of a hundred tales. The blue frog god, the Tone ferry, the souls of a brother and sister, the crab, the well at Shimizu, the flute mound, Ryōma's pond: most of the stories are drawn from ordinary life between the Edo and Meiji periods, and rather than raising the voice they let a chill gather inside a hushed telling. With the Hanshichi stories, Kidō's high point, and a classic of the modern Japanese ghost story.

Summary

About noon on the third of March a special-delivery letter was thrown into "my" house. It was an invitation from the master of the Seiadō in Azabu, who wrote that, having obtained a green-frog god brought over from China, he wished to hold a gathering of ghost stories that evening. Twelve people, old and young, men and women, gathered through the falling snow. The master first told a strange Chinese tale about the green-frog god, and with that for a beginning the guests set out each his own experience. The uncanny night a blind masseur met at the Tone ferry. The story of a brother and sister whose souls could only have changed places. A crab as red as blood. The clear-water well that someone broke the prohibition and looked into. The mound where the sound of a flute would not stop. The rancor of a horse sunk in a pond. With each change of teller the room grows colder and the stories lean further into the dark. In the photograph the master had taken last of all, there appeared, faintly, the figure of a boy who could not have been there. The uncanny had not stayed within the storytelling; it had been stealing up on the night itself.

Characters

  • I　The narrator, invited to the gathering of ghost stories at the Seiadō
  • The master of the Seiadō　Who lives on the heights of Azabu and, having got the green-frog god, holds a gathering of ghost stories
  • The twelve guests　The men and women who each tell of an uncanny thing they saw or heard

The opening (original text)

The green-frog god I "Special delivery!" About noon on the third of March a letter by special delivery was thrown into the entrance of my house.

Seiadō kidan by Okamoto Kidō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"What seems to me strange further is that in one of the five photographs I took that day the figure of a boy appears, faintly. It is thin as a shadow, and of course I cannot make it out clearly; but it does somehow seem to me to be the figure of Shōkichi. How are those you took? I should be most grateful if you would let me know the result of the developing." That was about the sense of it; so I at once developed the ones I had taken myself, but nothing that looked like the shadow of a person was to be found anywhere. What sort of shadow appears in Yokota's photograph I cannot tell, since I have not seen the thing itself.

Seiadō kidan by Okamoto Kidō (Aozora Bunko)
