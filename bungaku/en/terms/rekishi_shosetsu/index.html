What is Rekishi Shōsetsu (Historical Fiction)?

Category
Form / Genre

Fiction that draws on the historical record and real figures of the past to depict human beings within history. In modern literature, its true starting point is generally taken to be Mori Ōgai's "Okitsu Yagoemon no Isho" ("The Testament of Okitsu Yagoemon," 1912), written after Ōgai was shaken by the death of General Nogi Maresuke, who took his own life to follow the Meiji Emperor into death; Ōgai went on to immerse himself in historical fiction and biography with works such as "Abe Ichizoku" ("The Abe Family," 1913) and "Sanshō Dayū" (1915). The two stances Ōgai laid out in his essay "Rekishi Sonomama to Rekishibanare" ("History As It Was, and Departure from History," 1915) — remaining faithful to historical fact, and freely creating apart from historical fact — became the classic framework for thinking about this genre. Many writers left representative works in this form: Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's court tales, drawing on the classics to read modern psychology into them; Kikuchi Kan's "On to no Kanata ni" ("Beyond Forgiveness and Enmity," 1919); Tanizaki Jun'ichirō's "Mōmoku Monogatari" ("A Blind Man's Tale," 1931); and Nakajima Atsushi's "Ri Ryō" ("Li Ling"), engraving questions of human existence into the world of the Chinese classics. As against the jidai shōsetsu, chiefly aimed at entertainment, it is distinguished by placing its weight on the interpretation of historical fact and on human interiority.
