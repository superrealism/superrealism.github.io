Biography

Born in Yokohama, Kanagawa Prefecture, in 1892. Born into a family descended from low-ranking samurai of the Odawara domain that had fallen into poverty, he went to work from boyhood, drifting through numerous jobs — inlay craftsman, ship carpenter, newspaper reporter — while honing his literary gifts largely self-taught. He became a popular writer with "Naruto Hichō" ("The Secret Scroll of Naruto"), published in 1925, and "Miyamoto Musashi," serialized in a newspaper from 1935, achieved a popularity unprecedented in the history of newspaper fiction. Through great works drawing on history and Chinese classics — "Sangokushi" ("Romance of the Three Kingdoms"), "Shin Heike Monogatari" ("New Tale of the Heike"), "Shihon Taiheiki" ("My Version of the Great Peace") — he captured a wide readership with stories of loyalty, human feeling, and personal growth, and became a truly national writer, celebrated as a "writer of the people's literature." He received the Kikuchi Kan Prize in 1953 and the Order of Culture in 1960. He died in 1962 of lung cancer at the age of 70.
