Biography

Born in 1905 in Hiroshima City. He studied at Keio University and, affiliated with the magazine "Mita Bungaku," published delicate poetry and fiction. On August 6, 1945, he was exposed to the atomic bombing at his family home, where he had evacuated. "Summer Flowers," which records that harrowing experience in calm, lucid prose, is known as a landmark work of atomic-bomb literature. He continued writing while carrying both the grief of losing his beloved wife to illness and the memory of the bombing, but in 1951 he ended his life by throwing himself in front of a train.
