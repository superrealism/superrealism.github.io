Biography

Born in Kōjimachi, Tokyo, in 1896, the fourth son of the doctor of medicine Katō Terumaro; he later succeeded to the Hamao family and became a viscount and a member of the House of Peers. A graduate of the law faculty of Tokyo Imperial University, he served as a public prosecutor and a lawyer, and drew on that experience to write detective fiction that looked hard at the psychology of criminals and the judgment of the law. His career as a writer lasted barely six years — "Kare ga Koroshita ka" ("Did He Kill Her?"), "Satsujinki" ("The Killer"), "Akuma no Deshi" ("The Devil's Disciple") — but it left a sure mark on the history of prewar detective fiction. He died suddenly in 1935 at the age of 39.
