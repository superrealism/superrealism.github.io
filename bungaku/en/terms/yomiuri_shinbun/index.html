What is Yomiuri Shimbun?

Category
Society / Group

A daily newspaper founded in 1874. At first a "koshinbun" ("small newspaper"), written in plain prose fully furbished with furigana readings and beloved by ordinary readers, it was known from early on for the energy it devoted to literature. Once Ozaki Kōyō joined the paper in 1889 and became the center of its literary pages, it published many works by Ken'yūsha-school writers and came to be called "the Yomiuri of literature." It carried Yamada Bimyō's "Musashino," Izumi Kyōka's "Giketsu Kyōketsu" ("Righteous Blood, Chivalrous Blood"), depicting a woman warrior, Higuchi Ichiyō's "Utsusemi," and Takayama Chogyū's prize-winning "Takiguchi Nyūdō," and Kōyō's own unfinished last work, the great novel "Konjiki Yasha" ("The Golden Demon," from 1897), stands as the paper's representative serial. In the late Meiji era, Shimazaki Tōson serialized his autobiographical epic novel "Ie" ("The Family"), and Tokuda Shūsei serialized "Arakure" ("Rough Living"), giving rise to further masterworks of Naturalism, and in the Shōwa era popular fiction by writers such as Yoshikawa Eiji also won great popularity. This tradition of valuing literature has made the paper play a major role as one of modern literature's leading venues for publication.
