About this work

A fantastical short story Satō Haruo published in the magazine Seiza in 1917, subtitled as a short story for people who like to dream. Led by his dog, the narrator strays deep into a coppice and into a strange Western house; the experience is drawn in a poetic style in which the real and the dreamed dissolve into each other. It shows early the gifts of a writer who studied under Nagai Kafū and began as an aesthete, and stands historically as the forerunner of the fantasy and melancholy he would establish the next year in Rural Melancholy. In effect his literary debut.

Summary

"I" walk a mountain road by a way I do not usually take, led by my dog Frate. After nearly two hours we come out at a place with a good view, and Frate goes further in among the coppice, and "I" follow. Deep in the wood Frate finds a house with Western windows and barks. There is a sign of someone within, and yet no answer, and "I" peer in at the window. In the middle of the room is a stone basin with water welling up in it, and on the table the smoke of a half-smoked cigarette is still rising in a thin line. Unable to hold back my curiosity, "I" go into the house without leave, and there a jet-black Spanish dog is lying like a watchman. Frate and that dog make friends at once, and "I" go about looking with interest at the basin, at a curious clock with a picture on it, at the foreign books piled on the desk. Presently, turning back to look into the house from the window as I am leaving, I see the Spanish dog get slowly up and, in what seems no more than the time it takes to mutter something to himself in a human voice, change in the space of a blink into an elderly man in spectacles. It is a warm spring afternoon; the coppice on the mountain is quite still.

Characters

  • I　The narrator, who strays into a strange Western house deep in the wood while out walking with his dog Frate
  • Frate　My dog. Very clever, and always the one who decides where the walk goes
  • The Spanish dog　The black dog in the Western house, who makes friends with Frate at once and then turns into an elderly man

The opening (original text)

Frate (that is the dog's name) suddenly runs on and waits for me at the fork where the road turns beside the farrier's. This dog is a very clever dog and a friend of mine of long standing, and I believe he is a great deal cleverer than my wife, to say nothing of the majority of human beings. And so, whenever I go out for a walk, I am sure to take Frate with me.

Supein ken no ie by Satō Haruo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Ah, I have been startled today by a queer fellow." So, I thought, he said, in a human voice. And while I was wondering at it, he yawned as dogs will yawn; and in the time it took me to blink, the fellow had become an elderly man of about fifty in spectacles and a black coat, leaning back in the chair before the great desk, holding calmly in his mouth a cigarette he had not yet lit, and turning the pages of one of those large books he had opened. It is truly a warm spring afternoon. It is in the quiet coppice on the mountain.
