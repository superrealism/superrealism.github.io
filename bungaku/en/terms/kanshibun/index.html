What is Kanshibun (Sino-Japanese Poetry and Prose)?

Category
Form / Genre

Poetry (kanshi) and prose (kanbun) composed by Japanese writers in the Chinese language. A mode of expression that arrived from China along with the Chinese characters themselves, it remained, right up to the Meiji era, one pillar of literature alongside waka and Japanese prose. The oldest surviving anthology of Chinese poetry, the "Kaifūsō," was compiled in 751 and gathers some 120 poems dating from the Ōmi court onward. In the early Heian period, Sino-Japanese poetry and prose were prized as the accomplishments of the state, and three imperially commissioned anthologies of Chinese poetry were compiled: the "Ryōunshū" (814) and "Bunka Shūreishū" by order of Emperor Saga, and the "Keikokushū" by order of Emperor Junna. Sugawara no Michizane collected his own poetry and prose in the "Kanke Bunso," and Kūkai's "Shōryōshū" is renowned as well. In the medieval period, Gozan literature flourished in the Zen monasteries of Kyoto and Kamakura, and in the early modern period, as Confucian learning flourished, it became part of the common education of the samurai class, producing figures such as Rai San'yō. Even in the Meiji era, a grounding in Chinese poetry remained fundamental for a man of letters: Natsume Sōseki composed Chinese poems from his student days to his final years, and Masaoka Shiki too grew close to Chinese poetry from boyhood. The vocabulary and cadence of Sino-Japanese poetry and prose entered deeply into Japanese writing through kanbun-kundoku reading practice, underpinning the mixed Sino-Japanese style and modern classical prose alike.
