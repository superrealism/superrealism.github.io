What is Gesaku?

Category
Form / Genre

A general term for the playful, popular reading matter that flourished in and around Edo from the late eighteenth century, in the late Edo period. The name derives from its being written by educated men as a kind of playful diversion, and it covers various genres: sharebon depicting the pleasure quarters, kokkeibon centered on comedy, ninjōbon depicting romance, fantastical yomihon, and the illustrated kibyōshi and gōkan. Representative works include Jippensha Ikku's "Tōkaidōchū Hizakurige" ("Shanks' Mare"), Shikitei Sanba's "Ukiyoburo" ("The Bathhouse of the Floating World"), and the yomihon master Kyokutei Bakin's "Nansō Satomi Hakkenden" ("The Eight Dog Chronicles"). The tradition survived even into the Meiji era, as when Kanagaki Robun satirized the manners of the civilization-and-enlightenment era in the gesaku style in "Aguranabe" ("Sitting Cross-Legged Before the Hotpot"), but it ceded its leading role to the modern novel once Tsubouchi Shōyō criticized its moralistic "reward good, punish evil" convention in "Shōsetsu Shinzui" ("The Essence of the Novel"). Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's "Gesaku Zanmai" ("Absorbed in Gesaku") depicts this world through the figure of an aging Bakin, and its spirit also flows into Saitō Ryokuu, who studied under Robun and carried the Edo gesaku tradition into the modern era, and into the Buraiha writers who were called the "neo-gesaku school" after the war.
