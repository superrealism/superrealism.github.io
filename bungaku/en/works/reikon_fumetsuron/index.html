About this work

Told that his Refutation of Materialism was too lofty to reach ordinary readers, Inoue Enryō set out the same subject again in plain, popular lectures. To the question that has held people's minds longest of all — whether the soul perishes at death or not — he argues from philosophy, religion and science, and concludes that the soul is immortal. His preface is famous for calling it the egoism of scholars to toy with lofty argument in words the public cannot follow, and for declaring that he will take the road of teaching the world at large even at the cost of being called unscientific.

Summary

Enryō had once written The Refutation of Materialism, meaning to put down the vulgar arguments of the world; but the specialists called it unscientific, and ordinary people said it was too lofty to be understood. One night, while traveling in the provinces, he was asked whether the soul perishes after death, and answered that the questioner should read The Refutation of Materialism; whereupon he was begged to explain it in plain, popular terms, since that book was too abstruse for the ill-read. So he tried an evening's popular talk, and this volume is the expansion of what he thought out then. In the preface Enryō says that to make what one knows known widely to others, and to enjoy widely with others what one enjoys oneself, is the scholar's philanthropy; and he declares that, knowing well that popularizing tends to be called unscientific, he chooses that road all the same. In the body of the book he examines in order the relation of mind and body, the border of life and death, and the question whether mental phenomena can be reduced to matter, refuting the position of materialism point by point. He further appends a "Collection of Views on the Soul," gathering doctrines from ancient and modern times — from Confucian and Buddhist teachings to the Kijin rigenshō and the Takuan zatsuroku — to show how widely the idea of the soul's immortality has taken root in the thought of the East.

The opening (original text)

Written by the author upon The Immortality of the Soul Wishing some time ago to put down the vulgar arguments of the world, I wrote a book entitled The Refutation of Materialism; but the specialists in philosophy criticized it as unscientific, or as empty conjecture, while people in general said that its argument was still too lofty to be understood. I therefore came to feel the need of putting before the world an explanation more popular and more homely than The Refutation of Materialism.

Tsūzoku kōgi: Reikon fumetsuron by Inoue Enryō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The foregoing "Collection of Views on the Soul" is no more than a gathering of what I have at some time copied out or remembered; so there must be many more views on the soul held by various writers besides these. But being busy at present, I have no leisure to search them out. I mean therefore, when the book is reprinted another day, to gather up what is left and enlarge it. Readers, I beg you to understand this.
