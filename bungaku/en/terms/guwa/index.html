What is Fable?

Category
Form / Genre

A short narrative that conveys ideas about human conduct or criticism of society through another set of events. Animals speaking and acting like people are common, but plants, objects, and human characters may also appear. Its distinctive feature is the interplay between the events narrated and the underlying lesson or satire. In Aesop's fables, animal traits and behavior illustrate human desires, weaknesses, and practical wisdom. In France, La Fontaine drew on Aesop and other traditions to compose verse fables, developing the form as poetry. In modern Japanese literature, Yumeno Kyūsaku's "Kinoko kaigi" depicts poisonous mushrooms arguing that being harmful and useless will keep them safe, only for the ending to overturn their expectations. Nakajima Atsushi's "The Master" (1942) offers a paradoxical tale in which Ji Chang, who seeks mastery of archery, ultimately forgets even what a bow is. Fables are not limited to moral instruction for children: they can also challenge human judgments and values. They may overlap with children's stories and traditional tales, but the term emphasizes the way events convey another meaning, rather than the intended audience or whether a story has been handed down.
