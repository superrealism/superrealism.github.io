About this work

One of Akutagawa's best-known stories for children, published in the magazine Akai Tori in July 1920. Based on the Tang dynasty tale of Du Zichun, it follows a young man who wants to become an immortal and is tested in hell on his feeling for his parents, and draws from it the worth of living as a human being. Its plain style and vivid fantasy have kept it in Japanese school textbooks for decades.

Summary

Toshishun, a young man who has run through his fortune in the capital of Luoyang and is at his wits' end, is twice given a great quantity of gold by a one-eyed old man, Tekkanshi, and becomes rich; and each time the friends who had grown tired of his luxury abandon him, and he is sickened by the coldness of human beings. The third time, Toshishun says he wants no more money and asks to become Tekkanshi's disciple and study the arts of the immortals. Tekkanshi leaves him alone on a rock on Mount Emei and warns him that whatever happens while he is away in the heavens, he must not utter a sound. Toshishun holds his tongue through wild beasts and thunder and a terrible divine general, and is at last killed by the general. But his soul falls into hell, and even when he is dragged before the King of Hell he keeps his silence. Exasperated, the King has Toshishun's father and mother, fallen into the realm of beasts, brought out in the form of starved horses, and has the demons whip them to force him to speak. Toshishun endures even this — until he hears the gentle voice of his mother, whipped and still thinking of her son, and, forgetting Tekkanshi's warning, cries out "Mother!" When he comes to himself Toshishun is standing under the gate at Luoyang as before. He has not become an immortal; but he says that he is rather glad he could not abandon his parents in hell, and Tekkanshi tells him to live honestly and like a human being from now on, gives him a house at the southern foot of Mount Tai, and disappears.

Characters

  • Toshishun　A young man of Luoyang come down in the world. He aspires to be an immortal, but cannot bear his parents' love in hell and cries out. The protagonist
  • Tekkanshi　An immortal who lives on Mount Emei. He appears before Toshishun as a one-eyed old man and tests his humanity

The ending (original text)

"Do not forget those words. Then I shall not see you again after today." Tekkanshi was already walking away as he said it; but suddenly he stopped and looked back at Toshishun. "Ah — luckily I have just remembered. I have a house at the southern foot of Mount Tai. I shall give you that house and its fields, so go there at once and live in it. About now the peach blossom must be out all around it," he added, and seemed very pleased with himself.

Famous passages

It was a certain spring evening. Beneath the western gate of Luoyang, the capital of Tang China, stood a young man, gazing vacantly up at the sky.

A children's story that opens in the voice of a folk tale. It moves quietly toward the question of what Toshishun, who has seen both glory and hell through an immortal's sorcery, will choose in the end.

Forgetting the old man's warning entirely, Toshishun stumbled forward and ran to her side, threw both arms around the neck of the half-dead horse, and, tears streaming down, cried out once: “Mother!”

The scene in which, on seeing his mother reduced to a beast in Hell, he finally breaks his vow of silence. Choosing human feeling over his hope of becoming an immortal, this is the very heart of the story.

“Whatever I become, I mean to live an honest life, as a human being should.”

Words Toshishun speaks to Tekkanshi near the end, having given up his wish to become an immortal. After passing through both wealth and magic, he shows the simple human way of life he has chosen.
