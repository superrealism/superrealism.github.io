About this work

An unusual short story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Asahi Graph in 1936. At a works where shells are burnt to make lime, labourers with shaven heads work in silence, covered head to foot in lime — and it draws, in a desolate hand, an incident of violence and silence in a life that looks like that of savages. A piece with an extraordinary force, rare in his work.

Summary

A factory that burned shells to make lime stood at the edge of a wasteland. From roof to ground the building was smeared white, as though painted, and it ceaselessly spewed out a thick, fog-like ash smoke, so that it looked exactly like an abandoned house left stranded in a raging blizzard. Inside the factory, seven laborers work. They are naked, their heads shaved like convicts, and their tanned, bronze bodies are wholly caked in ash, ash creeping in even beneath their eyebrows, their faces as blank and expressionless as masks. Four men, and three of the men's wives — the women too are naked, their hair cropped short, indistinguishable from the men. Should they open their mouths, ash comes flying in without mercy, and so they go on working, silent as stone, in absolute stillness. Amid this life so like that of "savages," an act of violence occurs. One of the laborers, Mokichi, tried to do harm to one of the women, and had his head split open by a shovel. Why he had tried to harm her, he never once said. However persistently the villagers pried and probed for the reason, the men of the factory only ever shook their ash-covered, shaven heads in silence. All manner of rumor and speculation spread, plausible-sounding but nothing more. The laborers themselves paid no mind to any of it, and went on, as before, moving silently through the ash smoke. A strange and unusually powerful piece, unlike anything else in Yamamoto's body of work.

Characters

  • Mokichi　A laborer who tries to harm a woman and has his head split open by a shovel
  • The ash-covered laborers　Men who work in silence at the lime factory

The opening (original text)

A factory that burned shells to make lime stood at the point where Nakabori gave way to the wasteland beyond it. ...From roof to ground, every surface was smeared white as though coated in pitch, and from the small windows set high up, and from the gaps here and there, a thick, fog-like ash smoke poured out without ceasing, swirling about the building until it looked, for all the world, like an abandoned house left stranded in a terrible blizzard.

Banjin by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The villagers, in their eagerness to learn the cause of the incident, pressed and pried with great persistence, but the men of the factory only ever shook their ash-covered, shaven heads in silence. Yet they themselves took no interest at all in such rumors, and went on, as before, moving silently through the ash smoke — the thick, milk-colored dead ash pouring ceaselessly from the building the whole day long, burying the grass and the bare earth like a fine mist, and drifting, fine and shimmering, up into the air.

Banjin by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
