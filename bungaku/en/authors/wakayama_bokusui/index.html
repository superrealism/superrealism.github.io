Biography

Born in 1885 in Tsuboya, Higashiusuki District, Miyazaki Prefecture (now Hyūga City); his real name was Shigeru. His pen name, "Bokusui," is said to derive from his mother's name, Maki, and the mountain stream he loved as a child. He studied English literature at Waseda University, where from his student days he studied tanka under Onoe Saishū. His classmate Kitahara Hakushū became a lifelong friend, and the two shared lodgings and devoted themselves to literature together. He published his first tanka collection, "Umi no Koe" ("The Voice of the Sea"), in 1908, and his signature collection, "Betsuri" ("Separation"), in 1910, and songs such as "Shiratori wa kanashikarazuya sora no ao umi no ao ni mo somazu tadayou" ("Is the white bird not sad, drifting unstained by the blue of sky or sea") and "Ikuyamakawa koesari yukaba sabishisa no hatenamu kuni zo kyō mo tabiyuku" ("If I go on crossing mountain after mountain, is there a land where loneliness ends? Today too I journey on") made him an overnight star of the poetry world. A devoted lover of travel and drink, he was widely loved for a plain, fresh style singing of nature and the feeling of the road, and left many tanka collections and travelogues over his life. He led the tanka magazine "Sōsaku" ("Creation"). He was also close with Ishikawa Takuboku, and was present at his deathbed. In his later years he settled in Numazu, Shizuoka Prefecture, and died in 1928 at the age of 43.
