What is Garakuta Bunko?

Category
Society / Group

The organ magazine founded in 1885 by the newly formed Ken'yūsha, regarded as Japan's first literary coterie magazine. The title puns on "garakuta" (junk, odds and ends) and "bunko" (library); at first it was purely a circulated magazine, with members' handwritten pieces bound and passed around, but in 1886 it became a printed, non-commercial booklet, and from May 1888 it developed into a publicly sold magazine available in bookstores (later retitled "Bunko"). Alongside founding members Ozaki Kōyō, Yamada Bimyō, Ishibashi Shian, and Maruoka Kyūka, Iwaya Sazanami and Kawakami Bizan also wrote for it, and it sent into the world the early works of writers who would go on to lead the literary establishment, beginning with Yamada Bimyō's "Chōkai Shōsetsu Tengu" (1886), an early attempt at genbun'itchi prose. The process by which a booklet begun as a students' pastime grew into a commercial magazine is itself the story of how the Ken'yūsha became a dominant force in the literary world, and it left a great influence on later generations as the prototype of the coterie magazine as a form of publication.
