About this work

A historical story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Kōdan Zasshi in 1948. A samurai client of exceptional good looks and exceptional free spending comes every night to the pleasure quarter at the Kanaya post station on the Tōkaidō. He is in fact a young lord going about incognito on an investigation. A bracing piece drawing in light comedy a young lord thrown about by the duty of making up to women.

Summary

At the edge of the post town of Kanaya on the Tōkaidō, there is a sensuous little quarter. With the Ōi river in flood and travel across it halted, the bustle of a post town overflowing with stranded travellers spills over into this pleasure district too. At a house there called Osome, a samurai customer comes calling every single night. Samurai who frequent a place like this are almost always foot soldiers or the lowest sort of retainer, and yet this customer is finely dressed, strikingly handsome, and, on top of it all, spends money with a frightening ease. The plain, guileless courtesan is completely taken with him, and giggles, "You're just like some daimyo's young lord." "Might not be far off, actually," the customer laughs — but in fact, it was no joke at all. The true identity of this handsome customer was none other than the young lord Daisuke himself, going about the pleasure quarter incognito to investigate some scheme. In the darkness outside, his trusted retainers Chūya and Kingo are always waiting, ready to bring the young lord home once the time is right. Even while being pulled about by the task of wooing women, Daisuke presses on with his investigation of the conspiracy. In time the case is happily resolved. Sharing a drink freely with his trusted men, the young lord says, "Do you know what I'm happiest about right now? Simply this — that I don't have to woo women anymore." A rousing historical story, full of light comedy.

Characters

  • Lord Daisuke　A young lord who goes about the pleasure quarter incognito
  • Chūya and Kingo　The young lord's trusted retainers

The opening (original text)

At the edge of the post town of Kanaya on the Tōkaidō, there is a sensuous little quarter. Narrow-fronted, flat-roofed board buildings stand in a row, each with a coloured curtain dyed with its own shop name hung out front, and behind them, sensuous young women, heavily made up with rouge and white powder, line up in rows. They put on the appearance of a teahouse or a snack shop, or a place selling souvenirs, but anyone who wanders in for a meal, or to buy a souvenir, is in for a bad time.

Wakadono jonanki by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Well, it seems the matter has been settled, one way or another. It's all thanks to your help, every one of you. But do you know what I'm happiest about right now?" The young lord said this, and smiled. "— Simply this: that I don't have to woo women anymore."
