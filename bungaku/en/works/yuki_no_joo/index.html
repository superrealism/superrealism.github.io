About this work

The longest and the most fantastic of Andersen's tales. As its subtitle — "a fairy tale in seven stories" — says, it begins with the splinters of a mirror made by the Devil entering the eye and the heart of the boy Kay, and tells in seven parts the wanderings of the girl Gerda, who journeys north in search of the boy who has been carried away. In Kusuyama Masao's version. The enchanted flower garden, the crows, the robber girl, the woman of Lapland — a structure in which she goes forward with the help of those she meets on the way became a model for many later works of fantasy, and in Japan it is known as the source of an animated film.

Summary

The mirror the Devil made was such that whatever was beautiful shrank away in it and only what was ugly showed clearly. When it was carried up toward heaven it was dropped, and the splinters flew out over the whole world; a splinter in the eye made everything look crooked, and a splinter in the heart turned that heart into a lump of ice. Kay and Gerda lived happily together, growing roses at their attic windows. One day such splinters pierce Kay's eye and his heart, and the boy begins to abuse the roses and to sneer at Gerda. Then, in a snowy square, he hitches his own sled to a great white one and is carried away by the Snow Queen. Gerda floats her red shoes down the river to ask where Kay has gone, is kept in the flower garden of an old woman who works magic, is guided by a pair of crows to the castle of a prince and princess, has her life spared by a robber girl, and is carried on the back of a reindeer from Lapland to Finnmark. In the Snow Queen's palace Kay sits frozen, trying to lay out splinters of ice into the word "eternity." Gerda's hot tears melt the splinter in his breast, and Kay's own tears wash the splinter from his eye. Hand in hand the two go home, and find that they have grown up; but their hearts were children's hearts still.

Characters

  • Gerda　The girl who journeys north in search of Kay, who has been carried away
  • Kay　The boy in whose eye and heart the splinters of the Devil's mirror lodge, and who is carried off by the Snow Queen
  • The Snow Queen　She carries Kay away and shuts him up in her palace of ice
  • The old woman　The old woman who keeps Gerda in her enchanted flower garden
  • The robber girl　She saves Gerda and sends her on her way with a reindeer

The opening (original text)

The First Story Of a Mirror and Its Splinters Now then, listen. We are beginning. When we have come to the end of this story we shall know more than we know now, for it is about a wicked troll — one of the very worst of them; in fact, it was the Devil himself. Now, one day the Devil was in a really excellent humor.

The Snow Queen by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

And the grandmother sat in God's clear sunshine, reading aloud from the Bible: "Except ye become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven." Kay and Gerda looked into each other's eyes, and all at once they understood the meaning of the old hymn: The roses bloom and fade away, but we shall see the Christ Child one day. And so the two of them sat there, grown up in body and children still, children at heart. And it was summer — warm, blessed summer.
