Biography

Born in 1887 in Kizu, Osaka (now Naniwa Ward, Osaka City). He graduated from Kokugakuin University and studied under Yanagita Kunio, entering the field of folklore studies. As a professor at Kokugakuin University and Keio University, he traveled the country investigating the origins of ancient beliefs and performing arts, and built the concept of the "marebito," the divine visitor from beyond, along with a sweeping system of ancient studies, leaving an enormous influence on Japanese folklore and classical literary studies. As a poet and tanka poet he was active under the pen name Shaku Chōkū, singing his own distinctive lyricism in tanka collections such as "Umi Yama no Aida" ("Between Sea and Mountains"). "Shisha no Sho" ("The Book of the Dead"), which crystallized his scholarly imagination into the form of a novel, stands as his celebrated masterpiece, fusing his erudition and poetic sensibility. He died in 1953 at the age of 66.
