About this work

Minakami Takitarō's representative work, serialized in "Josei" from 1925 into the following year. It follows Mita, a company employee transferred from Tokyo to Osaka, who settles at the cheap inn Suigetsu on the bank of the Tosabori and passes his days among the people who come and go there. Storming out of a lodging house whose grasping master has disgusted him, he finds, on the strength of a word picked up in an oden shop, an inn with a mistress who talks like a man, a drunken old fellow, a man called the Python, and the maid Orika. With the cool, moral eye of an author who lived in both business and literature, it captures the squalor and the vitality of the city of Osaka.

Summary

Mita, only half a year in Osaka, has had enough of the master of his lodging house and the old woman there, specimens both of meanness, greed and stinginess, and pushes out a handcart without having decided where to go. The travelers' inns in front of Umeda station are noisy, with no quiet in which to sit at a desk. Searching on his way to and from the office, he finds that the houses he likes are dear and the cheap ones he does not like. Throwing back a glass of spirits at an oden shop by Tenjinbashi and grumbling about it, he is interrupted by a filthy old man who has been dozing beside him: "If it's cheap and comfortable you want, it's the Suigetsu at Tosabori." The inn he visits the next day proves a pleasant house, where a big mistress who talks like a man laughs and grants him a monthly rate: "We're not in business here to make money." Next morning the flat-faced man who greets him at the washstand turns out to be the drunkard of that night. So Mita's Osaka days begin. The people of the inn, his friend Tahara, the man called the Python, the maid Orika — and while he steeps himself in the breathing of a city squalid and unreflecting and for that very reason full of life, the day comes at last when he too must go back to Tokyo.

Characters

  • Mita　A company employee transferred from Tokyo to Osaka. He settles at the inn Suigetsu in Tosabori
  • The mistress of the Suigetsu　A big woman who talks like a man. She runs her house by temperament rather than profit
  • The old fellow　The drunken old man who helps at the Suigetsu. It was he who told Mita of the place
  • The Python　A man Mita comes to know, who drinks bottomlessly when he drinks at all
  • Tahara　Mita's friend, who lives at Mikage. He comes running with beer on the day of the farewell
  • Orika　A maid at the Suigetsu

The opening (original text)

1.1 Even the sky of Osaka, which feels overcast the year round for all the soot, deepened toward indigo in early summer, and the drifting clouds began to shine white. Muddy as the water is, the river reflected that color of the sky vividly; it had risen, and flowed with little waves that seemed to dance. Sitting in a rattan chair on the second-floor veranda of the inn Suigetsu on the riverbank, Mita watched the boats going up and down.

The ending (original text)

Those seeing him off and the one being seen off, with all the curious eyes around them full upon them, drank the toast together. "Long live Mita!" Tahara led it off at the top of his voice, but at that, naturally enough, nobody joined in. "Long life!" Tahara shouted it three times regardless, and by then the train had already slid away, leaving them behind. And it ran east and east in haste, belching soot from its stack, as if to say farewell, farewell to Osaka, active and developing without order, without reflection, without morality, under that dingy clouded sky.
