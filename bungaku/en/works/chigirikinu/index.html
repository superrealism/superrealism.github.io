About this work

The story of Onatsu, a girl who has run away from the house she was sold into and survives by disguising her origins. Through the repeated sound of rain, she tells the history of her life mixing lies and facts, and in time is joined to a man. In the parting scene, a mother points out to her child the back of the father walking away through the snow without looking round — and the ending, in which she lifts the child high while holding back her tears, is brilliantly done.

Summary

"Drunk again, is she? There is no doing anything with that child" — the story begins from an exchange between the mistress of a house in the pleasure quarter and one of the girls. The mistress does not hide her irritation at a girl who earns well enough but turns away customer after customer. That girl is O-Natsu, who is there hiding her birth. ... On a rainy night O-Natsu tells her own history. Her family were samurai of the lowest rank, four of them: her father and mother, her elder brother and herself. She lost her father and her brother at once in a flood; her mother fell gravely ill, and money was borrowed for her treatment; and after her mother's death she was bound by the borrowed money and brought to this place. Learning that she was to be married to a man of doubtful character, she ran out meaning rather to die — and the quiet sound of the rain outside wraps that story, in which lies and truth are mixed. In time she comes to be bound to a man named Seinosuke. But there was no road by which the two of them might be joined. With the words "It rains well — a quiet rain.... This will make it altogether autumn," the time of parting comes. Years pass, and on a day of snow O-Natsu lifts up her child Takajirō and points at the back of a man striding off through the snow without looking round, and says: "Look well, Taka-chan; that is your father...." With the tears running as they will, she smiles with all her might and lifts the child higher still.

Characters

  • O-Natsu　A samurai's daughter living under a false name
  • Seinosuke　The man to whom O-Natsu is bound
  • Takajirō　O-Natsu's child

The opening (original text)

1.1 "Drunk again, is she? There is no doing anything with that child. What has become of the customer?" "O-Kiku has gone out to him now. This will not do, mother; have I not told you that samurai are no good for this girl?"

Chigirikinu by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

He went off through the snow with great strides. He did not look round again. Showing his strong shoulders, he goes steadily away with a firm, wide step. O-Natsu lifted Takajirō up and, pointing at his back, said: "Look well, Taka-chan; that is your father.... You too will be a fine man like your father one day."

Chigirikinu by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
