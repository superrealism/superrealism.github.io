About this work

A novella Kobayashi Takiji published in Chūō Kōron and Senki in 1929. With The Crab Cannery Ship it is a central work of his proletarian writing: set in a farming district of Hokkaido, it draws the hard lives of tenant farmers and poor peasants exploited under an absentee landlord living in the city, and their struggle as they gather into a farmers' union. It is drawn from the Isono farm tenancy dispute, which Takiji, then working at the Hokkaido Colonial Bank, supported himself.

Summary

Lured by the grandiose dream of an "immigrant's guide," Ken's family crosses to the deep snows of Hokkaido, only to have the landlord's promise — that half the land would be given free of charge once cleared — reneged on, and after fleeing their first "settlement plot" within two years, they finally settle as tenant farmers on the "Kishino Farm." Even the rice they had set aside as food is seized, piled up under the eaves where they cannot lay a finger on it — as tenant farmers such as these, Ken and his family live crawling along the very bottom of poverty. Even as a new age approaches, with electric lights coming to the village and utility poles marching closer, pole by pole, there are farmers bound hand and foot, unable to move, by "seizure of standing crops," "demands for the return of land," and "crushing rents." And the landlord who matters most of all is never in the village at all. Amid the collapsing, ramshackle huts of the tenant farmers, the landlord's white-walled house stands out, clean and sharp as a crane — but its master is an "absentee landlord" living in the city. Ken, facing his conscription examination and supporting the household while attending the youth training institute, in time throws himself into the farmers' union movement together with the tenant farmers who rise up against exploitation and poverty. At the close of the story, Ken, with firm resolve, sets out for Asahikawa, and begins working for the "farmers' union." A representative work of proletarian literature in which Takiji sharply draws the harsh reality of tenant farmers in Hokkaido, and their awakening to organized struggle.

Characters

  • Ken　A young tenant farmer on the Kishino Farm in Hokkaido. Supports the household while facing conscription, then throws himself into the farmers' union movement and sets out for Asahikawa
  • Kishino　The master of the Kishino Farm. An "absentee landlord" who lives in the city and never shows his face in the village
  • Yoshizō　Ken's younger brother. A boy living out a poor tenant farmer's life

The opening (original text)

I dedicate this piece, as a "new farmer's reader," to every tenant farmer and every poor peasant in every corner of the country. I hope you will read it lying down between your bouts of work, the way you would read Araki Matabei or Naruto Hichō.

Fuzai jinushi by Kobayashi Takiji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Ken set out for Asahikawa with firm resolve. Kinu's younger sister came to see him off. And there he began working for the "farmers' union."
