What is Hakkin (Banning)?

Category
Other

The censorship measure by which the state prohibited the sale and distribution of publications it deemed disruptive of public morals or a threat to public order and safety. From the Meiji era to the defeat in the Shōwa era, this authority lay with the Home Ministry, and many literary works fell under its ban. Mori Ōgai's frank treatment of sexuality, "Vita Sexualis," was banned along with the magazine that carried it, "Subaru," in 1909, and that same year Nagai Kafū's "Furansu Monogatari" ("French Stories"), which let his criticism of Japanese officialdom and customs learned in the West show through, was banned the moment its publication was registered. Issues of "Mita Bungaku" carrying works by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō, and the Pan no Kai's coterie magazine "Okujō Teien" ("Roof Garden"), were likewise banned. Into the Shōwa era, suppression extended to ideas as well, and Kobayashi Takiji's "Kanikōsen" ("The Crab Cannery Ship") was published riddled with blacked-out passages and still banned. Writers tried to evade censorship through blacked-out text and devices of expression, and this tense relationship with censorship deeply shaped the expression of modern literature itself.
