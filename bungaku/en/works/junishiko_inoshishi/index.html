About this work

The piece for the year of the boar — the folklore and legends of the boar — serialized in four parts in Taiyō in 1923. It is known for the comic opening, in which he puzzles over a telegram from Hakubunkan whose meaning he cannot make out. It gathers traditions of the boar from Japan, China and the West: the distinction between the domestic pig and the wild boar, the opening of business on the day of the boar, songs to ward off vipers.

Summary

At the beginning of December in the eleventh year of Taishō a telegram came from the Hakubunkan reading "SEND THE BOAR MANUSCRIPT QUICKLY." Unable to make out what it meant, Kumagusu thought this way and that; and since in Kazusa they invoke bracken and in Ōshū they chant a song about the wild boar to keep off the harm of vipers, boar and bracken are not without connection, so that this must mean that he should make a verse on the subject of the wild boar and bracken. But his wife, reading the telegram over his shoulder, explained that since next year is the year of the boar it means, as usual, send the manuscript of the boar story quickly; and only then did he see it and set about this essay — such is the self-mocking opening. Thereafter he investigates that in China and Korea the character for boar means the domestic pig, and that unless one writes "mountain boar" the wild animal is not understood; that the Erya says the young of the pig is called chu; and he sets down for his readers his distinction between the domestic and the wild animal. The passage in which he hunts with bloodshot eyes for the earliest use of the word buta and finds it in the Ōu eikei gunki is charmingly enthusiastic. After that the boar's eating of snakes, the song for keeping off vipers, and legends of the boar from many places are set out, and the talk spreads, as always, in unforeseen directions.

The opening (original text)

At the beginning of December (in the eleventh year of Taishō) there came a telegram from the Hakubunkan reading "SEND THE BOAR MANUSCRIPT QUICKLY." Not understanding what it meant, I thought this way and that: in Kazusa they invoke bracken, and in Ōshū they chant a song about the wild boar, to keep off the harm of vipers. That being so, boar and bracken are not without connection.... My wife, reading the telegram over my shoulder, said this was nothing so difficult: since next year is the year of the boar it means, as usual, put together the manuscript of the boar story quickly and send it. Only then did I see it, and set about this essay.

Jūnishikō (inoshishi) by Minakata Kumagusu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

When Zeuxis, that great master of figure painting, was asked for a portrait of Helen to be hung in the temple of Hera at Croton, he chose the five most beautiful maidens of Croton and put together the best feature of each, and what he made was a thing without equal in the world.... From these one may judge that Helen was a beauty beyond all bounds, and know that her breasts too were exceedingly lovely.

Jūnishikō (inoshishi) by Minakata Kumagusu (Aozora Bunko)
