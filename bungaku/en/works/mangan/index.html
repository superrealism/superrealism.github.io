About this work

A very short piece Dazai published in the magazine Bunpitsu in 1938. Only a few manuscript pages long, it is prized as a clean, lyrical thing from his middle years. Staying at Mishima in Izu, the narrator sees a young woman every morning at the house of a town doctor he has come to know. For three years, while her husband was treated for illness, the doctor forbade the couple to sleep together — and now the day of her fulfilled vow has come. She goes off twirling a white parasol, and the freshness of that last image stays with the reader.

Summary

Four years ago the narrator spent a summer on the upper floor of an acquaintance's house at Mishima in Izu, writing a story called "Romanesque." One night, drunk, he fell off his bicycle and hurt his leg, and so became friendly with the town doctor, a cheerful man who looked like Saigō Takamori; and after that it became his habit to read the newspaper almost every morning on the doctor's veranda. At that same hour a young woman came without fail to fetch medicine. Clean-looking, in a simple dress and wooden clogs, she became someone he found himself waiting for. One morning the doctor's wife whispers to him: permission was given this morning. For three years, for the sake of a husband ill in the chest, the doctor had forbidden the couple to be together — and now the term of that vow was fulfilled. Along the little path in front of his eyes, that clean figure goes by as though flying, twirling a white parasol. The more the years pass, the more beautiful that woman seems to him.

Characters

  • I　The narrator, staying at Mishima and writing a story
  • The town doctor　A cheerful man who looks like Saigō Takamori, and who becomes friendly with the narrator
  • The doctor's wife　Who whispers to the narrator that permission has been given
  • The young woman　A wife whose husband is ill in the chest, and who comes every morning for medicine

The opening (original text)

This is a story of four years ago — of the time when I spent a summer on the upper floor of an acquaintance's house at Mishima in Izu, writing a story called "Romanesque." One night, drunk, I was riding a bicycle through the town and hurt myself. I tore the skin above the ankle of my right foot.

Mangan by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Looking up, I saw a clean figure in a simple dress going swiftly, as though flying, along the little path just in front of me. She gave her white parasol a twirl. "Permission was given this morning," the doctor's wife whispered again. Three years — one says it in a word, but... my breast was full. The more the years and months pass, the more beautiful that woman seems to me.
