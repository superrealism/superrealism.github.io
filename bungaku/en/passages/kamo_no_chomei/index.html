Famous passages by Kamo no Chōmei

Well-known lines from the works of Kamo no Chōmei.

4 passages

An Account of My Hut

The flowing river never stops, and yet the water never stays the same. The bubbles that drift on its pooling eddies now vanish, now form again, but never endure for long.

The opening line entrusts the flux of all things to the image of a ceaselessly flowing river and the bubbles on its surface. It is the emblematic passage of Kamo no Chōmei's literature of seclusion, a lifelong meditation on impermanence.

I do not know: those who are born and die — from where do they come, and to where do they go?

A passage following the opening image of flowing water. Layering the question of where people come from and where they go, it deepens still further the sense of impermanence.

The old capital had already fallen into ruin, and the new capital was not yet built. Everyone, one and all, felt like a drifting cloud.

A passage recording the move of the capital to Fukuhara in 1180. The old capital lay in ruins and the new one was not yet built: the unease of people who had lost their footing is expressed as “the feeling of a drifting cloud.”

Now, the Three Worlds are nothing but a single mind. If the mind is not at peace, oxen and horses and the seven treasures are of no use, and palaces and towers are nothing to wish for.

A passage near the end, describing life in his ten-foot-square hut. All worlds lie in the mind alone, and without peace of mind wealth and palaces mean nothing: the state Chōmei finally reached.
