About this work

A short story Ranpo published in Kuraku in 1925. Hikotarō suffers from sleepwalking since childhood and even commits thefts without knowing it. Around a certain murder, the riddle of strange footprints arises about him. An early mystery drawing an ironic mix-up and the pitiful fate of a sleepwalker.

Summary

Having failed at his job at a cotton wholesaler, Hikotarō has been living off his aged father. Since childhood he has had a habit of talking and walking in his sleep — sleepwalking. He talks clearly in his sleep, and, in the middle of the night, gets up and wanders about. At his worst, he has, while still asleep, unlocked the door and gone once around the neighbourhood, or brought home other people's belongings without any awareness of it. Because of this "unwitting thief" in him, he was at last driven out of the shop where he had been apprenticed, and where, in three more years, he would have been given his own branch of the family business. In due course a murder occurs at the mansion of Count M, where his father works as a servant. People make a great fuss, taking the footprints left at the scene for the culprit's own, but the truth was something entirely ironic — those footprints belonged to the victim himself, who had switched into a pair of cracked paulownia clogs. The assumption that a murder must leave the culprit's own footprints had led everyone astray. In the midst of the affair, Hikotarō, the sleepwalker, tries for some reason to flee, and meets a mysterious death together with his father. Though he draws the sympathy of the world, why he tried to flee at that moment remains a puzzle that can never be solved. An early mystery of Ranpo's, drawing the pitiful fate of a sleepwalker and an ironic mistake.

The opening (original text)

It had already been three months since Hikotarō, having failed at his job at a cotton wholesaler, came back to live with his father. Living off his father, a man past fifty who barely got by working as a kind of odd-job servant at the mansion of his former domain lord, Count M, was never, for Hikotarō himself, any pleasant thing.

Muyūbyōsha no shi by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Two days later, two coffins passed out through the gate of Count M's mansion. They held, needless to say, the unfortunate sleepwalker Hikotarō and his father. There was not a single person, among all those who heard the rumour, who did not pity the strange death of father and son. But on one point alone — why Hikotarō had tried, at that moment, to flee — a puzzle was left behind that could never be solved.
