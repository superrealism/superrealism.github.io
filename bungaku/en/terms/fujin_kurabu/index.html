What is Fujin Kurabu?

Category
Society / Group

A monthly magazine for women founded in October 1920 by Kōdansha (then the Dai Nihon Yūbenkai Kōdansha) under Noma Seiji (its title at founding was written with a slightly different orthography). It began with educational articles preaching the ideal of the "good wife, wise mother," then gradually shifted its center of gravity to practical articles useful in home life, and for decades divided the readership of married women with "Shufu no Tomo," being counted among the four great women's magazines through the prewar and postwar eras alike. Within Kōdansha's stable of magazines tailored to different readerships — "King" and "Shōnen Kurabu" among them — this one carried the banner for women readers. It also placed weight on fiction: Yosano Akiko contributed the essay "'Onnarashisa' towa Nanika" ("What Is 'Womanliness'?," 1921). Above all, Yamamoto Shūgorō published, intermittently from 1942, his linked stories "Nihon Fudōki" ("Chronicles of Japanese Womanly Ways"), each episode depicting the spirit of nameless women who lived within samurai households, and this won the 17th Naoki Prize (Shūgorō declined the award). The magazine also carried period fiction such as "Irimuko Jūman Ryō." It continued long after the war as a home magazine, but, overtaken by the rise of lifestyle-information magazines, ceased publication in 1988.
