About this work

A play in five scenes published in Chūō Kōron in 1929. In a small Japanese-run hotel in a port town in French Indochina, it draws the lives — going nowhere — of the trading-company men, kept women and photographers who linger there. Nothing that could be called an incident occurs, and out of the aimless talk and the silences rise the tedium and loneliness of Japanese washed up in the south. With "The Paper Balloon," Kishida Kunio's central work, and famous for bringing the standard of the dialogue play to modern Japanese theatre.

Summary

A port town in French Indochina, at the end of September, just before the rainy season sets in. In a quarter that touches both the French residential district and the native village stands the Ushiyama Hotel, kept by Japanese. In the mistress's room, which serves also as the office, there are mats on the floor, and from the large window a part of the wharf and the horizon can be seen. Those who gather here are Makabe, the former head of the branch office of the S— company, and his mistress Sato; Mitani, the newly arrived head, and his wife; the company's employees; the master of a foreign-goods shop; a photographer; a fencing master; and a woman who is a Frenchman's mistress — all of them far from their own country and passing their days with no prospect of going back. Gossip about people, talk of money, tangles of love, news from home. The conversation gets nowhere and nothing decisive happens. Still, as people come and go the relations among them are slowly recombined, and in time Sato leaves the hotel. After the car carrying her has gone, Makabe appears on the stairs in his pajamas and looks down for a while. It is only to see that she has really gone; and he turns quietly on his heel and goes back to his room.
