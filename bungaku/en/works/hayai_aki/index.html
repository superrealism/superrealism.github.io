About this work

He came back to Tokyo tanned past recognition, but that day he seemed somehow low — a piece from Yamakawa Masao's series "Close Friends." On a rock offshore in August he hears the voice of a woman in a bright red bathing cap. Facing an empty sea, she was breaking off an affair in a monologue. In the last few lines, when it is disclosed who this man who has fallen in love actually is, the story lands from an angle nobody expected.

Summary

To "me," teasing him with "What on earth is the matter? Has some girl thrown you over?" he began slowly to talk. — The sixteenth of August, the day the heavy swell came and the sea grew calm. Running into his classmate Makita, and meaning to get both the duty of company and release from it at once, he challenged him to race out to a rock two hundred meters offshore. As he expected, Makita soon turned back, and he climbed onto the rock alone and lay down in a hollow. And then he heard a woman's voice. "Don't be absurd. What do you suppose you know about me?" A married woman seemed to be dismissing a man not her husband. He shrank down and held his breath. "I am tired of you now." "Who do you imagine can throw away his own past, or go back to the self he used to be?" "Life, I sometimes think, is a truly grotesque thing." ... Looking through a cleft in the rock, a woman of refined features in a bright red woolen cap was swimming slowly on her breast with a face that seemed to be laughing. And then he understands why he had never once heard a man's voice. On the water there was no one but the woman's red cap. "Ah, I hate the thought of going on living like this forever! I hate the thought of living until I am an old woman!" the woman screamed, with the same laughing face. — He fell in love with that woman, and swam out to the rock every day until the last day of the holidays and waited, and she never appeared again. And today he meets her at a bus stop. Makita came running up and called, "Mother, where are you going? Shopping?"

The opening (original text)

He had come back to Tokyo grown so sturdy that one hardly knew him, and burned very dark. He had swum in the sea almost every day, he said, and the skin of the tip of his nose seemed to have peeled three or four times. But strangely, that day he was low in spirits. However hard I tried to start his engine, his eyes soon went vacant, and before I knew it he would be staring blankly through the window at an autumn sky that had come too early. Something had to be done to change the mood. I lit a cigarette and said teasingly: "What on earth is the matter? Has some girl thrown you over?"

The ending (original text)

"...I see. You want to say that I am nothing but your tutor." With a smile like a resigned, solitary old man he gazed blankly out of the window again. On the tips of the branches, where the leaves were beginning to fall, the setting sun lay red, and beyond them was the madder-colored sky of an autumn evening. "Ah, autumn again. Somehow I feel that I have become dreadfully childish." Stretching out his sunburned arm, he said it, and at last spread the arithmetic textbook and his notebook on the desk.
