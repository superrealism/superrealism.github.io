About this work

A short story Hayashi Fumiko published in a special number of Shōsetsu Shinchō in 1949, titled with the English word Downtown alongside the Japanese. A central short story of her last years, later filmed. With her husband still held in Siberia, Riyo peddles tea about the downtown quarters of Tokyo, and the story draws the brief warm friendship she forms by chance with one man, and its sad end. A fine short story copying down in a clear hand the joys and sorrows of ordinary people living through postwar poverty.

Summary

In a working-class quarter of Tokyo not long after the war. With her husband still interned in Siberia, Riyo, carrying her young child, lives day to day as a peddler selling tea from Shizuoka. On a day when a cold wind blows, Riyo, ducking into a shed at a scrapyard of iron materials to ask if she might warm herself there, meets a good-natured, craftsman-like man wearing a headband — Tsuruishi. Gruff but warm-hearted, Tsuruishi looks after Riyo, numb with cold, lets her sit by the fire, and buys tea from her. From this beginning, a brief, warm bond grows between the two of them, each with no one else to lean on. But that small happiness did not last long. One day Riyo learns that Tsuruishi has died in an accident. She thinks she would at least like to offer a single stick of incense, but gives even that up in the end. It would change nothing, after all — Waiting for her husband in Siberia, yet still determined to go on living in Tokyo, Riyo once more hoists the burden onto her shoulder, and calls out at the next house, "Wouldn't you like some Shizuoka tea?" A fine short story in which Fumiko, in the clear, spare style of her later years, depicts the modest yet poignant days of a woman living in the working-class Tokyo of the postwar years.

Characters

  • Riyo　A peddler woman who sells tea in the working-class quarters of Tokyo, her husband still interned in Siberia. Carries a young child and lives day to day
  • Tsuruishi　A craftsman-like man Riyo meets at a scrapyard of iron materials. Gruff but warm-hearted, he forms a brief, warm bond with Riyo, but dies in an accident

The opening (original text)

Since the wind was cold, Riyo chose to walk on the sunny side of the street. She made a point of heading toward the smaller houses. It being around noon, she looked for a house where she might be given a cup of tea. Following the eaves along, she turned past a board fence like one around a construction site, and, peering in past a pile of rusted iron scrap, found a small shed where, behind a glass door, a fire crackled and popped.

The ending (original text)

She had come up to Tokyo meaning to try her hand at peddling, and to go back to Shimizu if the tea did not sell — but Riyo had come to think that Tokyo was better for her, whether business went well or not, and that even to die by the roadside, Tokyo was, for now, the better place to be. Riyo sat down on the green grass of the embankment. Below, beside a broken chunk of concrete, lay the corpse of a kitten, cast aside, turned away from her. Riyo rose at once, hoisted the burden onto her shoulder, and walked on toward the station. Turning suddenly into a side lane, she called out to a poor-looking house, its glass-latticed entrance boarded over: "Wouldn't you like some Shizuoka tea?"
