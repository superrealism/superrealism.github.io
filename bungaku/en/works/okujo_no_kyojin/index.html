About this work

A madman of an elder brother who sits on the roof gazing at the sea, and the clever younger brother who watches over him. A one-act play Kikuchi Kan wrote as a student, asking sharply what happiness is. The opposition between parents who call in an exorcist to cure him and a brother who means to leave him as he is comes to a quiet conclusion in the golden light of evening.

Summary

In the 1900s, on an island belonging to Sanuki in the Inland Sea. The stage is the back garden of the Katsushima family, among the wealthiest households on the island. The eldest son, Gitarō, is twenty-four. Since falling from the roof as a small child, he is said to have lost his mind, and nearly every day he climbs to the very ridge of the roof to sit gazing out at the sea. His father Gisuke tries to have a manservant bring him down, but Gitarō will not listen — for in the clouds of the sky he sees a golden palace, and hears the sound of a flute. His father and mother, hoping somehow to cure their eldest son, send for a woman from the mainland who calls herself a shaman. The rite of exorcism begins: Gitarō is dragged down from the roof, smoked, and tormented. Just then his younger brother Suejirō, a seventeen-year-old middle-school student, comes home. Suejirō drives the shaman away and turns on his parents — his brother, he says, is happiest of all up there on the roof, so what possible sense is there in forcing a "cure" upon him? Against their parents, who worry only for appearances, Suejirō takes his stand on the side of the world his brother sees. The shaman is driven off, and the parents retreat into the main house. As the golden evening sun, breaking free of the clouds, pours down over the whole roof, Gitarō calls out to his brother: "Sue, look — you can see a golden palace over there in the clouds, can't you. Look, just look! Isn't it beautiful." Suejirō answers, "Ah, I see it. It's lovely." The mad elder brother on the roof, and the wise younger brother on the ground, both gaze together at the golden evening sun, and the curtain falls.

Characters

  • Katsushima Gitarō　The eldest son, who sits atop the roof gazing endlessly out at the sea
  • Suejirō　His younger brother, a seventeen-year-old middle-school student
  • Gisuke and Oyoshi　Gitarō's parents
  • The woman who calls herself a shaman　Comes to perform the rite of exorcism

Characters The madman: Katsushima Gitarō, twenty-four years old His younger brother: Suejirō, a seventeen-year-old middle-school student Place: An island belonging to Sanuki in the Inland Sea Gisuke: (unseen) That Gi, up on the roof again, is he. The ending (original text)

Gitarō: (in a state of rapture) Listen! From inside the palace, I can hear my favorite flute song playing! What a beautiful sound.
