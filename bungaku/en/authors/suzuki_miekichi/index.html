Biography

Born in Hiroshima in 1882. While a student at Tokyo Imperial University he wrote his debut story, "Chidori" ("Plover"), and, highly praised by Natsume Sōseki, was published in "Hototogisu" on his recommendation, emerging as one of the outstanding talents among Sōseki's disciples. He was known for lyrical stories such as "Yamabiko" ("The Mountain Echo") and "Kuwa no Mi" ("Mulberries"), but the birth of his daughter turned him toward children's literature, and in 1918 he founded the children's magazine "Akai Tori" ("The Red Bird"). Gathering first-rate writers such as Akutagawa Ryūnosuke, Kitahara Hakushū, and Arishima Takeo, he led the "Akai Tori Movement," which advocated artistic, high-quality fairy tales and children's songs against the vulgar reading matter then aimed at children, and laid the foundation of modern Japanese children's literature. He also left many retellings of the classics, such as "Kojiki Monogatari" ("The Story of the Kojiki"). He died in 1936 at the age of 53.
