What is Bungei Fukkō (Literary Renaissance)?

Category
Other

A term for the period from around 1933 into the early 1930s' second half when literature regained its vitality. The background was that, after the crushing of the proletarian literature movement under suppression and the retreat of Modernist experimentation, interest returned, in the vacuum that followed, to literature itself rather than to politics. In October 1933 "Bungakukai," with Kobayashi Hideo, Kawabata Yasunari, and Hayashi Fusao among its founders, gathered on its pages both former proletarian writers and Modernists who had once been at odds, and in 1935 the Akutagawa and Naoki Prizes were established, drawing journalistic attention to literature. Established writers, too, produced work in quick succession: Shimazaki Tōson completed his major work "Yoake Mae" ("Before the Dawn"), modeled on his father and depicting the upheaval of the late shogunate and Restoration, in 1935, and Tanizaki Jun'ichirō built a classically beautiful world in "Shunkinshō" ("A Portrait of Shunkin," 1933). New writers such as Ozaki Kazuo, Niwa Fumio, and Ishikawa Tatsuzō also emerged, and the Nihon Roman-ha (Japan Romantic School) and the poetry magazine "Shiki" ("The Four Seasons") arose as well. This vitality quickly deflated amid the tightening of speech controls after the outbreak of the Second Sino-Japanese War in 1937 — a brief season of fruitfulness just before the turn toward the wartime system.
