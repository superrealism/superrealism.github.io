About this work

A short story Higuchi Ichiyō published in the magazine Shinbundan in February 1896. It follows the mind of Oritsu, a young wife going out to a secret meeting behind a good-natured husband's back, torn a thousand ways between guilt and desire. Broken off after the first chapter alone and unfinished, it is a problem work from Ichiyō's last months, cutting sharply into the psychology of an adulterous woman.

Summary

Orisu, the young and beautiful wife of Komatsubara Tōjirō, a dealer in Western sundries, reads, one evening, a letter in a woman's hand that has just been delivered, pretending to her husband that it concerns some matter her elder sister wishes to discuss with her. In truth it is a summons from her lover, Yoshioka. Tōjirō, good-natured and utterly without suspicion, cheerfully gives her leave to go out at night to see her sister, even urging her to take a rickshaw — and that very kindness pierces Orisu's heart all the more sharply. Reproaching herself, who deceives her husband to run to an illicit love, as lower than a dog or a beast, Orisu stands rooted at the crossroads, wavering again and again over whether to go on or turn back. If only, she thinks, she could purify herself and, unknown to anyone, sever this shallow bond with Yoshioka once and for all — it would be for the best, both for his sake and her own — and, resolved at last, she turns to go home; but no sooner does the night wind blow against her than her resolve begins to waver once more. She had, from the very start, never truly given her heart to Tōjirō — theirs had only ever been a marriage in name, she tells herself — and Orisu at last hardens her heart entirely. Let him call her wicked, let him call her a mischief-maker, she does not care; as long as she draws breath, she resolves, she will never part from Yoshioka. Pressing her hands to her ears beneath her hood, she quickens her pace, and before she knows it, the pounding of her heart has stilled, and upon her colorless lips there rises even a cold, faint smile. (Unfinished)

Characters

  • Orisu　The young and beautiful wife of Komatsubara Tōjirō, a dealer in Western sundries. She deceives her good-natured husband to visit her lover, torn between guilt and desire
  • Komatsubara Tōjirō　Orisu's husband. Wealthy but naive and good-natured, he never so much as suspects his wife's infidelity
  • Yoshioka　Orisu's lover. Summons her by letter to meet him at night

The opening (original text)

A single letter in a woman's hand, tossed in at the shop front by the postman as evening fell — she read it by the light of the lamp in the kotatsu room, and rolled it up quickly to tuck away within the folds of her sash; and her every movement afterward betrayed a mind troubled beyond the ordinary, the color of it showing plainly on her face, until her good-natured husband asked her, has something happened? — No, nothing in particular, she answered, only that my elder sister in Nakachō seems to have some worry on her mind.

Uramurasaki by Higuchi Ichiyō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

She had decided, from the very start, that though her body might go to him, her heart she would never truly give — so what obligation, what sense of duty, could possibly bind her now? Let him call her wicked, let him call her a mischief-maker, she did not care; if she displeased him, let him cast her off — to be cast off would, in the end, only be what she truly wished for. Why, even for a moment, had she ever entertained the thought of offering herself up to that fool of a husband and turning her back on Yoshioka-san? As long as she had breath in her body, she would go on meeting him, would never be parted from him; whether her husband took another wife or not, she had vowed never to break this promise, and no matter how kind anyone else might be to her, no matter how gratefully they might speak to her, she had no husband but Yoshioka-san — I will think of nothing more, nothing more, she told herself, and, pressing her hands over her ears beneath her hood, broke into a hurried pace for five or six steps; and before she knew it, the pounding of her heart had stilled, her mind grown calm and clear, and upon her colorless lips there rose even a cold, faint smile.
