What is Bungei Kurabu?

Category
Society / Group

A literary magazine founded in January 1895 when the publisher Hakubunkan consolidated its various literary magazines into one. Centered on fiction, it was read by a broad readership and stands as a leading literary magazine of the Meiji era. In its founding year it carried Izumi Kyōka's "Yakō Junsa" ("The Night-Duty Policeman") and "Gekashitsu" ("The Surgery"), which gave rise to the term "ideas novel," along with many masterworks of the late 1890s, including Higuchi Ichiyō's "Nigorie" ("Troubled Waters") and "Jūsan'ya" ("The Thirteenth Night") and Hirotsu Ryūrō's "Imado Shinjū" ("Love Suicide at Imado"). Its April 1896 issue reprinted Ichiyō's "Takekurabe" ("Growing Up"), which had been serialized in "Bungakukai," in a single collected installment, and Mori Ōgai, Kōda Rohan, and Saitō Ryokuu's joint review "San'nin Jōgo" ("Three Men's Idle Talk," in "Mesamashigusa") praised it lavishly, cementing Ichiyō's reputation once and for all. It later shifted its center of gravity toward more popular fare, and it was in this magazine that the serialization of Okamoto Kidō's "Hanshichi Torimonochō" began in 1917. It ceased publication in January 1933.
