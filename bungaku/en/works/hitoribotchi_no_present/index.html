About this work

A month ago the couple lost four-year-old Hiroshi to the waves, and now they are at a hotel facing the sea — a short story by Yamakawa Masao. Saying that she cannot bear a Christmas without Hiroshi, the wife decides to live apart and moves to a friend's flat in Azabu. After an Christmas Eve walking a town where he has no one to give to and no one to receive from, a bouquet arrives for her the next morning. A quiet, clear piece asking whom a present is for.

Summary

On a bench in the seaside park, Ryōko and her husband are watching the roaring winter sea. In September, Hiroshi, just four years old, was carried off by a wave, and he has not been found. "They say that the moment a man gives in, it is over," says her husband, and tells her that from tomorrow he will go back to the shop. But to Ryōko the man before her seems nothing but a stranger. If she threw herself into the sea, could she be one with Hiroshi? ... "It is like yesterday," her husband murmurs. "And in no time at all, Christmas will be here." At those words Ryōko's cheek trembled. Christmas used to be nothing to her. That day, when she planned the decorations and the presents and the feast, had existed for Hiroshi. "Hiroshi was going to give me a present this year. He said he would give Mother a bunch of flowers, because she likes them...." The one I truly want to give something to, the one it is a pleasure to think about — that is the only person I really need, the only person I really love — and saying so she tells him she is leaving, and moves to the flat in Azabu of Mayumi, an old schoolfellow. ... On Christmas Eve, having seen Mayumi out, Ryōko cannot bear her loneliness and goes into the streets. Everyone carries a beautifully wrapped parcel and looks happy, and she alone has nothing to do with any present at all. Going unsteadily into a haberdasher's, she buys a pair of men's leather gloves. Near noon the next day Mayumi, back from a night out, was standing there with a long cardboard box in her arms. "Look — a present, a bouquet." The name of the giver was Hiroshi, who was dead.

Characters

  • Ryōko　A mother who lost her four-year-old Hiroshi in the sea
  • Her husband　The young master of an old shop, a man of about thirty in spectacles
  • Hiroshi　The four-year-old son carried off by a wave
  • Mayumi　Ryōko's old schoolfellow, a television performer

The opening (original text)

The hotel faced the sea. Though between the hotel and the sea there was first a wide paved road, and then the narrow green belt of the seaside park. Beyond that the sea, with white and pale green foreign steamers of elegant lines, and countless flat barges and the rubbish of the harbor floating on it, was slowly lifting and lowering its deep indigo skin.

Hitoribotchi no purezento by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"You know, I never told Mayumi that I sent the bouquet to myself. So from the beginning she has been convinced it was your present." "Mayumi has?" As he says it the man takes out his lighter and his cigarettes. The woman's hands shelter the flame, and the man blows out white smoke. "Yes. And it put her in high spirits." "Why?" "Come, think it out for yourself," she said, and laughed again. "She says that when I am there her conduct becomes wonderfully good."

Hitoribotchi no purezento by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)
