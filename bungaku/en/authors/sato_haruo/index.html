Biography

Born in 1892 in Shingū, Higashimuro District, Wakayama Prefecture (now Shingū City). Born into a family of physicians, his father was a man of letters devoted to Masaoka Shiki. He immersed himself in literature from his middle-school years, and in 1908 his tanka won recognition in a selection by Ishikawa Takuboku. He entered the literature department of Keio University and studied under Nagai Kafū, starting out as a writer of the Aesthetic school. He drew attention with the fantastical short story "Supein-inu no Ie" ("The House of a Spanish Dog"), published in 1917, and established his standing as a writer with "Den'en no Yūutsu" ("Rural Melancholy") in 1918. He carried on a long friendship, and rivalry, with Tanizaki Jun'ichirō, and in 1930 he married Chiyo, Tanizaki's former wife, causing a public sensation (the "wife-transfer incident"). He served as a member of the Akutagawa Prize selection committee from its founding in 1935. He died suddenly of a heart attack in 1964 while recording a radio program at his home, at the age of 72.
