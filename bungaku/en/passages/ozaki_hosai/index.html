Selected Haiku of Ozaki Hōsai

Even coughing, alone

This anguished cry of free-verse haiku, in a mere handful of syllables, distills to its utmost extreme the solitude of one who is sick and alone.

No vessel to hold it — I receive it in both hands

A verse composed of nothing but two hands held out empty. Hōsai's life, stripped of all possessions, takes shape directly as this haiku.

Such a fine moon — I watch it alone, and go to sleep.

A haiku of gazing at the moon from his solitary hermitage. The loneliness of having no one to share such a moon with and a stillness that is nonetheless content melt together in a few words.

I go round to the back of the grave.

A free-verse haiku of only seven syllables. Presenting nothing but the casual act of walking round to the back of a grave, it leaves to the reader the stirrings of a heart facing the dead.

The flesh wasting away — how thick the bone is.

A haiku that gazes at his own body wasting with illness. In the matter-of-fact observation that the thickness of the bone stands out as the flesh falls away lies the calm gaze of Hōsai near death.
