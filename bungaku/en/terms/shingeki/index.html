What is Shingeki (New Theater)?

Category
Form / Genre

A theater-reform movement that sought to build, in opposition to kabuki and shinpa, a new form of theater grounded in the principles of Western modern drama. It began with the Bungei Kyōkai, formed in 1906 by Tsubouchi Shōyō and Shimamura Hōgetsu, and the Jiyū Gekijō, founded in 1909 by Osanai Kaoru and the second Ichikawa Sadanji, and, through translated stagings of modern drama such as Ibsen's, it bound theater together with modern literature. In the Bungei Kyōkai's 1911 production of "A Doll's House," Matsui Sumako played Nora, becoming the starting point for shingeki and the actress to take root in Japan, and it also stirred up the "Nora question" debated among the women of "Seitō." In 1924, Osanai Kaoru and Hijikata Yoshi opened the Tsukiji Little Theater, Japan's first shingeki venue built for that purpose, as the base of the movement, and in the Shōwa era Kishida Kunio supported it from the side of playwriting, founding the Bungakuza theater company together with Kubota Mantarō and Iwata Toyoo in 1937. Izumi Kyōka's fantastical plays such as "Yasha ga Ike" ("Demon Pond") also found new life on the shingeki stage in later years.
