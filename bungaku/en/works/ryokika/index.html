About this work

An unusual body of tanka that Yumeno Kyūsaku, the author of Dogra Magra, serialized in detective magazines for nearly ten years. In a free three-line form, he writes of nothing but the dark parts of the mind — the will to kill, obsession, perversion, self-loathing. Neither crime fiction nor confession, it crystallizes the madness that wells up in the middle of ordinary life: a body of work without parallel in Japanese tanka.

Summary

"To kill would be nothing at all — / thinking so, through the crowd / I stride along" — from the first poem the strangeness of this group of tanka is plain. The state of mind that writes out a certain name carefully and then carefully obliterates and burns it; the injecting of red ink into the eyes of a woman's photograph; the daydream of strangling the lady of the house in the drawing room and letting oneself be taken. "In my breast is a forest of wickedness; / now and then / the woodpecker comes and will not stop its knocking." He looks at a razor with a fine edge and smiles like a madman before the mirror; he prays that a man climbing a chimney may fall; he asks a doctor where a needle must be stuck into the human body for instant death. Before long the point of view changes, and he sings from the side of the killed and the dying as well: "The fellow who came first to set up the incense sticks / did to me..."; "The young doctor said he had taken charge of my life / and grinned, curse him." A convict, a wayfarer taken ill on the road, a homicidal maniac, Freud, men who never came back from the North Pole — figures who appear without connection offer each a single instant of madness. "The homicidal maniac / had a clock with no hands; / every time he killed he wound it up." And the last poem is oddly quiet: he kicks a stone that is gnashing its teeth in the middle of the road, fondles it along for a league, grows fond of it, and then suddenly throws it into a pond.

To kill would be nothing at all — thinking so, through the crowd I stride along To write out a certain name carefully and carefully to obliterate it and burn it: that state of mind Into the eyes of a certain woman's photograph I try injecting the red ink from my pen
