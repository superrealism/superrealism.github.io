About this work

A horse wakes from a nap with one hind leg gone to sleep. Convinced that someone has stolen it, the horse goes about kicking the legs of chairs and tables one after another — a children's story by Niimi Nankichi, as short as a joke.

Summary

Two horses were dozing by a window. One of them, woken by a sneeze from a cool breeze, finds one of its hind legs asleep, and staggers unsteadily. However hard it tries, it just can't get any strength into it. "This is terrible — somebody's gone and stolen one of my hind legs!" — even when its friend says, "It's right there where it always is," the horse won't back down, insisting that since it won't move the way it wants, this can't be its own leg. When its friend gives it an experimental kick and it doesn't hurt at all, the horse's conviction only deepens, and it sets off to search for its stolen leg. It kicks the leg of a chair, kicks the leg of a table, kicks the leg of a bed — and every one of them just breaks, without a word of complaint from any of them. Deciding, then, that its friend must be the culprit, the horse goes back and, seeing its chance, gives the friend's hind leg a good hard kick. When the startled friend kicks back with all its might, the horse — whose numbness has by now worn off — jumps up too, crying "Ouch!", and only then does it finally understand what has really happened. A short piece in which Nankichi, writing lightly in plain hiragana prose, spins out the comedy of pushing forward on a single mistaken piece of logic.

The opening (original text)

Two horses were dozing by a window. Then a cool breeze came up, and one of them sneezed and woke up. But one of its hind legs had gone to sleep, so it staggered and lost its footing. "Well, well." However hard it tried to put strength into that leg, it just wouldn't go in at all.

Ashi by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"You dunce, you." Its friend kicked back with all its might. The numbness having worn off by now, that horse too cried, "Ouch!" and jumped up. And at last, it finally understood: its leg had never been stolen at all — it had only been asleep.

Ashi by Niimi Nankichi (Aozora Bunko)
