About this work

The first of the Tange Sazen stories, serialized as "Ōoka Seidan: A New Version" in the "Tokyo Nichinichi Shimbun" and the "Osaka Mainichi Shimbun" from 1927 into the following year. A matched pair of famous blades forged by Seki no Magoroku: Kenun-maru and Konryū-maru. Parted from each other, they are said to sob through the night and to raise a whirlpool of blood without fail. Around these swords move Suwa Eizaburō, a young fencer of the Onozuka school, the bannerman Suzukawa Genjūrō, and the nihilistic one-eyed, one-armed swordsman Tange Sazen. Through the overwhelming popularity of Sazen, who entered the story as a supporting character, it became the work that changed the history of popular historical fiction.

Summary

In Akebono no Sato, behind the Nezu Gongen shrine in Edo, the house of Onozuka Tessai, who keeps a town fencing school of the Shinpen Musō school, has handed down a matched pair of famous blades forged by Seki no Magoroku. The larger is called Kenun-maru, the smaller Konryū-maru. So long as the two are in one place all is well; but once they are parted, it is said, they sob and choke in the dead of night and never fail to raise a storm in which blood is shed. Every year at the great tournament held on the day of the Genchō, the highest scorer is permitted to wear Kenun-maru and the runner-up Konryū-maru. On the eve of it, inspecting the swords for the first time in a year, Tessai asks his daughter Yayoi, "And what do you think of Eizaburō?" — and then writes out a paper granting Kenun-maru and his daughter alike to the winner of the tournament. But Tessai's pupils are not the only ones set on the swords. Beginning with a sword collector's mania in Sōma in Ōshū, and with the mansion of the bannerman Suzukawa Genjūrō for its stage, the struggle for the two blades becomes a whirlpool that draws in all Edo. And at the center of that whirlpool stands a swordsman of monstrous shape, who has lost his right eye and his right arm: Tange Sazen.

Characters

  • Tange Sazen　A swordsman of monstrous shape, who has lost his right eye and his right arm. He slips into Edo in pursuit of Kenun-maru
  • Onozuka Tessai　An old fencing master who keeps a Shinpen Musō school at Akebono no Sato. The Kenun and Konryū blades have come down to him
  • Suwa Eizaburō　A young fencer of Tessai's school, who loves and is loved by Yayoi
  • Yayoi　Tessai's daughter, eighteen years old
  • Suzukawa Genjūrō　A bannerman of Honjo, master of a mansion rumored to be haunted
  • Oen　A woman drawn into the whirlpool of the story

The Sword That Weeps at Night An autumn night wearing quietly on. The wind must have risen; now and then there is a rustle against the closed rain shutters — the diseased leaves of the persimmon in the garden blowing against them. Each time that wind leaks through a crack and stirs the flame of the lamp, the two shadows on the wall waver like huge goblins —

The ending (original text)

Listening vaguely to this talk as to something that had little to do with her, Oen made her unsteady way to the railing of the bridge. The world turns and changes, and the shapes differ, but the waves of people swirl with their own greed laid bare. The madder-colored evening sun filled heaven and earth, and threw Oen's shadow long and black and slanting on the earth of the crossroads. Lamps come on along the bank… like a dream. And Edo's festival goes on into the night.
