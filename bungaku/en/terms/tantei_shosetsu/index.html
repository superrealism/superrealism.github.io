What is Tantei Shōsetsu (Detective Fiction)?

Category
Form / Genre

A form and genre of fiction taking crime and mystery-solving as its theme. In Japan it flourished chiefly on the pages of the magazine "Shinseinen," from the late Taishō era into the early Shōwa era, when Edogawa Ranpo, Yokomizo Seishi, and Oguri Mushitarō, among others, published a great many works. In the early Shōwa era a classification spread, distinguishing "orthodox detective fiction," centered on logical mystery-solving, from "unorthodox detective fiction," strongly colored by the grotesque, the fantastic, and the macabre; Kōga Saburō, among others, is credited with coining these names. Many of Ranpo's own representative works fall into the unorthodox category, known for grotesque works such as "Ningen Isu" ("The Human Chair") and "Injū" ("The Beast in the Shadows"), while Yumeno Kyūsaku left the abstruse fantasy and madness of "Dogura Magura," and Oguri Mushitarō left the erudite orthodox mystery-solving of "Kokushikan Satsujin Jiken" ("The Case of the Black Death Mansion"), each school producing distinctive writers of its own. Beloved as one branch of popular fiction by readers of the Taishō and Shōwa eras, it leads directly into the flourishing of postwar mystery fiction.
