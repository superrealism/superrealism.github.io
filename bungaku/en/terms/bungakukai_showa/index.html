What is Bungakukai (Shōwa)?

Category
Society / Group

A literary magazine founded in October 1933 with Kobayashi Hideo, Takeda Rintarō, Hayashi Fusao, and Kawabata Yasunari, among others, as its coterie. It began at a time when proletarian literature was in decline, setting out to defend serious literature independent of politics and commercialism; its publisher later passed through Bunpodō and others to the Bungei Shunjū company, and, with interruptions and revivals along the way, it continues to this day. Its February 1936 issue carried "The First Night of Life" by Hōjō Tamio, a writer with Hansen's disease discovered by Kawabata Yasunari, bringing his name into the world; in 1939 it carried Dazai Osamu's "Joseito" ("Schoolgirl"), and in 1942 it carried "Sangetsuki" ("The Moon over the Mountain") and "Moji-ka" ("The Curse of Words") — published together under the title "Kotan" ("Old Tales") — by the then-unknown Nakajima Atsushi, making the magazine the venue for a number of important works of Shōwa literature. Nakajima's posthumous work "Ri Ryō" ("Li Ling," 1943) and Miki Kiyoshi's serialized "Jinseiron Nōoto" ("Notebooks on Life," from 1938) were also published here. It shares its name with, but is a separate magazine from, the Romantic-era "Bungakukai" founded in 1893.
