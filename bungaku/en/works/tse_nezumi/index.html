About this work

The mouse Tse, who demands compensation for every least thing, is little by little left alone by everyone around him — one of the stories in Miyazawa Kenji's collection The Restaurant of Many Orders. It draws the end that comes to someone who blames everyone but himself, in a tone mixing laughter and bitterness. The very abruptness of the close is what stays with one.

Summary

In the pitch-dark loft of an old house lived a mouse named Tse. One day a weasel told him that sugar candy was spilling out of a hole in the cupboard; and when he ran there delighted, an army of ants had already thrown a fourfold cordon round it and he was driven off. In a rage Tse went to the weasel's house and pressed him: "To deceive a weak creature like me. You must make it up to me." And so it went from then on. If he failed where a pillar had directed him, he demanded it of the pillar; then of the weasel, then of the lead soldier, then of the bucket — whenever anything happened, he insisted: "To deceive a weak creature like me is too bad. You must make it up to me." Those who at first felt sorry and apologized grew sick of it little by little, and no one would speak to Tse any more. In the end the only thing that would talk to him was the mousetrap in the kitchen. The mousetrap told him to come, there was a fine fish cake — but when he went, the fish cake had gone bad. "Mr. Mousetrap. This is too much. You must make it up to me" — and when he flew at it as usual, the mousetrap was so angry that its wire rang, and with that the hook holding the bait came loose and the door shut. Tse shouted all night and stamped his feet and went on crying; and in the morning a manservant with a bright red face came and jumped for joy. "Got him! Got him! Caught at last. A nasty-looking mouse. Come out, then, young fellow."

Characters

  • Tse the mouse　The mouse who demands that everything be "made up to" him
  • The weasel　Who told him about the sugar candy
  • The mousetrap　The kitchen implement that talked with Tse to the last

The opening (original text)

In the pitch-dark loft of a certain old house there lived a mouse whose name was Tse. One day, as Tse was walking along the highway under the floor, looking about him in every direction, a weasel came running toward him like the wind, carrying a great many fine things. And seeing Tse, he stopped a moment and said quickly, "Here, Tse. There is sugar candy spilling out of the hole in your cupboard. Go quickly and pick it up."
