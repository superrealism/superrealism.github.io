What is Renga (Linked Verse)?

Category
Form / Genre

A collaborative poem that flourished in the medieval period, in which multiple poets alternately compose long verses (5-7-5) and short verses (7-7) in succession. It arose from the pastime of two people dividing the upper and lower halves of a waka between them, and from the Kamakura period on, the hyakuin form, linking a hundred verses, along with a set of rules (shikimoku) governing which motifs could and could not appear near one another, was established, and it matured as an art of the poetic gathering. In the Nanboku-chō period, Nijō Yoshimoto, with the help of Gusai, compiled the first renga anthology, the "Tsukubashū" (1356), and renga gained standing as a literary art on a par with waka. Sōgi, in the mid-Muromachi period, raised it to artistic heights, and the "Minase Sangin Hyakuin" (1488), which he composed at the Minase Shrine with his disciples Shōhaku and Sōchō, was revered as the model of the hyakuin form. That each verse builds a new world while taking up the previous one, and that the whole nonetheless forms a single flow, well expresses the collaborative nature of Japanese poetry. Departing from this formality and incorporating colloquial and comic language was "haikai no renga," as in Yamazaki Sōkan's "Inu Tsukubashū," which went on to become the mother body of early-modern haikai and, further, of haiku. In early-modern haikai, the hyakuin was shortened to the thirty-six-verse kasen, which became the mainstream form of renku.
