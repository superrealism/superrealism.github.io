About this work

A short story published in Kaihō in 1926. Its stage is the decrepit cargo ship Daisan Kintokimaru, whose compass is out of true and whose bottom is rotted enough for seawater to seep through, and which goes on sailing all the same on the strength of a shipping boom. When cholera breaks out on the way back from Manila, the captain orders the sick and the dead thrown into the forepeak. Down fifteen feet there is nothing for it but to jump of one's own accord. A piece rooted in his years at sea, pressing with irony and anger the question of what is left of a ship that has treated human beings as consumables.

Summary

The Daisan Kinji Maru was a ship so far gone that its compass could no longer hold a steady bearing, and its hull was so rotten that a hammer's blow would punch clean through. Yet it kept sailing all the same, driven by wartime prosperity, lurching about the Inland Sea like a drunkard. Loaded with coal at Miike Harbor, it sailed for Manila. Its cargo exchanged, and setting off on the return voyage, one of the sailors began to suffer as though struck down by heatstroke on the deck. His mates laughed it off, saying he'd probably had too much 'electric brandy,' and dumped him into his bunk. But what was raging in his gut was cholera. He died alone on the slatted floor of the dark sailors' quarters, with no one to watch over him. A ship's boy, coming in to prepare the evening meal, stepped on what he took to be a rope in the dark — and it was his shipmate's corpse. That night, while the crew waited for the burial at sea the next day, another sailor and a fireman also fell ill. Once he knew it was cholera, the captain ordered that both the sick and the dead be put into the forepeak. But the only way down into that hold was by rope ladder, and a sick man had to climb down and drop into it on his own. 'I'll get better,' 'Just let me stay in the living world as long as I'm still alive' — the sailors, torn between pity and fury, watched their comrades beg, hands pressed together, and sent them down all the same. Seventeen laborers and four crewmen went down 'of their own accord,' leaving behind on the ship only six senior officers, two sailors, and one fireman. Even with the chief engineer shoveling coal and the captain himself at the helm, the ship would move no more.

Characters

  • Yasuda　The first sailor struck down by cholera. Dies alone on the slatted floor of the sailors' quarters, with no one to watch over him
  • The ship's boy　A young man who, coming in to prepare the evening meal, steps on his shipmate's corpse in the dark
  • The bosun　The head of the lower crew, who at first dismisses his shipmate's death as nothing more than a drunken stupor
  • The captain　The man in command of the Daisan Kinji Maru, who orders that both the sick and the dead be thrown into the forepeak

The opening (original text)

It was that kind of ship. Sailing from Hokkaidō toward Yokohama, one had, without fail, to keep the Kinkasan lighthouse on the starboard side. The Daisan Kinji Maru — a strong-sounding name — had passed the Kinkasan lighthouse thirty minutes before, keeping it to starboard. The sea was middling. Neither calm nor rough. Thirty minutes later, the helmsman of the Daisan Kinji Maru saw the lighthouse on the port side.

Rōdōsha no inai fune by Hayama Yoshiki (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

When at last she was found by a destroyer, aboard her were the half-mad, half-witted captain — of whom the shipping company's president would say, 'No wonder the ship couldn't move' — a handful of mummy-like laborers, and many rotting corpses.
