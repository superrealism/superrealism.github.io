About this work

Tanizaki's first published story and the work that made his name, printed in the second Shinshichō in November 1910; Nagai Kafū praised it warmly and Tanizaki was noticed at once. A tattooist works the figure of a female spider into the skin of a beautiful girl and in doing so transforms her into a bewitching woman. The themes at the root of all Tanizaki's work — absorption in female beauty, and the masochism of the man it masters — are already fully present.

Summary

Seikichi, a tattooer of great skill, carried a secret wish that when he met a woman with beautiful skin he would drive his own soul into it. One day, in a white bare foot glimpsed through the blind of a palanquin, Seikichi finds the ideal skin he has sought for years. Several years later, when the girl comes to his house on an errand from a geisha house in Fukagawa, Seikichi keeps her there, puts her to sleep with a drug, and with every ounce of his strength cuts a great spider onto her back. Waking, the girl suffers terrible pain; but as the tattoo is completed, the bewitching and powerful woman sleeping within her is called out. She is no longer the timid girl she was, and, as if looking down at Seikichi, she tells him that he was the first to become her fertilizer, and shows him the tattoo on her back without a trace of shame.

Characters

  • Seikichi　A tattooer of great skill. The protagonist, who seeks out a woman with beautiful skin and sets about making his ideal woman
  • The woman　A girl who comes on an errand from a geisha house in Fukagawa. Seikichi cuts a great spider into her, and she is transformed into a bewitching woman

The opening (original text)

It was still a time when people had the precious virtue of foolishness, and the world did not grate so harshly as it does now. It was a time so easy-going that professions which sold nothing but chatter — the tea-attendants and the jesters — could make a perfectly good living, keeping the clouds from the placid faces of lords and young masters, and seeing that laughter never ran short among the ladies of the great houses and the courtesans.

The ending (original text)

"Before you go, let me see that tattoo once more," said Seikichi. The woman nodded without a word and slipped the robe from her shoulders. Just then the morning sun struck the face of the tattoo, and the woman's back blazed.

Famous passages

It was in the days when people still possessed that precious virtue called folly, and the world had not yet come to grate against itself as harshly as it does now.

A celebrated opening that announces Tanizaki's aesthetic world. Out of a longing for an age when folly could still be a precious virtue, an uncanny story begins to take shape.

Master, I have thrown away, once and for all, that timid heart I used to carry. — And you, it seems, were the very first to become my fertilizer.

The closing words the girl, now tattooed on her back with a golden orb-weaver spider, hurls at the tattooist Seikichi. A single instant in which the one who tattoos and the one who is tattooed change places.
