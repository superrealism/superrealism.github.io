What is Minzokugaku (Folklore Studies)?

Category
Other

A discipline that seeks to uncover, through folk traditions such as legends, folk tales, customs, and beliefs, the history of the life and culture of nameless common people (jōmin). In Japan its founder is Yanagita Kunio, and "Tōno Monogatari" ("The Legends of Tōno," 1910), in which he set down in dignified classical prose the local legends he heard from Sasaki Kizen of Tōno, Iwate Prefecture, is regarded as the monument marking its starting point. Through founding the magazine "Kyōdo Kenkyū" ("Local Studies," 1913) and forming the Minkan Denshō no Kai (later the Folklore Society of Japan), Yanagita organized the field of study and established the theory and methodology of Japanese folklore studies. Minakata Kumagusu, known worldwide for his research on slime molds and drawing broadly on legends from around the world in "Jūnishikō" ("Studies on the Twelve Zodiac Animals"), and Orikuchi Shinobu, who studied under Yanagita and built a system of ancient studies including the theory of the "marebito" (divine visitor) while also standing as a tanka poet under the pen name Shaku Chōkū, are counted as towering figures alongside him. As in Orikuchi's novel "Shisha no Sho" ("The Book of the Dead"), in which his scholarship and poetic sensibility fused, folklore studies also intersected deeply with modern literature, and played a great role in the literary rediscovery of ghost stories and legends.
