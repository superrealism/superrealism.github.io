About this work

A short story Kikuchi Kan published in the Osaka Mainichi Shimbun in 1919. Sakata Tōjūrō, a great actor of Genroku kabuki, must play an adultery piece with a married woman written for him by Chikamatsu Monzaemon, and to perfect his art he works a false love on Okaji, the wife of a teahouse. The pitilessness of an art-for-art's-sake that will sacrifice even a person's life to the way of the actor, drawn in the kabuki world of Genroku Kyoto: one of Kikuchi's central works, later staged and filmed again and again.

Summary

In Genroku-era Kyoto, Sakata Tōjūrō of the Miyako Mantayū troupe, celebrated as the foremost actor of the three great cities, finds himself eclipsed by the popularity of Nakamura Shichisaburō, newly arrived from Edo, and stakes his revival on a new play written for him by Chikamatsu Monzaemon. But the role — a married woman's secret lover — is one Tōjūrō has never once played. Stalled in rehearsal, he sets a false love in motion against Okaji, wife of Sōsei, a woman who has secretly adored him for twenty years. Late one night in her room, Tōjūrō's words break through her chaste resolve, and with trembling hands she puts out the lamp. But there in the darkness, Tōjūrō, seizing at last the fear and rapture of an illicit lover — the very sensation of the role he had been chasing — slips away without a word. On the new play's opening day, Tōjūrō's performance of the adultery scene rings so true that it becomes the talk of the whole capital. But one morning in mid-March, Okaji is found hanged from a beam in the dressing room of the Mantayū theater. Hearing the news, Tōjūrō's color changes as though struck by lightning, even as he repeats to himself: for the sake of Tōjūrō's art, what is the life of one woman, or two. After Okaji's death, Tōjūrō's art only grows more brilliant still — the art of a man who has learned, in his very bones, the terror of sin.

Characters

  • Sakata Tōjūrō　The great actor of Genroku-era kabuki, who sets a false love in motion for the sake of perfecting his art
  • Okaji　Wife of Sōsei. Her years of secret devotion to Tōjūrō are exploited, and she takes her own life
  • Chikamatsu Monzaemon　The playwright who writes the new play for Tōjūrō

The opening (original text)

It was toward the end of February in a year when the era name Genroku had, before anyone quite noticed, run past its tenth year. In the capital, the scent of spring wrapped itself about everything. Only just recently, patches of snow had still clung, mottled, to the very summit of Mount Hiei, but beneath the soft light of spring they had melted away entirely, leaving the mountain's bare flank — a yellow tinged faintly with pale violet — standing out sharp against the wide sky.

Tōjūrō no koi by Kikuchi Kan (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Ever since Okaji's death, Tōjūrō's performance as Mōemon grew more and more brilliant. His eyes carved out, vivid and unmistakable, the anguish of a sinful man who steals another man's wife. When he took Osan's hand in the darkness, the fear and unease of the secret lover, and the terror of his own sin, welled up and filled his whole body. There seemed, in that moment, to be no distinguishing at all whether it was Tōjūrō playing Mōemon, or Mōemon become Tōjūrō. It was, no doubt, because Tōjūrō himself had learned, in his very bones, the terror of courting another man's wife.

Tōjūrō no koi by Kikuchi Kan (Aozora Bunko)
