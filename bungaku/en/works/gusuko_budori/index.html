About this work

One of Miyazawa Kenji's central children's stories, published in the second volume of the magazine Jidō Bungaku in March 1932 — one of the few of his works printed in his lifetime. In Ihatov, an ideal country afflicted by cold summers and famine, Budori loses his parents and is separated from his sister, makes his way by study and work to become an engineer at the volcano bureau, and finally gives his own life to save the people from an oncoming cold disaster. The threat of nature, science, and devotion through self-sacrifice: Kenji's thought and his prayer, crystallized in a story.

Summary

Gusukō Budori lives in the great forest of Ihatov with his father, a woodcutter, his mother, and his little sister Neri. Then one year a bitter cold spell brings a ruinous harvest to the forest, and in the hunger his father and mother go off, saying only that they are going into the woods, and never come back, leaving the two children alone. Before long Neri too is carried off by a stranger, and Budori, left without a soul in the world, works for a man who raises silkworms and then for Kūbō, a farmer of the marsh fields, struggling with mud and fertilizer while teaching himself and building up a hunger for learning. Going up to the city of Ihatov, he is recommended by the great Doctor Kūbō, whom he reveres, to a post as an engineer at the Bureau of Volcanoes, and throws himself into the work of controlling eruptions to protect people's lives. He builds tidal power stations and spreads volcanic ash as fertilizer, enriching the land by the power of science — and then learns that the same cold that once took his family is about to come down on Ihatov again. If the volcanic island of Carbonado were made to erupt artificially, the carbon dioxide in the air would increase, the temperature would rise, and the cold could be turned aside — but at the end of the work one man must stay on the island and be lost in the eruption. Refusing the older engineers who try to stop him, Budori takes that part himself. By his sacrifice the climate revives, the crops of that year ripen, and many parents and children like his own family long ago are able to pass a warm and happy winter.

Characters

  • Gusukō Budori　A young man born in the forest of Ihatov. Having lost his family to the cold, he becomes an engineer at the Bureau of Volcanoes and sacrifices himself to save the people. The hero of the tale
  • Neri　Budori's younger sister, carried off by a man during the cold years and lost to him, though they meet again later
  • The great Doctor Kūbō　The teacher under whom Budori studies, and who opens his way to the Bureau of Volcanoes

The opening (original text)

Gusukō Budori was born in a great forest in Ihatov. His father was a famous woodcutter named Gusukō Nadori, a man who could fell any tree, however large, as easily as putting a baby to sleep.

The ending (original text)

But three or four days later the weather grew warmer and warmer, and that autumn the harvest was very nearly an ordinary one. And so a great many fathers and mothers of Budoris — who would otherwise have come to just such a beginning as this story had — were able, together with a great many Budoris and Neris, to pass that winter happily, with warm food and bright firewood.

Famous passages

Guskō Budori was born in the great forest of Ihatov.

The opening of the tale. It begins the life story of Budori, born the son of a woodcutter in the forest of Ihatov, who lives through cold summers and famine and becomes an engineer who gives himself for the people.

And so the many fathers and mothers of Budori, who would otherwise have met the fate with which this story began, were able to spend that winter happily with many Budoris and Neris, with warm food and bright firewood.

The close of the tale. After Budori stays behind on the volcanic island and warms the climate at the cost of his life, it tells how people like his own family long ago were saved, bringing a story of self-sacrifice quietly to an end.
