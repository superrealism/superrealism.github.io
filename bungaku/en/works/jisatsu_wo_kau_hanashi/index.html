About this work

A detective story of the early Shōwa years, beginning from that one bizarre line in a newspaper's advertisement column. The author, Hashimoto Gorō, wrote for the magazine Tantei Shumi, and Edogawa Ranpo acknowledged his gift as a poet. Not a puzzle that reads on its trick, but a piece full of pathos, with poverty, goodwill and loneliness showing through the idea of buying and selling a death.

Summary

Having lost his job, "I" am scanning the classified advertisements in the newspaper when a line under "miscellaneous" stops me cold: "Wish to buy a suicide. Details on request. Only that of a decent young man will do." The advertiser turns out to be a close friend, the young painter Nonomura Shinji. Sensing something is deeply wrong, I call on him and ask why he placed such a notice. Nonomura, his face clouded with melancholy, begins to tell the story behind it — how, in the days when he was too poor even to buy paints, he collapsed from hunger at the edge of a ruined temple, his mind fixed on death. It was there that a young man named Bandō appeared, likewise resolved to die. Bandō made Nonomura an offer: buy his "suicide." That is, if Nonomura would live on in his place and accomplish what he himself could not, Bandō would give him the whole of his fortune. Sent on an errand, Nonomura returned with three hundred yen — only to find his benefactor already dead, his throat cut with a razor. Guided by the dead man's testament, Nonomura used that money to continue his study of painting, and so arrived at the standing he holds today. "When I think of it, it weighs terribly on my conscience — but now a fate like Bandō's own has come to visit me as well." Even so, I cannot bring myself to say that I will buy his suicide. Worrying for my friend, and praying that no buyer will ever come forward — or, if one must, that it be some good woman who will take up the debt — I leave my sorrowful friend's house behind me. Over the town, the gentle winter sun was just beginning to thaw.

Characters

  • I　The narrator, who has lost his job and calls on his friend
  • Nonomura Shinji　A young painter who placed the "wish to buy a suicide" advertisement
  • Mr. Bandō　A young man who "sold" his own suicide to Nonomura

The opening (original text)

—Wanted: a wife worthy of the name. Any respectable, amiable woman of sound sense between eighteen and twenty-seven or twenty-eight, of good lineage and refined bearing, willing to remain faithful to her husband for life, will be welcomed and treated generously as a wife, rich or poor. Smiling at that notice, I went on reading the classifieds in the newspaper, until, coming to the item filed under "miscellaneous," I found myself picking the paper back up without meaning to. —Wish to buy a suicide. Details on request. Only that of a decent young man will do. Nonomura, ××-chō—.

The ending (original text)

"He left me a testament, you see, and it was that which let me go on studying, right up to today. That I can live as I do now, I owe entirely to Bandō. When I think of it, it weighs terribly on my conscience — but now a fate like Bandō's own has come to visit me as well—" Even so, I could not bring myself to say that I would buy his suicide. Worrying for my friend, and praying that no buyer would ever come forward — or, failing that, that it be some good woman who would take up the debt — I left my sorrowful friend's house behind me. Over the town, the gentle winter sun was just beginning to thaw.
