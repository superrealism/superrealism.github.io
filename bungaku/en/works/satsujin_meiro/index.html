About this work

The sixth instalment, written by Hashimoto Gorō, of Murder Labyrinth — a relay detective novel continued in turn by ten writers including Morishita Uson, Ōshita Udaru, Yokomizo Seishi, Edogawa Ranpo and Yumeno Kyūsaku — published in the magazine Tantei Club. Hoshida Daiji, a writer of detective stories being framed for the murder of the actress Miyabe Kyōko, goes to a café to look for the identity of an unseen enemy. Note that it is one section in the middle of a long labyrinth and does not stand complete on its own.

Summary

Having parted from Tsumura, a magazine reporter, at Shinbashi station, the detective writer Hoshida Daiji could not bear the uneasiness rising steadily into his breast. What until a moment ago he had taken for a joke had suddenly come at him as dreadful fact. And in this hateful affair he has been neatly saddled with the part of the criminal. The fingerprints, the footprints, the spectacles, and his past relations with the victim, Miyabe Kyōko. Of any certain proof that he had been worked by a third person there is none. ... This is no time to wait for the enemy's attack. Hoshida drives to that café and asks the waitress of the other night about the sharp-eyed man and the beautiful woman in Western dress who had been at the table opposite. And the waitress answers: those people were not two but three; there had been another behind the palm; and that one was Miyabe Kyōko, the star of the San'ei Kinema. Hoshida, who has just come from seeing her dead body at Kamakura, nearly leapt out of his chair. And more: the challenge had been sent by express from this very café by Kyōko herself. "That woman changed her hand on purpose," Kyōko had laughed. — Then did Kyōko kill herself in order to demonstrate a "perfect crime"? No: someone has worked both Kyōko and himself, choosing her for the victim and him for the criminal. That sharp-eyed man must be the puppeteer. So deciding, Hoshida goes home. But in his room Inspector Masaoka and two detectives were waiting for him.

Characters

The opening (original text)

The unseen enemy Having parted from Tsumura, the magazine reporter, at Shinbashi station, the detective writer Hoshida took a car from there and withdrew for the time being to his own house. An uneasiness he cannot get hold of rises steadily, little by little, into his breast. What he had taken until a moment ago for a joke has suddenly come at him as dreadful fact. And in this hateful, unwelcome affair he has been neatly saddled with the part of the criminal.

Satsujin meiro by Hashimoto Gorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

In the room was Inspector Masaoka. And behind the inspector he saw two figures, plainly detectives, standing with grim faces. "Hoshida. I am sorry for it, but you will come with us to headquarters." The inspector said it in a weighty tone. "W-what for?" Hoshida Daiji barely held himself up beside the door. "What for? There is no need to explain it now. As a suspect in the murder of Miyabe Kyōko —"
