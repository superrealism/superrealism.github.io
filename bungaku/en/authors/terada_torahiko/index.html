Biography

Born in Tokyo in 1878, and spent his boyhood in Kōchi Prefecture, his father's home province. At the Fifth Higher School he studied under Natsume Sōseki and the physicist Tamaru Takurō, receiving his introduction to haiku from Sōseki and to the fascination of physics from Tamaru, and came to love both for the rest of his life. After graduating from the Faculty of Science at Tokyo Imperial University, he threw himself into geophysics and experimental physics as a professor there and a researcher at the Institute of Physical and Chemical Research (RIKEN), winning international recognition for his work on X-rays and crystals, among other subjects. At the same time, as one of Sōseki's disciples, he also devoted himself to writing, publishing many essays under pen names such as Yoshimura Fuyuhiko that combined a scientist's keen powers of observation with a gentle poetic sensitivity. From the lyrical short piece "Donguri" ("Acorns"), remembering his late wife, to celebrated essays discussing science and civilization such as "Tensai to Kokubō" ("Natural Disasters and National Defense") and "Bakemono no Shinka" ("The Evolution of Monsters"), he left a wide range of work and opened a distinctive territory in modern Japanese essay writing. Known too for the maxim "Disaster strikes when it is forgotten," he is revered as a pioneer of disaster-prevention science. A man of many talents, he was also versed in linked verse, haikai, painting, and music. He died in 1935 at the age of 57.
