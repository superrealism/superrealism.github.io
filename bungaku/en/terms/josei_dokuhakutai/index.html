What is Josei Dokuhakutai (Female Monologue Style)?

Category
Style & Technique

A name for a group of stories Dazai Osamu wrote in the first-person voice (monologue) of a woman. Its distinctive feature is the way the female narrator, as if confiding in the reader or someone close to her, recounts her shifting, rambling feelings, shame, and self-consciousness in the rhythms of speech. "Tōrō" ("The Lantern"), published in the magazine "Wakakusa" in October 1937, is regarded as the earliest example, relating as her own confession how Sakiko, a clog-maker's daughter, is caught shoplifting for the student she loves. The next, "Joseito" ("Schoolgirl," 1939, in "Bungakukai"), based on a diary sent to Dazai by a woman reader, depicts the wavering heart of a girl over a single day; it was praised by Kawabata Yasunari, and the collection bearing its title was runner-up for the Kitamura Tōkoku Memorial Literary Prize. There followed many fine pieces from before and during the war, such as "Hazakura to Mateki," in which an elder sister recalls her younger sister's secret, "Hifu to Kokoro," which tells of a wife's heart shaken by a rash spreading over her skin (both 1939), "Kirigirisu" (1940), in which a wife bids farewell to her painter husband as he grows vulgar, and "Chiyojo" (1941), about a girl buffeted by fame that came too early. After the war this voice was carried on in "Villon's Wife" (1947) and the novel "The Setting Sun" (the same year), built around the narration of Kazuko, the daughter of a declining aristocratic family, and it forms one of the major pillars of Dazai's literature.
