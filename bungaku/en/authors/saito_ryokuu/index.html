Biography

Born in 1868 in Kanbe, Ise Province (now Suzuka, Mie Prefecture), the son of a domain physician of the Tsu domain; his real name was Masaru. His family moved to Edo while he was still a child, and their residence in Honjo Midori-chō is said to be the source of his pen name, Ryokuu ("Green Rain," a homophone of "midori"). He studied at the Meiji Law School but left before completing his studies, becoming a disciple of Kanagaki Robun and entering the world of letters. Showing early promise as a sharp-witted, biting critic, he was also recognized as a novelist with "Aburajigoku" ("Hell of Oil") and "Kakurenbo" ("Hide-and-Seek"), published in 1891. Together with Mori Ōgai and Kōda Rohan, he took charge of the anonymous joint criticism column "San'nin Jōgo" ("Three Men's Idle Talk") in the magazine "Mesamashigusa," and was among the first to recognize Higuchi Ichiyō's talent and champion it to the world. After Ichiyō's death he took charge of editing her complete works. Known for his gift for irony and epigram, his witty, urbane prose carried the tradition of Edo-period comic fiction into the modern era. He spent his later years in poverty, suffering from tuberculosis, and died in 1904 at the age of 36.
