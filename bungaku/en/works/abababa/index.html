About this work

A short story published in Chūō Kōron in 1923, one of the Yasukichi pieces whose protagonist, the young teacher Horikawa Yasukichi, is said to be a figure of the author. One day a young wife appears at the small tobacconist's he frequents. Shy and fresh at first, she bears a child as the months pass and changes into a sturdy mother — watched through Yasukichi's eyes with a faint amusement. The title is the sound she makes playing with her baby. A fine piece in which Akutagawa's softer side shows: the change in a woman, and the toughness of life, seen in an ordinary street scene.

Summary

Horikawa Yasukichi, a young teacher, begins at some point to see a young wife at the small shop that sells tobacco and sundries where he always goes. The shop used to be minded by a surly man with a squint; the woman, who seems to have come there newly as a bride, is terribly shy, so shy and fresh that it is all she can do to meet the eyes of a customer like Yasukichi. Every time he buys something he is amused by her innocence, and finds her charming as well. But after a while, not having called at the shop for some time, he looks in again and finds that the wife has become pregnant. In time she has her child, and there she is at the shopfront, holding the baby up high and dandling it in a loud voice: "Ababababa, boo!" Of her old bashfulness there is no trace; what stands there is a "mother," brazen and dependable, who will fear nothing for the sake of her child. Seeing that change before his eyes, Yasukichi finds himself grinning as he looks up at the spring sky. A short story warm with Akutagawa's regard, which finds in one small scene of ordinary life the toughness of the life that turns a woman into a mother.

Characters

  • Horikawa Yasukichi　A young teacher, taken to be the author's alter ego
  • The wife　The young woman who has married into the tobacco shop

The opening (original text)

Yasukichi has known the master of this shop since long ago. Since long ago — or perhaps since the very day he took up his post at that naval school. He happened to go into this shop to buy a box of matches. The shop had a little display window, and inside the window, around a model of the battleship Mikasa flying an admiral's flag, were ranged bottles of curaçao and tins of cocoa and boxes of raisins.

Ababababa by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Abababababa, boo!" With the woman behind him, Yasukichi found himself grinning. The woman is no longer "that woman." She is one of the bold-hearted mothers. Once a woman has become a thing for her child's sake, she is one of those fearful "mothers" who have committed, since ancient times, every sort of wickedness. ... Walking on, Yasukichi looked up blankly at the sky above the houses. In the sky, amid a south wind passing over, hung a single round spring moon, white and faint. ...
