About this work

The collection gathering the last poems Tachihara Michizō went on writing before his death at twenty-four, published in 1947 as the fourth of the Hyacinth Books. It begins with "Song of the Swallow," passes through "To a Child Who Likes Thistle Flowers" and "In the Rain of a Midwinter Night," and closes with the poem that sings: what I dreamed of was one happiness; what I wished for was one love. Soft colloquial Japanese and line breaks full of white space turn into sound a heart that goes on wishing while knowing it will lose.

Summary

"Gray, and all alone, you hang upon my dream, / far-off village" — the collection begins with "The Song of the Swallow," calling out to a spring in which the snow still lies. The days when the plantain lily and the sedge put out flowers that faded quickly, and the goats bleated, and one day and another day went past. Believing that today too, over there, someone is surely waiting as he is, the poet sings that in the summer days to come he will go visiting, to your dream, to my eaves. In the sequence "To a Child Who Loves the Thistle Flower" that follows, a wind flowing through pale green carries a single human word, and everyone receives it with a smile. A world of all things, of wide wind and light, with no hunter and no thief in it. In "The Ring of the Rainbow," while the palms of a girl angel, soft as cloud, scatter flowers, two people twine their arms like gentle trees, and tremble. ... But this clear world is always next door to parting. At the end of the collection the poet imagines a quiet village beyond the range of hills. The blue sky of a bright Sunday; country girls with parasols, dressed up and singing; a great round ring in which they dance. On a low branch a small bird with blue wings sings and tells of it: "What I dreamed of was one love; / what I wished for was one happiness; / and all of it is here."

The opening (original text)

The Song of the Swallow Gray, and all alone, you hang upon my dream, far-off village. In those days the plantain lily and the sedge put out flowers that faded quickly, and the goats bleated, and one day and another day went past. It was full of gentle mornings —

Yasashiki uta by Tachihara Michizō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

What I dreamed of... What I dreamed of was one happiness; what I wished for was one love. Beyond the range of hills too there is a quiet village; there is the blue sky of a bright Sunday. Country girls with parasols, dressed in their best, are singing; making a great round ring, the country girls are dancing. What tells of it and sings is one small bird with blue wings, singing on a low branch: what I dreamed of was one love, what I wished for was one happiness, and all of it is here.

Yasashiki uta by Tachihara Michizō (Aozora Bunko)
