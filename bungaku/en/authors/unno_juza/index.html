Biography

Born in Tokushima City in 1897; his real name was Sano Shōichi. After graduating from the science and engineering department of Waseda University he worked as an engineer at the Ministry of Communications' Electrical Testing Laboratory, researching radio engineering, and while doing so made his debut as a detective writer with "Denkiburo no Kaishi Jiken" ("The Mysterious Death in the Electric Bath"), published in "Shinseinen" in 1928. Making full use of his scientific knowledge, he wrote extensively in detective fiction and in science fiction and boys' fiction such as "Jūhachiji no Ongakuyoku" ("The Eighteen O'Clock Music Bath") and "Kasei Heidan" ("The Martian Legion"), and later came to be called "the founding father of Japanese science fiction." He created the great detective Homura Sōroku, and, as a writer of "unorthodox" detective fiction, formed close ties with Edogawa Ranpo and others. During the Pacific War he was active as a war correspondent, but after the defeat, dispirited, his health failed, and he died of illness in 1949 at the age of 51.
