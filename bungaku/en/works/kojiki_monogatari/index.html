About this work

Suzuki Miekichi, who opened up children's literature in Japan by founding Akai Tori, retold the myths and legends of the Kojiki, Japan's oldest classic, in language plain enough for children. The beginning of heaven and earth and the birth of the gods, the country-making of Izanagi and Izanami, the heavenly rock cave, the killing of the eight-headed serpent, the white hare of Inaba, the luck of the sea and the luck of the mountain, and the campaigns of Yamato Takeru — a classic of children's literature bringing back in warm, unhurried prose the great stories the Japanese have handed down.

Summary

At the very beginning of the world, when heaven and earth were first born, the gods came into being one after another in the High Plain of Heaven. The two gods Izanagi and Izanami give birth to the land of Japan and to many gods besides, but Izanami dies after giving birth to the god of fire, and Izanagi, longing for his wife, travels to the land of Yomi, the underworld, to find her. This book is Suzuki Miekichi's gentle retelling, for children, of these tales handed down in the Kojiki, from the age of the gods to the age of men. The sun goddess Amaterasu shuts herself away in the heavenly rock cave and plunges the world into darkness; Susanoo slays the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi; the kind-hearted god Ōkuninushi helps the white hare of Inaba; Yamasachihiko visits the palace of the sea; and the tragic hero Yamato Takeru travels the land, subduing province after province — these grand and eventful episodes are told one after another. In time the story leaves the age of the gods behind and moves into the reigns of the successive emperors, closing with the history of Japan's beginnings down to the reign of Empress Suiko. Bringing a difficult classic back to life in warm words that let children read with a pounding heart, this book stands as the representative work of Miekichi's career as a writer for children.

Characters

  • Izanagi and Izanami　The first male and female gods, who give birth to the land of Japan and to many gods besides
  • Amaterasu　The sun goddess. Grieving over her brother's violent conduct, she shuts herself away in the heavenly rock cave, plunging the world into darkness
  • Susanoo　Amaterasu's younger brother. Slays the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi in Izumo
  • Ōkuninushi　The kind-hearted god who helps the white hare of Inaba. Rules the land of Izumo
  • Yamato Takeru　The tragic hero who travels the land, subduing province after province

The opening (original text)

At the very beginning of the world's creation. When heaven and earth had first taken shape, together with them was born, at a place above the heavens called Takamagahara, the god named Ame-no-Minakanushi, the very first ancestor of us Japanese. After him were born the two gods Takamimusubi and Kamimusubi. At that time, neither heaven nor earth had yet hardened firmly into shape; both floated softly, softly, like jellyfish, all runny as though oil lay spread upon them. Into the midst of this, just as a reed shoot sprouts forth, two gods were born. Then two more gods were born, and after them, one male god and one female god together, eight gods in all were born in succession, and at last were born the male god Izanagi and the female god Izanami.

Kojiki monogatari by Suzuki Miekichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

After ruling the realm for eight years, the Emperor passed away at the age of thirty-eight. The Emperor had no children of his own, and so his elder brother, Prince Ōke, ascended the throne after him as Emperor Ninken. The Emperor moved his palace to the Hirotaka Palace of Ishinokami in Yamato, and took as his empress the Lady Kasuga no Ōiratsume, daughter of Emperor Yūryaku. After this Emperor, Prince Oshinabe no Wakasazaki ascended the throne as Emperor Buretsu. After him, the Emperors Keitai, Ankan, Senka, Kinmei, Bidatsu, Yōmei, Sushun, and Suiko ascended the throne one after another in succession.

Kojiki monogatari by Suzuki Miekichi (Aozora Bunko)
