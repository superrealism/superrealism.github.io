About this work

An old man walking the hills and fields calling out to ask the tongue-cut sparrow where its lodging is, and the choice between a large basket and a small one — one of the five great folk tales of Japan, retold by Kusuyama Masao in prose meant to be read aloud. The ending, in which greed is rewarded with a basket packed full of monsters, is one of the scenes from the old tales that stays with children most strongly.

Summary

An old man who had no children kept a young sparrow in a cage and cherished it. One day, while he was out, the sparrow licked up all the starch for the washing. The ill-natured old woman was angry, cut the sparrow's tongue with her scissors, and drove it away. When the old man came home from the hills and learned of it he grieved bitterly, and from the next morning he went out over field and mountain calling, "Tongue-cut sparrow, where is your house? Chū, chū, chū." At last from within a great thicket came an answer — "Tongue-cut sparrow, my house is here" — and the sparrow came out of a pretty red house to meet him. The sparrow's family entertained him with a feast and with dancing, and as he was leaving they offered him two wicker baskets, a big one and a small one, and let him choose. Being an old man, he chose the small one; and when he opened it at home it was packed full of treasure — gold and silver and coral. Seeing it, the old woman wanted some too and forced her way to the sparrow's house, and chose the big basket and carried it home on her back. Unable to bear the weight, she rested by the roadside and opened the lid — and out came a crowd of monsters: a three-eyed goblin, a one-eyed goblin, a toad monk. "You greedy old woman!" they glared at her and licked her, and she ran home shrieking. One should not do cruel things, nor be too greedy — so the old man said.

Characters

  • The old man　The old man who loves his sparrow and goes searching for it
  • The old woman　The ill-natured old woman who cuts the sparrow's tongue
  • The sparrow　The little sparrow that goes home to its house in the thicket with its tongue cut

The opening (original text)

Long, long ago, in a certain place, there were an old man and an old woman. Since they had no children, the old man kept a young sparrow in a cage and cared for it dearly. One day the old man went to the hills to cut brushwood as usual, and the old woman was washing at the well. While she was away, having left in the kitchen the starch she used for the washing, the young sparrow came pattering out of its cage and licked up every bit of it.

The ending (original text)

When the old woman told him what had happened to her and said, "Ah, I have had quite enough of it," the old man said, as if sorry for her, "Dear, dear, that was a bad business. That is why one should not do such cruel things, nor be too greedy."
