About this work

The masterpiece of diary literature Nagai Kafū went on writing for more than forty years, from 1917 to the day before his death in 1959. By his own account, he made a fair copy of what he had been jotting into a notebook from 16 September 1917 and called it the first volume, adding a volume a year until by the spring of 1933 it ran to seventeen. It sets down, in a dignified literary style mixing Japanese and Chinese, the weather and the plants in his garden each day, his walks about the city, theatre and music, his friendships with men of letters and with geisha, and a cold eye on the changing manners of the age. A monument of modern diary literature, in which the lonely spirit of Kafū — mourning a vanishing Edo and turning his back on the world — is cut. This app carries the years 1917 to 1921, volumes one to five, as published in Aozora Bunko, in separate volumes.
