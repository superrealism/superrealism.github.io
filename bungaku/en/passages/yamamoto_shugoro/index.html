Famous passages by Yamamoto Shūgorō

Well-known lines from the works of Yamamoto Shūgorō.

2 passages

The Fir Tree Remains

Holding fast to pride and honor is brave, and it may look heroic in people's eyes. But a samurai's true duty lies in patience and endurance — in living as long as one can live, and serving. This is not only for samurai; that is what the way of human life is. In every age, what truly upholds and protects the country is this patience and endurance — a force at work where no eye sees and no name appears.

Words spoken by Harada Kai to Toneri, who has been banished on a false charge. The view that the way of human life lies not in the pride of a glorious death but in patience and endurance unseen by others speaks for the very way Kai lives, protecting the domain even at the cost of bearing a traitor's name.

Tales from the Red Beard Clinic

What we can do now, and what we must do first of all, is to fight against poverty and ignorance. By overcoming poverty and ignorance, we make up for what medicine lacks. Do you understand?

Words spoken by Niide Kyojō, called Red Beard, to the young trainee doctor Yasumoto Noboru. Admitting the limits of medicine, he declares that one must fight the poverty and ignorance at the root of illness — the conviction that runs through the whole work.
