What is Shinshichō?

Category
Society / Group

A literary magazine founded by Osanai Kaoru in 1907. Its first incarnation had strongly the character of a personal magazine introducing new European theater and literature, but thereafter students affiliated with Tokyo Imperial University inherited the title, and it was revived intermittently as a coterie magazine. In its second incarnation (from 1910), Tanizaki Jun'ichirō made a spectacular debut in the literary world with "Shisei" ("The Tattooer") and "Kirin," and Watsuji Tetsurō joined the coterie as well. The third and fourth incarnations (1914–17) were centered on Akutagawa Ryūnosuke, Kikuchi Kan, Kume Masao, and Matsuoka Yuzuru, and Akutagawa's "Hana" ("The Nose"), carried in the inaugural issue of the fourth incarnation, won lavish praise from Natsume Sōseki and became his breakthrough work, while Kikuchi's play "Chichi Kaeru" ("Father Returns") was published here as well. These writers came to be called the "Shinshichōha" from the magazine's name, and, with their intellectual style, occupied one corner of the Taishō-era literary establishment. Later still, from the sixth incarnation with Kawabata Yasunari onward, it kept sending new writers into the world with each revival, sustaining its life through the Shōwa era as one of the leading magazines for launching writers in modern literature.
