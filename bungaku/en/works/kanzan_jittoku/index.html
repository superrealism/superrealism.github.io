About this work

A short story published in Shinshōsetsu in January 1916. Drawn from a Tang-dynasty tale, it tells how Lü Qiuyin, governor of Taizhou, is told that the two wild men who work in the kitchen of the Guoqing temple — Hanshan and Shide — are incarnations of Mañjuśrī and Samantabhadra, and goes to worship them; but when he names them for what they are, the two laugh and run away. A light, insouciant satire on the blind reverence of a man who leans on authority. It began as a story Ōgai told his own child, as he records in "How Hanshan and Shide Came to Be Written."

Summary

In the Zhenguan era of the Tang, Lü Qiuyin, appointed to office at Taizhou, was suffering from a fearful headache, and was cured on the instant by a monk named Fenggan who happened to be passing. Lü asked him whether there were in the province to which he was going any sage of the stamp of Mañjuśrī or Samantabhadra. Fenggan replied that Hanshan and Shide, who did the cooking and scullery work in the kitchen of the Guoqing Temple, were none other than incarnations of those two, and went away. Arriving at his post, Lü went at once to the Guoqing Temple and respectfully asked to be brought to Hanshan and Shide. Led by the monks to the front of the kitchen stove, he found two wild-looking men in filthy rags, warming themselves at the fire and talking and laughing. When Lü drew himself up in the full dignity of a provincial official and made his reverence, the two looked at each other and burst out laughing from the bottom of their bellies; and crying "So Fenggan has been talking!" they bolted out of the kitchen and ran away. Behind them were left the dumbfounded Lü and the monk Daoqiao, standing white-faced and rooted to the spot. It is a piece in which Ōgai, with a light and playful brush, quietly satirizes the blind reverence of a man who would worship a sage from behind the shield of his own authority.

Characters

  • Lü Qiuyin　An official posted to Taizhou, who believes what Fenggan tells him and goes to the Guoqing Temple to worship Hanshan and Shide
  • Hanshan　One of the wild men doing scullery work at the Guoqing Temple, said to be an incarnation of Mañjuśrī
  • Shide　The other, working in the same kitchen, said to be an incarnation of Samantabhadra
  • Fenggan　The monk who cures Lü's headache and tells him who Hanshan and Shide are

The opening (original text)

It was in the Zhenguan era of the Tang — the beginning of the seventh century in the West, and in Japan the time when era names were only just coming into being. There is said to have been an official named Lü Qiuyin. Though there are those who say that no such person existed. The reason is that although he is handed down as having been recorder at Taizhou, there is no biography of him in either of the Tang histories. A recorder is an office equivalent to a cishi or a taishou. All China was divided into circuits, the circuits into prefectures or commanderies, these into districts, and beneath the districts were townships and villages. In a prefecture the officer is called cishi, in a commandery taishou. Mr. Yoshida Tōgo, for one, objects that it is improper for us in Japan to give the name of commandery to something smaller than a prefecture. If Lü was in fact recorder at Taizhou he would have been an official of about the rank of a governor of one of our prefectures — and in that case, they say, he ought to appear in the biographies of the Tang histories. But since without Lü there is no story, we shall assume at any rate that he existed.

The ending (original text)

The two looked at Lü at the same moment. Then they looked at each other, and, with a laugh that seemed to come welling up from the bottom of their bellies, they both stood and bolted out of the kitchen. As they went, Hanshan was heard to say, "So Fenggan has been talking." Around Lü, who stood staring after them in astonishment, the monks who had been serving out the rice and the vegetables and the soup came crowding one after another. Daoqiao stood rooted to the spot with a face gone white.

Famous passages

As they fled, Hanshan could be heard to say, “Fenggan has been talking.”

The final lines, in which Hanshan and Shide laugh and run away from Lü Qiuyin, who has come to worship them on hearing that they are incarnations of Mañjuśrī and Samantabhadra. The story closes by lightly brushing aside a reverence that leans on authority and hearsay.
