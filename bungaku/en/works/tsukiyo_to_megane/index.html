About this work

A short children's story Ogawa Mimei published in the children's magazine Akai Tori in 1922. On a mild evening with a fine moon, an old woman at her sewing is visited by an old man selling spectacles and by an unknown girl who has hurt her finger. A lyrical little piece by the father of Japanese children's literature, letting a faint fantasy and a tenderness toward every living thing show through the quiet scene of a night wrapped in pale moonlight.

Summary

It is a mild, fine moonlit evening in early summer, the town wrapped in green leaves. An old woman living alone at the edge of the quiet town sits beneath her window doing needlework. Her eyes have grown too dim to thread her needle, and as she struggles with it, an old peddler selling eyeglasses comes to call. At his urging she buys a pair, and finds, sure enough, that she can see much better through them. Before long, a strange girl with an injured finger appears at her door, silent as a butterfly. The old woman tends to her kindly, dressing her wound. But when she turns to see the girl off, she finds that the girl has vanished without a sound, she knows not when. "Everyone's gone to sleep — well then, I shall go to sleep too," the old woman murmurs, and goes inside. It really was a fine moonlit night.

Characters

  • The Old Woman　An elderly woman living alone at the edge of town. She sets aside her needlework to welcome each visitor kindly
  • The Old Eyeglass Peddler　Calls on the old woman on a moonlit night and sells her a pair of eyeglasses
  • The Girl　A strange girl who appears with an injured finger, is tended to, and then vanishes without a sound

The opening (original text)

It was the season when the town, and the fields too, everywhere, lay wrapped in green leaves. It was a mild evening, with a fine moon out. At the edge of a quiet town lived an old woman, and she sat alone beneath her window, doing needlework. The lamplight shone peacefully all around. The old woman was already well along in years, and her eyes had grown dim, so that she could not easily thread her needle; again and again she held it up to the lamplight to peer at it, and twisted the fine thread between her wrinkled fingertips.

Tsukiyo to megane by Ogawa Mimei (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Where has the girl gone?" the old woman said, stopping short all at once and turning around. The girl, who had been following behind her, had vanished somewhere without a sound of footsteps, and was nowhere to be seen. "Everyone's gone to sleep — well then, I shall go to sleep too," the old woman said, and went inside her house. It really was a fine moonlit night.
