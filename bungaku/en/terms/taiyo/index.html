What is Taiyō?

Category
Society / Group

Japan's first true general-interest magazine, founded by Hakubunkan in January 1895. Covering every field from politics and economics to literature, it wielded a national influence as the largest magazine of the Meiji era. The critic Takayama Chogyū wrote vigorously as its chief writer, and his essay "Biteki Seikatsu o Ronzu" ("On the Aesthetic Life," 1901), preaching the satisfaction of instinct under the influence of Nietzsche, was published in this magazine and stirred great controversy. Its literary pages carried Higuchi Ichiyō's "Yuku Kumo" ("Passing Clouds") and Kawakami Bizan's "Shokikan" ("The Clerk"), a representative work of the ideas novel, and in the Taishō era Minakata Kumagusu serialized "Jūnishikō" ("Studies on the Twelve Zodiac Animals"), drawing broadly on legends from around the world, over more than ten years. It faded under pressure from the new general-interest magazines of the Taishō Democracy era, and ceased publication in 1928.
