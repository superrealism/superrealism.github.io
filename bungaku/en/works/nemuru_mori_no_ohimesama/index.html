About this work

Kusuyama Masao's version of the tale by Charles Perrault known in Japan as "Nemureru Mori no Bijo." Cursed by an old fairy who was not invited, the princess pricks her hand on a spindle and falls, with the whole castle, into a sleep of a hundred years. Unlike the Grimm version, the story goes on after she wakes, and its second half is given to the struggle with the prince's mother, who has ogre blood in her — so that it conveys the original well. Here one can read the first shape of a story spread over the world by animated film and by ballet.

Summary

To a king and queen who have had no children, a princess is at last born. To the christening are invited seven fairies of the kingdom as godmothers, and each gives her a gift — beauty, singing, dancing. But an old fairy who had shut herself in a tower for fifty years and had been forgotten appears and lays a curse: the princess will prick her hand on a spindle and die. A young fairy who has not yet given her gift steps forward and alters it: she shall not die, but sleep a hundred years, and a prince shall wake her. The king forbids spindles throughout the kingdom; but at fifteen or sixteen the princess comes upon an old woman spinning at the top of a tower in the castle, takes the spindle up in play, pricks her hand and falls. So that the princess may not be left alone when she wakes, the young fairy puts everyone in the castle into the same sleep and makes a forest of briars grow up around it. A hundred years later a prince who has come hunting makes his way to the castle deep in the wood and finds the princess asleep behind curtains embroidered in gold. The term of the sleep was exactly run out. The two are secretly married and have two children, named Dawn and Day; but when the prince comes to the throne, the queen mother, who has ogre blood in her, begins to lie in wait for her grandchildren and her daughter-in-law.

Characters

  • The princess　She pricks her hand on a spindle and, with the whole castle, sleeps a hundred years
  • The old fairy　Not invited to the christening, she lays the curse on the princess
  • The young fairy　She alters the curse into a sleep of a hundred years
  • The prince　A hundred years later he crosses the briar wood and enters the castle
  • The prince's mother　She has ogre blood in her, and lies in wait for her daughter-in-law and her grandchildren

The opening (original text)

1 Once upon a time there were a king and a queen who were so grieved at having no children — so grieved that it cannot be told. They went to all the waters in the world; vows, pilgrimages, small devotions — everything was tried, and nothing came of it. At last, however, their devotion was answered, and the queen was brought to bed of a daughter. A fine christening was arranged at once, and for godmothers to the little princess they called every fairy that could be found in the kingdom. There were seven of them in all.

The ending (original text)

The reason was that the little brother was far finer and more beautiful than his sister. Two years after that the king died, and the prince came to the throne. Then for the first time he declared his marriage to the princess openly to the whole kingdom, and, with a splendid ceremony, went to fetch her from the sleeping wood. The princess entered the king's castle in a fine procession of carriages, with her two children one on either side of her. It is a hard thing to wait for a partner who is beautiful and fine and of a good heart. But by waiting, happiness is only increased, and never lessened.
