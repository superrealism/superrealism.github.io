What is Rakugo?

Category
Form / Genre

A traditional Japanese art of storytelling, established in the Edo period, in which a single performer, using gesture and voice to distinguish characters, tells comic tales, human-interest tales, and ghost stories. Also called "otoshibanashi," it is distinguished by closing the story with a punch line ("ochi," or "sage"). In literary history the figure of San'yūtei Enchō, active from the closing years of the shogunate into the Meiji era, looms large. Enchō composed and performed on stage his own ghost stories, such as "Kaidan Botan Dōrō" ("The Peony Lantern") and "Shinkei Kasanegafuchi" ("The Ghost of Kasane Swamp"), and human-interest tales of duty and feeling such as "Bunshichi Mottoi" and "Shiobara Tasuke Ichidaiki" ("The Life of Shiobara Tasuke"), raising rakugo to artistic heights. The 1884 shorthand transcript of "Kaidan Botan Dōrō" in particular is celebrated for how its narrative voice, faithfully transcribing the spoken word onto the page, offered a major model for the genbun'itchi writers, including Futabatei Shimei, in shaping their prose style. Tales such as Enchō's "Meijin Chōji" also make deeply satisfying reading in their own right, and this stands as a valuable achievement linking the oral storytelling art to modern literature.
