About this work

An early sketch by Dazai. Taking as a heading the archaic phrase for wretched things, in the manner of The Pillow Book, he lists the weakness and wretchedness of human life — a man who swears off drink and breaks his word, and the rest. A short piece full of self-mockery, turning the pen at the end on the wretchedness of the man writing it.

Summary

"Wretched Things" — Dazai, borrowing an archaic phrase in the manner of The Pillow Book, lists off in small anecdotes the weakness and wretchedness of human life. A man who swears off drink for the sake of an adorable girl at a tobacco shop, only to be found drunk and standing at her shop-front again two days later. And — a man charged with marriage fraud, who finds the faint sneer of a prosecutor with a face like a Noh mask more wretched to bear than five years' imprisonment. Dazai sets down, coldly, these weak and wretched figures of the human world. But at the end he turns his pen on himself. "Now then, what of this fellow himself?" Writing such little sketches out of a craving for three cups of cheap sake, he is himself, he says, nothing but a "shameless hack scribbling for the marketplace," and that in itself is "wretched." "Alone I fancy myself something of a master, but sadly, no one else takes me for one. A laugh." — A short piece full of self-mockery, thoroughly Dazai.

The opening (original text)

At an archery wager, the bow trembles, and trembles on, so long that the loosed arrow flies wide and goes off in quite another direction. I heard a story like this. There was a girl at a tobacco shop, small and adorable. A man resolved, for this girl's sake, to give up drinking. Hearing of his resolve, the girl murmured, "I'm glad," and lowered her eyes.

Asamashiki mono by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

I have coldly listed three weak and wretched figures of the human world; but now, what of this fellow himself? (...) Wretched, shameful indeed — alone I fancy myself something of a master, but sadly, no one else takes me for one. A laugh.

Asamashiki mono by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)
