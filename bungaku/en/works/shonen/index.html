About this work

An early short story Tanizaki published in the magazine Subaru in 1911. The narrator, brought up at Kakigarachō in Nihonbashi, recalls the secret games played twenty years before, in his boyhood, at the mansion of his classmate, the young gentleman Shin'ichi. The positions of bully and bullied are reversed, and in time Shin'ichi's elder sister Mitsuko reigns as a queen with three boys at her command — drawn together with the feeling of the downtown Tokyo of Meiji. A central work of Tanizaki's diabolism at his debut, and, with "The Tattooer," the piece warmly praised by Nagai Kafū.

Summary

Eitarō, ten years old and a pupil at a school in Nihonbashi, is invited one spring day by his classmate Hanawa Shin'ichi to come play at his grand mansion in Kayaba-chō. At school, Shin'ichi is notorious as a coward who never comes without a maid in attendance, but within his own house he becomes an entirely different creature — a petty tyrant who does exactly as he pleases with Eitarō and the errand boy Sankichi, casting them in games as wolves or condemned criminals. In the mansion's dim storehouse and its deserted Western-style wing, the games grow steadily more cruel, and Eitarō finds himself discovering a strange, sweet pleasure in being tormented. Before long Shin'ichi's elder sister Mitsuko joins in their play, and the balance of power turns once again. Beautiful as a doll, Mitsuko brings all three boys — her own brother included — to heel like servants, making them stand candles on their foreheads and cut her toenails fresh from the bath, ruling them through one fierce little cruelty after another, and "for a long time remained the queen of this little country."

Characters

  • I (Hagiwara Eitarō)　The narrator, a ten-year-old boy drawn into the games at the Hanawa mansion
  • Hanawa Shin'ichi　A well-off classmate. A coward at school, he becomes a petty tyrant within his own house
  • Mitsuko　Shin'ichi's elder sister. Beautiful as a doll, she comes in time to reign as the boys' "queen"
  • Sankichi　An errand boy in the Hanawa household. Made the victim in their games

The opening (original text)

It must be nearly twenty years ago now. I was about ten at the time, walking each day from our house in Kayaba-chō 2-chōme to the Arima School behind the Suitengū shrine — the sky over Ningyōchō Street hazy, the sun falling warm and hazy on the dark blue noren hung before shop after shop, a cheerful season in which even my aimless, dreamlike child's heart could somehow feel that spring had come. On one bright, cloudless day, when the drowsy afternoon lessons had at last let out and I was making for the school gate, my ink-stained hands cradling my abacus, someone came running up behind me, calling out my name with a great clatter of footsteps — "Hagiwara! Ei-chan!"

Shōnen by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

From the next day on, both Sankichi and I turned meek as cats before Mitsuko, dropping to our knees the moment she appeared; and whenever Shin'ichi tried, now and then, to defy his sister's word, she would seize and pin him down at once, binding or beating him without the least ceremony, until in time even the once-proud Shin'ichi became entirely his sister's vassal, turning, even at home, into the same utterly craven weakling he had always been at school. The three of us obeyed Mitsuko's every command with delight, as though we had discovered some new and wonderful game — the instant she said 'be my footstool,' we would drop at once to all fours and turn our backs to her; the instant she said 'be my spittoon,' we would bow low and open our mouths without a word. Little by little Mitsuko grew ever more overbearing, driving the three of us like slaves — making us clip her toenails fresh from the bath, ordering us to clean out her nostrils, making us drink her urine — keeping us always in attendance at her side, and for a long time remaining the queen of this little country. I never once went back to that Western-style wing after that. Even now, thinking it over, I still cannot say for certain whether that big green snake was real or merely a fake.

Shōnen by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)
