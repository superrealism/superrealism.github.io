Biography

Born in 1867 into a samurai family of the Matsuyama domain in Onsen District, Iyo Province (now Matsuyama, Ehime Prefecture); his real name was Tsunenori. He lost his father at five and grew up steeped in Chinese classical studies at his grandfather's private school. He went to Tokyo to study at the preparatory school for Tokyo Imperial University, where he formed a lifelong friendship with his classmate Natsume Sōseki. In 1893 he became a reporter for the newspaper "Nippon" and serialized "Dassai Shooku Haiwa," criticizing the conventional "tsukinami" style of old-school haiku and launching a movement to reform haiku on the principle of "shasei," or sketching from life. In tanka too, in "A Letter to Practitioners of Tanka" he rejected the Kokinshū in favor of the Man'yōshū, and leading the Negishi Tanka Society, he laid the foundations of modern tanka. In 1895, while serving at the front during the First Sino-Japanese War, he coughed blood and was diagnosed with tuberculosis, and in 1896 he developed spinal caries. Thereafter he lived almost entirely bedridden, yet continued to write prolifically, and, with the essay "Byōshō Rokushaku" ("A Six-Foot Sickbed") as his last work, died on September 19, 1902, at the age of 34.
