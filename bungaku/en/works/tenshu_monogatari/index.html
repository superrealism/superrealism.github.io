About this work

A play by Izumi Kyōka, published in Shinshōsetsu in September 1917. It draws the love, across the divide of the human world and the other world and across rank, between Tomihime, the demon princess who lives on the fifth storey of the keep of Himeji Castle, and Zushinosuke, a young falconer who strays in while hawking. With its mysterious, splendid language and its demand for bold stage design, it stands among the very best of his plays and is still performed again and again.

Summary

On the fifth story of the keep of the White Heron castle at Himeji in Banshū, where no human being sets foot, the Lady of the Keep, Princess Tomi, lives with her waiting women. On a stormy night Princess Kame of Inawashiro comes to visit; and because the lord of the domain has taken as hostage a hawk he dotes on, Princess Tomi lets the hawk go into the sky by way of revenge on the lord of the castle. Himekawa Zushonosuke, a young falconer who has strayed into the keep in pursuit of the hawk, is prepared to die for the fault; but at his dignified bearing Princess Tomi's heart goes out to him, and the two fall at once in love. Pursuers press up into the keep to take Zushonosuke, and driven to the last extremity he is about to meet his end at Princess Tomi's hand. There appears the old craftsman Ōminojō Tōroku, who puts his chisel to the eyes of the lion-head mask the two are holding; light shines as though a soul had entered the mask, and Princess Tomi and Zushonosuke, joined together, fall into a quiet sleep with the wars of the world outside them.

Characters

  • Princess Tomi　The spirit princess who dwells on the fifth story of the keep of the White Heron castle, and whose meeting with Zushonosuke wakes love for the human world in her
  • Himekawa Zushonosuke　A young falconer who strays into the keep after the loosed hawk and is joined with Princess Tomi
  • Princess Kame　A spirit princess of Inawashiro, a younger sister to Princess Tomi, who visits her on the stormy night
  • Ōminojō Tōroku　The old craftsman who carved the lion-head mask, and who appears at the crisis in the keep to save the two

The opening (original text)

Time: Unknown. But the feudal age — late autumn. From before sunset until deep in the night. Place: Himeji in Banshū. The fifth story of the keep of the White Heron castle. Three young girls — in chorus — Whose narrow path is this, this narrow path, it is the narrow path of the god of Tenjin, this narrow path. — singing, the curtain rises —

Tenshu monogatari by Izumi Kyōka (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

TŌROKU: Mm — they can see, they look bashful about it, they look put out about it, but they look glad all the same, ha, ha, ha, ha. How fond of each other, the young things. (Strikes a light on the flint, sets it to the tinder, and puffs at his pipe held sideways.) Rest after all your trouble; take a quiet nap. In the meantime I shall give you eyes a little clearer yet. He tries the chisel. Moonlight falls. There, the light comes in — let there be moonlight, eyeballs. (Tries the chisel, inclines his ear, and hears the voices below the keep shouting like a war cry.) Let the world be at war; the butterflies dance, the pinks and the bellflowers are in bloom. — Fools. (Laughs loudly.) Here is a lion. Take it for a festival and make your noise. (Setting his chisel to it.) Spears, swords, bows and arrows, muskets — you fellows of the castle. — Curtain —
