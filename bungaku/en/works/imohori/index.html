About this work

A short story following one day of the farmer Kenji and his wife Osuga digging sweet potatoes in a field on a hill in the mild weather of late autumn, set down carefully together with the landscape of a village on the Kinu river. Nothing showy happens; the lives of people working the soil, the shifting light of late autumn and the view of Mount Tsukuba are drawn with a fine sketching brush. A piece in which the flavour of Nagatsuka's sketch prose, copying nature and labour as they are, comes out well.

Summary

On a fine, mild day in late autumn, in the fields on a hill ringed by the Kinu river and a grove of sawtooth oaks, the farmers are hard at work digging sweet potatoes. Kenji breaks up the potato hills with his hoe; his wife Osuga cuts the stems, breaks apart the clods of dug-up earth, and packs the small potatoes into straw sacks. In the distance, the snow-capped mountains of Ryōmō and Mount Tsukuba lie hazy, and the glass windows of the observatory on Tsukuba catch the sun and glitter. Villagers and horses come and go across the fields, exchanging idle words — the scene of one day's labour, with no particular plot to it, builds up quietly amid the shifting of the season and the smell of the soil. In time the sun sinks low, and, together with the smoke of the evening meal rising from the bamboo groves of the village, the farmers cross the paddies and set off for home. A quiet piece of sketch prose, taking the very life of nameless farmers as its subject.

The opening (original text)

The mild sunlight of early winter pours down over the whole of the hillside field. The hill is ringed by paddy fields, a grove of sawtooth oaks, and the bank of the Kinu river, and slopes down on its remaining side to the highway running from village to village. The paddies stretch on, narrow, along the hill. Beyond the paddies, a village grove mixed with bamboo thickets runs along beside them. Between the bamboo, thatched houses come in and out of view here and there. Zelkova trees, their branches spread as though cut off partway like a broom of dried grass, stand tall here and there. Few sheaves of hanging rice remain in the paddies now, but the bamboo racks, the oda, on which the rice had hung, still stand, not yet taken down. Along the bank of the Kinu river the dwarf bamboo grows so thick that the water nearby is hidden behind it, out of sight. A white sail rising into view shows only half above the tips of the bamboo, and yet looks large all the same.

Imohori by Nagatsuka Takashi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Kenji, bending his waist into the shape of the character ku, pulls hard with his legs, hauling to the left. The blue smoke rising from the village's bamboo groves trails in several threads, as though come out to meet the farmers in the fields, reaching from the paddies toward the hill. Just then, through the dusk, the farmers come home one after another from the paddies. A snipe cried out sharply from somewhere and was gone. Pressing the farmers' retreating backs into the village, the hand of night before long covered the paddies, the fields, and the whole space between heaven and earth.

Imohori by Nagatsuka Takashi (Aozora Bunko)
