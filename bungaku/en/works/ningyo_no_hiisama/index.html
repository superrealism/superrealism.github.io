About this work

Kusuyama Masao's version of Andersen's best-known tale, called "Ningyo-hime" in Japan. The youngest princess, raised in a palace at the bottom of the sea, comes to the surface for the first time at fifteen and saves a prince from a wrecked ship. Giving up her voice to gain human legs and an immortal soul, and seeking love while she bears at every step a pain like treading on knives, she has been handed down as the figure of a devotion that is never repaid. The translation keeps the religious coloring of the original to the end, down to the throwing of her sisters' knife into the sea and her joining the daughters of the air as foam.

Summary

In a palace at the bottom of the sea lived the six daughters of the sea king. The youngest is the quietest of them; in her garden she sets a marble boy sunk from a wreck and plants a red willow to grow over him. By custom a mermaid is allowed to rise above the sea at fifteen, and the youngest princess longs for it as her sisters tell her in turn what the world above is like. On the night of her own turn she sees a birthday feast on a great ship, and on board a beautiful prince. When the storm breaks the ship she holds him up and carries him to the shore, and watches from behind a rock. The prince goes away believing that a girl from the temple saved him. Told by her grandmother that human beings live shorter lives than mermaids but have immortal souls, she goes to the sea witch and gets human legs in exchange for her beautiful voice — with the condition that every step will be like treading on knives, and that if the prince marries another she will become foam on the sea. Taken into the palace, she is petted; but the prince, certain that the princess of the neighboring kingdom is the one who saved his life, marries her. On the wedding night her sisters hold out the knife they have bought with their hair and tell her that if she stabs the prince's breast she will be saved; but she throws it into the sea and at sunrise turns to foam. And so she joins the company of the unseen daughters of the air.

Characters

  • The little mermaid　The youngest daughter of the sea king, who gains human legs in exchange for her voice
  • The prince　Saved by her from a wrecked ship, though he never knows it
  • The sea witch　She gives human legs and takes the voice in payment
  • Grandmother　The grandmother who tells the princess about the immortal souls of men
  • The five sisters　They give their hair to the witch for a knife and hold it out to their youngest sister

The opening (original text)

Far out at sea the water is as blue as the petals of the loveliest cornflower, and as clear as the purest glass; but it is very deep — deeper than any anchor cable reaches. Many church towers would have to be set one upon another to reach from the bottom up above the water. Down there live the sea people. Now you must not think that there is nothing down there but bare white sand.

The Little Mermaid by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Unseen we glide into the houses of men where there are children," one of the daughters of the air whispered to her; "and for every day on which we find a good child who gives his parents joy and deserves their love, our time of trial is shortened. The child never knows when we fly through the room; but when we smile for joy over a good child, a year is taken from the three hundred. And if we see a naughty or a wicked child, and must weep tears of sorrow, then every one of those tears adds a day to our time of trial."

The Little Mermaid by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)
