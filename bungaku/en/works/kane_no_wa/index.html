About this work

A very short children's story Ogawa Mimei published in the Yomiuri Shimbun in 1919. Tarō, a boy able to get up at last after a long illness, sees on two successive days a beautiful boy he does not know running past bowling a golden hoop. In a dream the two run on and on and vanish into a red sunset sky — and then Tarō's fever returns and he dies without warning. The frailty of a young life and a fantastical beauty that reaches beyond death, compressed by Mimei into a very short piece.

Summary

Tarō, who had lain ill in bed for a long time, is at last well enough to leave it and go outdoors. It is still the end of March, and since the mornings and evenings are cold, his mother has told him to go out only while the sun is shining. One evening, Tarō catches sight of a strange, beautiful boy running along the road, spinning a pair of golden hoops. The two golden hoops glitter and shine in the sunlight. The boy looks at Tarō's face, smiling, and runs past him as though they were old friends. The next day, at the very same hour, the boy runs by again in just the same way, spinning his golden hoops. Tarō finds himself longing, more and more, to become that boy's friend — to be given one of the golden hoops, and to go running on and on together with him. That evening, Tarō tells his mother about the boy, but she does not believe him. Before long, Tarō dreams that he has become the boy's friend, that he has been given a golden hoop, and that the two of them go running down the road together, until, before he knows it, they enter into the red evening sky. And from the next day, Tarō runs a fever again, and on the second or third day, he dies, at the age of seven.

Characters

  • Tarō　A boy at last able to rise after a long illness. He longs for the beautiful boy who spins golden hoops, dreams of him, and then, falling ill with fever again, dies at the age of seven
  • The Boy with the Golden Hoops　A strange, beautiful boy whom Tarō sees running with golden hoops two days running. In Tarō's dream, the two of them vanish together into the evening sky

The opening (original text)

Tarō had lain ill in bed for a long time, but at last he was well enough to leave it and go outside. Still, it was only the end of March, and the mornings and evenings could turn cold. And so, while it was fine for him to go outdoors when the sun was shining, his mother had told him again and again that he must come home early once evening drew on.

Kin no wa by Ogawa Mimei (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

That evening, Tarō told his mother about the boy he had seen running with a golden hoop at the very same hour, two days running. His mother did not believe him. Tarō dreamed that he had become friends with the boy, that the boy had given him one of the golden hoops, and that the two of them went running on and on together down the road. And then, before he knew it, he dreamed that the two of them had entered into the red evening sky. From the next day, Tarō ran a fever again. And on the second or third day after that, he died, at the age of seven.
