About this work

A young woman who admired a novelist and wrote him a fan letter goes to meet him and is put to a thorough humiliation — a novel by Dazai written in a woman's first person, in the form of a letter to a friend. The presumption of a reader in love, and how it is betrayed, are painfully funny, and at the end the true nature of the writer's own presumption becomes visible to the reader.

Summary

"Kikuko dear. I have made a fool of myself. A dreadful fool of myself." So begins this confession, written in the form of a letter to a friend. To say her face was on fire would be far too mild; she wants to roll about in a field and scream. The writer, Wako, confesses that she had been secretly writing letters to the novelist Mr. Toda. At the start of September she had sent him a very affected letter reading, "I imagine that not a single woman has ever read your novels." Before long a reply came, and Wako's spirits soared. Picturing the poor, unwashed figure of the "I" depicted in Mr. Toda's novels, Wako decided it would only be proper to call on him looking equally shabby, and, with the resolve to go so far as to chip her own front tooth, deliberately dressed herself in filthy clothes and visited the Toda household. But the Mr. Toda who appeared bore not the slightest resemblance to the character in his novels — he was a neat, tidy head of an ordinary household. The conversation never once found its footing, and Wako, treated coldly, was sent away. Some days later, a small parcel arrives from Mr. Toda. Opening it with trembling hands, Wako finds it empty — only her own two letters sent back to her, with not a single word added. She examines even the backs of the letter paper with great care, but they remain blank. "Can you imagine such shame? I want to pour ashes over my own head." Novelists, she curses, are worthless — human rubbish — and behind Wako's bitter words lies a device that lets the reader glimpse, quite clearly, the very comedy of her own self-delusion.

Characters

  • Wako　A young woman who wrote letters to a novelist
  • Kikuko　The friend to whom Wako addresses her letter
  • Mr. Toda　The novelist Wako admired

The opening (original text)

Kikuko dear. I have made a fool of myself. A dreadful fool of myself. To say my face was on fire would be far too mild a way of putting it. Even to say I want to roll about in a field and scream still falls short. It is written in the Second Book of Samuel: "And Tamar put ashes on her head, and rent her garment of divers colours that was on her, and laid her hand on her head, and went on crying." Poor sister Tamar.

Haji by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Can you imagine such shame? I want to pour ashes over my own head. I feel as though I have aged ten years. Novelists are worthless. Human rubbish. Nothing but liars, every one of them. Not the least bit romantic. Settled cozily into an ordinary household, looking with cold contempt on a girl in filthy clothes with a chipped front tooth, never even seeing her off, content to go on wearing a stranger's face forever — it's appalling, that's what it is.

Haji by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)
