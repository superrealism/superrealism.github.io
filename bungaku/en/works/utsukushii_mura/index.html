About this work

A linked novella Hori Tatsuo set at Karuizawa. The narrator, staying alone in an upland village in early summer before the holiday-makers arrive, watches the turning of the season and the nature of the village closely, works out the plan of a novel, and comes in time to a meeting with a girl. In four parts — "Prelude," "The Beautiful Village," "Summer" and "The Dark Road" — it sets down the landscape of a mind in a transparent, musical style. A central work of Hori's lyricism, leading on to The Wind Has Risen.

Summary

In early June, "I" arrive alone in a highland village not yet visited by summer holidaymakers. Quite unlike the forlorn autumn I once spent here after an illness three years before, the vivid early-summer nature, waiting eagerly for summer to come, soothes my heart, and as I walk about the village I quietly warm to the idea for a novel I had been struggling to write. Church, villas, a fir grove, the mill road — as I look carefully over the sights of the village and the faces of the people I pass there, I find memories reviving of girls I once met on this village's slope. Before long summer deepens and the village regains its bustle, and I grow close to a young woman, and the two of us come to walk the village together at dusk. One evening, walking with her along a path through the woods, I unexpectedly run into a group of old female friends and companions from my student days. As I go down the dark slope alone ahead of the others, the voices of those descending behind me reach my ears, and the story closes as past encounters and the present faintly overlap.

Characters

  • I　A novelist staying in a highland village. Watches the seasons and the village's nature while working out the idea for a novel, moving toward an encounter with a girl
  • The girl　A young woman who grows close to "me" in the village that summer. Walks with me at dusk
  • Old female friends and companions　A group of acquaintances from my student days whom "I" unexpectedly run into in the village

The opening (original text)

June 10th — At Village K... It has been a long time since I wrote. Since the beginning of this month I have been staying here. I had often said before that I wanted, just once, to come to this highland village in early summer like this, and now at last that long-held wish has been granted. No one else has come yet, so it is lonely, lonely enough — and yet I spend each day in pleasant mornings and evenings. And yet, lonely as it is, it seems altogether different from that loneliness of early autumn in the mountains three years ago, when I fell ill and stayed on here alone, if I remember, until around October.

The ending (original text)

Looking up, I saw that whatever seemed still to be rolling about halfway down the slope looked, from where I stood, exactly like a shrub in full bloom. And I stood there, gaping, not moving a single step from where I stood, watching as all the others stopped and turned to look back at the girl who had come down last of all. And then, quite suddenly, I found myself thinking of that lame flower-seller who climbs up and down this slope every morning, and wondering, of all things, why on earth she should choose, of all the roads there were, to go by way of so treacherous a slope as this — thinking of something that had, in truth, nothing whatever to do with the matter at hand. ……
