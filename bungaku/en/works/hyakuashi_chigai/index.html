About this work

Akinari Matashirō, newly posted to the domain's upper residence in Edo, finds himself whispered about wherever he goes: that's him, the wrong centipede. Tracing the origin of a nickname he cannot account for, he arrives at an incident five years earlier — a humorous piece about rumour and what people take for granted. It tells how Matashirō comes to understand, in his own person, the priest's remark that people in this world are altogether too much in a hurry.

Summary

Akinari Matashirō, newly arrived at the upper mansion in Edo, was for a while put to a good deal of trouble. All sorts of people spoke to him with no business to speak of, and when he was in the office room someone would look in. Going to the mansion and coming away, as he walked, those who passed would whisper to their companions, "That is he, that is he — the centipede mistake" — and the story begins from this discomfort. ... In time the attention goes off like an ebbing tide. "Perhaps they have forgotten me," thinks Matashirō, and following his mother's advice he takes pains to show his face among his relations and those in authority; but all he has to show for it is having come to know Miss O-Kane of the Matsuya. The forwardness with which she calls after him — "Wait, wait a moment!" — only bewilders him. To find out the origin of the nickname, Matashirō calls on Kariga Yuheiji, who was concerned in the affair of five years ago. His is an imposing establishment with stables and tenements and three storehouses inside the barracks; and Yuheiji, leaning back like some landlord, repeats, "Being much occupied, I will ask you to leave out the useless greetings and state your business briefly." "My business is brief; I only want you to remember something of five years ago" — and from there the truth of the matter comes undone. At the close, Matashirō says to the abbot that the people of the world are too much in a hurry, and that because they fret over what is before their eyes they miss the essential thing. And calling up in his mind the noisy scene in the drawing room of the Matsuya house, he shudders and waves one hand.

Characters

  • Akinari Matashirō　The young samurai whom people call "the centipede mistake"
  • Kariga Yuheiji　The superior concerned in the affair of five years ago
  • O-Kane of the Matsuya　A forward young lady
  • The abbot　Who preaches to Matashirō the virtue of waiting

The ending (original text)

Into Matashirō's eyes there came suddenly that noisy scene in the drawing room of the Matsuya house. He shuddered, and then waved one hand and said, "No indeed, no indeed; I still think that in this too I did well to wait through three cups — er — that I did a good thing."
