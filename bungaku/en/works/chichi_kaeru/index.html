About this work

A one-act play published in the coterie magazine Shinshichō in 1917. Through the events of a single night, in which a father who abandoned his wife and children returns after twenty years, broken, it sets the eldest son who cannot forgive him against the younger brother and sister moved by family feeling. Staged by Osanai Kaoru's Free Theatre as a forerunner of modern colloquial drama, it remains Kikuchi Kan's best-known work and a classic of modern Japanese theatre, still performed.

Summary

A coastal town on the Nankaidō. Ken'ichirō, the eldest son, who has worked without a break since his days as an office boy at the prefectural office and has carried the family on his back, lives modestly with his mother O-Taka, his younger brother Shinjirō and his sister O-Tane. On the very night when word goes round that someone has seen an old man who looks like Sōtarō — the father who ran off twenty years ago, leaving debts and a woman behind him — Sōtarō himself comes home, utterly down in the world. His mother receives him in tears and the younger children look at his face with longing; only Ken'ichirō refuses him stubbornly, declaring that the man who put them through all this has no right to call himself their father. With nowhere left to go, Sōtarō stumbles out of the house; and when Ken'ichirō will not go after him, his mother and sister cannot bear it and plead with him — "Ken'ichirō!" "Brother!" Moved at last, Ken'ichirō orders Shinjirō to bring their father back; but Shinjirō, having gone to look, returns to say only, "I can't find him." Beside himself, Ken'ichirō rushes out with his brother into the darkness to search for their father.

Characters

  • Kuroda Ken'ichirō　The eldest son, twenty-eight. He has carried the family since his days as an office boy at the prefectural office, and stubbornly refuses the father who abandoned them
  • Shinjirō　The second son, twenty-three. A primary school teacher, who cannot quite put away his feeling for his father
  • O-Tane　The eldest daughter, twenty, who meets her father's return with mixed feelings
  • O-Taka　The mother, fifty-one, who raised three children after her husband left her
  • Sōtarō　The father, who ran off twenty years ago and comes home a broken man

The ending (original text)

SHINJIRŌ: I looked along the road to the south and I can't find him. I'm going to look to the north — brother, come with me. KEN'ICHIRŌ (aghast): What, you can't find him! How can he not be found? (The two brothers rush out like madmen.) — Curtain —

Famous passages

Shin! Go and call Father back!

The words Ken'ichirō, the eldest son, cries to his younger brother at the end, having once driven away the father who abandoned the family. The dramatic ending of this one-act play, in which family feeling overflows beyond hatred.
