Biography

Born in Niigata City in 1906; his real name was Heigo. He studied Indian philosophy and ethics at Toyo University. He had published fiction before the war, but the essay "Darakuron" ("On Decadence") and the story "Hakuchi" ("The Idiot"), published just after the defeat, brought him into the spotlight overnight alongside Dazai Osamu, Oda Sakunosuke, and Ishikawa Jun as a standard-bearer of the Buraiha ("decadent" school), which set out to demolish the moral conventions of the old order. While he showed an original vision in fantastical short stories such as "Sakura no Mori no Mankai no Shita" ("Beneath the Full Cherry Blossoms"), he was also known for the detective novel "Furenzoku Satsujin Jiken" ("The Discontinuous Murder Case"), and his freewheeling, unruly way of life came, together with his work, to symbolize postwar Japan. He died suddenly of a cerebral hemorrhage in 1955 at the age of 48.
