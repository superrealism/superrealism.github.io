About this work

The finest daughter must have the finest husband in Japan — so a mouse father, having set his heart on it, goes asking after the greatest thing in the world: the sun, the cloud, the wind, the wall. Known for the neatness of a structure in which the answer comes round at last to the mice themselves; stories of the same type are found all over the world. A retelling by Kusuyama Masao, pleasant in its repetitions.

Summary

To a rich pair of mice living in plenty in a storehouse, with rice and wheat and millet and beans of their own, a daughter was born at last, long awaited. She grew up dazzlingly beautiful, the finest daughter in Japan, with no one in all the mouse country to compare with her. "Since our daughter is the finest in Japan, we must have the finest son-in-law in Japan" — so thinking, the father mouse first went up to heaven to the Sun, who shines upon all the world, and asked him to take his daughter. But the Sun said, "There is someone greater than I." For when the clouds come out he is hidden. The father mouse called on the Cloud. The Cloud said he was no match for the Wind. He called on the Wind, and the Wind said that the Wall alone he could not blow away. So he called on the Wall, and the Wall answered: "It is no one else — it is you, Mr. Mouse. However hard I make myself and hold out, you mice go calmly on eating through my body and passing right through." The father mouse clapped his hands and was heartily impressed. Then we are the greatest in the world — how splendid, how splendid! Going home beaming, he made Chūsuke, the mouse next door, his daughter's husband at once. The young couple lived happily together and had a great many children, and the family of mice in the storehouse flourished more than ever.

Characters

  • The father mouse　The father who goes about looking for the finest son-in-law in Japan
  • The daughter　The most beautiful daughter in all the mouse country
  • The Sun, the Cloud, the Wind and the Wall　The great ones called upon in turn
  • Chūsuke the mouse　The mouse next door, who becomes the daughter's husband

The opening (original text)

Long, long ago, in the storehouse of a certain house, there lived a rich mouse who had rice and wheat and millet and beans, and lived in great plenty. Having no children, they prayed to the gods, and at last a daughter was born. The child grew and grew and became dazzlingly beautiful, the finest daughter in Japan, with not one in all the mouse country to compare with her.

Nezumi no yomeiri by Kusuyama Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"I had never noticed that until now. Then we are the greatest in the world. How splendid. How splendid." And beaming and swaggering, he went home. And as soon as he got home he made Chūsuke, the mouse next door, his daughter's husband. The young husband and wife lived happily together and took good care of the father and the mother. And they had a great many children, and the family of mice in the storehouse flourished more than ever.
