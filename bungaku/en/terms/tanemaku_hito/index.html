What is Tanemaku Hito (The Sower)?

Category
Society / Group

A literary and intellectual magazine founded in February 1921 in Tsuchizaki, Akita Prefecture, by Komaki Ōmi, Kaneko Yōbun, and Konno Kenzō, among others — regarded as the starting point of the proletarian literature movement. Komaki, who had studied in France, was inspired to found it by his encounter, on his return to Japan, with Henri Barbusse's antiwar movement Clarté. The Tsuchizaki edition ran to only three issues, but publication resumed in Tokyo that October, becoming a united-front magazine championing anti-militarism and the defense of the Russian Revolution. Yanase Masamu and Sasaki Takamaru joined as well, and once Hirabayashi Hatsunosuke and Aono Suekichi later took up its polemics, it began to argue clearly for literature as one wing of the class struggle. It ran to twenty-one issues despite repeated government bans on sale, but ceased publication amid the disruption of the Great Kantō Earthquake in 1923. It was this small magazine that opened the stage on which Hayama Yoshiki and Kobayashi Takiji would later be active. The following year, 1924, its coterie members founded "Bungei Sensen" to carry on its aims, and this in time developed into the two great currents of proletarian literature, standing alongside "Senki" ("The Battle Flag").
