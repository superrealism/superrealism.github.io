About this work

An essay putting his own laziness on the block, published in the magazine Bungei in April 1939. It opens by saying that among his many vices the most conspicuous is idleness, sets out the excuses of the lazy, and then strings together fragments in the form of the old alphabet card game. A characteristically free piece, weaving in a fable of Venus, the origin of Shinobazu pond, and a memory of fox-fire over a marsh.

Summary

Few vices allow for as many excuses as idleness — the sleeping dragon, nine years facing a wall, careful deliberation, great talents ripen late. Mocking myself for lining up nothing but the excuses of a lazybones, I sit down at my desk. But I do nothing. I prop my chin in my hand, get up to use the toilet, heat an iron when a member of the household asks me to run an errand, and smoke a cigarette with the look of a man who has just finished some great task. Eventually I begin writing fragments in iroha order, under the title "A Card Game for the Idle." A tale of Venus, born of the sea foam, standing at a crossroads to divine her lover; the origin of Shinobazu Pond, where two samurai on their way to a duel once walked beneath a single umbrella; a marsh's fox-fire I once went to see by bicycle in my higher-school days — while I go on writing, the deadline presses close, and the manuscript must be delivered to the printer.

Characters

  • I　A writer who confesses his own idleness and strings together fragments in the form of an iroha card game

The opening (original text)

Of the many vices I possess, the most conspicuous is idleness. This is beyond any doubt. It is quite something. On the subject of idleness alone, I am the genuine article. I am not, of course, boasting of it. I am thoroughly appalled at myself. This is, for me, the greatest of flaws. A flaw, certainly, to be ashamed of.

Randa no karuta by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

WO — the wind in the pines on the peak of Obasute mountain. Let this be a lesson to myself. If I repeat such an ugly performance one more time, it really will be Obasute mountain for me. A card game for the idle. Word for word, this has truly turned into a card game for the idle. Wasn't that my intention from the very start? No — I won't tell that lie any longer. WA — I lift up my eyes unto the mountain. KA — the lowly people are easy to oppress; high heaven is hard to deceive. YO — after the night, morning comes.

Randa no karuta by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)
