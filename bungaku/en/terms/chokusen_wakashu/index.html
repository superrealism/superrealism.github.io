What is Chokusen Wakashū (Imperial Poetry Anthologies)?

Category
Form / Genre

Waka anthologies compiled by imperial or retired-emperor command. The first was the "Kokin Wakashū," presented in 905 by order of Emperor Daigo, and over roughly the next 530 years twenty-one such anthologies were compiled, together called the Twenty-One Collections. The "Kokin Wakashū" was compiled by four editors — Ki no Tsurayuki, Ki no Tomonori, Ōshikōchi no Mitsune, and Mibu no Tadamine — who arranged some 1,100-odd poems across twenty scrolls under categories such as the four seasons, celebration, parting, love, grief, and miscellaneous. This organization of poems around the seasons and love became the norm for all that followed. The first eight anthologies, from the "Kokin Wakashū" to the eighth, the "Shin Kokin Wakashū" (1205), are together called the Eight Collections, regarded as the finest flower of court poetry. They are distinguished, in this respect, from privately compiled anthologies and family collections put together by individuals or clans without imperial command. To have one's poem included in an imperial anthology was the highest honor a poet could receive, and the families entrusted with compiling them fiercely contended for that standing. No imperial anthology has been compiled since the "Shin Shoku Kokin Wakashū" of 1439. When Masaoka Shiki, in the modern era, rejected the "Kokin Wakashū" in favor of the "Man'yōshū," that too was a rebellion against this long-standing authority.
