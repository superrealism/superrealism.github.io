About this work

An autobiographical essay Sakaguchi Ango published in Bungei in 1947, recalling the year around the age of twenty when he served as a substitute teacher at an elementary school in Tokyo. After a wild boyhood of expulsions and failed years, the young Ango stood at a teacher's desk. He sets down frankly and vividly his deep affection for the children, days as satisfying as a hymn to the sun out in the open, and the unease and the search for a way that lay under that contentment. A fine essay.

Summary

Sakaguchi Ango, expelled and held back time and again, finally graduated from middle school at twenty. His father had died when he was eighteen, leaving nothing behind but debts. Ango, who hated schoolwork, did not go on to university, but became a substitute teacher at a Tokyo elementary school. Willful by nature and unable to bear taking orders from anyone, he had, since boyhood, been the kind of child who skipped school to lie in the grass of a graveyard, gazing up at the sky. And yet the year this same Ango spent standing at the front of a classroom, living alongside children, was, in the whole course of his life, a strangely "fulfilled" time, as though he had become an entirely different person. He loved the children deeply, lost himself in the feel of the natural world, and something like a hymn to the sun seemed to flow constantly from his very soul. But beneath that fulfillment lay hidden, too, the anxiety and emptiness peculiar to youth. In time, drawn by a vague longing for "enlightenment," Ango resolves to cast everything aside and become a monk, and after much wavering, quits his post as teacher. If I throw everything away, surely something will come of it — he dimly senses that even this desperate impulse to "cast aside" was itself only one more footstep of his own youth passing by. A fine essay, fresh and yet tinged with a bittersweet ache, recalling the year of wind and light that filled Ango's twentieth year.

Characters

  • I (Sakaguchi Ango)　After a willful, unruly boyhood, becomes a substitute elementary-school teacher at twenty. Finds fulfillment in the year spent with the children, while carrying within him the anxiety and spiritual searching of youth
  • The children　The elementary-school pupils in Ango's charge. The ones on whom he lavishes his deep affection

The opening (original text)

I was expelled, and held back, and did not graduate from middle school until I was twenty. When I was eighteen my father died, and once it became clear that nothing was left behind but debts, my family came to live in a row house. Everyone around me said there would be little point in someone like me, who hated schoolwork so much, going on to university — not that anyone had actually ordered me not to go, but the reasoning was sound enough, so I decided to go to work instead. I became a substitute teacher at an elementary school.

Kaze to hikari to hatachi no watashi to by Sakaguchi Ango (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

I had wanted to become a novelist since I was a boy. But I had convinced myself I had no talent for it, and no doubt, underneath everything, there was at work in me a wholesale resignation from that one legitimate hope of mine. That strangely fulfilled year of my teaching days feels, in the history of my life, like something that did not truly belong to me — and whenever I call it to mind, I am left with a strange, hollow feeling, as though it were somehow untrue.
