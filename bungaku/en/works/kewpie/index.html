About this work

The American-born Kewpie has vanished, and the toy box is in an uproar. The daruma, the German tin soldier and the French clown all leap out to search — a very short children's story Yumeno Kyūsaku wrote for the Kyūshū Nippō. Within its small plot, in which a rat's telling-tales throws suspicion on the tortoiseshell cat, the last line places a chill that shows the seed of his later manner.

Summary

Since the Kewpie doll born in America had disappeared, a great commotion began inside the toy box. The Japanese Daruma, with a headband tied round him and riding a bear made of scouring cloth, dashes out first of all; after him the tin soldier born in Germany drives off in a wooden motorcar. The harlequin doll born in France runs out with the sleeping doll born in England, and everyone went out until the toy box was empty. When Daruma asks a rat passing by the threshold, the rat laughs "chū, chū" and answers that the little tortoiseshell cat that came to the house lately must have carried the Kewpie off to play with. They catch the tortoiseshell cat sunning itself on the veranda, and when the soldier levels his bayoneted rifle at her she is astonished and says that she has played with the young mistress's sash and her ball but has never touched the Kewpie, and that no doubt the rat has arranged it all to have her driven out. Seeing that this is so, they all run up into the ceiling, and from a corner they hear a voice crying "kī, kī, pī, pī."

Characters

  • The Kewpie　The doll born in America, who goes missing
  • Daruma　Born in Japan. He leads the search
  • The tin soldier　Born in Germany. He levels his rifle at the tortoiseshell cat
  • The tortoiseshell cat　The newcomer, suspected by the rat

The opening (original text)

Since the Kewpie doll born in America had disappeared, a great commotion began inside the toy box. The Japanese Daruma, with a headband tied round him and riding a bear made of scouring cloth, dashes out first of all. After him the tin soldier born in Germany drives off in a wooden motorcar. The harlequin doll born in France runs out after them together with the sleeping doll born in England. Everyone went out, and the toy box was empty.

The ending (original text)

And there, poor thing, lay the Kewpie, his stomach and everything flattened out, sleeping with his blue eyes swollen from crying. Everyone was delighted and brought him home, and gathering round and nursing him, blew him up again as he had been. The tortoiseshell cat grew big after that, and caught and killed all the rats in the house.

Kewpie by Yumeno Kyūsaku (Aozora Bunko)
