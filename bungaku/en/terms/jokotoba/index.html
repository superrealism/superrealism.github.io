What is Jokotoba (Prefatory Verse)?

Category
Style & Technique

In waka, a long preamble of two lines or more placed before a poem to lead into a particular word or phrase. It resembles the makurakotoba (pillow word) in function, but unlike the makurakotoba, a fixed five-syllable phrase, it runs to seven syllables or more, the word it leads into is not fixed, and it is newly composed for that one poem alone. Just as "ashihiki no yamadori no o no shidario no" leads into "naganagashi yo" ("the long, long night"), most jokotoba describe a scene from nature and then bridge from it to human feeling. The celebrated case of "kaze fukeba oki tsu shiranami" leading into "Tatsuta-yama" by way of a kakekotoba, shifting from scene to human affairs, is well known. Some lead in through similarity of sound, some through a connection of meaning, and some by way of a kakekotoba, and they are often combined with kakekotoba and engo. A jokotoba can occupy as much as half of a poem's thirty-one syllables. Because the jokotoba portion can be read as a scene in its own right, a double world of scene and feeling arises within the single poem, adding depth and expanse. It appears frequently in the "Man'yōshū," and, carried forward after the "Kokin Wakashū" as well, it was gradually refined into a technique that forms one of the structural pillars of court waka's rhetoric.
