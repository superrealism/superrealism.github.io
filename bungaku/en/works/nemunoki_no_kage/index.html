About this work

A historical story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shin Yomimono in 1948. At the nō performance for the opening of the second bailey palace, Nao, a girl with no friends, feels someone's steady gaze. A freshly drawn piece about the fulfilment of a quiet love that began from those watching eyes.

Summary

Someone is watching me — Nao had been aware of it for a while now. At the nō performance held for the opening of the second bailey palace, with the Kanze troupe summoned from Edo and the lord himself taking the part of Benkei. Nao, motherless, has come attended by her aunt, but as her aunt falls into talk with old friends, she is left outside the conversation. Around Nao, who has no real friends of her own, girls of her own age whisper happily to one another. And on Nao falls a hot, steady gaze from the men's seating. Turning casually to look, she finds the eyes quickly averted, impossible to catch. It is not unpleasant, and yet it grows irritating — but before long, the spot where that gaze falls grows warm, and that warmth spreads to her chest, and Nao is wrapped in a strange, sweetly disturbing sense of happiness. A feeling almost like a sharp pleasure, one she has never known in her seventeen years. Who could it be, she wonders — a friend of my brother's, perhaps? The love that begins from that watching gaze comes, in time, to bear fruit. At the close of the story, Nao presses her face against the chest of Iwata Hanzaburō, now become her husband, half laughing, half crying. "May I say it? That I love you this much — that I love you this much, this much..." A piece of Yamamoto's, warmly drawing the fulfilment of a fresh, innocent love.

Characters

  • Nao　A friendless girl
  • Iwata Hanzaburō　The man who watches Nao, and later becomes her husband

The opening (original text)

Someone is watching me. Nao had been aware of it for a while now. From the men's seating to the right, of course; turning casually to look, it seemed the person quickly averted their gaze each time, and no matter what she tried, she could not catch those eyes.

Nemunoki no kage by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"May I say it?" Nao pressed her cheek hard against her husband's chest, and said this, half laughing, half crying. "May I say it? That I love you this much — that I love you this much, this much..."

Nemunoki no kage by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
