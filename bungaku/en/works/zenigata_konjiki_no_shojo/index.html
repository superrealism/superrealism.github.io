About this work

The first and memorable installment of "The Casebook of Zenigata Heiji," published in "All Yomimono" in 1931. Zenigata Heiji, the young constable whose skill at throwing coins is the terror of criminals, is handed an impossible commission by Sasano Shinzaburō, chief lieutenant of the South Magistracy: find the man who loosed a poisoned arrow at the Shogun Iemitsu. The clues are the Ōtsuka physic garden, where Iemitsu stops on his way back from hawking, and Osayo, daughter of the herbalist there. Before long the case is joined by a single thread to another: beautiful girls are vanishing all over Edo and turning up as corpses gilded from head to foot. Heiji's first meeting with Oshizu, who will one day be his wife, is told here as well.

Summary

Sasano Shinzaburō, chief lieutenant of the South Magistracy, summons the constable Zenigata Heiji. During the hawking at Zōshigaya, a poisoned arrow grazed the Shogun Iemitsu's hat; find the man who loosed it, he says, before the next hawking day. Learning that Iemitsu is so fond of hawking because on the way home he stops at the Ōtsuka physic garden for the medicinal tea brewed by Osayo, daughter of the herbalist Tōge Sōjuken, Heiji fixes his attention on Karahanaya, a cosmetics shop on the Otowa road whose signboards advertise "Physic Garden Gourd Water" and "Sundry Secret Remedies of the Tōge School." He asks Oshizu, a girl from a Ryōgoku teahouse, to go in as a customer and see what she can learn — but Oshizu is drugged with something put in her tea and carried off by palanquin to the physic garden. That autumn, beautiful girls famous in Edo had been disappearing one after another and turning up days later, murdered, with the marks of gold leaf laid over their whole bodies — and this was how it was done. Heiji climbs the wall and slips in, only to be shut up in a hidden space above the ceiling, from which, through glass set in the floor, he looks down into the great hall. A grotesque idol, a company in black, and Oshizu set on a stand with gold leaf being laid over her skin. The hawking day was about to dawn.

Characters

  • Zenigata Heiji　A young constable of twenty-four or five, the terror of criminals for his skill at throwing coins
  • Oshizu　A girl from a Ryōgoku teahouse. At Heiji's request she goes into Karahanaya to spy, and is taken prisoner
  • Sasano Shinzaburō　Chief lieutenant of the South Magistracy, called the magistrate's brain. He entrusts the search to Heiji
  • Tōge Sōjuken　The herbalist in charge of the Ōtsuka physic garden. He and his daughter Osayo hold a secret between them
  • Osayo　Sōjuken's daughter, a beauty said to have no equal in the city, who offers Iemitsu his medicinal tea

The opening (original text)

This is the first of Zenigata Heiji's exploits, and it was this case that made Heiji famous. At this time Oshizu was not yet Heiji's wife, and Garappachi had not yet appeared. 1 "Heiji, I have a particular favor to ask. Will you undertake it?" "Yes, sir —" Zenigata no Heiji, unable to gauge what the other really meant, quietly raised his face. A good-looking man of twenty-four or five with a hard-bitten air, in a narrow lined kimono of fine indigo check, he hid his knees under it and brought together in front of him the hands that knew how to hold a coin.

The ending (original text)

Zenigata no Heiji had done all this and put down the enemy who sought the Shogun's life; but because he had not taken the culprits before the hawking day as he had promised Sasano Shinzaburō, and because the nature of the case was what it was, he was never publicly rewarded for the feat. Still, the name of Zenigata Heiji had been printed deep in Iemitsu's memory, and he had firmly won the love of the golden maiden — of Oshizu; and with that alone the young Heiji was entirely satisfied.
