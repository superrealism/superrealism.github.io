Biography

Born in 1871 in Chōshi, Chiba Prefecture; his real name was Tetsuo. He studied at Tokyo Semmon Gakkō (now Waseda University) and was baptized as a Christian by Uemura Masahisa. He joined Tokutomi Sohō's Min'yūsha, and during the First Sino-Japanese War drew attention as a war correspondent serializing letters to his younger brother in newspaper form, "Aitei Tsūshin" ("Letters to My Beloved Brother"). Drawing on his travels through the frontier settlements along the Sorachi River in Hokkaido and the natural groves near Tokyo, he built a style that combined a fresh appreciation of nature learned from Wordsworth with a keen eye for the joys and sorrows of ordinary people. He left the celebrated essay "Musashino," along with gem-like short stories such as "Gyūniku to Bareisho" ("Beef and Potatoes"), "Wasureenu Hitobito" ("Unforgettable People"), "Gen Oji" ("Uncle Gen"), and "Haru no Tori" ("Spring Birds"), and is regarded, beginning from Romanticism, as a pioneer of Japanese Naturalism. He was close friends with Tayama Katai and Yanagita Kunio. Suffering from pulmonary tuberculosis in his later years, he died in 1908 at the Nangōin sanatorium in Chigasaki, Kanagawa Prefecture, at the young age of 36.
