About this work

A long novel serialized in the Tokyo Asahi Shimbun in 1918 and 1919. Kishimoto Sutekichi, a writer who has lost his wife, forms a relationship with his niece Setsuko, who keeps house for him; fleeing the anguish of it he goes to France, and on his return, still unable to break the tie, at last confesses everything in the hope of a new life. Tōson took as material, without alteration, the affair he actually had with his niece Komako, and it raised a storm of praise and blame from the first — Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's scathing charge of an old and cunning hypocrite among them. The furthest point of the Japanese confessional novel.

Summary

To the writer Kishimoto Sutekichi, who has lost his wife and lives with four children on his hands, his elder brother's daughter Setsuko comes to help. In his loneliness and exhaustion Kishimoto crosses a line with his niece that must not be crossed, and in time Setsuko is with child. In fear of the world's eyes he puts the child born to them into other hands, and himself leaves for France as though running away. Passing days of war alone in a foreign lodging, Kishimoto goes on looking at his own sin. When he comes home after three years and more, Setsuko's feeling has not cooled in the least, and the two are drawn back into their perilous relation. His brother presses him, and the rumor spreads among the relatives. Driven into a corner, Kishimoto resolves at last to set the whole of it down publicly as a novel. That confession has the result of driving Setsuko out of the house, and she goes far away across the sea. Having lost everything, Kishimoto reburies the roots of a plant left in the garden, and feels that Setsuko is still there, within himself and in the earth of the garden as well.

Characters

  • Kishimoto Sutekichi　A writer who has lost his wife, and who suffers over his relation with his niece and seeks a new life through confession
  • Setsuko　The daughter of Kishimoto's elder brother, who comes to help in her uncle's house and falls into a deep relation with him
  • Kishimoto Yoshio　Setsuko's father and Kishimoto's elder brother, who learns of the affair and reproaches him
  • Senta and Shigeru　The children left to Kishimoto

The opening (original text)

Prefatory chapter I "Kishimoto — I mean to write out for you the fragments of my life and thought of late. But to tell the truth, I have no material to write about. It is a thing one may pass over in silence. The deeper the friendship between you and me, the more proper it would be to stay silent. Having left the old house and moved into a new one, I am about at the point of rejoicing that the days I spend in idleness have grown many...." A letter from a friend living out toward Nakano in the suburbs lay open before Kishimoto.

Shinsei by Shimazaki Tōson (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Ah, that's right, O-Setsu left it here, didn't she?" said Senta, coming down into the garden too. "Here. Let me help as well." With these children for company, Kishimoto reburied the root deeply, so that it should not be hurt by the frost that would soon come. Setsuko was now not only within Kishimoto; she was in the earth of the garden as well.
