Biography

Born in 1891 in Ueda, Nagano Prefecture. His father, a school principal, took his own life after losing the emperor's portrait in a fire at the school, and Kume was raised in Kōriyama, Fukushima Prefecture, his mother's home town. At the First Higher School he mixed with Kikuchi Kan, Matsuoka Yuzuru, and Akutagawa Ryūnosuke, and while studying English literature at Tokyo Imperial University he founded the third and fourth incarnations of "Shinshichō." Recognized for the play "Gyūnyūya no Kyōdai" ("The Milkman's Brothers"), he established his standing as a writer with "Chichi no Shi" ("A Father's Death"), told through the eyes of an eight-year-old at his father's death, and "Jukensei no Shuki" ("A Notebook of an Exam Candidate"), framed as a young man's account of failing his entrance exams. He became a disciple of Natsume Sōseki, but his love for Sōseki's eldest daughter Fudeko went unrequited; her marriage to his close friend Matsuoka Yuzuru left a lasting wound, which he later drew on for the novel "Hasen" ("The Shipwreck"). Following the success of the serialized novel "Hotaru Gusa" ("Firefly Grass") in the "Jiji Shimpō" in 1918, he turned to popular fiction and became a widely read author. In the essay "'Watakushi' Shōsetsu to 'Shinkyō' Shōsetsu," he coined the term "state-of-mind novel" and helped ignite the debate over the nature of the I-novel, and he is also credited with coining the word "bikushō," a wry, bittersweet smile. Settling in Kamakura, he became a central figure among its writers, served on the selection committee for the Akutagawa Prize, and after the war headed the Kamakura Bunko publishing house. He died in 1952 at the age of 60.
