About this work

Written in August 1950. A short story following the month before the rainy season in the life of a widow who lost her husband in Siberia and lives with her small son and an old manservant. Through the almost comic uproar of a love affair between the aged servant and a young maid, the narrator notices in herself a feeling she cannot tell from jealousy or from pity. One is astonished that a writer of nineteen could get this far into the loneliness of a woman so unlike her in age and circumstance.

Summary

Sweeping the garden after killing caterpillars with a stick, "I" watch the burning scraps of paper and remember my husband. He went to the war, and four years later, last autumn, we were told that he had died of illness in Siberia. The land and the villa and the mansion of my husband, the only son of a great landowner, are all gone, and now I live in a rented house in the suburbs with my six-year-old son Yukio and old Sakue, who has served the family since I was a child. As my piecework painting chintz grew busy, at the end of last year I hired a young maid — and her name was O-Haru, the same as that of Sakue's dead wife.

Sakue, sunk in gloom since he lost his wife, comes at some point to dote on O-Haru, far younger though she is. The sound of the massage at night, the air of the two of them appearing together one evening. "I" am angry, and cannot judge whether that anger is a sense of responsibility or a jealousy that comes from my own loneliness. In time a match is found for O-Haru and she marries; but Sakue will not have it. He forces his way into the new household, and O-Haru is sent back to us divorced. She says quite coolly that the old man should be sent off somewhere.

"I" make up my mind and tell Sakue to go home to his own country. Next morning Sakue is gone, and there is left a scrap of paper addressed to the young master, and a wooden boat with a sail made of his patterned work clothes. The sky clouds over suddenly and rain falls. The rainy season has begun.

Characters

  • I　A widow who lost her husband in Siberia and lives by painting chintz
  • Sakue　The old servant who has served the family since I was a child. He has lost his wife, O-Haru
  • O-Haru　The young maid newly taken on, who bears the same name as Sakue's dead wife
  • Yukio　"My" son, six years old

The opening (original text)

I had gone down into the garden to look for caterpillars, and was killing them with a bamboo stick. It was just at the time before the rains, when the young leaves sway in the wind and look full of life and a wool kimono is a little too warm; and with my bare feet in an old pair of enameled sandals I was busy with the garden. From that garden of hardly two tsubo more than ten caterpillars came out. I burned some scraps of paper, meaning to throw the corpses, which I had laid out neatly on the stepping stone, one by one carefully into the fire.

Nyūbai by Kusaka Yōko (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Sakue would be on the train by now, his false teeth clicking; how would he be feeling? But that no longer mattered. "There, rain. Let us go inside." I took Yukio up and went into the room. It was a rain that looked as if it would last two or three days. The garden shrubs had glossy leaves, and from behind them the scent of daphne came faintly drifting.

Nyūbai by Kusaka Yōko (Aozora Bunko)
