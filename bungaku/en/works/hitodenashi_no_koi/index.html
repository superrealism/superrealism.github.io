About this work

A grotesque short story published in Sunday Mainichi in 1926, told as an old woman's account of an unforgettable episode of her youth. Married to a strikingly handsome man, she should have been happy — but she notices that her husband slips out to the storehouse night after night. Driven by jealousy, she follows him, and sees what he loves: not a living person but a single old doll. The perverse love that gives the story its title — a love not fit for a human being — carried to a weird and pitiful close. A fine piece of Ranpo's aestheticism and horror.

Summary

"Kadono — you will know the name. He was my former husband, who died ten years ago." So an old woman begins, quietly, to speak of her youth. On a go-between's recommendation she was married to Kadono, a strikingly handsome man of the Kadono house. She was quite bewitched by that beauty — pale as if transparent, somehow gloomy, with even a touch of the sinister about it. But in the midst of a married life that ought to have been happy, she notices that night after night her husband slips out of bed and steals away to the storehouse at the back of the garden. Tormented by jealousy, supposing there is another woman, she resolves one night to follow him. What was being carried on secretly on the second floor of the storehouse was a scene of the strangest abnormality: her husband exchanging endearments with a beautiful old doll as though with a living lover. Learning that his love is for a doll, the wife in her jealousy smashes the thing to pieces. But that night, running to the storehouse in anxiety because her husband does not come back, she finds him lying in a pool of blood, dead by his own hand, with the broken doll in his arms. A lovers' suicide between a human being and a lump of clay — and the blood dripping from the lips of the half-shattered doll makes it look as though the doll itself were laughing. A representative work of Ranpo's aesthetic weird fiction, on a maddened love for something that is not flesh and blood.

Characters

  • I　The woman who married into the Kadono house, telling of her youth in old age
  • Kadono　Her strikingly handsome husband, who pines with love for an old doll

The opening (original text)

Kadono — you will know the name. He was my former husband, who died ten years ago. When so many months and years have passed, even to say "Kadono" aloud makes him seem quite like someone else's affair, and as for that business, it comes to seem to me somehow — was it not a dream?

Hitodenashi no koi by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

And when I looked, from the lips of the doll, half of which was left after I had beaten it to pieces, one spatter of blood dripped down onto my husband's arm that held its head, exactly as if the doll itself had vomited it; and the doll was laughing an uncanny laugh, the laugh of its death agony.

Hitodenashi no koi by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)
