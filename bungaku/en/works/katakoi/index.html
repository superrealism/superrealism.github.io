About this work

A short story in the form of something the narrator was told by a university friend on the Keihin train. At a banquet on a business trip, the friend meets Otoku, once a maid at a restaurant he used to frequent, now working as a geisha. The woman who used to be sweet on one of their group now speaks of a different love — and the listener, watching her tell it in earnest, laughing and crying at once, wonders whether it is hysteria. A postscript to youth, contained in the short time before the train reaches Shinbashi.

Summary

Meeting on a summer afternoon in a Keihin train one of the close friends who left the university with him, he was told this story — so the frame begins. The other day, when I went to Y— on the company's business, they held a party there and invited me. In the alcove there was a lithograph of General Nogi and artificial peonies in front of it; but because it rained and because the party was small, it was pleasanter than I had expected. And among the women who poured the wine was O-Toku, the maid at U— where we used to go and drink. That woman with the flat nose and the narrow forehead, who was the greatest tease in the place, was sitting there in her party dress with a bottle in her hand, putting on the same airs as her fellows. Her way of jerking her chin whenever she spoke was as it used to be. ... The narrator feels the impermanence of things. Even she was once the object of Shimura's hopeless fancy. Shimura used to buy a little bottle of peppermint in all seriousness and press it on her, saying, "Try it, it is sweet." In time the talk moves to the love that O-Toku tells of herself. At her manner — laughing and crying, and speaking as if she meant to be spiteful — the listener remarks, "That one may well be hysterical," and yet remembers that there was something oddly earnest about her. It may be that the story of falling in love with a photograph was an invention, and that she was really once in love, hopelessly, with one of our set — and just as he finished, the train the two of them were in reached Shinbashi station in the dusk.

Characters

  • The friend　The man who met O-Toku again at the party at Y—
  • O-Toku　A teasing woman who was once the maid at U—, and is now a geisha
  • Shimura　One of our set, on whom O-Toku had a hopeless fancy in our student days
