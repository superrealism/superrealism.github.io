About this work

A short story Sakaguchi Ango published in Aoi Uma in June 1931. It tells the fortunes of Professor Wind, who left a testament and went missing, and of his sworn enemy Professor Octopus, in a bizarre oratorical style full of "even so" and "alas": a nonsense farce. Warmly praised by Makino Shin'ichi, it launched the unknown Ango into the literary world at a stroke. The speed of a style in which runaway logic turns straight into laughter already foreshadows the leaps of argument in the later "Discourse on Decadence."

Summary

Do you, gentlemen, know of Professor Wind? The great Professor Wind vanished without a trace, leaving behind only his last testament, and I, the narrator, find myself under suspicion of conspiracy. According to his testament, Professor Wind's archenemy, Professor Octopus, is a detestable man who hides his bald head beneath a wig, and a great villain who stole away the maiden who ought to have been Professor Wind's betrothed. Having sworn revenge, Professor Wind, on the very day of the wedding, breaks boldly into Professor Octopus's mansion in broad daylight, intent on tearing off his wig and exposing his baldness to the whole world. But when he opens the bathroom door, there before him stands Professor Octopus's bride. His plan undone, the despairing Professor Wind sets down his last testament — and yet what I myself witnessed was the professor, in his silk hat, dashing off like a gale and vanishing in an instant through a doorway that showed no trace of ever having been opened. The professor had become the wind. Had he truly become the wind? Indeed — indeed he had become the wind. And as its irrefutable scientific proof: at that very same instant, the detestable Professor Octopus was struck down with influenza.

The opening (original text)

Do you gentlemen know, perhaps, of the residence of Professor Wind, at such-and-such number, such-and-such street, in the city of Tokyo? You do not. That is most regrettable. And do you gentlemen know of the great Professor Wind himself? You do not. Alas. Then do you gentlemen not even know that only his last testament was discovered, and that the great Professor Wind himself vanished, utterly, without a trace? You do not. Alack.

Kaze hakase by Sakaguchi Ango (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Gentlemen, the great professor became the wind. Did he truly become the wind? Indeed, he became the wind. For did not his form vanish clean away? Is that which cannot be seen thereby the wind? Indeed, it is thereby the wind. For is not his form invisible? This can be nothing other than the wind. It is the wind. Indeed it is the wind, the wind, the wind. Do you gentlemen still doubt this self-evident fact? That is most regrettable. Then let me add one further, irrefutable scientific proof. On this very day, that detestable Professor Octopus was struck down with influenza at precisely this same instant.

Kaze hakase by Sakaguchi Ango (Aozora Bunko)
