About this work

Kunieda Shirō's representative work, serialized in "Kuraku" from 1925 into the following year. Broken off unfinished, it is nonetheless called the summit of the tale of the fantastic. In Kōfu in 1558, on a hazy spring night, Tsuchiya Shōzaburō is made to buy a length of deep crimson cloth by an uncanny old man. No one else would buy it, he is told, for fear of a color like cloth dyed in human blood — and woven into it is the name of the father who vanished sixteen years before. The Kōketsu Castle floating on a lake at the foot of Mount Fuji, a masked lord of the castle, people rotting with disease: from here rises a gorgeous narrative world in which the weird, the fantastic and the decadent swirl together.

Summary

A hazy spring night in 1558. Tsuchiya Shōzaburō, a retainer of the Takeda house, is walking the streets of Kōfu to look at the blossoms when an old man stops him in the precincts of the Shinmei shrine. He wants him to buy a length of deep crimson cloth. When Shōzaburō says it is a color a young woman would be glad of, the old man answers that at every mansion he was told it was "dreadful" and no one would buy it. A red as though dyed in human blood, blazing — that is what they find dreadful. Shōzaburō laughs and tosses him a gold piece; but when he holds the cloth up to the moon he shudders in spite of himself. Woven into it is the name of his father, Shōhachirō Masatake, who vanished mysteriously sixteen years ago. From the curse of the crimson cloth bought that night, wave upon wave of disaster piles up on Shōzaburō. The Kōketsu Castle floating on a lake in the foothills of Fuji, the lord of the castle with his mask, the people whose blood is pressed out of them, those who wield the spells of the dharani. Against the Kai of the warring states with Takeda Shingen at its center, the threads of the uncanny and of obsession twine layer upon layer.

Characters

  • Tsuchiya Shōzaburō Masaharu　A retainer of Takeda Shingen. Buying the crimson cloth draws him into the uncanny
  • The old cloth seller　The man who sold Shōzaburō the deep crimson cloth at the Shinmei shrine
  • The lord of Kōketsu Castle　The figure in the castle on the lake at the foot of Fuji, who hides his face behind a mask
  • Tsuchiya Shōhachirō Masatake　Shōzaburō's father, who vanished mysteriously sixteen years ago
  • Takeda Shingen (Harunobu)　Lord of Kai, whom Shōzaburō serves

The opening (original text)

Part One 1 Tsuchiya Shōzaburō left his mansion and wandered idly through the streets. He passed before the Kōsaka mansion, the Baba mansion and the Sanada mansion, and walked on toward Kaji Lane. It was a hazy spring night and the hour was already late, so that the mansions were still; but as the poem has it, "the voluptuousness of a spring night, an indefinable fragrance spilling over, no one about and yet a feeling of gaiety" — there was nothing gloomy about it at all.

The ending (original text)

Five fingers, trembling violently! The thumb and forefinger just caught the chin of the mask, and after what seemed a moment's hesitation the mask came away from the face with a start. (Unfinished)
