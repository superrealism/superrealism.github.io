About this work

An essay on tanka Masaoka Shiki serialized in the newspaper Nippon in February and March 1898. With the sentence that Tsurayuki was a bad poet and the Kokinshū a worthless anthology, he cut down the authority of the old school at a stroke, held up the Man'yōshū and Minamoto no Sanetomo, and raised the flag of a reform of tanka based on sketching from life. The scathing argument, driven home over ten instalments in the form of letters, is Shiki's central work of literary reform after his renewal of haiku, and a landmark essay at the starting point of modern tanka.

Summary

A treatise on tanka in ten letters, opening with the words, "As you say, tanka poetry has of late been in a thoroughly poor state." Shiki first praises Minamoto no Sanetomo as a first-rate poet whose "own nature rises up sheer, vying in height with the mountains and competing in light with the sun and moon," and then, with the same stroke of the sword, flatly denies an authority a thousand years standing: "Tsurayuki was a poor poet, and the Kokinshū is a worthless collection." He shows how confined the world of tanka has been within narrow convention, killing off poems with stock reasoning and associative wordplay. Taking up example poems one by one, Shiki argues concretely for their merits and faults, and holds that elegant diction, the vulgar tongue, Chinese words and foreign words alike can all serve as the language of poetry, so long as they are fit to express a feeling of beauty. The argument proceeds strictly by way of the concrete, down to such practical advice as the art of composition when singing of the machinery of civilization, a train or a railway. Freeing tanka from the hands of the old school, toward a new poetry grounded in sketching from life and real feeling — this manifesto, fired off from a sickbed at Negishi, led men such as Itō Sachio and Nagatsuka Takashi onto the path of tanka, and, through the Araragi school, became the wellspring of modern tanka.

The opening (original text)

As you say, tanka poetry has of late been in a thoroughly poor state. To speak honestly, it has been in a thoroughly poor state since the Man'yōshū, since Sanetomo. That man Sanetomo, dying an untimely death at not even thirty, just when he was coming into his own, is truly a thing to be regretted.

The ending (original text)

And things that are simply unlovely to look at are better seen from a distance. A train seen beyond a field of rape blossoms, or running along the far edge of the summer grass, might, I think, be one way of dispelling that unloveliness. I have gathered up and set down whatever I felt like saying, just as it came. There will no doubt be occasion another day to go into it more fully. That is all for now.

Famous passages

Tsurayuki was a poor poet, and the Kokinshū is a worthless collection.

A line that cuts head-on into a tanka establishment that had long held the Kokinshū supreme. Shiki's reform of tanka poetry began with this declaration.
