About this work

A long novel by Hayama Yoshiki published in 1926, and a central work of Japanese proletarian literature. Aboard the steamer Manjumaru, carrying a full load of coal from Muroran to Yokohama, stokers and seamen worked past endurance on a freezing northern run come little by little, out of inhuman labour and the order of rank on board, to combine and to stand up. Convincing description backed by his own experience as a seaman, and a hand that writes the raging sea itself almost as a character, gave a real body to a labour literature that had tended to run to abstraction.

Summary

The port of Muroran in the depth of winter. The steamer Manju-maru, with three thousand tons of coal packed into her belly, puts out for Yokohama through a snowstorm driving sideways. It is the most dangerous of winter runs on the northern sea, with the hull sunk to the deck and exposed to the raging surf of the Pacific. In the stokehold at the bottom of the ship the young stokers go on shoveling coal at the scorching furnace mouths. Covered in coal dust and sweat, given wretched food and cramped bunks, hurt in accidents and taken ill, they are not thought of. Meanwhile on the upper deck the captain and the chief engineer drink in warm cabins and guard nothing but the profits of the shipping company. At this plain difference the anger of the young seamen piles up day by day. In time, on the occasion of a death, they present a written demand for better treatment and set about organizing themselves aboard. There is an informer, there is a splitting of the ranks, there is the pressure of authority. And still those who have once awakened do not go back. When the ship comes into port the leaders are taken and sent behind iron bars. In a cell with a cold wind blowing in, they wait for their sentences to be settled.

Characters

  • The stokers and sailors of the Manju-maru　The young seamen worked to the bone on a run through bitter cold, who come at last to stand together
  • The captain and the chief engineer　The senior officers, who guard the company's profits and do not treat the crew as human beings

The opening (original text)

I At the mouth by which the deeply indented port of Muroran opens to the Pacific, the island of Daikoku sits like a stopper. The snow came driving sideways, covering the whole of Hokkaido to a thickness from the ground to the clouds. The steamer Manju-maru, with three thousand tons of coal packed into her belly, went on through the snowstorm toward Yokohama. The ship was just then rounding the island of Daikoku. Beyond that island great waves were breaking.
