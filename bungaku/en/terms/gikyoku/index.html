What is Gikyoku (Plays)?

Category
Form / Genre

Literature composed of characters' lines and stage directions, written on the premise of theatrical performance. It has kabuki and jōruri scripts as its classical antecedents, but the modern play, as an independent literary form to be read and appreciated in its own right, was established through the importation of Western modern drama and the shingeki movement. Tsubouchi Shōyō's translations of Shakespeare and Mori Ōgai's translation of Goethe's "Faust" set the model, and Izumi Kyōka built his own distinctive world of beauty in fantastical plays such as "Yasha ga Ike" ("Demon Pond") and "Tenshu Monogatari" ("The Castle Tower"). Okamoto Kidō left masterworks of the new kabuki with "Shuzenji Monogatari" ("The Tale of Shuzenji") and others, and Kikuchi Kan was likewise recognized as a playwright for "Chichi Kaeru" ("Father Returns," 1917), which distilled the conflict between parent and child. From the end of the Taishō era, Kishida Kunio established a sophisticated conversational drama depicting everyday psychology through the nuances of dialogue in works such as "Kamifūsen" ("Paper Balloon"), raising the artistic level of modern Japanese drama. While it is completed only through performance, the play also holds its own appeal as literature to be read, and many writers set their hand to this form alongside fiction.
