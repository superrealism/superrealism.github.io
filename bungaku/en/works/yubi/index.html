About this work

A very short piece published in Hitchcock Magazine in 1960. A pianist whose right wrist has been cut off by someone, and the narrator, the doctor who has not yet told him. The patient tries to play an imaginary piano, not knowing his hand is gone — and at the end the fingers of the wrist, preserved in alcohol, move inside the jar. A sharp little piece of terror from Ranpo's last years.

Summary

The patient, waking from the anesthetic, does not know that beneath the thick bandage on his right hand the wrist has been cut off. He is a pianist of some name, and in the dark of night someone — most likely a fellow musician envious of his fame — struck the wrist from his right hand with a sharp blade. "I," his doctor and also his close friend, cannot bear to cast him down, and mean to keep the fact from him for a while. But the patient says he cannot rest unless he plays every day the piece he has newly composed, and begins to move his fingers on the blanket. On the instant "I" press the ulnar nerve in his upper arm so that the sensation of the lost fingers is carried to his brain as a phantom, and let him play an imaginary piano while he says, "They move well." Escaping from that pitiful scene, "I" pass in front of the operating room and follow the eyes of a nurse gone pale — to a glass jar on the shelf. The patient's wrist, preserved in alcohol, and its five fingers, like the legs of a white crab, were moving helplessly and unceasingly, in the rhythm of striking the keys of a piano. A short piece from Ranpo's late years that leaves a chilling resonance.

The opening (original text)

The patient woke from the anesthetic of the operation and looked at my face. There was a thick bandage wound about his right hand, but he did not know at all that the wrist had been cut off. Since he was a pianist of some name, the loss of his right wrist was a mortal blow. The criminal may have been a fellow musician envious of his fame.

Yubi by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Before I knew it I had gone into the operating room and looked at that shelf. There stood a large glass jar with his wrist preserved in alcohol. One glance at it, and I could not move. In the alcohol in the jar his wrist — no, his five fingers — were moving like the legs of a white crab. In the rhythm of striking the keys of a piano; but much smaller than the real movement, helplessly, like an infant's, moving and moving without stopping.

Yubi by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)
