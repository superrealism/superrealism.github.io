About this work

A detective story for boys that Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shin Shōnen in 1937. Behind the rumour of a ghost said to appear at a certain hotel lay a terrible case of espionage. A bracing piece in which a professor's lucid reasoning unravels the truth from the unnatural behaviour of a waiter.

Summary

At the hotel where the young Itō Toyoji is staying, a waiter brings his morning coffee. Young Itō is in none too good a mood — there is an eerie rumor at this hotel that a "ghost" appears. But behind that rumor lay a dreadful scheme. It is a doctor of a clear and penetrating mind who unravels the case. The doctor grows suspicious the moment he notices that the waiter volunteered the story of the ghost with such relish, unasked. A man in the hospitality trade, after all, would have no reason to go out of his way to bring up a ghost story his guests would find unpleasant. Further tying together the disappearance of a woman named Midori, the regimental barracks visible from the hotel window, and three foreigners who come to the lobby every morning, the doctor sees through to the true nature of the affair: an espionage case. The ghost rumor had been a smokescreen to cover up spy activity. "In short, they overdid it," the doctor concludes — needlessly spreading the ghost rumor, and confining Midori, had, if anything, become the very clue that exposed their own crime. A rousing spy-detective story for young readers.

Characters

  • Doctor　A clear-minded man who solves the case
  • Itō Toyoji　A young man staying at the hotel
  • Midori　A woman who goes missing

The opening (original text)

Just as young Itō Toyoji finished washing his face and was getting changed, a waiter in uniform brought in his morning coffee. "Good morning." "Ah, good morning." "Did you sleep well?"

Bōrei hoteru by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"In short, they overdid it. They spread the ghost rumor when there was no need to, and then, trying to steal the secret of my research, they confined poor Midori, who happened to come along at the wrong moment. Between the two, they all but handed us the very clue that exposed their own crime. As the saying goes, isn't it — too much is as bad as too little. Ahahahaha."

Bōrei hoteru by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
