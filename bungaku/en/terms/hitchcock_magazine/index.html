What is Hitchcock Magazine?

Category
Society / Group

A monthly mystery magazine founded by Hōseki-sha in 1959. It began as the Japanese edition of the American magazine of the same name, and Nakahara Hikoko (later the novelist Kobayashi Nobuhiko) served as editor-in-chief from the inaugural issue through the March 1963 issue (the inaugural issue was numbered the August issue and went on sale in June of that year). Known for its urbane pages, which paired translated mysteries with film reviews, jazz criticism, columns, roundtables, and illustrations, it gave Hoshi Shin'ichi, Tsuzuki Michio, and Yūki Shōji, among other Japanese writers, a stage for short-short fiction. Yamakawa Masao was a regular contributor, offering many stories and short-shorts in 1962 alone — "Natsu no Sōretsu" ("The Summer Funeral Procession"), "Merry Christmas," "Hakase no Me" ("The Doctor's Eye"), "Shūshū" ("Collection") — establishing here his style of capturing, in spare prose, the irony and dread lurking within everyday life. Just as "Shinseinen" had nurtured writers of detective fiction, this magazine nurtured the writers of the short fiction of the postwar era. It ceased publication with its July 1963 issue, but its editorial approach is said to have influenced later magazines such as "Heibon Punch" and "Hanashi no Tokushū."
