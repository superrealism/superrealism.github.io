What is Shishōsetsu (the I-Novel)?

Category
Form / Genre

A form of fiction distinctive to modern Japanese literature, depicting, in a confessional manner and with almost no fictional embellishment, the writer's own real life and inner self. It arose when Naturalist literature, which had set out to "depict reality just as it is," gradually shifted its center of gravity from the observation of society to the exposure of the writer's own private life, and Tayama Katai's "Futon" ("The Quilt," 1907), candidly depicting a middle-aged writer's infatuation with a young female disciple, is regarded as its starting point. This stance — of excluding fiction and setting down without concealment even the "I"'s failures and humiliations — achieved a purity distinct from the Western autobiographical novel, and spread as well among the Naturalist writers' Taishō-era successors. This lineage of confessional literature flowed on into the Buraiha writers as well: Dazai Osamu, in "Ningen Shikkaku" ("No Longer Human"), candidly depicted a self-destructive life, and Sakaguchi Ango, in the critical I-novel "Darakuron" ("On Decadence"), questioned postwar morality. The I-novel tendency is often spoken of in contrast to the narrative drive of popular fiction and the technical artifice of the New Sensationist school, and is regarded as one of the important characteristics of modern Japanese literature.
