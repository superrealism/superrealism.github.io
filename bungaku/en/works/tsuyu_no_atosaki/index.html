About this work

One of Nagai Kafū's central works, published in Chūō Kōron in October 1931. Set in the cafés of a Ginza modernized after the earthquake, it takes for its protagonist Kimie, a waitress who plays a great many men and lives cheerfully and dissolutely. Kafū draws, with a distanced and ironic eye, the underhand revenge plotted against her by Kiyooka, the writer who once kept her, and Kimie living on toughly, unshaken by it. Held to be the high point of his fiction of the pleasure quarters and of manners, catching vividly the shifting customs of the city.

Summary

Kimie, a waitress at a café in Ginza, is a good-looking woman who attracts men easily, but is utterly indifferent to any notion of chastity, forming relationships one after another, without a care, with the customers and lovers who court her, and moving on again. Kiyooka Susumu, a writer who once kept her as his mistress, cannot bear Kimie's free-living ways, and, nursing too a grudge at having been thrown over by her, secretly resolves on revenge. Kiyooka uses other people to have Kimie's clothes and belongings secretly damaged, trying to shake her with anonymous, threatening acts. But Kimie herself, though frightened for a time by the eerie incidents that befall her one after another, is at heart an optimist and a tough one, and, without ever brooding deeply over it, goes on living her days as before, playing about with her men. The revenge comes to nothing, and it is the men, scheming underhandedly, who end up looking all the more ridiculous. Kafū vividly portrays the pleasure-seeking manners of a modern Tokyo, and the ways of the men and women living in it, with an eye cool and set apart from any moral yardstick.

Characters

  • Kimie　A free-living waitress at a Ginza café. Indifferent to chastity, she lives on toughly through her involvements with many men — the protagonist of this piece
  • Kiyooka Susumu　A writer who once kept Kimie as his mistress. Angered by her way of life, he plots a spiteful revenge

The opening (original text)

The waitress Kimie, having only to be at the café on the Ginza main street from three in the afternoon that day, walked leisurely along the moat from her rented room in Ichigaya Honmurachō, boarded an omnibus outside the gate, and got off at Hibiya. Then, with the railway line's overpass ahead of her, she turned down a side street where nothing but the flags of eating and drinking establishments caught the eye, as though she had wandered into some run-down part of town, and called on a fortune-teller who kept, on the glass window of his rented office, gold lettering reading "Kinkidō, Divination by the Book of Changes."

Tsuyu no atosaki by Nagai Kafū (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Kimie sprang up and went on crying out, half out of her senses, "Auntie, Auntie!"
