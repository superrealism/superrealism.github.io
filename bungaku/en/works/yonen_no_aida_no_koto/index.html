About this work

The earliest surviving piece by Kusaka Yōko, published in the coterie magazine VIKING in 1949, when she was eighteen. It follows how the feeling a girl of sixteen forms for a man she only passes each morning on a narrow flight of stone steps grows over four years, and how it loses anywhere to go. The hand, in which girlishness and self-mockery live together, is already distinctive, and the attitude that runs through all her later work — choosing one's own wretchedness — is germinating here.

Summary

Carrying an armful of sweet peas, "I" get off at A— station in the next town instead of going home, and find myself standing before a Western-style house I know. The moment something white flickers behind the frosted glass I run off without knowing what I am doing, and only come to myself again by the river — who was that figure?

Memory goes back four years. At sixteen, a schoolgirl, "I" used to pass one man every morning on the narrow stone steps of the way to school. Two months in which nothing was exchanged but a "thank you" as they passed. On the day she took to her bed with a lacquer rash, the doctor who came to the house turned out to be that figure — a young surgeon named Sasada Akio. When he began coming every day that summer with injections for her beriberi her feeling grew deeper; but her mother meant to choose Akio for her elder sister Hiroko's husband. "I" begged him on the stone steps to break the match, and in the end Akio was adopted into another doctor's family and went away.

A year, and another year. Sentiment turned into desire, and "I" took a post, meaning to hide in the crowd, and only came nearer to him instead. On every business trip she went round by the hospital; and going in at last on a rainy day, she found there another man of the same surname — he had left long ago. Passing wild days in her despair, she hears from her sister that Akio has set up practice in the city of A—. And today, without knowing how, she has come as far as this.

Characters

  • I　A girl who goes on thinking for four years of the figure she passed on the stone steps one morning at sixteen
  • Sasada (Toda) Akio　A young surgeon living up on the hill, later adopted into a doctor's family
  • Hiroko　"My" elder sister, for whom a match with Akio is proposed
  • Mother　Who means to choose Akio for her elder daughter's husband

The opening (original text)

Wearing a dress of pale mouse-gray wool, I am carrying a bouquet. I have just been to visit a house I know, and have been allowed to cut as many as I liked of the sweet peas that were blooming there in the garden as if overflowing, and am on my way home. The flowers have a heavy scent, a little too much for the nose, and what rises from each of the colors — the deep purple, the pale pink, the white — comes together again and makes something new and other, and throws it over me. Is there, in this scent, some strange working?

Yonen no aida no koto by Kusaka Yōko (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

When I reached the station the train pulled out with a great noise. My shadow, standing dejected at the crossing, is trampled down by the sound of the train and by its great body. (My insides are torn open and thick blood falls drop by drop.) At that moment I felt clearly the end of him and me. Not one person was left on the platform. He and the children had gone away on the train. "Good-bye, Akio." The bunch of sweet peas, drooping and limp, still gave off its uncanny scent. The May sun still poured the last of its light over the earth. But none of it brought me anything at all.

Yonen no aida no koto by Kusaka Yōko (Aozora Bunko)
