About this work

A historical story by Yamamoto Shūgorō, of the human-feeling kind. A poor mother and daughter living in a back tenement at Okachimachi in Shitaya. Cornered by the world's eyes as the family of a murderer, and having thought again and again of dying together, they nevertheless live on and reach a small happiness. A piece looking warmly at the sorrow and the redemption of those living in the ordinary streets of Edo.

Summary

"Say — let's die. Let's die together, the two of us, all right, Mother?" In a back-alley tenement in Shitaya Okachimachi, where the lively music of the autumn festival can be heard, the daughter Oume says this to her mother Omiki, as though she has made up her mind. "I'm afraid to die alone." This mother and daughter live under the world's cold gaze as "the murderer's child," "the murderer's daughter" — stared at wherever they walk through town, carrying a burden of shame that is not truly theirs to bear. Omiki, too, has thought countless times of dying together with her daughter. "Why is it that we poor people are always so quick to wish for death — is it that dying seems an easier thing than going on living?" But the story does not end in despair. Through a series of events, the troubles surrounding mother and daughter come, in the end, "all to be set right." At the story's close, Oume says she wants to go to the shrine of Ushi no Gozen, where her late grandfather's spirit lingers most strongly, and tell him, "Everything has been set right." As she walks toward the ferry landing together with a young man named Yoshizō, there is no longer any shadow of death in her step — so much so that they exchange the light banter, "Already carrying on like a wife, are you." A piece of human feeling by Yamamoto, looking with warmth upon the sorrow, and the small salvation, of the poor living in the back streets of Edo.

Characters

  • Oume　The daughter of a poor tenement family
  • Omiki　Oume's mother
  • Yoshizō　The young man courting Oume

The opening (original text)

—Say, let's die, Oume said, as though she had made up her mind. Let's die together, the two of us, all right, Mother? From the main street came the lively festival music of flute, bell, and drum. It was in the back alley of Shitaya Okachimachi, on the evening the autumn festival began.

Masuotoshi by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"I want to tell Grandfather," Oume said, lowering her eyes as she walked. "—I want to go to the shrine of Ushi no Gozen, and just tell him that everything has been set right." "I understand. Shall we go by the Takeya ferry?" "Watch out!" Oume pushed Yoshizō aside. "It's a palanquin — do be careful." "Already carrying on like a wife, are you — the ferry landing's this way."
