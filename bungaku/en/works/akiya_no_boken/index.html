About this work

The story (1903) in which Sherlock Holmes, who should have vanished into the falls of Reichenbach, appears before Watson after three years. It stands at the head of "The Return of Sherlock Holmes," and is famous as one of the great moments of the series, the point at which the author, pressed by his readers, brought the detective back to life. The translator is Mikami Otokichi, the author of "An Actor's Revenge," and it came out in 1929 as a volume of Heibonsha's "Complete Detective Stories of the World." The translation is also a record of how Holmes was read in Japan in the early Shōwa years.

Summary

In the spring of 1894 the Honourable Ronald Adair is shot dead in a room that is a locked room in all but name, and the uncanny case sets all London talking. Watson, who has lost his wife and is long without Holmes, walks about the scene out of professional habit, and there collides with a stooping old man carrying books. When that same old man appears in his consulting room and, before his eyes, straightens up into Holmes himself, Watson faints for the first time in his life. Holmes tells him where he has been for three years. After he sent Professor Moriarty down into the gorge at Reichenbach he survived by slipping aside with his knowledge of a Japanese art of wrestling, and hid himself on the ledge above; the man who then tried to kill him by rolling down rocks was the most dangerous of Moriarty's surviving associates, Colonel Sebastian Moran; and he traveled under changed identities through Tibet, Persia and France, kept hidden with no help but his brother Mycroft's. That night the two of them slip into the empty house across from Baker Street. A wax figure's shadow wavers at the window, and presently a man comes in with soundless steps, assembles an air-gun that makes no noise, and takes aim at that shadow.

Characters

  • Sherlock Holmes　The great detective, back in London after three years away
  • Watson　The narrator, who faints at his old friend's return
  • Colonel Sebastian Moran　The survivor of Moriarty's gang, and a marksman with the air-gun
  • Inspector Lestrade　He is present at the capture in the empty house
  • The Honourable Ronald Adair　The young nobleman shot dead in a room that was a locked room in all but name

The opening (original text)

It was in the spring of the year 1894 that all London was interested, and the fashionable world dismayed, by the murder of the Honourable Ronald Adair under most unusual and inexplicable circumstances. The public has already learned those particulars of the crime which came out in the police investigation; but a good deal was suppressed upon that occasion, since the case for the prosecution was so overwhelmingly strong that it was not necessary to bring forward all the facts. Only now, at the end of nearly ten years, am I allowed to supply those missing links which make up the whole of that remarkable chain.

The Adventure of the Empty House by Mikami Otokichi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Such is my case. Now, does it pass muster?" "Yes, indeed; put like that, it hangs together very well." "Well, all that will be verified or disproved at the trial. In any case, Colonel Moran will trouble us no more; the famous air-gun of Von Herder will embellish the Scotland Yard Museum; and once again Mr. Sherlock Holmes is free to devote his life to examining those interesting little problems which the complex life of London so plentifully presents."
