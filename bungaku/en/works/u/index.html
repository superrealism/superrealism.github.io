About this work

A historical story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Kōdan Kurabu in 1954. Over more than twenty days, Fuse Hanzaburō searches out a pool below a cliff far from any village — for a certain dark purpose. A piece with a deep resonance, hiding a suppressed obsession beneath a quiet scene of angling.

Summary

It took Fuse Hanzaburō more than twenty days to find that pool. There were many fishing spots on the Kanō river — his fellow laborer Danpei had said there were thirty-eight of them — but the pool that met the conditions Hanzaburō was looking for was that one alone. Beneath a cliff some seventy shaku high, there was no way down but by clinging to jutting rocks and tree roots, and the far bank too was a cliff, so that the pool formed a kind of sack, narrowing toward its end — completely cut off from everything else, with no worry of anyone else coming there. "Nothing to complain of. It couldn't be more perfect," Hanzaburō murmurs, nodding with satisfaction. Why did he seek out, so persistently, a pool hidden from every eye? Beneath the quiet scene of angling lay some suppressed, dark obsession. At the close of the story, Hanzaburō's rod bends sharply, and a fish leaps on the surface of the water. Still holding the bent rod, he bows his head low, and murmurs under his breath, "Tadako..." A great splash sounded on the water as the fish leapt. A fine short story of Yamamoto's, saying little yet leaving a deep resonance behind.

The opening (original text)

It took Fuse Hanzaburō more than twenty days to find that pool. There were many fishing spots on the Kanō river. His fellow laborer Danpei said there were thirty-eight of them. Hanzaburō went around and looked over all of them once, but the pool that met the conditions he was looking for was that one alone. ...It lay beneath a cliff of about seventy shaku. There was no way down but by clinging to jutting rocks and tree roots.

U by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

The rod in Hanzaburō's hand bent. It bent further still, and a fish leapt on the surface of the water. Still holding the bent rod, he bowed his head, bowed it low, and murmured under his breath. "Tadako..." A great splash sounded on the water as the fish leapt.
