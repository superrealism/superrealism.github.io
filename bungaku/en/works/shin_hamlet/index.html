About this work

A long work Dazai wrote for publication and issued by Bungei Shunjū in 1941. Borrowing only the names and the situation from Shakespeare's Hamlet, he draws three days of what he calls one unhappy family, in his own reading and almost entirely in dialogue. Around the irresolute prince Hamlet, tormented by his father's death and his mother's remarriage, the psychology of the new king Claudius, his mother Gertrude and his love Ophelia crosses. Following the plot of the tragedy while giving every character the weakness and the excuses of a modern person, Dazai made of it a domestic drama of his own: an experiment in psychology.

Summary

Hamlet, prince of Denmark, deeply wounded and sunk in gloom by his father's sudden death and the swift remarriage of his mother, Gertrude, to his uncle, Claudius. Carrying in his heart the suspicion, voiced by his father's ghost, of "poisoning by his own uncle," Hamlet feigns madness to probe those around him, and presses hard, one after another, his beloved Ophelia, her father the minister Polonius, and even his mother and the new king. But the people of this "New Hamlet" are not the heroic protagonists of the original tragedy; every one of them appears instead as a modern person, full of excuses, weak of will, and self-serving. Amid Hamlet's irresolute suspicions and the crossed purposes of each character, the tragedy of a mere three days in one unhappy household unfolds. The new king Claudius encourages himself, "Be strong," and vows to live and fulfil his fate to the end, while Hamlet closes the curtain declaring, "My suspicion I shall carry with me until the day I die."

Characters

  • Hamlet　An irresolute prince, tormented by his father's death and his mother's remarriage
  • Claudius　Hamlet's uncle. Becomes the new king after his father's death and marries his mother
  • Gertrude　Hamlet's mother. Consort of the new king
  • Ophelia　Hamlet's beloved
  • Polonius　Ophelia's father. A veteran minister

The opening (original text)

There is nothing to say but that I have written this thing. I would only note for the reader that this work is by no means a commentary on Shakespeare's Hamlet, nor is it in any sense a new interpretation of it. It is, after all, nothing more than the author's own self-indulgent game of creation. Borrowing only the characters' names, and the general circumstances of their world, from Shakespeare's Hamlet, I have written of one unhappy household.

The New Hamlet by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

KING: "Be strong, Claudius! Forget love. Forget vanity. Fight for one thing alone, the highest banner of all — the honour of the kingdom of Denmark! Hamlet, there is a man weeping, deep within, more than even you." HAM: "I cannot believe it. My suspicion I shall carry with me until the day I die."
