About this work

A man he has known for years comes to call on a young doctor who has just set up in a back street of a decayed country town. A short story built on the conversation the two hold across a kotatsu on a freezing winter night, copying down the lives and the manners of people living in the provinces. A piece of Nagatsuka's sketch prose, looking evenly at the scene of a town with a half-ruined sake brewery in it, and at the trade of medicine.

Summary

A back street in a run-down country town, lined with a half-collapsed sake brewery and rotting board fences. There, on a freezing winter night, an old acquaintance of the same age calls on a young doctor who has only recently set up practice. In the six-mat room next to the waiting room, the two sit facing each other across a kotatsu, talking. On the transom hangs a photograph of his company from his time in the army and his diploma from medical school, and a lamp quietly lights the room. In their rambling, aimless talk, the life of a man practising medicine in the country, and the human feeling and state of the world in a small country town, gradually seep through. As the night deepens, in a deep silence broken only by the sound of the lamp drawing up its oil and the distant clapper of the night watchman, the two go on talking. A short story, worthy of a master of sketch prose, that captures a country night, without event or incident, plainly, just as it was.

The opening (original text)

It is a certain country town. If one looks in on a certain part of a back street, there is a laundering shop with cloth stretched out in its yard, and just past that yard a field of greens, so that whenever the chickens wandering about stray into the field of greens, one can hear the scolding voice of the farmer chasing them off, hoo-hoo. Come spring, from that field, and from all around it where they had been hiding, the greens lift their yellow heads all at once and stand up on tiptoe, and white peach blossoms bloom along the hedge, and the laundering shop pours water over the short green grass of its yard, scrubbing the stretched cloth with a brush — such is a certain back street of this town. At its corner stands a broken-down sake brewery, sad and forlorn.

Kaigyōi by Nagatsuka Takashi (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

It is nothing but a hushed, dreadfully quiet night. Close to the ear one can faintly hear the sound of the lamp drawing up the oil at the bottom of its wick. The lamp's flame flickered, and the round patch of light on the ceiling, which had been quietly looking down on the two of them until now, wavered and swayed. The clapper of the night watchman was heard from far off, and then, seeming to enter the back street at last, suddenly set up a loud, clattering, fussy racket close by.

Kaigyōi by Nagatsuka Takashi (Aozora Bunko)
