About this work

A short story Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Ōru Yomimono in 1959: the quiet, enigmatic love story of Hiramatsu Shōshirō, a young domain samurai who takes as his wife a woman of unknown origins who has lost her memory entirely. Fusa settles into his house without anyone knowing where she came from, becomes a good wife and bears a child, and her past is never learned. One day she goes through the gate on the bamboo-grass path and vanishes beyond her lost memory. A fine late work drawing loss and the sorrow of waiting, with a faint air of the fantastic about it.

Summary

Hiramatsu Shōshirō, a young samurai serving in his domain's accounting office, is recalled to his home domain and given a new post, after the elder Tahara Gon'emon presses him to confirm he has left nothing unresolved in Edo. One day, a well-dressed young woman wanders, dazed, into Shōshirō's residence. She remembers nothing — not her name, her lineage, nor where she has come from. Unable to turn away this memoryless woman, Fusa, Shōshirō takes her into his household, and in time makes her his wife. Fusa proves a modest, good-natured wife; a daughter, Yuka, is born to them, and peaceful, warm years pass. Yet Fusa's past remains a blank, and from time to time she wears an expression as though gazing at something far away. Then one day she vanishes without a trace. Their daughter Yuka tells him that her mother went off toward the bamboo-grass path, where there was a gate. It seems to Shōshirō that Fusa must have passed through that gate — a gate that could not exist in reality — back into the forgotten world of her past. Surely, he thinks, someday she will remember her present husband and child and come home. Shōshirō quietly calls out to the wife who is no longer there: he will wait until she returns. Behind him, their daughter's bright voice rings out, singing a children's song.

Characters

  • Hiramatsu Shōshirō　A young samurai serving in his domain's accounting office. Takes a memoryless woman as his wife. The protagonist
  • Fusa　A woman who has lost all memory of her name and past. Becomes Shōshirō's wife and bears him a daughter, but eventually vanishes beyond the gate
  • Yuka　Shōshirō and Fusa's daughter
  • Tahara Gon'emon　An elder of the domain who recalls Shōshirō to his home province
  • Yoshizuka Sukejūrō　A fellow samurai and colleague of Shōshirō's

The opening (original text)

Hiramatsu Shōshirō was going over the accounts when a young page from the elders' chamber came and told him that Master Tahara wished to see him. Shōshirō went on studying the ledger as though he had not heard, and the young samurai came closer and repeated the same words. "Me?" Shōshirō turned around. "What is it? Hiramatsu, you say — ah, I see," he said, catching himself with a wry smile. "So it's me, Hiramatsu, is it. Understood — tell him I'll come at once."

Sono kido o tōtte by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Yuka said her mother had gone that way. Fusa must have passed through that gate, he thought, and it seemed to him he could see the gate that did not exist in reality, and his wife standing there. But Fusa would come back, he thought. This time she had a husband, and a child named Yuka; surely she would not fail to remember them. Someday, without fail, she would remember and come home — through that very gate. "Everyone is waiting for you. Come back, Fusa," he murmured to the wife who was not there. "I'll wait until you come back." Behind him he heard Yuka's bright voice, singing a children's song.
