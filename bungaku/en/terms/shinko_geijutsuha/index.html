What is Shinkō Geijutsuha (New Art School)?

Category
Literary Movement

A general term for a group of writers in the early Shōwa era who, in reaction against proletarian literature's rise to the mainstream of the literary establishment and its increasingly strong demand for political and ideological content, sought to defend the value of art independent of politics. The Shinkō Geijutsuha Club, formed in 1930 with Ryūtanji Yū, Funahashi Seiichi, and Ibuse Masuji, among others, serves as its central body, but rather than a coterie with a clearly unified doctrine, it was a loose gathering of diverse writers sharing an anti-Marxist literary stance. Kajii Motojirō, though suffering from pulmonary tuberculosis, left "Lemon" (1925), depicting with a keen sensibility the anxiety and beauty lurking in everyday life, and Makino Shin'ichi stood out for his distinctive style, "Zeron" and other works, that dissolved reality and fantasy together, and both are placed within this lineage. As a unified literary movement it was short-lived, but its individualistic styles, kept at a distance from politics, brought diversity to Shōwa-era literature.
