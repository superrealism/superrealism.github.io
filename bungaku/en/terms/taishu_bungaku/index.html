What is Taishū Bungaku (Popular Fiction)?

Category
Literary Movement

A general term for the literature of the late Taishō era into the Shōwa era that placed weight on entertainment and broad readability for a wide readership, as against serious literature. Nakazato Kaizan's sweeping epic "Daibosatsu Tōge" ("The Great Bodhisattva Pass"), which he began serializing in a newspaper in 1913, is regarded as its forerunner, and it went on to develop into a wide range of genres, including period fiction, detective fiction, and romances of the fantastic. Against the backdrop of the magazine "Shinseinen," Edogawa Ranpo established orthodox detective fiction with "Nisen Dōka" ("The Two-Sen Copper Coin," 1923), and this lineage extends to Yumeno Kyūsaku's fantastical, macabre fiction and Oguri Mushitarō's erudite orthodox mystery "Kokushikan Satsujin Jiken" ("The Case of the Black Death Mansion"). Yoshikawa Eiji, Yamamoto Shūgorō, and Ozaki Shirō mass-produced period and human-interest fiction for newspaper and magazine serialization and won nationwide popularity. Where proletarian literature pursued political subject matter and the New Sensationist school pursued avant-garde technical craft, popular fiction placed the highest value on entertainment and narrative pleasure, and it leads directly into the flourishing of postwar period fiction and detective fiction.
