What is Kōdan Kurabu?

Category
Society / Group

A popular entertainment magazine founded in November 1911 by Noma Seiji's Dai Nihon Yūbenkai (later Kōdansha). It was at first billed as "stenographic kōdan," printing shorthand transcripts of kōdan performances heard at variety halls just as they were told, but it soon shifted to "written kōdan" (also called "new kōdan"), stories composed from the start as reading matter, becoming a major stage for popular fiction centered on period stories. As Kōdansha's flagship magazine, alongside "King," founded in 1925, leading popular fiction writers such as Yoshikawa Eiji, Edogawa Ranpo, and Yamamoto Shūgorō repeatedly wrote for its pages. Ranpo serialized the popular full-length novel "Majutsushi" ("The Magician," 1930), in which his great detective Akechi Kogorō crosses swords with a monstrous villain, and Yamamoto Shūgorō serialized "Goben no Tsubaki" ("Five-Petal Camellia," 1959), depicting a young woman living for revenge. It continued publication, with interruptions and revivals around the time of the defeat, but ceased publication in 1962.
