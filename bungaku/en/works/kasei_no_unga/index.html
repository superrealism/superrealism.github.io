About this work

An unusual piece with no story to speak of, in the manner of a prose poem, published in Shin Seinen in 1926. The narrator, wandering an endless dark forest, finds beside a bottomless marsh that he has himself become the naked body of a beautiful woman, and at the end of a maddened dance and of self-wounding paints, in the blood covering him, the pattern of the canals of Mars. Dream, fantasy and a cruel beauty dissolving into one: a piece compressing Ranpo's aesthete imagination.

Summary

Trembling with a strange, chill fascination — the thought, "here I am again" — "I" wander without direction through the undergrowth of an endless, boundless forest, sealed in a leaden darkness where sound, smell, even touch itself seem to have vanished. Walk as I might, the scenery never changes, and the forest's very infinity presses in on me as a fathomless terror. At last, in the half-light, I arrive at a bottomless black swamp, perhaps a hundred yards across, opening in the heart of the forest. Standing on its bank, I suddenly discover that my own naked body has changed — no longer a man's, but the full, ripe body of a young woman, one that is the very image of my beloved. Standing upon a black rock at the swamp's center, against this unearthly, ashen backdrop, I begin a frenzied dance. But this perfect scene lacks one single thing: a touch of crimson. With sharp fingernails I cut myself again and again, crossing and recrossing my own body with wounds, until I have dyed myself entirely red with the dripping blood — and the figure reflected on the surface of the swamp is nothing other than an eerie, uncanny "canal of Mars." At the height of this mad dance, a voice calling "darling, darling" wakes me — it was all a dream, and before my eyes, close at hand, my beloved's face, monstrously huge, is moving. An unusual, prose-poem-like work by Ranpo in which dream, fantasy, and cruel beauty melt into one.

The opening (original text)

Here I am again, at that place once more — a chill, uncanny fascination made me shudder. A leaden darkness had swallowed my entire world. Sound and smell, and even touch itself, had surely evaporated from my body, until nothing remained around me but a thick, stagnant color, dense as a bar of steamed bean-jelly. Overhead, like a bank of thunderclouds, dark layered leaves lay silently settled, and from among them huge trunks of blackish-brown wood came pouring down to the earth like waterfalls, ranked on every side as far as the eye could see, like soldiers standing at review, their ends vanishing at last into a darkness of unknown depth.

Kasei no unga by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Darling. What is it — you're having a nightmare." Opening my eyes in a daze, I found my beloved's face, monstrously huge, moving close before my own. "I had a dream," I murmured absently, gazing at her face. "Why, you're soaked through with sweat. ...Was it a frightening dream?" "It was a frightening dream."

Kasei no unga by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)
