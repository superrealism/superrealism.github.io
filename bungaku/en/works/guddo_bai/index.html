About this work

An unfinished serial novel published in Asahi Hyōron in 1948, and the last thing Dazai wrote. Tajima, an editor with a wife and children, has taken up with a great many women in the confusion after the war, and resolves to turn over a new leaf and break with all of them. His scheme is to dress up the extraordinarily beautiful and very large Nagai Kinuko as a stand-in wife, walk her round in front of the women, and open the subject of parting. An unusual comedy of light dialogue and farcical premise, broken off in the middle of its thirteenth instalment by the author's death. A phantom last work, showing an unexpectedly bright gift for laughter.

Summary

On his way home from the funeral of a grand old man of letters, Tajima Shūji, a magazine editor, realizes that in the confusion of the postwar years he has somehow got himself entangled with nearly ten women. Resolving to go back to his wife and children, Tajima means to say "good-bye" to every one of them; but to talk of parting face to face is dangerous in the extreme. So he hits upon a stratagem: he will take along, as his "wife," Nagai Kinuko, a big woman of surpassing beauty who is also enormously strong, show the others that he has so beautiful a wife whom he adores, and make them give him up. Hiring at an outrageous fee this Kinuko, who used to do rough work not far from black-marketeering, Tajima plays the hastily assembled false husband and goes round the women's houses one after another. But Kinuko's gluttony and brazenness, so out of keeping with her matchless beauty, throw him about, and the plan keeps rolling off in unforeseen directions. The story sets off briskly as a comedy whose light dialogue is genuinely funny — and then, at the beginning of the thirteenth installment, it was cut off forever, unfinished, by the author's suicide by drowning.

Characters

  • Tajima Shūji　A magazine editor who tries to break off with his women
  • Nagai Kinuko　The beautiful, big woman who plays his false wife

The opening (original text)

A certain grand old man of letters had died, and about the end of his funeral it began to rain. An early spring rain. On the way back, two men are walking under one umbrella. Both had come only for form's sake to the funeral of the departed old master, and their subject is women, and highly improper. The big, middle-aged man in a crested formal coat is a writer. The much younger, good-looking man in the round Lloyd spectacles and striped trousers is an editor.

The ending (original text)

"All right? I dare say it will be nothing, but there's one violent fellow there, and if he raises his arm, would you just hold him down lightly, like this. Oh, he seems to be a weak sort of fellow." He had come, very noticeably, to speak to Kinuko in polite language. (Unfinished)
