What is Wakakusa?

Category
Society / Group

A literary magazine founded by the publisher Hōbunkan in October 1925. It was born as a sister magazine to the company's girls' magazine "Reijokai," in response to readers' requests that its contributors' section be expanded. Its cover illustrations were by Takehisa Yumeji, and its literary section printing readers' submissions became popular, winning a wide readership centered on young women but including male contributors as well. Alongside readers' work, its pages also carried fiction and essays by established writers. It had particularly close ties with Dazai Osamu: "Tōrō" ("The Lantern"), published in the October 1937 issue, is regarded as the earliest example of Dazai's "female monologue style," written in a woman's first-person voice. Dazai went on to contribute to the magazine in 1939 "Hazakura to Mateki," in which an elder sister recalls her younger sister's secret, the short piece "I Can Speak," born of his days in Kōfu, and "A, Aki," framed as notes a poet has jotted down about autumn, and in 1940 he serialized "Kojiki Gakusei," which depicts as a daydream an odd friendship with boys he meets by the Tamagawa Aqueduct. It was suspended in wartime in 1944 and revived after the war, continuing until 1950, when it ceased publication.
