What is Jiyūritsu Haiku (Free-Verse Haiku)?

Category
Form / Genre

Haiku that, unbound by the conventions of the 5-7-5 fixed form or the seasonal word, seeks to capture emotion directly in a free rhythm true to the breath of feeling itself. Its source lies in Kawahigashi Hekigotō's "new tendency" haiku movement, which grew out of Masaoka Shiki's line of sketch-based composition, and Ogiwara Seisensui, founding the haiku magazine "Sōun" in 1911 as its base, advocated and established free verse that discarded both seasonal words and fixed form. From among Seisensui's disciples at "Sōun" came two representative haiku poets who led starkly contrasting lives. Ozaki Hōsai, who threw away his elite career and his family to die alone as a temple caretaker, carved solitude into verses such as "Even coughing, alone," while Taneda Santōka, who lived a wandering, mendicant life, left verses such as "Pushing on and on, still the green mountains," gathering his life's work into the collection "Sōmokutō" (1940). Their style, distilling the felt reality of life into plain words, continues to hold many readers even today.
