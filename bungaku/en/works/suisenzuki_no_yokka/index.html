About this work

A children's story by Miyazawa Kenji, drawing a day of blizzard through the eyes of those who govern the snow — the snow boy, the snow wolves and the old snow woman. The snow boy secretly tries to protect a child hurrying home with his mind on burnt sugar. The cold, beautiful scene of the snow and the gentleness lodged in it make one of the clearest crystals among Kenji's children's stories.

Summary

The old snow-woman had gone off somewhere far away. A child wrapped in a red blanket, hurrying home along the foot of a snow-covered hill shaped like an elephant's head, was thinking hard about how to make caramel candy. Up on that hill walked the snow boy, wearing a triangular cap of white bear fur, leading two snow wolves. The snow boy remembered that this same child had once looked up at the mistletoe growing on a tree branch, and so, in secret, he broke off a branch and threw it down to him. But just then the old snow-woman came back from far across the sky. "Now then — let it fall, throw it down, let it fall!" A furious blizzard began, and the snow boy and the snow wolves, obeying her commands, made the snow fall and whipped the wind into a howling storm. In no time the child was caught in the blizzard and collapsed. The old snow-woman ordered, "Bury that child in it." The snow boy could not disobey, but he laid his own blanket over the child and whispered, "Lie still. Don't move." He had the snow wolves heap up snow to cover the child completely, so as to keep him from freezing. When dawn broke and the old snow-woman had gone, the hill and the fields alike lay buried under fresh snow. The snow boy ran with his wolves to the spot where the child lay buried and kicked the snow away, scattering it. Seeing the child's father hurrying up from the village on snowshoes, the snow boy dashed up the hill and, raising a single plume of blown snow, cried out: "Your father's come! Open your eyes now."

Characters

  • The snow boy　One who makes the snow fall. Secretly protects the child
  • The snow wolves　Two wolves who serve the snow boy
  • The old snow-woman　An old woman who governs the raging blizzard
  • The child　Hurries home wrapped in a red blanket

The opening (original text)

The old snow-woman had gone off somewhere far away. With her cat-like ears and her fuzzy, ash-gray hair, the old snow-woman had crossed beyond the curled, glittering clouds of the western mountains, and gone off somewhere far away. A single child, wrapped in a red blanket, thinking hard and constantly about caramel candy, was hurrying anxiously homeward along the foot of a snow-covered hill shaped like the head of a great elephant.

Suisenzuki no yokka by Miyazawa Kenji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"That's enough now." The snow boy cried out when he saw the edge of the child's red blanket flash into view from beneath the snow. "Your father's come! Open your eyes now." The snow child dashed up the hill behind and cried out, raising a single plume of blown snow. The child seemed to stir, just faintly. And the man in furs came running with all his might.
