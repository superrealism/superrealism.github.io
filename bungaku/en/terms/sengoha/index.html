What is Sengoha (the Postwar School)?

Category
Literary Movement

A general term for the writers who emerged from the defeat in World War II into around 1950, carrying the experience of war and revolution. Their base was the magazine "Kindai Bungaku," founded in January 1946, whose seven coterie members — Honda Shūgo, Hirano Ken, Ara Masahito, Haniya Yutaka, Odagiri Hideo, Sasaki Kiichi, and Yamamuro Shizuka — argued for reexamining the relationship between politics and literature. Noma Hiroshi, Shiina Rinzō, Takeda Taijun, Umezaki Haruo, and Haniya Yutaka are called the first-generation Sengoha, and Ōoka Shōhei, Mishima Yukio, and Abe Kōbō, who emerged somewhat later, the second-generation Sengoha. What they share is their depiction, in a weighty style steeped in existentialism and contemporary Western literature, of human existence pushed to its extreme limits, on the battlefield or in the prison camp. Noma Hiroshi's "Kurai E" ("Dark Pictures," 1946), drawing on his own experience of war and imprisonment, and Shiina Rinzō's "Shin'ya no Shuen" ("Midnight Banquet," 1947), published in "Tenbō," are known as the works that announced the arrival of this school. Following these writers, who broke through the confines of the established I-novel, came the "Third Generation of New Writers" — Yasuoka Shōtarō, Yoshiyuki Junnosuke, Endō Shūsaku, and others — who looked closely at the weakness within everyday life, and together they shaped the currents of postwar literature.
