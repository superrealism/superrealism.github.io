About this work

A short story by Yamakawa Masao, known for "Summer Funeral." A woman receiving a young man in a hotel room, and their parting. A piece cutting sharply into the instant when a relationship ends, in dry dialogue and vivid scene-setting.

Summary

The door opens, and in comes a young man, dressed tightly in a fashionable suit, his face hidden behind sunglasses. She, rising from the bed, greets him: "Welcome, boy." A fair, feminine face, heavy eyebrows — on the young man's face floats a smile that is unmistakably artificial, unnatural. A dry exchange of words in a hotel room. Is what lies between the two of them love, or a transaction, or simply inertia? And then, at last, the moment when a certain relationship comes to its "end." In the room the young man has left, she pours the last of the cognac into a glass, drinks it down in one breath, and sinks deep into the sofa, like someone who has finished living out every last measure of life. Even as the telephone goes on ringing, she does not so much as stir. In the room, growing dim as the sun goes down, her calm, sleep-like expression is at last moved by a small yawn. A sharp short story of Yamakawa Masao's, cutting out the end of a love affair in dry prose and vivid scene-setting.

The opening (original text)

The door opened, and a young man came in. He was dressed tightly in a fashionable suit of silky fabric, his face hidden behind dark green sunglasses. Just as he was, he quickly glanced around the room. She rose slowly from the bed. "Welcome, boy."

Ai no owari by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

She poured the last of the cognac into her glass. Tipping back her throat, she drank it down in one breath, then sank deep into the sofa, as though she were someone who had finished living out every last measure of life, and closed her eyes. After a while, the telephone began to ring. But she did not so much as stir. In the room, growing dim as the sun went down, her calm, sleep-like expression at last moved. It was a small yawn.
