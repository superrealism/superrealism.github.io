What is Torimonochō (Detective Casebook)?

Category
Form / Genre

A form of period detective fiction set in Edo, in which an informant or a low-ranking constable solves the mystery of a crime. The name derives from the ledgers in which Edo-period magistrate's offices recorded the criminals to be apprehended and their charges. Its first work is Okamoto Kidō's "Hanshichi Torimonochō: Obun no Tamashii," published in "Bungei Kurabu" in 1917; Kidō called his protagonist, the aging informant Hanshichi, "a hidden Sherlock Holmes of the Edo period," and framed the stories as tales the old man tells of his investigations in his youth, weaving in the manners and human feeling of Edo. "Zenigata Heiji Torimonokae," which Nomura Kodō began writing from the inaugural issue of "Ōru Yomimono" in 1931, made the coin-throwing master Heiji its hero and won nationwide popularity, continuing until 1957 and running to 383 episodes in all. Many other popular series were born as well, including Sasaki Mitsuzō's "Umon Torimonochō," Yokomizo Seishi's "Ningyō Sashichi Torimonochō," and Hisao Jūran's "Agojūrō Torimonochō." As a distinctively Japanese form drawing on the blood of both detective fiction and period fiction, it became a great pillar of popular fiction, and has long been beloved as material for film, theater, and television as well.
