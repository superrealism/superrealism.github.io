About this work

"I want to become an immortal, so please place me somewhere I can learn how" — the story of Gonsuke, a cook's servant who turns up at an employment agency in Osaka saying so. A bracing piece by Akutagawa, like a rakugo, in which Gonsuke, neatly worked for twenty years without pay by a doctor and his wife, shows at the last what he can do. A short piece written for children, with a bright flavour.

Summary

Long ago, a man came to Osaka seeking service. His name is not recorded; since he had come only to cook rice, he is known to us only as Gonsuke. Arriving at an employment agency, Gonsuke asked the head clerk to place him somewhere he could become an immortal. The astonished clerk tried to turn him away, but Gonsuke pointed at the shop's curtain, which read "Agency for All Manner of Positions," and pressed him: if it truly says "all manner," then surely that includes this — or is the sign a lie? At a loss, the clerk consulted the wife of a doctor he knew. The greedy wife suggested a scheme: tell him to work twenty years without pay, and promise to teach him the art of becoming an immortal in return. And so Gonsuke worked in the doctor's household for twenty years, drawing no wage at all. When the term was up and Gonsuke pressed them, as promised, to teach him the art, the doctor and his wife were at a loss, and the wife hastily ordered him to climb the pine tree in the garden. Made to climb to a high branch, he was told to let go with his right hand, then his left — if he fell, the ground below was stone, and there would be no surviving it. This, they calculated, would surely make him give up. But Gonsuke did exactly as he was told and let go with both hands. And his body, instead of falling, came to a halt in midair in broad daylight, standing there as neatly as a puppet on strings. "I'm ever so much obliged to you. Thanks to you, I've become a full-fledged immortal myself" — Gonsuke made a polite bow, and treading quietly upon the blue sky, rose little by little into the high clouds and vanished. What became of the doctor and his wife after that, nobody knows.

The opening (original text)

Ladies and gentlemen. I am in Osaka just now, so let me tell you a tale of Osaka. Long ago, a man came up to the town of Osaka to enter service. No one knows what his name was. Since he had come only to serve as a rice-cook, he has come down to us only as Gonsuke. Gonsuke passed beneath the curtain of an employment agency and made his request to the head clerk, who sat there with a pipe in his mouth: "Mr. Clerk. I want to become an immortal, so please place me somewhere I can do that."

Sennin by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"I'm ever so much obliged to you. Thanks to you, I've become a full-fledged immortal myself." Gonsuke made a polite, courteous bow, and then, treading quietly upon the blue sky, rose little by little into the high clouds and was gone. What became of the doctor and his wife after that, nobody knows.
