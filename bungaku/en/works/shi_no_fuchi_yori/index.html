About this work

A collection of poems Takami Jun, known as a novelist, went on writing through surgery for oesophageal cancer and the illness that followed. Published in 1964. Writing that he cannot sustain the energy and cannot yet write fiction, he made these from notes taken in a notebook at his bedside and worked up after he was discharged. The fear and resignation of a person facing death, a thirst for life, and love for nature and for days gone by are sung straight, in plain unadorned words. It opened new ground for him as a poet late in life, and is widely read as a classic of poetry written in illness.

Summary

A collection of poems written during Takami Jun's convalescence after surgery for esophageal cancer. According to the author's own preface, the surgery was a heavy one lasting nearly three hours, and afterward he lacked the sustained energy to write novels. Writing two or three lines, then pausing for days, sometimes weeks or months, before taking it up again — in this way, wringing out what little strength he had, the poems were born. What is recorded in them is the naked heart of a person facing death: raw images of death, ominous crows flocking at the hospital window, the memory of ants swarming on a dead man's eyes. A poignant self-projection onto a single aged, scarred starfish, longing to be granted "a muddy death." Fear and resignation, and yet an unquenched clinging to life, a love for days gone by and for nature — such feelings are set down in plain, direct words stripped of all artifice. "I thought to write a single poem titled 'From the Edge of Death,' but I could not," the author writes — and yet, in place of that one poem he could never write, the whole of this volume crystallizes the poems wrung out from the very edge of death itself. It is the earnest world of poetry that a novelist who had already won his fame arrived at only in his final years.

The opening (original text)

My surgery for esophageal cancer took place on October ninth of last year, so it has already been eight months. Everything I have managed to write in these eight months is here, in full. I still cannot write novels. My energy will not hold up long enough for that. Poems, though, I can write — though to say so might make poetry sound the easier thing, in truth a poem demands more energy than that. But I am grateful, at least, that it need not be sustained for long. I thought to write a single poem titled "From the Edge of Death," but I could not manage it. Had I managed it, I meant to make it the title for the whole collection of poems. That I could not do — and yet I have kept it as the title for the collection as a whole. The Dead Man's Nails Upon the cold brick ivy creeps At the bottom of night time piles up, heavy the dead man's nails grow long

The ending (original text)

The Old Starfish That jewel-like stars once shone in the night sky — that too is a thing of the past Now stars like white lice cling to a filthy sky The dignity of the sea's own star, too, is a thing of the past Do not send me back to the sea Give this old starfish a muddy death
