About this work

A short story Tanizaki serialized in the Tokyo Asahi Shimbun in 1914. Okamura, the narrator's friend since boyhood, pours an immense fortune into nothing but his own art — the beauty of the body and the gorgeous world of beauty that adorns it. Through the life of Okamura, who carries art for art's sake to its limit and at last paints his own body gold and dies a golden death, the aesthete Tanizaki's view of art is set out vividly: an unusual early work.

Summary

Okamura, my friend since boyhood, was a boy who, in his childhood, showed talent in every field, much as I did myself. But as we grew older, our views of art diverged sharply. While I set out on the path of literature, Okamura, who had inherited a vast fortune, came to devote everything he had to the beauty of his own body, and to realizing the gorgeous world of beauty that surrounded it. To him, the supreme art was neither painting, nor sculpture, nor literature, but nothing other than the beauty of the living human body itself. Deep in the mountains, far from any town, Okamura built a fantastical paradise of art adorned with every conceivable beauty, and there brought into being a living scroll of art, peopled with the naked bodies of beautiful men and women. And as the ultimate completion of it all, he painted his own body gold from head to foot, and met his "golden death." "I" say of Okamura, who gave his entire strength and his whole body to art and died amid ample success, that he was a happy man. His art appeared like a phantom and vanished from the earth together with his death, and yet he was, without doubt, a great genius, an artist unmatched in his age. —— An early and singular work in which Tanizaki, the aesthete, gives vivid expression to the doctrine of art for art's sake and rapture in physical beauty.

Characters

  • Okamura　My friend since boyhood. An advocate of art for art's sake who pours his vast fortune into building a paradise of physical beauty and gorgeous art, and meets his "golden death" after painting his own body gold
  • I　Okamura's friend. Walks the path of literature, and tells the tale of Okamura's strange art and life

The opening (original text)

Okamura was my friend from the days of my boyhood. In the early part of April of the year I turned seven, when I had just begun attending an elementary school not far from our house in Shinkawa, Okamura, too, was brought there by a maidservant in attendance on him. Our seats in the classroom happened to fall side by side, and the two of us always kept our small desks pressed close together. And it was not only that — in any number of ways, Okamura and I seemed to resemble each other closely.

Konjiki no shi by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Okamura was, without doubt, a happy man. And why do I say so? Because he gave the whole of his strength, the whole of his body, to his own art, and achieved, in full measure, success at it. His art vanished from the earth in an instant, like a phantom, and yet that he was a great genius, an artist unmatched in his age, is a fact that, perhaps, I alone now know.

Konjiki no shi by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)
