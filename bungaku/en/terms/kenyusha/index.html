What is Ken'yūsha?

Category
Society / Group

Japan's first literary society in the history of modern literature, formed in 1885 by Ozaki Kōyō, Yamada Bimyō, Ishibashi Shian, Maruoka Kyūka, and other students at the preparatory school for Tokyo University. It launched what is regarded as Japan's first coterie magazine as its organ, "Garakuta Bunko"; at first it was a circulated, non-commercial booklet, but it was later published commercially and read widely by young literature lovers. Specializing in fiction of manners that carried forward the tradition of Edo gesaku while incorporating realism, the society grew, on the strength of Kōyō's breakthrough work "Ninin Bikuni Iro Zange" ("Two Nuns' Confessions of Passion"), into a dominant force in the literary world, and in the 1890s it brought about, alongside Kōda Rohan, the age known as the "Kōro era." Iwaya Sazanami, Kawakami Bizan, and Hirotsu Ryūrō joined as well, and Kōyō's own disciples — Izumi Kyōka, Tokuda Shūsei, and Oguri Fūyō, among others — grew into the writers who would carry the next generation, forming a lineage known as the "Ken'yūsha school." It lost its cohesive force with Kōyō's death in 1903 and dissolved naturally, but its role in shaping the literary establishment as a group of professional writers was considerable, and its popular character later became a target of criticism from the rising Naturalist movement.
