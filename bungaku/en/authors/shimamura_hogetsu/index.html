Biography

Born in 1871 in Ogun Village, Nakaka District, Shimane Prefecture (now Hamada); his real name was Sasayama Takitarō. Raised in poverty, he fought his way through hardship on his own from his mid-teens and studied under Tsubouchi Shōyō at Tokyo Semmon Gakkō (now Waseda University). After graduating he served as a lecturer and professor at his alma mater, and, after studying in Britain and Germany, revived and edited "Waseda Bungaku." Through essays such as "Torawaretaru Bungei" ("Captive Literature") and "Bungeijō no Shizenshugi" ("Naturalism in Literature"), he became the theoretical pillar of Japanese Naturalist literature and exerted great influence on the literary world of the late Meiji era. In 1906 he founded the Bungei Kyōkai theater group with Shōyō and drove forward the shingeki (new theater) movement, translating and staging Ibsen's "A Doll's House." His romance with the actress Matsui Sumako, who played Nora, became a public scandal and he left the association, and in 1913 he formed the Geijutsu-za theater company with Sumako. Their staged adaptation of Tolstoy's "Resurrection" became a nationwide sensation, together with its theme song "Katyusha's Song," and brought shingeki to popular audiences for the first time. He died suddenly in 1918, aged 47, from acute pneumonia following influenza (the Spanish flu). The following year Sumako too took her own life, following him.
