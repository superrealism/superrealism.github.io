About this work

A short piece Akutagawa wrote about the red necktie Tanizaki Jun'ichirō was wearing on an early-summer afternoon when the two went out to browse in Kanda. Akutagawa says the passers-by stare at Tanizaki's face because of the necktie; Tanizaki insists it is because of Akutagawa's kimono coat. When a word from a café waitress settles it, Akutagawa does not begrudge a fifty-sen tip — the difference in the two writers' temperaments caught vividly in very little space.

Summary

One early-summer afternoon I went out with Mr. Tanizaki to look about Kanda. Mr. Tanizaki wore, that day too, a black suit with a red necktie. I felt a romanticism symbolized in this magnificent necktie; and it seems that I was not the only one, for not one person we passed in the street, man or woman, failed to stare at Mr. Tanizaki's face. But Mr. Tanizaki will not admit the fact. They are looking at you, he says, because you are wearing that michiyuki coat. It is true that instead of a summer overcoat I had borrowed my father's michiyuki. But a michiyuki is a thing worn by teachers of the tea ceremony and by the abbot of the family temple, and in the matter of astonishing the eyes of the vulgar it can be no match for an extraordinary necktie like a single red rose. But since Mr. Tanizaki is a poet, who does not honor logic as I do, I did not try to force this truth upon him. ... Presently the two of us sat down in a café in back Jinbōchō. Even while waiting for the soda water I went on gazing at the beacon of romanticism burning at Mr. Tanizaki's throat. And then a waitress with her powder worn off came up with the glasses in both hands, and leaving one hand on the table, peered long and hard at Mr. Tanizaki's breast.

Characters

  • I (Akutagawa)　Walking about Kanda in his father's borrowed coat
  • Mr. Tanizaki (Tanizaki Jun'ichirō)　The senior writer in a black suit with a red necktie
  • The waitress　Who brings the soda water at a café in back Jinbōchō

The opening (original text)

One early-summer afternoon I went out with Mr. Tanizaki to look about Kanda. Mr. Tanizaki wore, that day too, a black suit with a red necktie. I felt a romanticism symbolized in this magnificent necktie. Nor, indeed, was it only I. The people in the street too, men and women alike, must have received the same impression as I did. Not one of them, as we passed, failed to stare at Mr. Tanizaki's face.

Tanizaki jun'ichirō shi by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Unabashed by my senior's cold smile, I handed the crumpled note to the waitress. She had not merely brought us the soda water. For me she had also lifted up to the world the truth concerning a red necktie. Never yet have I given a tip of fifty sen with such sincerity in it as at that time.

Tanizaki jun'ichirō shi by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)
