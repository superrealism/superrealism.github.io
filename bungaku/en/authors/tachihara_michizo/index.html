Biography

Born in 1914 into a merchant family in Nihonbashi, Tokyo. He studied architecture in the Faculty of Engineering at Tokyo Imperial University, and was a brilliant young architect who won the Tatsuno Kingo Prize three times, while also being a poet discovered by Hori Tatsuo. Known as the writer whose Japanese most beautifully rendered the sonnet form, he self-published elegant collections of poetry set against the highlands of Shinano Oiwake — "Wasuregusa ni Yosu" ("Dedicated to the Forget-Me-Not") and "Akatsuki to Yūbe no Uta" ("Songs of Dawn and Evening"). Poems such as "Hajimete no Mono ni" ("To the First Thing") and "Nochi no Omoi ni" ("In Later Thought") continue to be read by young readers today. He died young of tuberculosis in 1939 at the age of 24.
