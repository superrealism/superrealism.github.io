About this work

Murō Saisei's first collection, published in the same year as Lyrical Pieces, the book of "home is a place to think of from afar": a collection of love and prayer in colloquial free verse. Dedicated to his dead father, it opens from a single Bible he had carried in his breast. The poems, accumulated in poverty and solitude, sing in plain words of love for people and of the faith that held him up.

Summary

After the dedication "to my late father," Saisei speaks of a soiled Bible that lies in his room — a NEW TESTAMENT bound in heavy chintz with sheepskin on the spine. In the dark years when not one of his poems appeared in the world, fighting cold and hunger and illness, he carried this book in his breast as he suffered and walked about. When he takes it up now, he shakes with long admiration and sighing and with the memory of his victory over himself. From there the collection begins. In "Memories of Childhood" he recalls the days of a boy who climbed the hills and lay down among the flowers, and the days of trudging on and on toward the sea. "A True Account of a Filial Son" draws one who carries his father on his back, and other pieces sing of those who work and suffer in a corner of the city and still try to love their fellows. Words of Dostoevsky, whom Saisei read and reread, are quoted throughout, and in the prose at the end his feeling for Nelly of The Insulted and Injured is spoken of with great earnestness. That man's long struggle, his endurance, his poverty, his humiliation, and his greatness like a mountain — thinking of these, one can truly live; there is joy in it. And the collection closes with the prayer: "God, let me too be added to this beautiful world."

The opening (original text)

To my late father Now you must be in heaven; your beautiful smile is reflected in me now, and my brows lift toward the sky.... In my room there is a soiled Bible. I made a cover for it of the heavy chintz used for chairs, and put sheepskin on the spine, and wrote NEW TESTAMENT on it, and I have had it a long while. I have had it more than ten years. Among all the many beautiful books in my room, it is the oldest and the most soiled.

Ai no shishū by Murō Saisei (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Thinking of these things, one can truly live. There is joy in it. God, let me too be added to this beautiful world. Show me too that I ought always to be pure and true in my heart, as he was.

Ai no shishū by Murō Saisei (Aozora Bunko)
