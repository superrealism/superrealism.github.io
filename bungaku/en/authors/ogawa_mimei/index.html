Biography

Born in 1882 in Takada, Niigata Prefecture (now Jōetsu); his real name was Kensaku. He studied under Tsubouchi Shōyō and Shimamura Hōgetsu at Waseda University, who gave him the pen name "Mimei." He left many children's stories steeped in lyricism and symbolism, such as "Akai Rōsoku to Ningyo" ("The Red Candle and the Mermaid") and "Nobara" ("Wild Roses"), and, as a pioneer of Japanese children's literature, is known as "the father of children's literature." Early in his career he also wrote Romantic fiction, but he later devoted himself entirely to children's stories, infusing his work with a warm regard for the weak and the poor and a quiet critique of society. He died in 1961.
