About this work

The Tenichibō affair that troubled the age of Tokugawa Yoshimune — what would happen if that grand imposture were done again, in miniature, in a back tenement in Edo? Cast as a storyteller's performance and proceeding by sittings — the first, the second and so on — this is an unusual comedy by Yamamoto Shūgorō. It laughs down how weak people are before titles and rumour, and rings down the curtain briskly with a single word of farewell.

Summary

In the time of Yoshimune, the eighth Tokugawa shogun, there was a commotion called the Tenichibō affair. As to the truth of it the accounts differ, and there are notices that would be over in four or five lines and enormous chronicles the size of this book — so the storyteller prefaces, and then enters upon his subject, "The Tenichibō of the Tenement." The scene is a back tenement in Edo. Gosuke, the agent of the tenement, is a man of half-knowledge who thinks himself versed in painting and in swords, and abuses the tenants as "firewood in human skin." And there appears among them one who hints at a birth suggesting a shogun's by-blow. ... Rumor breeds rumor and the whole tenement is carried away. Gosuke buys timber for the building of a mansion and borrows money on every side, and those about him are in high spirits — "after all, it's the genuine article and no mistake." Ginta and Kinta go round the whole tenement carrying the good news. But the greater the fuss, the nearer the day when the mask must come off. The performer announces the end: hearing of the disturbance, Ginta and Kinta made themselves scarce first of all; in the house of the Nawaya, O-Waki was, to speak plainly, deserted by her sixth husband; and Gosuke, to pay for the timber and the seventy ryō owed to the mansion of the Lord of Iga, sold the tenement together with his own shop and dwelling, and one night, under cover of darkness, the three of them fled, no one knows where. The performer wishes to add nothing further. Farewell.

Characters

  • Gosuke　The agent of the tenement, a man of half-knowledge who lives for appearances
  • O-Waki　The daughter of the Nawaya, deserted by her sixth husband
  • Ginta and Kinta　The men who make themselves scarce first when the fuss begins

The opening (original text)

First session: that Tenichibō was a great criminal, and that the accounts of him make a stir among the people In the time of Yoshimune, the eighth Tokugawa shogun, there was a commotion called the Tenichibō affair. As to the truth of it the accounts differ: there are notices which, if given here, would be over in four or five lines, and there are enormous chronicles the size of this volume.

The ending (original text)

The end of the great affair of the Tenichibō of the tenement is as above, and the performer wishes to add nothing further. Farewell.
