About this work

A short story Dazai published in Bungei in 1945. As its subtitle — a new setting of the Liaozhai zhiyi — indicates, it is a fantastical story drawn from one tale in that Chinese collection of the strange. Yorong, a poor and unlucky scholar despised by his wife and his relations alike, is turned into a crow by the power of the god of the shrine of the king of Wu, is joined to the beautiful female crow Zhuqing, and lives another life. Going back and forth between dream and waking, this world and the other, it asks what real happiness is. A fine piece full of Dazai's feeling and humour, though drawn from a foreign classic.

Summary

Yurong, a poor scholar of Hunan, lost both his parents early and was raised passed around from one relative to another, and even the wife his uncle forced on him — an ugly, ill-natured woman — looks down on his learning without a second thought. Resolving once and for all to sit the provincial examination, he fails miserably, and on his way home, sunk in despair, collapses from hunger in the corridor of the Shrine of the King of Wu on the shore of Lake Dongting, murmuring, "How lucky crows are, to know nothing of rich and poor," before falling asleep. Then the god of the Shrine of the King of Wu appears, gives Yurong a black feathered robe, and he becomes a crow. Among a flock of crows, Yurong meets a beautiful female crow named Zhuqing, and the two become husband and wife, living together in harmony. In this other life spent as a crow, Yurong comes to know a peace and a love he could never find in the human world. After passing back and forth between dream and waking, Yurong, returned once more to human form, no longer speaks even a word of his once-proud "way of the gentleman," and lives on quietly instead as an ordinary field labourer.

Characters

  • Yurong　An unlucky, poor scholar. Turns into a crow and is joined to Zhuqing
  • Zhuqing　The beautiful female crow Yurong meets in the world of crows
  • Wife　Yurong's ugly, unfeeling wife, who despises him and his learning

The opening (original text)

Long ago, in some county town or other of Hunan, there lived a poor scholar named Yurong. For some reason, it seems to have been settled from ancient times that a scholar must be poor. Yurong himself, though, was of no mean birth or upbringing, clear-featured and handsome, with an air of refinement about him as well; though one could not quite say he loved his books the way some men love women, still, from earliest childhood he had applied himself earnestly and single-mindedly to learning, and had never once behaved in a way that strayed from the proper path. And yet, for some reason, fortune never once smiled on him.

Chikusei by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

For the rest of his life he told no one, and never again spoke, even once, of his once-proud "way of the gentleman,"; he simply went on, silently as before, with the same poor day-to-day existence, still held in no particular esteem by his relatives, and yet showed no sign of minding it in the least, and was buried in the dust of the world as a wholly ordinary field labourer.
