What is Puroretaria Bungaku (Proletarian Literature)?

Category
Literary Movement

A literary movement of the late Taishō era into the early Shōwa era, depicting the harsh reality of the working and farming classes and the movement to liberate them. It began with the magazine "Tanemaku Hito" ("The Sower"), founded in 1921, and its organization advanced thereafter on the pages of "Bungei Sensen" and "Senki," among others. Kobayashi Takiji left a representative work of the movement in "Kanikōsen" ("The Crab Cannery Ship," 1929), depicting a mass of laborers driven to exhaustion in the crab-canning fishery, but in 1933 he was murdered while under interrogation, held under the Peace Preservation Law. Miyamoto Yuriko, with "Mazushiki Hitobito no Mure" ("A Flock of Poor People"), depicting a woman awakening to her own self, holds a pioneering place in this current, and Tsuboi Sakae is another writer who continued active after the war as well. In contrast to the New Sensationist and Shinkō Geijutsuha schools of the same period, which sought artistic autonomy and sensory expression, it is distinguished by foregrounding the political and ideological character of literature, but as an organized movement it was rapidly crushed by suppression around 1933.
