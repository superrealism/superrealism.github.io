About this work

A novel written to order and published by Kinjō Shuppansha in 1942. In the fresh form of a diary, the sensitive boy Serikawa Susumu records his days from leaving middle school to setting his heart on acting. Suggested by the real diary of a boy Dazai knew, it draws — through study, faith, a longing for art and the give and take of family life — a young man burning with ideals, troubled, and growing, in bright humour and poetry. Dazai's central work of youth, asking what real study is.

Summary

Serikawa Susumu is a keenly sensitive boy of sixteen. With his graduation from middle school ahead of him, he cannot decide whether to go on to higher school or to take the actor's road he has long dreamed of. In a frank diary he sets down the events of each day, the books he reads, his exchanges with friends and teachers, his feelings about his family, and his prayers to God. He rebels against the emptiness of school lessons, asks what "real study" would be, and longs after his dream of art. In time he takes the examination for the students' company of a theater and makes his first step as an actor. Through hard rehearsals and a poor life, supported by his dependable elder brother and by his family, and swaying between the ideal and the real, Susumu goes his own way single-mindedly. "If one gives up putting on airs, life seems, unexpectedly, to be flat country" — and with his eighteenth year almost upon him, humming a hymn, he sets a bright resolve in his breast.

The opening (original text)

April 16. Friday. A tremendous wind. The spring in Tokyo is disagreeable, with these dry gales. The dust invades even the inside of the room; the desktop is gritty and my cheeks are covered with it — a nasty feeling.

Seigi to bishō by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

If one gives up putting on airs, life seems, unexpectedly, to be flat country. Let me build a small house upon the rock. At the New Year I mean to go first of all to pay my respects to Mr. Saitō. This time I have a feeling he will see me. Next year I shall be eighteen.

Seigi to bishō by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

Famous passages

Do justice with a smile!

The motto Serikawa Susumu sets down as he begins his diary. Around this single phrase the story vividly shows both a boy's earnest ambition and the shyness with which he immediately makes fun of it himself.
