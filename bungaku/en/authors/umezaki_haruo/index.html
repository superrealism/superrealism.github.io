Biography

Born in Fukuoka City in 1915. After graduating in Japanese literature from Tokyo Imperial University he was called up, and was awaiting the decisive battle for the homeland as a code clerk on Sakurajima in Kagoshima Prefecture when the war ended. "Sakurajima," which crystallized that experience, made him a leading figure among the first wave of postwar writers. Besides works dealing with the war, he was admired for a style that drew the comedy and the pathos of ordinary people in caricature, and he received the Naoki Prize for "Boroya no Shunjū" ("Spring and Autumn in a Ramshackle House"). In his last years, suffering from failing health of body and mind, he completed "Genka" ("Phantasmagoria"), and died in 1965 at the age of 50.
