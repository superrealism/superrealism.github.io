About this work

A fantastical novel drawing a banquet of the mushroom spirits, published in April 1918. Mokuwaka, whom the townspeople call half idiot and half madman, disappears from time to time and goes home, he says, to his real house in the mountains, where he serves the mushroom princess. On the night of the autumn festival, the splendid scene of the feast he describes and the uncanny things happening in the town answer to each other. Known as the central work among Kyōka's pieces about mushrooms.

Summary

In the precincts of the shrine during the autumn festival, Mokuwaka, a young man of the town, sets up an odd stall — nothing more than an old straw mat with green bamboo poles stuck upright and hung about with several spiderwebs — calling out, "Fine garments here!" Once a live-in student and errand boy at the house of the town doctor, he is said to eat no cooked food, living instead on nuts and mushrooms, and from time to time to vanish from the town for days on end, returning, he says, to his "real home" in the mountains. On the night of the festival, three yamabushi wearing masks of a tengu, a hannya, and a fox appear and dance, and Mokuwaka, keeping time with their music, begins to tell of a banquet held in the mountains. A jorō spider offers up brocade, ground spiders and mountain leeches light lanterns, and a mushroom princess, attended by a thousand handmaidens, dances — and before long something strange befalls the shrine priest, and within ten days a girl of the town slips out through her lattice window, stark naked, in broad daylight.

Characters

  • Mokuwaka　A young man with a red nose. Said to be half an idiot, he sometimes returns to his "real home" in the mountains
  • The shrine priest (Setsuri Tayū)　The local Shinto priest. Hates Mokuwaka, and eventually goes mad
  • Tokimaru　The priest's son. Is made to undergo cold-water austerities by his father for playing a prank
  • The three yamabushi　A group who appear on the night of the festival wearing strange masks

The opening (original text)

"Moku, what's this...?" a child asks. Stroking the bright red tip of his nose, he answers, "Fine garments here." And a wretched business it is, too. Our friend Mokuwaka of the town has set up shop, planting green bamboo, leaves and all, at either end of an old straw mat, stringing a rope between them, and hanging any number of spiderwebs from it.

Kinoko no maihime by Izumi Kyōka (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Within ten days, a girl of the town, stark naked, in broad daylight, slipped out through her lattice window. She wanted that jeweled brocade of Mokuwaka's, beckoning to her from the gate. So sudden was it that not even her own parents could hold her back, and while they ran after her crying out, she darted straight into the watchman's box. From the towns of the market, before long, women came flocking in, one after another, scattering like fallen magnolia petals. — And at night, if one looked into that hut, the same sort of figures, like a pale shimmer of heat, could be seen surrounding Mokuwaka's nose.

Kinoko no maihime by Izumi Kyōka (Aozora Bunko)
