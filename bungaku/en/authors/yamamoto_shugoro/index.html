Biography

Born in 1903 in Hatsukari Village, Kitatsuru District, Yamanashi Prefecture (now Ōtsuki City); his real name was Shimizu Sazamu. His family moved to Yokohama after a flood in 1907 and he grew up there. In 1916 he was apprenticed to the Yamamoto Shūgorō pawnshop, and he later took the shop's name as his pen name out of gratitude for the kindness he had received there. He made his literary debut in 1926 with "Suma-dera Fukin" ("Near Suma Temple") and wrote period fiction for magazines such as "King." Beloved by readers for a style that lovingly portrayed the pride and affection of nameless commoners — craftsmen and women of the back alleys — he left works such as "Momi no Ki wa Nokotta" ("The Fir Tree Remained"), "Akahige Shinyōtan" ("Red Beard"), "Sabu," and "Nagai Saka" ("The Long Slope"). In 1943 he declined the seventeenth Naoki Prize, the only writer in the prize's history to do so after being chosen. He died in 1967 at the age of 63.
