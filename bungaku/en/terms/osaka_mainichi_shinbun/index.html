What is Ōsaka Mainichi Shimbun?

Category
Society / Group

A daily newspaper published in Osaka, known, together with its sister paper in Tokyo, the "Tokyo Nichinichi Shimbun" (the forerunner of today's Mainichi Shimbun), for the energy it devoted to literature in the Taishō and Shōwa eras. Rivaling the Asahi Shimbun, which had Natsume Sōseki, it brought in Akutagawa Ryūnosuke as a contract writer in 1919. Around the time he joined and after, Akutagawa published representative works, including "Gesaku Zanmai" ("Absorbed in Gesaku") and "Jigokuhen" ("Hell Screen"), in this newspaper. It also carried Mori Ōgai's late historical biography "Izawa Ranken," into which he sank deeply in his final years; Arishima Takeo's "Umareizuru Nayami" ("The Agony of Coming into Being"), depicting a young man torn between labor and art; and Tanizaki Jun'ichirō's "Haha o Kouru Ki" ("Longing for Mother"); and Kikuchi Kan's newspaper serial "Shinju Fujin" ("Madame Pearl," 1920), a bestseller and forerunner of popular fiction, touched off a sensation. Into the Shōwa era it also carried the continuation of Nakazato Kaizan's "Daibosatsu Tōge" and period fiction by Yoshikawa Eiji, making the literary pages of a great newspaper a stage that sent numerous masterworks into the world.
