Biography

Born in 1868 in Odawara, Kanagawa Prefecture; his real name was Montarō. He studied at Tokyo Senmon Gakkō (now Waseda University) and joined the Freedom and People's Rights Movement, but after its collapse he converted to Christianity and married Ishizaka Mina. Deeply drawn to Western Romantics such as Wordsworth, Emerson, and Byron, at twenty he self-published the long poem "Sōshū no Shi" ("A Prisoner's Poem") and then the verse drama "Hōraikyoku," opening a new frontier in modern Japanese poetry. In 1893 he founded the literary magazine "Bungakukai" with Shimazaki Tōson and Hoshino Tenchi and became its central figure. Proclaiming the supremacy of love in "The Poet of World-Weariness and Woman," and expounding the freedom of the human spirit and its "inner life" in essays such as "On Inner Life" and "What Does It Mean to Be Involved with Life," he was revered as a pioneer of Japanese Romantic literature. But tormented by the conflict between ideal and reality, he took his own life in 1894 at the age of just twenty-five.
