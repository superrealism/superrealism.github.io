About this work

An epistolary detective story Kosakai Fuboku, a medical scientist and detective writer, published in the magazine Shin Seinen in 1926. A poor medical scientist disappointed in love writes to A, a young millionaire who has taken the woman he loved, Yukie, by the power of money and married her, announcing that he will send a strange keepsake called a love curve. It is the record of a terrible experiment using an electrocardiograph that draws the beat of the heart as a curve. Under the cold guise of science, it draws a mad obsession of love: a chilling piece representing the dawn of Japanese detective fiction.

Summary

"My dear A—!" So begins a letter. Its sender is a poor, obscure man of medicine. Its addressee, "A," is the eldest son of a millionaire, due to be married tomorrow. The doctor reveals that A's bride, Yukie, is none other than the woman he himself once loved, only to have her taken from him by the power of A's wealth. But the letter voices no bitterness or resentment; instead, it speaks with elaborate courtesy, wishing the couple eternal happiness and offering to send, as a keepsake, something quite without precedent in the world: a "love curve." What, then, is a love curve? The doctor, recounting how he has buried the pain of his heartbreak in research, goes on at tedious length to explain the principle of the "electrocardiograph," a device that records the beating of the heart as a curved line. And at the letter's close, its true meaning is revealed. He is, at that very moment, conducting one final experiment in a locked room, together with Yukie, who has come running to him. The beat of Yukie's heart, wired to the electrocardiograph, and his own body, steadily draining itself of blood — when his blood runs out and he collapses, the device will record the very stopping of Yukie's heart as well. "If this is not the very extremity of love, then what is?" A masterpiece of epistolary mystery fiction, rendering a mad, obsessive experiment toward death alongside the woman he loves in the cold, precise language of science.

The opening (original text)

My dear A—! To celebrate the great ceremony of your lifetime, I am now about to send you, as a token from the very bottom of my heart, a thing called a "love curve." I cannot help but feel no small satisfaction in the thought that a gift of this kind — whether at the time of a wedding or on any other occasion whatever — has, to my knowledge, never once been attempted by anyone, not in Japan, nor in China, nor in the West, indeed, not by anyone at all since the very creation of the world.

Ren'ai kyokusen by Kosakai Fuboku (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

By now you must already have guessed the identity of the woman who has offered her heart to me. I feel, at this moment, a boundless joy. Though I cannot see the curve with my own eyes, I firmly believe, beyond any doubt, that a true love curve is even now being traced. The moment my blood runs out, her heart will stop. If this is not the very extremity of love, then what is? ……Ah — it seems my blood is running low, for her heart is even now on the very point of stopping. You! Yukie, who despised her loveless marriage of convenience with you, and who ran instead to me, her true lover — her heart, even now, is on the very point of stopping……

Ren'ai kyokusen by Kosakai Fuboku (Aozora Bunko)
