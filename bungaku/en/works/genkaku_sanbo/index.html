About this work

A dark masterpiece of Akutagawa's last years, drawing the wretched human pattern of a household gathered around a dying old man. Into the house of the old painter Horikoshi Genkaku, laid up with tuberculosis, his former mistress Oyoshi moves with her son to nurse him, and hatred, jealousy and hypocrisy swirl among Genkaku's wife, daughter and son-in-law. A central late work, staring at a distance under the shadow of illness and death at the hell that goes by the name of family, and often read alongside the author's own despair.

Summary

Horikoshi Genkaku, master of the elegant suburban house Genkaku Sanbō, lies on his deathbed with pulmonary tuberculosis. Into this house Oyoshi, once Genkaku's mistress, has moved in with her young son Takeo, under the pretext of nursing the sick man. Genkaku's wife Otori lies bedridden with paralysis, his son-in-law Jūkichi is a salaried worker, his daughter Osuzu grows more and more on edge, and even the nurse Kōno, who plays at devotion, hides her own calculations. Mistress and wife, daughter and mistress's child — amid this tangle of complicated relations, a dank hatred and hypocrisy settles like sediment throughout the household. Genkaku, under his laboured breath, calls to mind the sins and affairs of his past, and only wishes he could simply die all the sooner. In time Genkaku breathes his last, and the family holds a funeral that is a funeral in form alone. On the way back from the crematorium, Jūkichi catches sight of Oyoshi and her son, now with nowhere to go, and, looking on them coldly, once again lowers his eyes in silence to his book of the socialist Liebknecht.

Characters

  • Horikoshi Genkaku　An old painter on his deathbed with pulmonary tuberculosis. Repents his past affairs and sins while wishing for an early death
  • Oyoshi　Genkaku's former mistress. Moves in with her son to nurse him, stirring up trouble in the household
  • Jūkichi　Genkaku's son-in-law. Watches the family's collapse with a cold eye
  • Osuzu　Genkaku's daughter. Tormented by jealousy toward Oyoshi and her son
  • Kōno　A nurse who plays at devotion

The opening (original text)

It was a house neatly built, with a gate of quiet, tasteful reserve. Not that houses like this were rare in this neighbourhood. But the plaque reading "Genkaku Sanbō," and the garden trees visible over the wall, showed a more studied elegance than any other house around.

Genkaku sanbō by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

His cousin said nothing. But his imagination pictured a fishing town on a certain coast of Kazusa. And then, too, Oyoshi and her son, who would have to go and live in that fishing town. — He suddenly set his face hard, and, in the sunlight that had by now begun to slant in, took up his reading of Liebknecht once again.

Genkaku sanbō by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)
