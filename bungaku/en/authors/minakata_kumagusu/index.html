Biography

Born in 1867 in Wakayama City. A naturalist and folklorist who traveled to the United States and Britain while still young and pored over both Eastern and Western texts at the British Museum. He achieved internationally renowned results in the study of slime molds, while also displaying an encyclopedic erudition, drawing broadly on legends and folklore from around the world in works such as "Jūnishikō" ("Studies on the Twelve Zodiac Animals"). On returning home he ventured into the forests of Kumano to collect slime molds, and in the late Meiji period he fiercely opposed the government's shrine-merger policy, which was destroying the sacred groves protecting local shrines, becoming a pioneer of the nature-conservation movement in Japan. He is known as a "giant of knowledge." He died in 1941.
