About this work

The shorthand record of a lecture given at Kyoto in 1940 by Ishihara Kanji, the army officer who led the Manchurian Incident. Surveying the history of war as an alternation of wars of decision and wars of attrition, he argues that the development of weapons will bring about a final decisive war for mankind, after which the world will be unified. Read past all argument for and against as a strangely shaped philosophy of civilization in which Nichiren's faith is joined to modern strategic theory.

Summary

"War is the act of carrying out the policy of a state, using armed force directly as well" — so the lecture begins. Ishihara first divides war into two kinds: the "decisive war," in which the value of arms is high and which is thick and short, and the "war of endurance," in which political means weigh more heavily and which grows thin and long; and he explains that the military history of the West has alternated between the two. The universal conscription of antiquity, the mercenaries of the Middle Ages, Napoleon's decisive wars, the long endurance of the First World War — the pendulum has swung with the development of weapons and of society. And Ishihara predicts that with the development of the aeroplane an age will come when the center of an enemy country can be destroyed at a single stroke. War will then become a decisive war once more, and that will be the last great war of the human race — the "final war." The decision will be fought between the representative of the East and the representative of the West, Japan and America, and the world will be unified under the victor, after which war will disappear. In the second part, a question-and-answer session, Ishihara himself answers questions on a wide range of matters: Nichiren's prophecy and faith in the national polity, the conception of the East Asian League, the inevitability of a great industrial revolution. Noting that his own knowledge is almost nil outside military affairs, he says that this book is an interpretation, from the side of military science, of a final war he apprehended by intuition through faith; and the lecture closes by asking for examination and instruction from every quarter.

The opening (original text)

Part One: On the Final War A stenographic record of a lecture given at the Gihōkai in Kyoto on the twenty-ninth of May in the fifteenth year of Shōwa, with some additions made in August of the same year. Chapter One: A survey of the history of war Section One: The decisive war and the war of endurance War is the act of carrying out the policy of a state, using armed force directly as well.

Saishū sensō ron by Ishihara Kanji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

I am well aware that even in these questions and answers there is a great deal of presumptuous dogmatism beyond my proper part, and I am thoroughly ashamed of it. I earnestly beg those who have the will to examine the matter from every side — thought, society, economics and the rest — and to give me their instruction.

Saishū sensō ron by Ishihara Kanji (Aozora Bunko)
