What is Jōdai Bungaku (Ancient Literature)?

Category
Historical Period

A literary-historical period designating the dawn of Japanese literature, up through the Nara period, when written records began. Chinese characters arrived from the continent, and orally transmitted myths, legends, and songs came to be written down using them. Compiled in 712 by Ō no Yasumaro, the "Kojiki" records, in a distinctive style skillfully employing the sound and meaning of Chinese characters, the myths and history from the creation of heaven and earth through the reign of Empress Suiko; it is the oldest surviving book of its kind, and together with the subsequent "Nihon Shoki" (720) and the various regional "Fudoki," it conveys the ancient worldview down to us today. In poetry, the "Man'yōshū," compiled toward the end of the Nara period, reputedly by Ōtomo no Yakamochi and others, gathers some 4,500 forthright, expansive poems from every level of society, from emperors and aristocrats to nameless frontier guards and farmers, and stands as Japan's oldest poetry anthology and the wellspring of waka. Its simple, powerful, vital style, called "masuraoburi," was repeatedly looked to as an ideal in later ages, and in the modern era too it became the touchstone for the tanka reforms of Masaoka Shiki and Saitō Mokichi.
