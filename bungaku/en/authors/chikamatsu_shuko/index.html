Biography

Born in 1876 to a farming family in Wake District, Okayama Prefecture; his real name was Tokuda Kōji. He studied at Waseda University and began his career as a literary critic. He is known for frank, obsessive "stories of infatuation" such as "Black Hair," "Madness," and "A Frost-Bound Evening," which laid bare a man's fixation on the wife who had left him; as a thoroughgoing writer of the confessional "I-novel," candidly recording his own humiliations and obsessions, he became a representative figure of Naturalist literature. He spent his later years in the misfortune of blindness. He died in 1944.
