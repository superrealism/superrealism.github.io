About this work

A novella Nagai Kafū published in Shinshōsetsu in 1909. Home from his studies in France and America, Kafū put his nostalgia for a vanishing Edo into the manners of the downtown quarters along the Sumida: the central work at the starting point of his writing. Highly regarded as the forerunner of the Edo-flavoured pieces that mourn old feeling amid the changing manners of Meiji, and the origin of the downtown literature that leads to A Strange Tale from East of the River.

Summary

Chōkichi, a middle-school boy living at Imado on the bank of the Sumida, is secretly in love with O-Ito, a friend from childhood who has gone out as a geisha. Unable to fix his mind on his studies, he confesses that he wants to become an actor; but his uncle Ragetsu, a master of haikai, opposes it sternly, a rift opens between him and his mother O-Toyo, and his loneliness deepens. O-Ito too suffers in her condition as a geisha, and keeps her feeling for Chōkichi hidden in her breast. As the season turns from summer to autumn, the faint love of the two is drawn together with the scenes of the great river's bank; but before long Chōkichi falls ill with typhoid. Reading a letter in which the boy had set down what was in his heart, Ragetsu understands his true feeling for the first time, and makes up his mind to bring Chōkichi and O-Ito together.

Characters

  • Chōkichi　A middle-school boy of eighteen living at Imado, torn between his love for his childhood friend O-Ito and his dream of becoming an actor
  • O-Ito　Chōkichi's childhood friend. At sixteen she becomes a geisha of Yoshichō, and keeps her feeling for him hidden
  • O-Toyo　Chōkichi's mother, who makes her living as a teacher of Tokiwazu ballads
  • Ragetsu of the Shōfū hermitage　Chōkichi's uncle, a master of haikai, who opposes his wish to be an actor
  • Kichi　An old acquaintance of Chōkichi's. An actor in the Ii company

The opening (original text)

I Ragetsu of the Shōfū hermitage, a master of haikai, had ended by not visiting even at the Bon festival this year the younger sister who teaches Tokiwazu ballads at Imado; and the matter was on his mind every day.

The River Sumida by Nagai Kafū (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

In February of the third year of Shōwa, Madame Kimura Tomiko dressed up my Sumida River as a play and put it on the stage of the Hongōza. The actors who took the principal parts on that occasion were Ichikawa Sumizō, Ichikawa Shōchō, Ōtani Tomoemon, Ichikawa Kōjaku and others. Madame Kimura's play The Sumida River is included in her volume Ginsenshū. October of the tenth year of Shōwa, at the hermitage in Azabu Kafū Sanjin
