About this work

Pacing a hot, cramped room, the narrator does nothing but think about how to dispose of a corpse — a very short piece Yamakawa Masao published in Hōseki. Poe's old device, the university dissecting room, a manhole, the press of a demonstration. Alternating chocolate and cigarettes, he hits on a good plan, and when he claps his wife on the shoulder in the bed in his elation, he finally remembers. A dry little horror in which the boundary between work and reality comes off without a sound.

Summary

A room in a block of flats where the thick walls let through not a sound from next door. That was "my" castle. One cannot work in a noisy place. He drinks water in the kitchen because his throat is dry, takes a bar of chocolate from the desk drawer and bites into it, and as he sits down on the bed he remembers: Mr. O is coming at ten tomorrow morning. It must be finished by then. ... Lying on his back as his habit is, with four bars of chocolate and an ashtray on the side table, he stares at the ceiling as though he would bore a hole in it. The problem is the disposal of the body. The killing has already been done. Round the neck a cord is wound in the manner of a murder. After the so-called "crime of sudden impulse," how is the body to be made to vanish? — that is what it comes down to. Throw it into the crush of a demonstration; leave it in the mortuary of a university; drop it down a manhole. Every one of them is hard to carry. What, then, about boiling it? Pour the dissolved parts out of the bathroom, and patiently beat the bones into powder. That would make one human being's "disappearance" complete. — Two or three hours pass, and he sits up abruptly and, from long habit, taps his wife on the shoulder. "There, I have it!" But with her face to the wall, his wife shows no reaction at all.

Characters

  • I　A man whose work is to think up ideas
  • My wife　Who lies on the bed and does not move
  • Mr. O　The man due to come to the flat tomorrow morning

The opening (original text)

I wiped away the sweat. However much I wiped, more came running down. The room was terribly close. The electric light shone dimly at the edge of my consciousness. I may have been in a state close to absence of mind. Only my legs moved without stopping. My eyes were fixed on nothing but the carpet, and yet I was not looking at any thought there.... I had been walking up and down the narrow room for some time.

Lonely Man by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

But with her face to the wall my wife does not answer. She shows no reaction at all. ... Suddenly I remembered. Her talkative lips will never open again. The cord I had drawn round her neck a few hours before with all the strength I had lay coiled on the bed just as it was, like a dead snake. Without thinking, I threw the last piece of chocolate into my mouth. The closeness of the room came back.

Lonely Man by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)
