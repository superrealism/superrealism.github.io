What is Jinsei Sōshō Debate?

Category
Other

A debate waged in 1893 between Yamaji Aizan and Kitamura Tōkoku over what literature can do for human life. It began when Aizan, in an essay on the historical figure Rai Shunsui in the magazine "Kokumin no Tomo," argued that "writing is itself an undertaking," and that literature which brings no benefit to the world is empty. Tōkoku responded in "Bungakukai" with "What Does It Mean to Be Involved with Life?" countering that the value of literature cannot be measured by the visible utility of an undertaking, and that literature's meaning lies precisely in touching the inner life of human beings. In the course of the exchange Tōkoku wrote "On Inner Life," arguing for the freedom of the inner life over visible utilitarian gain, becoming the ideological pillar of Japanese Romanticism. Where Aizan, a historian, saw the enlightenment of the citizenry as the mission of writing, Tōkoku, a poet, sought literature's place precisely in inner freedom; the difference between the two men's positions well reflects what Meiji intellectuals expected of literature. As a debate that argued head-on for the autonomy of literature, it looms large in the history of modern Japanese literature. Tōkoku took his own life the following year, 1894, at twenty-five, but his argument was carried forward by the coterie of "Bungakukai," including Shimazaki Tōson.
