About this work

An adventure detective story for boys that Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shōnen Shōjo Tankai in 1930. The boy detective Haruta Ryūsuke and his partner Genkotsu Sōta chase the riddle of a vanished submarine as far as Skull Island, out across the sea. A bracing sea-spy adventure, exposing the villains' contrivance using the current of the Sea Wolf.

Summary

At the breakfast table of Dr. Haruta's house. "Ryūsuke, you've handled a good many cases by now — which was the most interesting?" the doctor asks, and young Ryūsuke, a second-year pupil at the prefectural middle school, begins telling of the cases he has taken on so far. There was the "Case of the Yellow Diamond Necklace," in which he fought a one-on-one duel of wits against the suspicious foreigner Jansen, with a birthmark on his forehead — and now, this time, the great adventure surrounding "Skull Island." With clear-headed deduction, Ryūsuke unravels the riddle of a submarine that vanished without a trace during a test run. After crashing in a flying boat and being swept far away, caught in a ferocious current called the "Sea Wolf," Ryūsuke sees through the trick: the villains had used the flow of the Sea Wolf to build, beneath the sea, an artificial island calling itself "Skull Island," rigged with devices to draw in and hide the submarine. The secret machinations of the military spy Dr. Metras, working alongside the sham Reverend Jansen, are also exposed. At the case's resolution, Ryūsuke introduces to everyone his friend Genkotsu Sōta, "who always serves me at the risk of his own life." "My boss is Mr. Haruta Ryūsuke, and I'm Genkotsu Sōta, and I fear neither arrow nor gun" — Sōta, stammering, makes a clumsy bow. And so the adventure of Skull Island comes to its happy end. A rousing piece drawing a boy detective's sea-going spy adventure.

The opening (original text)

At Dr. Haruta's house, breakfast was in full, lively swing. "Ryūsuke, you've handled a good many cases by now — which was the most interesting?" The doctor said this with a laugh, looking over at his eldest son, young Ryūsuke. Ryūsuke was a second-year pupil at Prefectural Middle School No. X, and a very clever boy.

Gaikotsujima no daibōken by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Genkotsu Sōta raised his head, gave a jerky, clumsy bow, and said, stammering all the way: "Er, er, um, my, my boss is Mr. Haruta Ryūsuke, and I'm, I'm Genkotsu Sōta, and I fear neither arrow nor gun, ahem." And so the adventure of Skull Island came, happily, to its end.
