About this work

A long novel published in Chūō Kōron in 1947, the second part of the autobiographical trilogy that runs from Nobuko to Signpost. Sasa Nobuko, separated from her husband, wavers between the garden of her parents' house and the new garden of life shared with Motoko — modelled on Yuasa Yoshiko — as she searches for a way to independence as a woman, and resolves at last to go to the Soviet Union. It follows in fine psychological detail an inner life torn between an old house and a new way of living.

Summary

Sasa Nobuko, who has broken her married life with Tsukuda and gone back to her parents' house, is trying to build a new life with writing to hold her up. But the old air of a middle-class household does not change, and her mother Takeyo's strong feeling and her rule reach into every corner of her daughter's way of living. The garden of her parents' house is the more stifling for being comfortable. Before Nobuko then appears Yoshimi Motoko, who studies Russian literature. Touched by Motoko's outspokenness and her sense of how to live, Nobuko feels the vague things inside her being pared away. In time the two rent a house in the suburbs and begin living together — another garden, neither family nor marriage, where two women live as equals. Talk of literature, friends coming and going, the managing of the household money — there is a freedom there that her parents' house did not have. And yet she cannot altogether cast off the old garden, and Nobuko goes back and forth between the two without a break. One must see the world with one's own eyes. Nobuko and Motoko resolve at last to go to the Soviet Union, and begin preparing for the journey.

Characters

  • Sasa Nobuko　A young writer who has divorced and gone back to her parents' house, and gropes toward independence between two gardens
  • Yoshimi Motoko　A woman studying Russian literature, who keeps house together with Nobuko
  • Sasa Takeyo　Nobuko's mother, who tries to rule her daughter by the strength of her feeling
  • Sasa Taizō　Nobuko's father, an architect
  • Sasa Tamotsu　Nobuko's younger brother

The opening (original text)

I The bamboo of the house next door has spread its roots and is putting up thin shoots into this passage. Turning at the bamboo fence there, she found the door of the garage in front open as it never was. It seemed the car was in the middle of being cleaned, but there was no one about; on the tin-sheeted wall a single bare bulb burned dimly and gloomily. Nobuko walked up the flagged path to the inner entrance with a puzzled look.

Futatsu no niwa by Miyamoto Yuriko (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Nobuko had remembered those words. I shall leave things so that you can find them even if I am not here.... Tamotsu too might go on a journey, and a letter from Nobuko asking him to send some book out of the trunk might arrive just then. But why had he made a point of saying it in that way? It must be Tamotsu's methodical habit, and yet — . Just then the taxi went past the chinquapin tree that was their landmark without meaning to. Motoko cried out in a hurry, "There! There!" And Nobuko, so as not to mistake the corner where they were to get out, half rose from the seat of the taxi as it began to back up.
