Biography

Born in 1908 in Matsue, Shimane Prefecture, and raised in Unnan. He specialized in radiological medicine at Nagasaki Medical College, and the very radiation he studied gave him leukemia. Just after being told he had three years to live, he was exposed to the atomic bombing in Nagasaki on August 9, 1945, and lost his wife, Midori. Though gravely wounded himself, he threw himself into relief work, and later, living in a two-mat hermitage with his two children which he named Nyokōdō, after the teaching "Love your neighbor as yourself," he continued writing from his sickbed — "Nagasaki no Kane" ("The Bells of Nagasaki"), "Kono Ko o Nokoshite" ("Leaving These Children Behind"), and others. His writings, weaving together the horror of the atomic bombing and his Catholic faith, were widely read in postwar Japan, and "The Bells of Nagasaki" was also made into a popular song. He died in 1951 at the age of 43.
