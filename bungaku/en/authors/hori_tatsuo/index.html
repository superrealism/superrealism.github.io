Biography

Born in Tokyo in 1904. While at the First Higher School he studied under Akutagawa Ryūnosuke and Murō Saisei. Absorbing the psychological realism of French literature and the literature of the Heian court, he wrote of life and death, love and loss, in a delicate prose style. He left works of transparent lyricism: "Kaze Tachinu" ("The Wind Has Risen"), which weaves the illness and death of his fiancée together with lines from Rilke's poetry; "Utsukushii Mura" ("The Beautiful Village") and "Naoko," both set in Karuizawa; and "Kagerō no Nikki" ("A Record of the Mayfly"), drawn from Heian court diaries. He himself suffered from tuberculosis, and died in 1953.
