What it does

Features

  1. 01

    A whole book, in one sitting

    Every work gives you an overview, the plot, the opening and the ending. Because the ending is there too, the app works just as well for finding out how a story actually turns out as it does for deciding what to read next.

    Around fifty major works go further, with a chapter-by-chapter synopsis and a character map that draws out who is bound to whom. In Kokoro, with Sensei, K and the narrator, or in Konjiki Yasha, with Kanichi and Omiya, the relationships more or less are the novel — and a diagram earns its place.

  2. 02

    The original, when you want it

    Anything that catches your interest opens as the complete original text from Aozora Bunko, inside the app. The reader switches between vertical and horizontal text, shows furigana above the kanji, and lets you set the type size and the background colour.

    Text to speech will read a work aloud, so a commute or a walk can carry a novel. Note that the original texts are in Japanese — the summaries are what carry you in English.

  3. 03

    A reason to open it daily

    The home screen offers a book of the day, which is the point: it puts you in front of authors you would never have picked yourself. Anything you started stays on the home screen with its reading progress, so you can pick it back up, and newly added summaries and terms show up as you go.

  4. 04

    Follow an author

    Around 150 authors have a profile covering their life and their major works. Like one story and you can see, on the spot, what kind of writer wrote it. Browse by work, by author or by genre.

  5. 05

    Look up the vocabulary

    A dictionary of 160 literary terms — the I-novel, naturalism, the Shinkankaku school — along with 200 selected passages. When a term turns up in a commentary, you can look it up without leaving the app.

Who it is for

Use cases

  • Readers who know the titles of the Japanese classics but not what happens in them
  • Students and book clubs who want the shape of a novel and its cast before the discussion
  • Learners of Japanese who want a summary in English and the original alongside it
  • Anyone who wanted to read Aozora Bunko but gave up on horizontal text in a browser
  • Anyone deciding where to start with modern Japanese literature

Questions

FAQ

Is the app free?

Yes. No account and no sign-in are required. The app is supported by ads.

Are the summaries in English?

Yes. The summaries, author profiles and character maps are written in English. The original texts themselves are in Japanese, since they come from Aozora Bunko.

Do the summaries give away the ending?

Yes. Every work has an overview, the plot, the opening and the ending, so you can use the app either to decide what to read or to find out how a story you never finished turns out.

Who wrote the summaries and character maps?

The summaries, commentary, character introductions and character maps were all written for this app and its companion reference site. The original texts are quoted from Aozora Bunko.

Can I read the original texts in the app?

Yes. Aozora Bunko originals open in a reader that switches between vertical and horizontal text, shows furigana, and can read the text aloud.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not at the moment. Only the Android version is published.
