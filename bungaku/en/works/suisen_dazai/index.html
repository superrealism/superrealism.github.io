About this work

A short story by Dazai, opening from a doubt about Kikuchi Kan's "The Conduct of Lord Tadanao." Was that lord not in fact a master of swordsmanship after all? — and the subject of the genuine and the false in talent is laid over the story of Madame Shizuko, who paints. A piece cutting sharply at what it means to acknowledge genius, and at the meanness of those who will not.

Summary

I cannot forget "The Conduct of Lord Tadanao," a story I read when I was thirteen or fourteen. It tells of a lord praised by his retainers for his skill at swordsmanship, who goes mad on learning that they had all, in fact, been deliberately losing to him. But lately a disquieting doubt has come to me — might that lord not, in truth, have been a genuine master? Might it not be that his retainers really were no match for him, and that the whispers in the garden were nothing but a cowardly excuse for their own defeat? It is entirely possible. Do not we ourselves, after being bested by some fine senior, sometimes speak ill of them behind their backs? Having written this much, "I" begin to speak of a Madame Shizuko, who comes and goes at the house of an old painter called Mr. Kusata. Shizuko, the wife of a wealthy man, paints pictures, and the painters around her praise her as a "genius." That this is mere flattery is plain to anyone, and I too believed as much. One day I give Madame Shizuko a thorough dressing-down; she gives up painting and takes to drink. In time I come across a painting of hers, titled "Narcissus," and tear it to pieces and throw it into the stove. "What a worthless picture. All you people were doing was flattering a rich man's wife." And yet, I write — the painting of the narcissus was not, in truth, a worthless picture at all. It was splendid. Why I tore it up, I leave to the reader's own guess. Madame Shizuko was taken in by Mr. Kusata, and took her own life at the end of that year. "My unease only grows and grows. ...Perhaps even in the twentieth century, a genius of art still walks among us."

The opening (original text)

It was when I was thirteen, or perhaps fourteen, that I read the novel "The Conduct of Lord Tadanao," and though I never had occasion to read it again after that, even now, twenty years later, I still remember the bare outline of its plot. It was a strange and sorrowful tale.

The ending (original text)

I tore the painting into ever smaller pieces and thrust it into the stove. I flatter myself that I understand something of painting. ...The painting of the narcissus was, without question, no worthless picture. It was splendid. Why I tore it up, I leave to the reader's own guess. Madame Shizuko was taken in by Mr. Kusata, and at the end of that year took her own life. My unease only grows and grows.
