About this work

A humorous romance about ninjutsu that Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shin Yomimono in 1948. A narrator who claims to be telling the true origin of the ninja's art gives, in a deadpan hand, the strange story of Yaosuke, a boy born to a family of jewel-makers in Hida with a triple affliction — lame, one-eyed and hunchbacked. With protests to the gods and absurd disputations with a bewitching beauty, a piece full of laughter and invention.

Summary

"Have my readers ever heard of ninjutsu?" — so begins the narrator, who scoffs at the petty, small-minded rationalism of the popular Sarutobi-style ninja tales flooding the magazines of the day, and grandly declares that he alone will set down the true origin of ninjutsu, launching into his grand tale. The setting is a chaotic age, just as the house of Toyotomi has fallen and the Tokugawa era has only just begun. In the village of Fukiya in Hida province, in a family of bead-carvers, there lived a boy named Yaosuke. Lame from birth, he lost the sight of one eye at two, and became hunchbacked at five — a boy afflicted with a threefold disability. His mother laments that he was conceived through prayers to the god Sokotsu of Horase Mountain, of all things. On hearing this, Yaosuke, at the age of seven, goes to the Sokotsu shrine to lodge a formal complaint, demanding that the enshrined deity correct the flaws in his body — but the god proves entirely unresponsive, being away on a visit to Izumo, or else lying down and refusing to answer. In time he goes about petitioning every god and buddha he can find, still burdened with his threefold disability, until he turns fifteen. Farce and fantastical invention run riot throughout, as when the mysterious beauty Osen (the lady) makes advances toward him, only for her true identity to turn out, through some illusion, to have been nothing but a notice-board post all along. The tale closes with the line "Here ends the first story" — a comic, fantastical piece by Yamamoto.

Characters

  • Yaosuke　A boy with a threefold disability, born into a family of bead-carvers in Hida province
  • Osen (the lady)　A mysterious beauty who makes advances toward Yaosuke

The opening (original text)

Have my readers ever heard of "ninjutsu"? Of late, one often comes across Sarutobi-style ninja tales scattered through various magazines. This writer, having been devoted to, indeed besotted with, ninjutsu since boyhood, has — purely for the sake of historical investigation and clarification — ranged widely through the old records and surveyed the ancient sites, and has already plumbed its deepest mysteries; and viewing this recent crop of Sarutobi tales with that same eye, I cannot help but pity their petty, small-minded triviality and their stingy, cheap rationalism.

En'yōki by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"— Well, really, what an insensitive blockhead, to make such a fool of a person," the lady said, stamping her foot and glaring, her eyes flashing with fury. "A wooden puppet who can't even see how beautiful I am — oh, how vexing!" Having finished his business in Fukiya village, Yaosuke now sets off to search for Dekiroku. And so, let this be the end of the first story.

En'yōki by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)
