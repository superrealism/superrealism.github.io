Biography

Born in 1913 in Handa, Chita District, Aichi Prefecture (now Handa City); his real name was Shōhachi. He lost his birth mother while still a child and spent a lonely boyhood with his stepmother. From Handa Middle School he went on to the Tokyo School of Foreign Languages, and from his student days he devoted himself to writing children's stories and poems. At eighteen, "Gon Gitsune" ("Gon, the Little Fox"), which he submitted to the magazine "Akai Tori" ("The Red Bird"), was recognized by Kitahara Hakushū and published there, an early sign of precocious talent. After graduating he worked as a substitute teacher and girls' school instructor in his home province while writing many children's stories filled with simple warmth and the poignancy of life — "Ojiisan no Ranpu" ("Grandfather's Lamp"), "Tebukuro o Kai ni" ("Buying Mittens"), "Hana no Ki Mura to Nusubito-tachi" ("The Village of Flowering Trees and the Thieves"). Frail in health, he died of tuberculosis in 1943 at the young age of 29. Most of his works were published after his death, and today he is widely loved alongside Miyazawa Kenji as one of Japan's leading writers of children's literature.
