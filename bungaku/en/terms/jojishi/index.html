What is Epic Poetry?

Category
Form / Genre

Poetry that tells a story about myths, legends, heroic deeds, or historical events. It is one of the traditional divisions of poetry, alongside lyric poetry, which centers on personal feeling, and dramatic poetry, which unfolds through dialogue and action. Typically a lengthy verse narrative, an epic expresses communal memory, ideals, or an understanding of the world through a protagonist's adventures and struggles. The "Iliad" and the "Odyssey," attributed to Homer, are representative examples, rooted in myths and heroic legends transmitted orally. Poets also composed literary epics drawing on this tradition, including Virgil's "Aeneid" and Dante's "The Divine Comedy." The latter weaves the medieval Christian worldview into a journey through Hell, Purgatory, and Heaven; this app includes Yamakawa Heizaburō's Japanese translation. Epic poetry is not exclusive to the West: the Indian "Mahabharata" and "Ramayana," and Ainu heroic yukar, also belong in its wider consideration. In modern literature, novels may be described as epic for their expansive portrayal of society or history, but this extended usage should be distinguished from epic as a verse form.
