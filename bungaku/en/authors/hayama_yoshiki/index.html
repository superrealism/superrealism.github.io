Biography

Born in 1894 to a family of former samurai stock in Toyotsu, Kyoto District, Fukuoka Prefecture (now Miyako Town). He dropped out of the preparatory course at Waseda University and worked as a sailor and factory laborer around the country while throwing himself into the labor movement. He was imprisoned over the 1923 Nagoya Communist Party incident and began writing in jail. After his release he became a leading writer of the "Bungei Sensen" ("Literary Front") group and a representative figure of Japanese proletarian literature. He is known for the novel "Umi ni Ikuru Hitobito" ("People Who Live by the Sea"), drawn from his experience as a sailor, and the short story "Letter Found in a Cement Barrel," which depicts the sorrows of cement-factory workers. In 1945, while returning from Manchuria, he died of a cerebral hemorrhage aboard a train at the age of 51.
