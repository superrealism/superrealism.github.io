What is Prose Poem?

Category
Form / Genre

A poem written in the form of prose. Rather than following a fixed syllable count or rhyme scheme, it generally uses sentences and paragraphs instead of verse lines, producing poetic expression through metaphor, symbolism, repetition, and other devices. Like free verse, it lacks a fixed form, but its prose appearance offers a useful distinction from free verse arranged in lines. Prose does not, however, imply the absence of musicality or an internal rhythm. In nineteenth-century France, Bertrand's "Gaspard de la nuit" was a precursor, and Baudelaire's "Paris Spleen" played a major role in developing the form; Rimbaud also wrote distinctive prose poems. In Japan, Hagiwara Sakutarō and Miyoshi Tatsuji explored it, and in the early Shōwa period it was associated with poets active around "Shi to Shiron." Sakutarō's "Shukumei" (1939) brings together examples and his thinking about the form. Its introductory essay on prose poetry recognizes musical qualities within prose and draws attention to its intellectual and philosophical content. The term does not encompass every beautifully written passage of prose: it identifies an approach that creates poetry through prose form.
