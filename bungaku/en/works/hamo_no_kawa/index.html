About this work

Kamitsukasa Shōken's representative work, published in "Hototogisu" in 1914, which won lavish praise from Tayama Katai and made the author's name. To Ofumi, who runs the eel restaurant Sanukiya in Dōtonbori single-handed, comes a thick letter from Fukuzō, the adopted husband who ran away to Tokyo. Send money — and then, in the last line: "kindly send me the skin of a pike conger." With her uncle Gentarō she recites the whole list of her husband's selfishness; and yet what Ofumi does that night is — . A piece that captures, in voluble Osaka speech, the air of an Osaka merchant house and a feeling between husband and wife that will not divide out evenly.

Summary

At the cash desk of the eel restaurant Sanukiya in Dōtonbori, Ofumi is handed a thick envelope with two red stamps on it. There is no sender's name, but she knows the hand: it is from Fukuzō, her husband, who ran off to Tokyo and has not come back. The name on it, "Fukushima Iso," is the new name given her by her uncle Gentarō, who is fond of name divination, and she cannot imagine how her husband came to know it. Afraid to open it, she has Gentarō read the long letter out by candlelight. He is ill and in difficulties, so send twenty yen; how are the children; this time he has truly learned his lesson and wants to come back into the fold — every phrase is one she could have recited without reading. With this, Fukuzō's debts come to three thousand yen. An expensive adopted son, Gentarō says drily. Ofumi, who keeps twenty-eight employees busy and misses nothing as she deals with the customers, goes on giving her answers as though it were somebody else's business. And then Gentarō bursts out laughing at the small characters at the very end of the letter: "kindly send me the skin of a pike conger." It is Fukuzō's favorite thing in the world. Late that night, drinking with her uncle behind the Hōzenji temple, Ofumi says she might go up to Tokyo and settle matters — and on the way home she stops at the fish-cake shop, which is still open.

Characters

  • Ofumi (Fukushima Iso)　The mistress of the eel restaurant Sanukiya in Dōtonbori, thirty-six. Left by her husband, she keeps the business going
  • Fukuzō　Ofumi's adopted husband. He has run off to Tokyo and sends begging letters while piling up debts
  • Gentarō　Ofumi's uncle. Fond of name divination, he recites the whole list of Fukuzō's selfishness
  • Okaji　Ofumi's mother, retired to Ōhama in Sakai, where she is raising three grandchildren

The opening (original text)

1 The postman came in with a tread like a policeman's. "Is there anyone here called Fukushima Iso?" He said it irritably, and pulled out from his bundle of cards and letters a thick envelope with two red stamps on it. It was just the hour when the evening in Dōtonbori begins; the theater diagonally opposite had evidently come to an interval, and the Sanukiya filled up all at once, so that with the sets sent up to the second floor and the deliveries to the theater's own house and to the teahouses in front of it, the cash desk and the kitchen alike were run off their feet.

The ending (original text)

Still grumbling to herself, Ofumi barred the front doors and screwed the key deep into her breast as usual; then she gave a little stroke to the parcel of pike conger skin she had just put away under the cash desk, and set about getting ready for bed herself.
