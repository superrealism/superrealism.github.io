About this work

A very short story published in May 1923 in the newly founded Bungei Shunjū. A fly with great eyes is the only witness to the fates of the people who share a stagecoach from a post town into the city in high summer — a farm woman hurrying to a dying son, an eloping couple, a mother and child, a country gentleman. Its detached, matter-of-fact handling of human tragedy anticipated the New Sensationalist movement of the following year, and with "The Sun" it made Yokomitsu's name.

Summary

The posting station in high summer is empty. The hunchbacked driver, who looks forward to nothing so much as the steamed buns coming out of the steamer, takes no notice of the passengers in a hurry and will not bring the coach out. A farm woman rushing to the side of a son at the point of death asks again and again whether the coach is not ready; a young man and woman hand in hand, apparently eloping; a mother with her innocent little boy; and a country gentleman who has worked forty-three years and at last got hold of eight hundred yen — one after another they gather at the station. When the buns are steamed, the driver finally sounds his horn and takes the coach out. Through the blaze of the midsummer day the coach runs on, and the passengers, each carrying his own troubles, give themselves up to a moment's ease. The driver, having eaten every one of the buns in his apron pouch, begins to doze. The only one that notices is that one fly with the large eyes, which has moved from the horse's back to the body of the coach. At the top of the cliff one wheel goes off the road, and coach and horse and people all fall together to the bottom. On the dry riverbed a mass of people and horse and broken boards lies piled up, silent and unmoving. Only the fly with the large eyes, putting strength into wings now perfectly rested, flew off alone at its leisure into the blue sky.

Characters

  • The fly with the large eyes　The one witness to the fall of the coach
  • The driver　A hunchbacked old man fixed on his steamed buns, whose dozing brings on the disaster
  • The farm woman　A mother rushing to the side of a son at the point of death

The opening (original text)

The posting station in high summer was empty. Only a single fly with large eyes, having caught itself in a spider's web in a dim corner of the stable, swung dangling there a while, kicking at the web with its hind legs. And then it dropped, plump, like a bean. And it crawled from the end of a straw standing aslant under the weight of the horse dung all the way up to the stripped back of the horse.

The ending (original text)

The coach came to the top of the cliff. The horse began to turn obediently, following the road that appeared before it within its blinkers. But at that moment it could not take into account the width of its own body and the width of the coach. One wheel went off the road. Suddenly the horse was pulled up on end by the coach. In that instant the fly flew up. And there before its eyes was the belly of the wanton horse falling to the bottom of the cliff together with the body of the coach. Then, when one high scream of people and horse had gone up, on the dry riverbed a mass of people and horse and broken boards lay piled together, silent and unmoving. But the fly with the large eyes, putting strength into wings now perfectly rested, flew off alone at its leisure into the blue sky.

Famous passages

The fly with the large eyes, putting strength into its now fully rested wings, flew off alone, unhurried, into the blue sky.

The close of the story. As the coach plunges from a cliff because the driver has dozed off and the passengers' fates are cut short in an instant, a single fly alone flies calmly away. Yokomitsu's cold gaze, viewing human affairs from a distance, stands out sharply.
