What is Utamakura?

Category
Style & Technique

A place name with a venerable pedigree in waka poetry. It originally also referred to words and phrases used in waka, and to books collecting them (such as the priest Nōin's "Nōin Utamakura"), but today it is used almost exclusively to mean a famous place celebrated in poetry. Names such as the cherry blossoms of Yoshino, the autumn leaves of the Tatsuta River, the moon at Suma, the barrier at Shirakawa, and Matsushima carry an established convention by which simply naming the place calls up a particular scene or feeling, and a poet could compose a poem around an utamakura even without ever having visited it. It is a device by which a place name stores up the memory of language, and the scene as it exists within poetry is often given priority over the actual landscape. In the medieval period, journeys to visit these places grew popular, and in the early modern period Matsuo Bashō, in "Oku no Hosomichi" ("The Narrow Road to the Deep North"), traveled to poetic sites such as the barrier at Shirakawa and Matsushima, overlaying the scene the poets of old had seen upon the scene before his own eyes. The travels of poets who set out to confirm an utamakura for themselves also nurtured the genre of travel writing. That a place name itself becomes a repository of literary memory is one great distinguishing feature of Japanese poetry.
