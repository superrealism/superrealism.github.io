About this work

A short story Higuchi Ichiyō published in the magazine Nihon no Katei in January 1896. A wife who, being strong-willed, has not been able to live happily with a taciturn husband is given a child, recognizes where she was wrong, and recovers the feeling between them — told in the mother's own voice. A warm domestic story, in which she reflects that it was the smile of a baby who cannot speak that taught her about life.

Summary

The wife of Yamaguchi Noboru, a judge, married three years now, begins to tell of how dear her own child is to her. Strong-willed and unwilling to be outdone, she had long suspected that her taciturn husband, who confided in her about nothing, was keeping secrets from her, and the two had clashed again and again over the smallest of things. She would push her husband away with silence, take out her frustration on the maid, and there were days when she lay in bed weeping. Her husband, in time, became a man of dissipation who stayed away from home, and the wall between husband and wife only grew thicker. But now, at last, the wife comes to understand: that her husband's silence about the details of his work had been born of consideration — a wish to protect himself from those who might try to bribe him — and that it was her own misunderstanding that had driven him to it. The baby born to them at the end of last year taught her more, through its innocent smile, than any book at school or any teacher's words ever could. Seeing her stern husband's face soften as he dotes on their child, the wife comes to know that his feelings and her own are, in truth, one and the same. Now she holds no resentment toward her husband at all, and has come to feel that it is only because of the suffering they endured that today's happiness is possible. It was, she reflects, this baby, not yet even able to speak, that taught her how to live her whole life.

Characters

  • The wife (Mrs. Yamaguchi)　Wife of the judge Yamaguchi Noboru. Unable, in her strong-willed way, to live happily with her husband, she comes to recognize her own fault after the birth of their child. The narrator of this tale
  • Yamaguchi Noboru　The wife's husband. A taciturn, stern judge. Estrangement from his wife drives him for a time to dissipation, but he dotes deeply on their child
  • The baby　The Yamaguchi couple's infant child. His innocent smile teaches the wife how to live

The opening (original text)

If I were to come right out and say how dear my own child is to me, I daresay all of you would burst out laughing at me — for there is no one anywhere who hates their own child, and you would surely laugh at how absurd it is for me to go about, as though putting on airs, proclaiming as if I alone possessed some splendid treasure. And so I do not go about saying such an overblown thing aloud, and yet—

The ending (original text)

"In this whole household, the only one who ever comforts me is this little rascal," he says, pressing his dark, sun-browned face close to the baby's — and I watch, wondering whether the child will cry, or be frightened — but no, he shows nothing but a face of pure delight, breaking into just the same smile he always shows me. Once my husband, twisting at his mustache, asked me, "Is this child dear to you too?" "Of course he is," I answered, rather primly, and he said, with a bit of teasing quite unlike his usual manner, "Well then, you're dear to me too" — and burst into loud laughter, a look on his face so like the baby's own that there was no mistaking the resemblance between them. Since this child is so dear to me, how could I ever go on hating my husband? If I am good to him, my husband is good to me in turn. They say even a three-year-old can see where the shallows lie, but it was this baby, not yet even able to speak, that taught me how to live out my whole life.
