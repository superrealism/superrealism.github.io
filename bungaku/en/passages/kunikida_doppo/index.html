Famous passages by Kunikida Doppo

Well-known lines from the works of Kunikida Doppo.

6 passages

Musashino

“The vestiges of Musashino now remain, but faintly, in Iruma District” — so I once read on a map made in the Bunsei era.

An opening that takes as its clue a line inscribed on an Edo-period map. It begins a landmark of modern Japanese essay writing, in which the author walks out on his own two feet to confirm the vanishing face of Musashino.

It seems that, until now, the Japanese have known little of the beauty of deciduous forests, oak and its kin.

A passage that objects to a Japanese literary tradition that had loved only pine forests. It was the first to put into words the beauty of the mixed woodlands of Musashino.

One who strolls in Musashino must not mind losing the way. Whichever path you take, if you go where your feet lead, there will always be something to see, to hear, and to feel.

A passage arguing that the beauty of Musashino is found by walking its paths without aim. It shows Doppo's new eye, which found beauty not in famous sights but in the woods and field paths themselves.

Unforgettable People

Cross the Futako ferry over the Tama River, go on a little way, and you come to a post town called Mizonokuchi. About halfway through it stands an inn called the Kameya.

An opening that begins with a single night at a post town in March. A short story steeped in Doppo's poetic sensibility, on the strange inability to forget a stranger glimpsed only in passing.

The unforgettable are not necessarily those one must not forget.

The phrase that the unknown writer Ōtsu shows Akiyama as the first line of his manuscript. It distills the theme of the work: that the people we cannot forget are not those to whom we owe a debt, but those we passed only once on a journey.

Gyūniku to bareisho

“What I wish for is to be astonished.”

The unexpected wish Okamoto reveals amid the young men's debate over ideals and reality. It holds Doppo's own thought: to shake a heart grown used to habit and to be truly astonished at the world.
