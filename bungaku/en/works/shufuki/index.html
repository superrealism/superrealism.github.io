About this work

A man who wishes he had never been born, and K, a woman two years older who wishes the same. A short story by Dazai drawing a few days at a mountain inn for two people who have set out on a journey having sworn to speak neither of the past nor of tomorrow. The talk they exchange, with the smell of death about it, is bitter as well as dryly funny.

Summary

"Now then, what sort of novel ought I write? I live in the midst of a flood of stories." So this piece opens, with that question to myself. Even should I die, there is one sorrowful person who would carefully paint my dead face — K. K is two years older than I, thirty-two. We share no blood, but she has been in and out of my family like a sister since we were children, and now K, too, thinks, "I wish I had never been born." Before she had lived even ten years, she had already seen the most beautiful thing this world holds, and she has no regret whenever death should come. And yet K goes on living, for her child's sake, and for mine. "K, you must hate me, mustn't you." "Yes," K nods gravely. "There are even times I wish you would die." The two of us set off on a journey. We will speak of neither past nor tomorrow — not our family circumstances, nor our own hardships, nor the fears of tomorrow, nor what others may think, nor yesterday's shame — let there be nothing but stillness in this one span of time — so we vow, firmly, in silence. In the bath of a mountain inn, the two of us talk of this and that, nothing in particular — a scar from an appendix operation, legs too long, the sound of the mountain stream, a window filled with autumn leaves. "Say, isn't there some word for a good evil deed?" K asks, and when I say, "I thought you meant to die together with me," K laughs and says, "A bad good deed, then." At the journey's end, K is slightly hurt in an accident and returns by car, while I return alone by train. A few days later, I buy a bronze ring in Ginza — set with a yellow stone shaped into a narcissus flower — and send it to K. What K sends back in return is a photograph of her three-year-old eldest daughter. This morning, I looked at that photograph.

Characters

The opening (original text)

Standing still, lost in thought, everything takes on the air of a story — (Ikuta Chōkō) Now then, what sort of novel ought I write? I live in the midst of a flood of stories. I ought to have become an actor. I can sketch even my own sleeping face. Even should I die, there is one sorrowful person who would carefully paint my dead face. K would surely be the one to do it.

Shūfūki by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

That night, I had a little money on me, just received from the magazine company. The bronze ring was set with a single narcissus flower fashioned from a yellow stone. I sent it off to K. In return, K sent me a photograph of her eldest daughter, three years old this year. This morning, I looked at that photograph.
