About this work

A novella by Kobayashi Takiji, published under the changed title "An Age of Transition" in Chūō Kōron in April and May 1933, just after the author was tortured to death by the special higher police: his last work. It draws exactly, in the first person and with the tension of an underground life, the days in which the narrator, having thrown himself into illegal activity, organizes a struggle against the dismissal of temporary workers at a munitions plant. It looks as far as his parting from his mother and the rightness of his relationship with Kasahara, with whom he lives: a problem work closing the history of proletarian literature.

Summary

"I," gone underground, work at the Kurata Industrial plant under a false identity, pushing forward the organizing of the workers together with Suyama and Itō. Riding a wartime munitions boom, the plant has taken on large numbers of temporary workers, men who can be dismissed at any moment. We hand out leaflets, form reading groups and circles, and prepare a struggle that reaches across the divide between temporary and permanent workers. Kasahara, the woman I lodge with, provides the "home" my activities need, and though I feel a pain over the shape of my relationship with her, I give priority to the movement all the same. The police begin to close in on the factory, and I leave my post and go into hiding while continuing to direct the work. My mother comes from my home province to see me, but, unable to say much of anything as a man living outside the law, I exchange with her a parting that may well be our last. In time the company strikes its own "pre-emptive blow" of dismissals, and both Suyama and Itō are driven from the plant. But the comrades who scatter become new sparks in their own respective workplaces, and the spores of the organization spread all the wider for it. "Right now, Suyama, Itō and I are working with more energy than ever on new tasks" — with this the notes close.

Characters

  • I　An underground activist organizing workers at a munitions plant. The narrator of this piece
  • Suyama　A comrade, glib but likeable
  • Itō　A young comrade who organizes the women workers
  • Kasahara　The woman I live with, who provides me a place to stay
  • Mother　Comes to see me, worried for my safety

The opening (original text)

As I was washing my hands at the sink, it seemed that the men from the second plant were just heading home beneath the window, and a stream of loud voices went on along with the shuffle of wooden sandals and shoes. "Not done yet?" Just then Suyama came up from behind and spoke to me. He worked at the second plant.

Tō seikatsusha by Kobayashi Takiji (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Those fellows probably believe that by striking their "pre-emptive blow" they have smashed our work to pieces — but they have no idea that it was by their own hand, none other, that they went and blew the spores of our organization even further afield! Right now, Suyama, Itō and I are working with more energy than ever on new tasks...
