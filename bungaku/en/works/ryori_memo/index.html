About this work

Fine eating comes to a full stop at the pufferfish — memoranda in which Kitaōji Rosanjin, the gourmet who ran the Hoshigaoka Saryō, set down in points, for each ingredient in turn — sweetfish, sea bream, soft-shelled turtle, pufferfish — its season, how to choose it and how to eat it. In each short line, assertion following assertion, his merciless attitude to food is compressed.

Summary

"The best time to eat ayu is from the first young fish of the season through early July" — so begins this memorandum, with the entry on ayu (sweetfish). Ones grown as large as mackerel are no good; they are best before they carry roe. Every region boasts of its own ayu, Rosanjin notes, but in the end what matters is eating them fresh, straight from the catch. Only "belly-intact" fish, with the innards left in, will do — though nine times out of ten, the ones that reach Tokyo have already been gutted. Sashimi of live ayu, washed and sliced, is the king of preparations: four to six slices from a single fish. A lively fish should be salt-grilled; a poor one, glazed and grilled. Salt-grilled ayu should be eaten head first — the extract inside the head is what's delicious. The pronouncements continue in this vein. What follows, entry after entry, are the season, selection, preparation, and manner of eating for sea bream, bonito, softshell turtle, and fugu (pufferfish). For softshell turtle soup, the Ōichi style of Kyoto is best of all — no kelp stock, no bonito stock needed whatsoever. For simmering, eight parts water to two parts sake, with a little light soy sauce. And last of all comes the fugu. "Fine eating ends with the fugu. The proof is that once fugu is served, nothing else can be eaten." No sashimi surpasses fugu sashimi; the part called the "Tōtōmi," between the flesh and the skin, is even better than the skin itself; the deliciousness of fugu is beyond comparison with softshell turtle or anything else. Having declared this outright, he adds: "There is no danger in the fugu of Shimonoseki." The piece closes with the observation that fugu carries a flavor beyond the ordinary — a flavor, like sake or tobacco, that one cannot give up.

The opening (original text)

Ayu * The best time to eat them is from the first young ayu of the season through early July. Ones grown as large as mackerel are no good. Best of all before they carry roe. * Every ayu-producing region boasts of its own, but in the end what matters most is eating them fresh, straight from the catch.

Ryōri memo by Kitaōji Rosanjin (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Fugu * Fine eating ends with the fugu. The proof is that once fugu is served, nothing else can be eaten. * No sashimi surpasses fugu sashimi. * The deliciousness of fugu is beyond comparison with softshell turtle or anything else — no fine eating compares to it. * Fugu carries a flavor beyond the ordinary — a flavor, like sake or tobacco, that one cannot give up.

Ryōri memo by Kitaōji Rosanjin (Aozora Bunko)
