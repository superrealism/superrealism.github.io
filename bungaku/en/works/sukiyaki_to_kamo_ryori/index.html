About this work

A piece in which Kitaōji Rosanjin, travelling in Europe and America, cuts up without reserve the sukiyaki of New York and the duck of the famous Paris restaurant La Tour d'Argent. His irritation at one-sided admiration of the West and his lament that the Japanese do not know Japan are set down in a sharp voice. The bluntness of the last line is very much his own.

Summary

Even before leaving Japan, Rosanjin had been hearing endless talk about French duck cookery. But he had privately suspected that, amid so much one-sided worship of the West, the truth of the matter was anyone's guess. The very people who kindly hold forth about France this and America that, he observes, don't actually know much about Japan — so the conversation is skewed from the start. Because Japanese who don't know Japan go abroad, they can't teach foreigners anything about it once they're there. If the only things they can boast of are Mount Fuji and geisha, or feeding crackers to the deer in Nara, how could anyone over there possibly understand Japanese cuisine? Rosanjin laments this first. As an example, he brings up sukiyaki in New York. When he went to try it, it wasn't sukiyaki at all — just something simmered in a pot with high sides like a tub. And then, at last, comes the duck at the famous Parisian restaurant Tour d'Argent. Going along with its pompous, ceremonial style of serving, Rosanjin takes out the wasabi soy sauce he had brought with him and eats it his own way. The brandy served was admittedly excellent, but when a companion, delighted by the novelty, was about to keep refilling his glass, Rosanjin checks him: "Just because it's a gift is no reason to get carried away drinking it — that would be a disgrace to the Japanese." There is no need, he says, to be needlessly intimidated just because a restaurant is famous. And a final remark: "Incidentally, everyone keeps saying duck, duck — but that's surely because the Japanese of old mistook the domestic duck for the wild one. The duck at Tour d'Argent, too, is in fact a domestic duck."

The opening (original text)

Even before leaving Japan, I had been hearing endless, insistent talk about French duck cookery. That was because so much of it amounted to one-sided worship of the West, and I had privately suspected that the truth of the matter was anyone's guess. The people who kindly go on about France this and America that don't actually know much when it comes to Japan — so the conversation is skewed from the very start.

Sukiyaki to kamo ryōri by Kitaōji Rosanjin (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Incidentally, everyone keeps saying duck, duck — but that's surely because the Japanese of old mistook the domestic duck for the wild one. The duck at Tour d'Argent, too, is in fact a domestic duck. Eaten with wasabi soy sauce, it was, for a domestic duck, quite good indeed.

Sukiyaki to kamo ryōri by Kitaōji Rosanjin (Aozora Bunko)
