About this work

A long work Dazai wrote for publication and issued by the Asahi Shimbun company in 1945. Although it was commissioned by the Cabinet Information Bureau and the Patriotic Association for Literature, Dazai kept the smell of propaganda out of it and drew the days when the young Lu Xun — Zhou Shuren — studied at the Sendai medical college, as a clean story of friendship told in the recollections of an old doctor who was his classmate. A distinctive and substantial work following, in an affectionate hand, a young man who took up medicine in a foreign country and turned in time to literature.

Summary

A newspaper reporter comes calling on an old doctor practising in a certain village in the Tōhoku region, asking him to tell his memories of being classmates with a Chinese exchange student, Zhou Shuren — later Lu Xun — at the Sendai medical college. The old doctor begins to look back on his student days some forty years before, spent in Sendai in the Meiji era. Mr. Zhou, a taciturn young man who had come alone from Qing China to study. As he studies in earnest, hoping to save the people of his homeland through medicine, "I" gradually come to understand him, and a friendship grows between us. Under the warm guidance of Professor Fujino in anatomy, in talks at their lodgings, amid the incomprehension and prejudice of the Japanese students — bearing his loneliness in a foreign land, Mr. Zhou comes to feel more and more certain that it is the spirit of his countrymen, before medicine, that must be changed. One day, seeing images of his fellow countrymen shown by magic lantern, Mr. Zhou at last resolves to give up medicine and turn to literature. With a heart full of the sorrow of parting, "I" see off my friend, bound for his homeland in pursuit of a "new life."

Characters

  • I (the old doctor)　Was a classmate of Mr. Zhou's at the Sendai medical college, now a village doctor in Tōhoku
  • Mr. Zhou (Zhou Shuren)　A taciturn student who has come from Qing China to study at the Sendai medical college. The later Lu Xun
  • Professor Fujino　The anatomy teacher who guides Mr. Zhou with warmth

The opening (original text)

This is the written account of an old doctor practising in a certain village in the Tōhoku region of Japan. Some days ago a middle-aged man of poor complexion, wearing a slovenly beard and calling himself a reporter for the local newspaper, came to see me and asked whether it was true, as he had heard, that I had graduated from the Sendai Medical School, the forerunner of the present medical faculty of Tōhoku Imperial University. That is correct, I answered.

Sekibetsu by Dazai Osamu (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"What sort of name is it?" "New Life." He answered with just that one word, and smiled. In that smile there was not the faintest trace of the servility of what he himself used to call the "slave's smile."
