What is Kusazōshi?

Category
Form / Genre

A general term for popular illustrated reading matter published in Edo, in which pictures were primary and text was written in the surrounding margin. Sold as inexpensive publications made up of several slim booklets bound together, they were widely read through lending libraries. They were distinguished by the color of their covers, beginning with "akahon" ("red books"), carrying children's folk tales, and developing through "kurohon" ("black books") and "aohon" ("blue books"), until, in 1775, Koikawa Harumachi produced "Kinkin Sensei Eiga no Yume," both text and pictures by his own hand, giving rise to "kibyōshi" ("yellow covers") — wit and satire aimed at adult readers. Kibyōshi made a hallmark of clever, knowing humor playfully mocking the fashions and social conditions of the day, but they came under heavy censorship during the Kansei Reforms, and thereafter the form shifted to "gōkan," longer works bound from several volumes sewn together. In gōkan, Ryūtei Tanehiko's "Nise Murasaki Inaka Genji" ("A Fraudulent Murasaki's Rustic Genji," 1829–42), transplanting the world of "The Tale of Genji" into the Muromachi period, became, together with Utagawa Kunisada's illustrations, the best-selling work of the Edo period — but this too was ordered out of print during the Tenpō Reforms. As a counterpart to the yomihon, centered on text, the lineage of this illustrated reading matter, in which picture and text formed a single whole, also carries through to the illustrated reading matter of later ages.
