About this work

The work that made Umezaki Haruo's name, published in the first issue of "Sunao" in 1946. Drawing on his own service in the navy, it depicts the days just before the defeat, and is a representative work of the first wave of postwar writers. Petty Officer Murakami, serving as a code clerk at the base at Bōnotsu, is ordered to Sakurajima — the front line for the decisive battle on the homeland, a place where he knows he will die. Asked by a prostitute with one ear gone, "And you will die there, won't you," he waits on Sakurajima for the day that must come, face to face with the half-crazed Warrant Officer Kira. A piece that cuts into the tension, the tedium and the absurdity mixed together in the time of men with death in front of them, in a prose of great restraint.

Summary

In early July, a telegram reaches Petty Officer Murakami, a code clerk working the base signals at the pass above Bōnotsu, ordering him to Sakurajima. That night he drinks alcohol cut with water until he is far gone, slips down the cliff and cuts his eyelid. The next morning the landscape of Bōnotsu, which he will never see again as long as he lives, looks frighteningly fresh. At a brothel in a town on the way he spends a night with a prostitute whose right ear is gone. "It is a fine place," she says; "there is fruit on the trees all year round." And then she asks, over and over, "And you will die there, won't you." "How will you die?" What waits for Murakami at the radar station on Sakurajima is a life of digging holes, being scolded over the form of his salute, drinking, and being eaten by suspicion, while they wait for a landing that may come at any hour. The men under him are numbed with death before them, and the section leader, Warrant Officer Kira, grips his sword and declares that he will cut down every cowardly, irresolute man among them. "If the enemy lands, do you think we will win?" Kira presses him, and at the very moment Murakami answers that whether one wins or loses cannot be known until the time comes, a sailor comes in at the mouth of the shelter and says that the midday broadcast is the imperial rescript ending the war.

Characters

  • Petty Officer Murakami　A navy code clerk ordered from Bōnotsu to Sakurajima. The narrator
  • Warrant Officer Kira　Section leader at the Sakurajima base. Gripping his sword, he declares he will cut down the cowardly and irresolute
  • The prostitute　A woman with her right ear gone, at a brothel in a town on the way. She asks Murakami how he will die
  • Sub-Lieutenant Tani　Commander of the forward watch unit at Bōnotsu. "To die beautifully — that is nothing but sentiment," he says

The opening (original text)

In early July I was at Bōnotsu. It is the place from which the embassies to Tang China once set sail. On the pass that looks down over that small beautiful harbor, I was working the base signals for the base unit. I was a code clerk. Every day I slid down the cliff to go fishing, or went up the hill to pick bayberries, or made friends with the girl clerk from the Bōnotsu post office who passed over the pass morning and evening — to an outsider I passed my days idly enough.

Sakurajima by Umezaki Haruo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

When we came out of the shelter, the sunset was shining brightly on the sea. The road ran through a dusk that had begun to lose its color. Warrant Officer Kira walked ahead. Above the cliff stood Mount Sakurajima, dyed by the setting sun. As I walked, the flank of the mountain, showing and hiding itself among the trees, dyed in gradations of red and blue, was of a beauty not of this world. Hurrying over the stony road so as not to fall behind Warrant Officer Kira, I felt hot tears come out of my eyes all at once, as though they would scald the lids. Wipe them as I would, they went on falling without end.

Sakurajima by Umezaki Haruo (Aozora Bunko)
