Biography

Born in 1914 in Keijō (now Seoul) and raised in Tokushima; his real name was Shichijō Kōji. At twenty he contracted Hansen's disease, and the following year entered the Zenshō Hospital (now the National Sanatorium Tama Zenshōen) in Kita-Tama District, Tokyo Prefecture. He sent Kawabata Yasunari "The First Night of Life," a work that stared unflinchingly at life and death from within the sanatorium, and on Kawabata's recommendation it was published in the magazine "Bungakukai" and won that magazine's prize. "Hōjō Tamio" was the pen name given him to conceal his illness, and his real name remained hidden for a long time. Just as he had found his resolve to live for literature, he died of intestinal tuberculosis in 1937 at the age of 23. The handful of stories he left in his brief career still move readers today.
