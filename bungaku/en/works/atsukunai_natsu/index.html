About this work

A white ceiling, white walls, white sheets. A woman of twenty who cannot leave her bed laughs and says that for her there is no longer summer or winter — a very short piece Yamakawa Masao wrote for the evening edition of the Asahi Shimbun. Having lost the use of her senses to a disease of one in several million, and been given a year to live, she asks a classmate from her university what summer was like. Walking out of the sick room into the summer streets, he is struck by a sudden shudder.

Summary

"...Summer has come, hasn't it." The woman said it, looking up at the ceiling. Outside the window is a summer sky of deep ultramarine from edge to edge. In this room on the fifth floor, lying as she does, she can see nothing but the sky. He and she had been classmates at the university. "Please, draw the curtain," she says, and goes on in a voice that seems to make light of it: "For me there is no longer summer or winter. I feel nothing any more. It is neither hot nor cold; it is as if I were always in an airtight room. All of it is only somebody else's summer, somebody else's winter." It was one of those diseases that come to one person in tens of thousands, or in millions. Last summer, at twenty, she suddenly ran a high fever, and her whole body stopped moving as it was. Her mind is clear, but she has almost no sense in any of her five senses, and the doctor said plainly that she would not last more than a year at most. That term is now close at hand. "I envy you, not knowing the heat," he says, brightly on purpose. "Perhaps in time civilization will advance and human beings will regulate the temperature and drive summer and winter out of this world.... I seem to be living in that country of the future." But before long she closes her eyes and says, "I can no longer remember summer clearly," and lets fall tears. "Tell me, what was summer like? What is it, to be hot?" ... Leaving her asleep, he goes out in front of the hospital, and walking through the summer streets he suddenly stops. It is not hot at all. If anything, he feels almost chilly. The dazzling sunlight is gone; there is no summer anywhere. He has no summer either.

Characters

  • The woman　Twenty years old, in her sickbed, her five senses lost to a rare disease
  • He　Her classmate from the university

The opening (original text)

"...Summer has come, hasn't it." The woman said it, looking up at the ceiling. A white ceiling. White walls. White sheets. The woman's face is white too. "I can tell by looking at the sky. Until just the other day it was nothing but dull days with the sky muddied. It was the color of a sheet of Kent paper dropped into water.... And now, look — it has turned that deep blue, and there are clouds like swelling muscles."

Atsukunai natsu by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

A shower was about to come. At last summer had come back to him. He began to walk. The sky darkened and great drops began to fall; and yet in his heart he was gripping tightly the memory of that one instant just now when he had felt that he too had no summer. Only then had he touched the "country of the future" where she lives; and that memory alone might be the one thread that bound her to him.... Black clouds spread rapidly over the sky and the rain came thick. Walking on purpose through that summer rain, he went on wiping his face with his handkerchief.

Atsukunai natsu by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)
