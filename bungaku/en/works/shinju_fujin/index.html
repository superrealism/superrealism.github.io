About this work

The newspaper novel serialized in the Osaka Mainichi and Tokyo Nichinichi in 1920 that made Kikuchi Kan a popular author overnight. Ruriko, a beautiful young woman married to a nouveau riche businessman to save her father, gives herself neither to her husband nor to any other man, and takes her revenge as a woman who plays with men. Using the frame of popular fiction, it drew the revolt of a woman who had been the property of men under a patriarchal system, and became the model for the Japanese popular romance. "Madame Pearl" is what they call Ruriko, who kept her chastity as unstained as a pearl.

Summary

Atsumi Shin'ichirō, a newly married company employee, is on his way to Yugawara when the young man sharing his car, Aoki Jun, is fatally injured; with his dying words Jun entrusts him with the plea "Give back the watch" and the name "Ruriko." That Ruriko is Shōda Ruriko, a beautiful widow rumored to be a temptress who toys with men. The story goes back two years. Karasawa Ruriko, the daughter of a baron, had a pure love for Naoya, the eldest son of Viscount Sugino; but the businessman Shōda Katsuhei, whom the two had mocked as a "nouveau riche" at a garden party, took his revenge by buying up her father's debts and setting a trap, and her father was charged with embezzlement. To save her father, Ruriko marries into the Shōda house of her own will. But from the night of the wedding Ruriko will not let her husband lay a finger on her. Having a wife and yet not having her, Shōda suffers in his frustration and dies at the villa one stormy night. Inheriting an enormous fortune, Ruriko gathers young men in her magnificent house, catching their hearts and casting them off, and giving herself to none of them. Those drawn to her are ruined one after another. Shin'ichirō too, coming near her, learns of the sorrow beneath that icy manner. But the chain of revenge swallows Ruriko herself, and she meets an end that no one had foreseen.

Characters

  • Shōda Ruriko　The daughter of Baron Karasawa, married to Shōda to save her father, who keeps her chastity intact and takes her revenge on men
  • Atsumi Shin'ichirō　A newly married company employee, who comes near to Ruriko's secret and serves as the narrating figure
  • Shōda Katsuhei　A nouveau riche businessman who has trapped Ruriko's father. He takes Ruriko to wife and can never make her his wife
  • Sugino Naoya　The eldest son of Viscount Sugino, whom Ruriko once loved
  • Karasawa Kōichi　Ruriko's elder brother, a painter, who paints his sister's portrait
  • Shōda Minako　The daughter of Shōda's first wife, who is devoted to Ruriko

A Strange Misfortune 1 From about the time the train left Ōfuna, Shin'ichirō's mind was filled with an impatience that grew fiercer and fiercer. That the train had to make its small stops at five or six more stations before Kōzu irritated him considerably. The ending (original text)

It was the standing figure of a young woman of a pure and noble beauty, like a pearl. And the success of the portrait, it was said, lay in the brightness of expression skillfully caught in her face — the intelligent, spiritual look peculiar to a modern woman conscious of herself. Anyone who saw the painting close at hand would have noticed the signature K. K. at the right-hand edge. It was the great work of Karasawa Kōichi, who had devoted himself to art under his sister's protection, completed in the tears of his grief for her violent death, and it must have been the only offering he could make to her. What will become of Minako's fortunes now that she has lost Ruriko is a matter of the future, and the author of this novel does not know. But I think we may safely leave her in Naoya's hands.

Shinju fujin by Kikuchi Kan (Aozora Bunko)
