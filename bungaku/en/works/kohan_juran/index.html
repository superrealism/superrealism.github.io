About this work

A representative short story by Hisao Jūran, published in "Bungei" in 1937. "This summer, for reasons I could not avoid, I killed your mother at the villa at Mitsuishi on the shore of Lake Ashi in Hakone, and that same day gave myself up to the Tokyo procurator's office" — it takes the form of a long memoir left by a man born into a former daimyo house for a son not yet two years old. Raised by a severe father, disfigured in a duel, bound all his life by suspicion and by vanity: married to the one girl who was not afraid of that face, how did he come to this? The facts, told in a coldly composed style, converge at the last upon a single design.

Summary

I was born in 1866 inside the castle of Nagasaka Matsuyama — so "I," the head of a former daimyo house, sets down his life in a memoir addressed to his son. Whipped into shape by a father who meant to make an English-style aristocrat of him, he acquired an insincere nature that feigned a love of learning to keep the accounts square. At nineteen he crossed to England and led a life of wretched dissipation, but with his fierce face and his gloomy manner no one loved him, and he was still searching for something true even among prostitutes. In Paris he accepted a challenge to a duel, and because he flinched and turned his head the ball shattered his right temple and the shell of his ear, leaving an ugly scar on his face. To a man of his vanity it was an unbearable injury. Back in Japan he met Tō, a girl of eighteen rowing a boat on Lake Ashi in Hakone. She alone said his scar "looks gallant, and even fine." They married and built a villa on the shore. But out of suspicion he set about remaking Tō into the wife of a nobleman, disciplining her sternly, and, too self-conscious to show affection, behaved brutally. Before long Tō fell into dissolute days at Hakone among vulgar literary men — and then it happened. Having given himself up and been released as of unsound mind, he lays down his pen resolved to disappear — and yet.

Characters

  • I (Okudaira)　The head of a former daimyo house. Disfigured in a duel, he has lived bound by suspicion and by vanity — the writer of the memoir
  • Tō　The second daughter of a Yokohama raw-silk broker. He met her at eighteen, and she was the only person not afraid of his scar
  • You　The son, not yet two years old, to whom the memoir is addressed
  • Takagi　A man who came and went at the villa. He wept without stopping through the whole funeral

The opening (original text)

This summer, for reasons I could not avoid, I killed your mother at the villa at Mitsuishi on the shore of Lake Ashi in Hakone, and that same day gave myself up to the Tokyo procurator's office. On examination I was judged of unsound mind, found not criminally responsible, and released; but before a month had passed I found myself, absurdly enough, liable to punishment once more.

Lakeside by Hisao Jūran (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Looking up from below, one saw Takagi hanging there, the temple where the white hair had suddenly increased shining silver, with a resigned look on his face and something knowing about it. The bones had broken so badly that I felt the satisfaction of having finished off some fine work of art, and for a while I stood gazing vacantly up at him with my hands in my sleeves.

Lakeside by Hisao Jūran (Aozora Bunko)
