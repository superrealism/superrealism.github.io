What is Bungakukai (Meiji)?

Category
Society / Group

A monthly literary magazine founded in January 1893. Young writers — Hoshino Tenchi, Kitamura Tōkoku, Shimazaki Tōson, Hirata Tokuboku, and Togawa Shūkotsu — broke away from the Christian magazine "Jogaku Zasshi" to found it, and Baba Kochō and Ueda Bin later joined as well. Tōkoku became its ideological pillar, championing the nobility of love and the freedom of the inner life in essays such as "On Inner Life" (1893), and the magazine became a base of the Japanese Romantic literary movement. Higuchi Ichiyō had contributed to it since "Yuki no Hi" ("A Snowy Day") in 1893, and it was here that she published "Ōtsugomori" ("The Last Day of the Year") and "Takekurabe" ("Growing Up") and was discovered. After Tōkoku's death in 1894, the pages were colored by Tōson's lyric poetry and by Ueda Bin and others' introductions of Western European literature, and the magazine ceased publication in January 1898 after 58 issues. Note that the literary magazine "Bungakukai," founded in 1933 and continuing to this day, shares the name but has no direct connection to this earlier magazine.
