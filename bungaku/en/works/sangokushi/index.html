About this work

The historical novel Yoshikawa Eiji serialized in the Chūgai Shōgyō Shinpō and elsewhere from 1939 to 1943, which settled how Japan pictures the Three Kingdoms. It begins in the disorder at the end of the Later Han with Liu Bei, a young man selling straw mats, swearing brotherhood with Guan Yu and Zhang Fei in the peach garden, and carries the rise and fall of the warlords — Cao Cao, that brilliant villain, and Sun Quan in the east — through to the death of the strategist Zhuge Liang on the Wuzhang Plains. It follows neither the older Japanese version nor the Chinese Romance of the Three Kingdoms closely, adding interpretation and invention throughout to deepen the characters and the poetry; "there is poetry in the Three Kingdoms," the author wrote in his preface. This app carries all twelve volumes.

Summary

Characters

  • Liu Bei (Xuande)　A young mat-seller of Han imperial blood, loved for his benevolence, who comes at last to found Shu
  • Guan Yu (Yunchang)　Xuande's sworn younger brother, a warrior of duty and loyalty who wields the Green Dragon crescent blade
  • Zhang Fei (Yide)　Xuande's sworn younger brother, a bold and open-hearted fighter
  • Cao Cao (Mengde)　Called the crafty hero of a disordered age, he lays the foundation of Wei
  • Sun Quan (Zhongmou)　He succeeds his brother Sun Ce and holds Wu in the lands east of the river
  • Zhuge Kongming　The strategist called the Sleeping Dragon, who enters Xuande's service after three visits to his hut
  • Sima Yi (Zhongda)　The able commander of Wei who becomes Kongming's greatest rival
