About this work

A short story Mori Ōgai wrote from his experience of studying in Germany and published in Shigarami Sōshi in 1890; with "The Dancing Girl" and "The Courier," one of the German trilogy. It draws the doomed love of Kose, a Japanese art student at the academy in Munich, and the beautiful flower girl Marie, in a dignified elegant style coloured with fantasy and lyricism. Marie's strange history is bound up with the tragedy of Ludwig II of Bavaria, of whose madness much was rumoured, and their love comes to an unlooked-for end. A fine romantic piece from Ōgai's early period.

Summary

Koze, a Japanese art student at the academy in Munich, finds himself drawn to Marie, a beautiful flower-seller he sees about the city. This girl, who also poses as a model at the art school, has an extraordinary history behind her. Her late mother, it is said, was a village girl once singled out by King Ludwig II of Bavaria, whose madness was already the talk of the land, and Marie is said to be the very image of her mother. As Koze and Marie are drawn to one another, they come to carry within their love the shadow of a sad destiny bound up with the royal house. One day, when the two go out together to the shore of Lake Starnberg, a great incident occurs upon that very lake: King Ludwig II, deposed and held in confinement, meets a mysterious death by drowning together with his physician. Amid the uproar over the king's sudden death, on that same lake, the daughter of the fisherman Hansl — none other than Marie herself — also drowns, quietly, unnoticed. But her death is drowned out by the rumors of the king's, and no one spares her a thought. Koze, having lost his beloved, kneels, wasted and grief-stricken, beneath the painting of the Lorelei in his studio. A masterpiece of Ōgai's early years, rendered in an elevated classical style steeped in fantasy and lyricism, weaving together the ill-fated love of a young artist and the tragedy of a mad king.

Characters

  • Koze　A Japanese art student at the academy in Munich. Loves the flower-seller Marie, and is left grief-stricken after losing her
  • Marie　A beautiful flower-seller. Her late mother was a village girl once singled out by the king, and Marie is the very image of her. Drowns in the lake on the same day as the king
  • Ludwig II　The King of Bavaria, whose madness is widely rumored. Deposed from the throne, he meets a mysterious death by drowning in Lake Starnberg

The opening (original text)

Part One Upon a chariot drawn by several lions stands, boldly erect, the statue of the goddess Bavaria — set upon this Arch of Triumph, they say, by the former king, Ludwig the First. Below it, where Ludwigstrasse bends to the left, rises a great building raised up of marble quarried from Trient. This is the art academy, famed as one of the sights of the Bavarian capital.

Utakata no ki by Mori Ōgai (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

On the morning of June fifteenth, the students of the art academy, having gone out to receive the king's coffin as it was borne from Berg Castle into the capital at midnight and returned, withdrew to the Café Minerva; and Ekstel, struck by some misgiving, went in to look at Koze's studio, and found him there, his face changed after these three days as though he had grown markedly thinner, kneeling beneath the painting of the Lorelei. Drowned out by the rumors of the king's sudden death, the fact that a single daughter of the fisherman Hansl, of Leoni nearby, had drowned at that very same hour, passed with no one to ask after it, and so came to an end.
