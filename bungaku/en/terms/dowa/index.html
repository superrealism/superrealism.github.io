What is Dōwa (Children's Stories)?

Category
Form / Genre

The genre and term, established during the Taishō era, for original stories written by adult authors for children. Replacing the earlier moralistic "otogibanashi" (fairy tales), it began in earnest as a movement toward artistically ambitious reading matter that spoke to a child's sensibility, sparked by the 1918 founding of Suzuki Miekichi's magazine "Akai Tori." Miekichi himself also worked on retellings such as "Kojiki Monogatari" ("The Story of the Kojiki"), and the inaugural issue carried Akutagawa Ryūnosuke's "The Spider's Thread," while the 1932 issue carried Niimi Nankichi's "Gon Gitsune" ("Gon the Fox"). Miyazawa Kenji of Hanamaki, though only loosely connected to "Akai Tori" itself, became a leading writer in this genre with the fantastical stories rooted in his own distinctive cosmology and religious vision — his only story collection published in his lifetime, "Chūmon no Ōoi Ryōriten" ("The Restaurant of Many Orders," 1924), and the body of work published after his death. The children's-story movement spread further through the Shōwa era and laid the foundations of Japanese children's literature.
