Biography

Born in Hirosaki, Aomori Prefecture, in 1887. He left the English literature department of Waseda University without graduating and, working with the coterie magazine "Kiseki," won recognition with "Kanashiki Chichi" ("Sorrowful Father"). He went on writing down just as it was his own broken life of poverty and illness, drink and women, and was called "the god of the I-novel." Leaving his wife and children behind in his home province to live with his mistress, and piling up debts, he produced such fine works as "Ko o Tsurete" ("With My Children"), "Shii no Wakaba" ("Young Leaves of the Oak"), and "Kohan Shuki" ("Lakeside Notes"). He exerted a strong influence on younger writers, Kamura Isota among them, but died of tuberculosis in 1928 at the age of 41.
