About this work

A woman whose face and name he never knew, with whom he spent seven nights in a room screened round by folding screens, nearly ten years ago. A short story in which a man who has gone on refusing offers of marriage, bound to that memory, at last reaches the truth. His father only told him to forget it. The ending, in which Yajūrō closes his eyes and feels as though he were watching the screens being folded away, is among the most unforgettable in Yamamoto Shūgorō's short fiction.

Summary

Yoshimura Yajūrō received that letter three times, and all three times threw it away as soon as he had read it. Since his match with the Kitajima had just been settled, he supposed it was somebody's prank. The sender was named only "Yuki," and the contents were always the same: that she was the nurse of a certain house, and that the young lady she had brought up had seen him and had fallen so ill with longing that she was almost sick, and would he not come once and see her. What was written was too old-fashioned and too much to a pattern. "Hm; some people have time on their hands," Yajūrō muttered. ... But he had a memory he could not forget. Nearly ten years ago, in a room shut about with a folding screen, he had spent seven nights with a woman. He never knew who she was; there remained only the throaty whisper at his ear, the hot skin, and the small, supple body. The woman appeared soundlessly out of the dark and always went before daybreak. Neither his father nor his mother would tell him: his mother said she knew nothing at all, and his father only said, "Forget it." Learning in time the name Chiyo, and believing that word must certainly come, Yajūrō refuses even when the Kitajima send to say that the wedding may be held. But no word comes, the year turns, and only the vividness of the memory grows exaggerated. On the night when he is told the truth of the matter, Yajūrō closed his eyes. And behind those closed eyes he seemed to see a pair of folding screens being folded up, and all at once his breast was lighter.

Characters

  • Yoshimura Yajūrō　A man bound by the memory of seven nights behind a screen
  • Chiyo　The woman who was within the screen
  • Yuki　The one who signs the letters as a nurse
  • Iyojirō　Yajūrō's father, who says "forget it"

The ending (original text)

Behind those (closed) eyes he seemed to see a pair of folding screens being folded up. And all at once he felt his breast lighter and his breathing easier. Yajūrō begged pardon for having disturbed them at such an hour, and having taken his leave he came out into the corridor and murmured with a forlorn smile: "Yes — the screens have been folded up." And he mimicked his father: "Forget it."
