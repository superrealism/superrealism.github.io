What is Chūsei Bungaku (Medieval Literature)?

Category
Historical Period

The literature of roughly four hundred years, from the establishment of the Kamakura shogunate at the end of the twelfth century to the Azuchi-Momoyama period. As warriors rose to prominence in place of the aristocracy, and against a backdrop of repeated warfare and natural disaster, a Buddhist sense of the impermanence of this world formed the basic tone of literature. The war chronicle "The Tale of the Heike" sings the glory and fall of the Taira clan as an epic in stately mixed Sino-Japanese prose, while Kamo no Chōmei's "Hōjōki" ("An Account of My Hut") and Yoshida Kenkō's "Tsurezuregusa" ("Essays in Idleness") crystallized in essay form the wisdom and aesthetic sensibility of living in an impermanent world. In waka, the imperial anthology "Shin Kokin Wakashū" (1205), compiled by Fujiwara no Teika and others, pursued a refined, dreamlike beauty of suggestion. Further, renga — linked verse in which multiple poets in turn compose the upper and lower halves of a waka — spread from the aristocracy to the common people, the father-and-son team of Kan'ami and Zeami perfected noh theater on the principle of yūgen (mysterious depth), and simple short tales known as otogizōshi were born as well: an age in which both the bearers of literature and the breadth of its expression expanded greatly.
