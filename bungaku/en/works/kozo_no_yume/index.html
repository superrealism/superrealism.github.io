About this work

A novella in the form of the notes of a boy apprenticed at a shop in Ginza, serialized in the Fukuoka Nichinichi Shimbun in March and April 1917. A sixteen-year-old, longing for art and made to walk the merchant's road, writes down his revolt against naturalism, his intoxication with music, and his experience of hypnosis at a show tent in Asakusa. An unusual early work in which Tanizaki's own view of art is spoken through a boy's mouth.

Summary

I am Shōtarō, sixteen years old, an apprentice boy at Ikedaya, a Western liquor shop on Ginza Sanchōme. Even while being scolded by the head clerk, my head is full of wild, unfounded fancies. What is art, really? When I ask the young mistress, she just laughs in my face; I read Shakespeare and cannot grasp its subtlety; and I cannot bring myself to agree with the arguments of Japan's naturalist writers. But the sound of Tannhäuser drifting over from the phonograph shop across the street sets my very soul drunk. Art is not the imitation of nature — that much I grasp intuitively. In time I begin skimming money from the register, and, having taken to drink, start going to Asakusa. There I see the magic act of the beautiful Russian, Miss Mary. I write in my diary the intoxication of the moment I was brought up onto the stage and hypnotized.

Characters

  • Shōtarō　A sixteen-year-old apprentice at a Western liquor shop in Ginza. The writer of these notes
  • The young mistress　The daughter of Ikedaya. Teases Shōtarō, calling him "Dorayaki"
  • Miss Mary　A beautiful Russian woman appearing at a sideshow tent in Asakusa. Performs hypnotism as magic

The opening (original text)

...My name is Shōtarō, and I am a shop apprentice, sixteen years old this year. The shop I work at, called Ikedaya, a Western liquor shop, stands on the main street of Ginza Sanchōme, and most people living around Kyōbashi probably know it. Coming from the direction of Nihonbashi, crossing Kyōbashi, and going five or six blocks, there is, on the left-hand side, a two-story Western-style building, not especially grand but with a neat, tidy shop window.

Kozō no yume by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

Ah, how dull, how dull it all is. I want, somehow, to meet Miss Mary once more. Even if I cannot meet her again, I want to encounter, once more, that same scene and that same pleasure I felt when caught under her magic. Unable to write poetry or paint pictures, I want at least, in some field like that, to satisfy my own hunger for art. ...Looking at today's crime page, there's an article about a delinquent girl of about twenty called Saegusa Mitsuko, who's become the boss of a gang of delinquent boys of fifteen or sixteen and is busy carrying out all sorts of crimes. Maybe I ought to run away from the shop myself, and get her to take me on as one of her followers. ...

Kozō no yume by Tanizaki Jun'ichirō (Aozora Bunko)
