About this work

A short story published in Taishū Bungei in 1926. One night a man called K begins to tell the story of another man possessed by lenses and mirrors. Enchanted from childhood by the strange world inside glass beads, lenses and mirrors, he lets the obsession grow as he ages until he shuts himself in a laboratory walled with every kind of mirror and sinks into a demon country of light and image. And then he goes inside the globe of mirrors he has built. A dazzling piece of horror in which Ranpo's gift for the grotesque and fantastic runs at full strength.

Summary

At a gathering where frightening and curious stories were being told, a friend named K began the last of them. — I had one unfortunate friend. From childhood he was abnormally taken with glass balls and lenses and mirrors. Playing with the old telescopes and glassware left by an ancestor who had gone over to the forbidden Christian sect, he grew an inexhaustible obsession with the strange world of light and images. When he came into a great fortune as a man, his passion only increased: he built a laboratory surrounded by countless lenses and mirrors, and sank without limit into the magic country of concave mirrors, convex mirrors and kaleidoscopes. And at last he made an enormous sphere lined entirely with mirror, and went inside it. The next morning, when he came out of the sphere, he had already lost his mind, and went on laughing madly. The little universe reflected inside that sphere of mirror was nothing of this world — and having stepped into the country of the mad, he saw what must not be seen and destroyed himself. It is one of Ranpo's finest pieces of weird fiction, drawing with a dazzling fantastic brush the horror that comes of an obsession with lenses and mirrors.

Characters

  • He　A man possessed since childhood by lenses and mirrors, who shuts himself in a laboratory ringed with mirrors and loses his mind at last inside a sphere of mirror
  • K　The friend who tells the story of that man at a gathering of strange tales
  • I　The narrator, who listens to K's telling

The opening (original text)

"A curious story, you say? Then how would this one do?" On a certain occasion, when five or six of us had been telling one another frightening and curious stories one after another, my friend K began in this way. Whether it really happened or was K's invention I do not know, since I have never asked him since; but perhaps because it came after a number of strange tales, and because the weather that day, near the end of spring, was so oddly overcast that the air lay as heavy and still as the bottom of deep water, so that teller and listener alike had grown a little unhinged, that story struck me strangely.

Kagami jigoku by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

He afterward left this world still mad, so that there is no way of making certain of the truth of it; and yet I at least have not been able, even now, to give up the notion that he destroyed himself precisely because he violated the inside of that sphere of mirror.

Kagami jigoku by Edogawa Ranpo (Aozora Bunko)
