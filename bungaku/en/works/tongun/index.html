About this work

A novel of the farming villages by Kuroshima Denji, published in 1926. Because of a tenancy dispute the fields lie untilled, and the tenant farmers of the village keep themselves alive by raising pigs. The landlord, the soy-sauce brewer, means to seize those pigs in place of the rent. If the bailiffs come, let every man turn his pigs out of the pen at once, so that no one can tell whose is whose — and out of the farmers who have agreed on this comes a traitor, a man who begrudges his own field and his own good pigs. Around the comic uproar of a herd of swine chased by court officers, the story shows in a dry hand what becomes of those who hold together and of the one who breaks ranks.

Summary

Kenji and his father have given themselves over to pig-keeping for half a year now. After the quarrel over the rent, the fields have lain untilled since last autumn, and every tenant in the village has turned pig-keeper overnight. One pig of thirty kan, slaughtered and sold, will feed a household for a month. The landlord, the soy-sauce brewer, meant to seize those pigs in place of the rent. So the farmers agreed that when the bailiffs came they would all at once turn their pigs out of the pens, so that no one could tell whose was whose. At the meeting not a single man spoke against it. But a traitor appeared. Uichi, who hated the thought of his barley field being trampled and begrudged having his own well-bred pigs exchanged for somebody's scrawny ones, was going about the village telling everyone not to let them loose. One morning three bailiffs come into the village. Kenji takes down his fence and drives out some ten head, but looking over the village from the hill he sees that only a few pigs have been loosed: half the households have kept theirs in. Before long the bailiffs strip off their coats, take up sticks, and charge into the herd. The more they chase, the further the pigs pour out into the fields, and in front of the barley Uichi stands guarding his own ground, beating the pigs back furiously. A whole day's work, and the officers manage to seal only the two or three sheds whose owners had never let their pigs out at all. Two weeks later Kenji climbs the hill and hears, from Uichi's sealed shed, the groaning of starving pigs.

Characters

  • Kenji　A young tenant farmer. He had gone out to work at the soy-sauce brewer's in town, was dismissed over the dispute, and now keeps pigs with his father
  • Father　Kenji's father, who hesitates to turn out the sow that is carrying young
  • Uichi　A tenant of the same village, who betrays the agreement out of regard for his field and his pigs
  • Tomekichi　A tenant who had left his wife and children in the village to go out to work. He looses his pigs along with Kenji
  • The bailiffs　The three officers who come to put seals on the pig sheds

The opening (original text)

1 The sow walked slowly and as if short of breath, dragging her red, inflamed belly over the filthy floorboards. After walking a while and eating, she went, as though quite worn out, to the bedding of chopped barley straw and lay down. Her belly bulged out fat. Now and then one could see from outside the young moving inside it. She looked likely to have a good many. Kenji leaned on the fence watching her and thought: would it really be all right to turn a creature like this out into the fields?

The ending (original text)

For a week or so afterward those filthy pigs went on squealing day and night, until at last, one after another, they began to drop dead. The pigs that had been loosed in the fields and made all the uproar are eating quietly now inside their pens. And what became of the ringleaders? Why, they had defied the officers, and in the end nothing whatever was done to them. They had got away with just as much as they did.
