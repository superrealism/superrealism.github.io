About this work

The camp on the night Xiang Yu was killed. Lü Matong, a Han general, holds forth that Xiang Yu was no hero's vessel after all, that he should have crossed the Wu river and come back to fight again, and that to lay it on the mandate of heaven was cowardice. The man with the high nose who has listened in silence answers at the end with a single sentence — one of the sharpest of Akutagawa's Chinese pieces, with a dramatic reversal built into very little space.

Summary

On the night of the victory in which the head of the Hegemon-King of Western Chu was taken, some ten faces surround the lamp in the camp tent. Lü Matong, a general of Han, making his already long face longer still and stroking his thin beard, says: "At all events, that Xiang Yu was no vessel for a hero." Strong he was, certainly. They say he could bend the stone tripod at the temple of King Yu at Tushan, and in today's battle there was a moment when I thought my life was gone. But when the army of Chu was driven to the Wu river there were only twenty-eight horsemen left, and though the village headman went to the trouble of offering to ferry him over to Jiangdong, Xiang Yu refused and died. If a man had the vessel of a hero he would swallow the shame and cross the Wu river and come back with a whirl of dust at his heels; this is no time to be troubling about face. Then a face with a high nose and sharp eyes says, with an ironic smile floating on it: "So the vessel of a hero means being good at reckoning?" A quiet laugh rises from the company, but Lü Matong does not flinch. Before the battle Xiang Yu told his twenty-eight men, "It is heaven that destroys Xiang Yu; it is no want of human strength," and he did in fact win nine times over. That very attitude of laying his own failure upon heaven is cowardice; he died where he might have lived splendidly. A hero is one who fights heaven, one who fights on even when he knows the decree of heaven — and when Lü Matong presses this home, in the eyes of the man with the high nose there kindles an unlooked-for heat of feeling.

Characters

  • Lü Matong　A general of Han, who argues that Xiang Yu was no vessel for a hero
  • Liu Bang　A man with a high nose and sharp eyes, who listens to Lü Matong in silence
  • Xiang Yu　The Hegemon-King of Western Chu, who took his own life at the Wu river

The opening (original text)

"At all events, that Xiang Yu was no vessel for a hero." So said Lü Matong, a general of Han, making his already long face longer still and stroking his thin beard. Around his face some ten faces, all catching the light of the lamp set in the middle, stand out red in the night of the camp tent.

Eiyū no utsuwa by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Then Xiang Yu —" Liu Bang raised his sharp eyes and gazed at the light of the lamp, which was blinking in the autumn air. And then, half as if to himself, he answered slowly: "That is exactly why he was a vessel for a hero."
