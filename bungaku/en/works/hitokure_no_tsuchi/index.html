About this work

A short story Akutagawa published in Shinchō in 1924, and unusually for him set in a farming village. The old woman Osumi, who has lost the son who lay ill for a long time, and her daughter-in-law Otami, who means to work the fields alone rather than take another husband. Through the friction and hatred piling up between them that neither can speak of, and the unexpected end that Otami's sudden death brings, Akutagawa draws, in the cool hand of his last years, the egotism without redemption and the sorrow of people living in the country.

Summary

Osumi is at last parted by death from her son Nintarō, who had lain bedridden, virtually crippled, for going on eight years. That death, following which she is called blessed for having been "released at last," is not a thing she can simply call sorrowful. After that, what to do with the widowed daughter-in-law, Otami, becomes the question. Osumi reckons that once the forty-nine days of mourning are past, she will have Otami take a husband to marry into the family, and have her go on working the fields as before. But on the morning after the seventh day, Otami, in her dead husband's place, takes on all the farm work herself and throws herself into it, working herself to the bone. She has not the least intention of taking a husband, and raises her young son Kōji alone while running the farm with a strength to match any man's. Otami's diligence, far from helping her mother-in-law Osumi, only piles up an unspoken resentment and hatred between the two of them. After nine years of this simmering grudge, Otami, having pushed herself too hard, is struck down suddenly by illness and dies. Left alone with her grandson, Osumi, now that her hardworking daughter-in-law is gone, is at a loss over what is to become of her life from here on. Gazing at her sleeping grandson's face, Osumi comes to realize that the daughter-in-law she had hated, and the son who had lain bedridden, and she herself, had all been nothing but "pitiable, worthless human beings." "Otami, why did you have to go and die" — the tears fall from Osumi's eyes, without end. A piece in which Akutagawa, with a cold, clear-eyed touch, depicts the hopeless egoism and sorrow of those who live in the countryside.

Characters

  • Osumi　Nintarō's mother (mother-in-law). Piles up years of unspoken resentment against her daughter-in-law Otami, but after Otami's death, left alone with her grandson, comes to recognize her own egoism
  • Otami　Nintarō's wife (daughter-in-law). After her husband's death, works the fields alone without taking a new husband, but dies of illness brought on by overwork
  • Kōji　Otami's young son. Osumi's grandson

The opening (original text)

Osumi was parted by death from her son at the season when the tea-picking begins. Her son Nintarō had lain in bed, virtually crippled, for going on eight years. Even to Osumi, who was called "blessed" for it, the death of such a son was not something she could simply call sorrowful. When she offered a single stick of incense before Nintarō's coffin, she felt, at any rate, as though she had at last managed to pass through some narrow mountain gorge or other.

Hitokure no tsuchi by Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

As Osumi gazed at that sleeping face, she gradually began to feel herself, too, to be a pitiable human being. At the same time she began to feel that her son Nintarō and her daughter-in-law Otami, both of whom had been bound to her by such an unhappy fate, were pitiable human beings as well. That change, before she knew it, swept away nine years of hatred and anger. No — it even swept away the future happiness that had until now comforted her. The three of them, parent and children, had all, every one of them, been pitiable human beings. But among them, she who alone had lived on to expose her own shame was the most pitiable human being of all. "Otami, why did you have to go and die?" — Osumi found herself murmuring this, unawares, to the new Buddha. And then, suddenly, without end, the tears began to fall, drop by drop.
