Famous passages by Yokomitsu Riichi

Well-known lines from the works of Yokomitsu Riichi.

4 passages

The Fly

The fly with the large eyes, putting strength into its now fully rested wings, flew off alone, unhurried, into the blue sky.

The close of the story. As the coach plunges from a cliff because the driver has dozed off and the passengers' fates are cut short in an instant, a single fly alone flies calmly away. Yokomitsu's cold gaze, viewing human affairs from a distance, stands out sharply.

Heads and Bellies

It was noon. The special express train ran full speed ahead, packed with passengers. The small stations along the line were ignored, as if they were stones.

The celebrated three lines that announced the arrival of the New Sensationist school. The leap of personification in the small stations being “ignored, as if they were stones” shocked the literary establishment of its day.

Haru wa basha ni notte

These flowers came riding in a carriage along the seashore, the very first of all, scattering spring as they came.

The close of the story. The words the husband speaks as he hands his bedridden wife a bouquet that has arrived for her. This line, announcing the coming of spring to a wife near death, gives the work its title.

Machine

In the beginning I sometimes wondered whether my employer might not be mad.

An opening written in a distinctive style, stripped down in its punctuation. The psychology of four men at a nameplate workshop meshes together like clockwork.
