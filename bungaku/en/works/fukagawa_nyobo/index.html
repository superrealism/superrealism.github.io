About this work

A short story counted among Oguri Fūyō's central works, published in Shinshōsetsu in March 1905. Kanenosuke, a fur-seal hunter and an old acquaintance believed dead, turns up unannounced at the house of Omitsu, a young wife married to a fishmonger of Fukagawa. Carrying feelings that threaten to catch fire again, Omitsu, out of duty to her sick husband and the pride of a downtown woman, busies herself with marrying Kanenosuke off to another girl. A piece in which the stylish dialogue learned in the Ken'yūsha and a close description of human feeling leaning toward naturalism dissolve into each other; it is said to have influenced Tanizaki's work as well.

Summary

On the second floor of a poultry-shop restaurant before Fukagawa's Hachiman shrine, the young wife Omitsu sat face to face over sake with her old flame Kinnosuke, whom she had not seen in three years. Kinnosuke had gone out to sea hunting fur seals and vanished without a trace, and everyone had believed him dead. He had, in fact, been seized by a Russian ship and marooned on an island, and after enduring these and other hardships had at last come home alive. The two had once been drawn to each other, but Omitsu, who had lost her father and had no one to rely on, had believed Kinnosuke dead and, the previous autumn, married Yoshida Shinzō, a fishmonger in Fukagawa's Kiyozumi-chō. Though her heart raced at their reunion, Omitsu was determined to hold to the line proper to a married woman. Just then, her husband Shinzō lay gravely ill in bed. As if to sever the rekindling feelings for Kinnosuke, and out of a downtown woman's stubborn pride besides, Omitsu set about matching Kinnosuke with the pretty girl Osen, going back and forth again and again between Osen's mother and Kinnosuke to arrange the match. In time Shinzō, on his deathbed, told Omitsu she was still young and should marry again, and breathed his last. After the funeral, as the young widow Omitsu sat alone before his memorial tablet, Tame-san, a young hand at the shop, came to her with a crude proposal that she take him as her second husband. Omitsu flung the incense burner at him and fled the house, running straight to the boathouse inn at Reiganjima, where she pressed Kinnosuke, whether he liked it or not, to go through with it, and had the informal wedding cup with Osen hurried through that very night.

Characters

  • Omitsu　A young wife married to the fishmonger Yoshida Shinzō of Fukagawa. A spirited, downtown-raised woman who once shared feelings with Kinnosuke
  • Kinnosuke (Kin-san)　Omitsu's old flame. Believed to have died at sea as a fur-seal hunter, he returns home after three years
  • Yoshida Shinzō (Shin-san)　Omitsu's husband. A fishmonger in Fukagawa's Kiyozumi-chō. Falls gravely ill and dies, leaving Omitsu his dying wish that she remarry
  • Shingorō　Shinzō's father. Runs a fish shop on Tsukudajima
  • Osen　The pretty girl Omitsu tries to match with Kinnosuke
  • Tame-san　A young hand at Shinzō's shop. After Shinzō's death, makes a crude advance toward the newly widowed Omitsu

The opening (original text)

On the second floor of a trim little poultry-shop restaurant before the Hachiman shrine in Fukagawa, a duck or some such fowl sizzled faintly away as a man and a woman sat facing each other over a hot-pot brazier. The man was dark-skinned, with a hard, weathered look and a stocky, rock-solid build — a young fellow of twenty-seven or eight, dressed in a plain indigo-checked kimono lined in pale blue, the collar of a matching indigo-checked haori showing beneath, its lining a subdued damask scattered with a seal pattern, an iron chain looped through a narrow, single-line navy Hakata sash; sitting cross-legged, he kept fussing over his bare shins showing through, tucking and re-tucking the hem of his kimono over them as he talked. The woman looked to be twenty-two or three, her features open and pleasant, her round face fair-skinned and full of charm; her hair was done up in a wide ginkgo-leaf coiffure, and she wore a kimono with an Isesaki collar, an obi of black satin patterned in shifting stripes worn front and back alike, and a Yoneryū haori pulled back slightly to bare the nape of her neck.

The ending (original text)

Omitsu snatched up the incense burner from the small desk without a moment's thought and hurled it straight at the side of Tame-san's face, then spun around and dashed straight out the front door. Having fled, Omitsu ran on the spot straight to the Shimoda-ya at Reiganjima, and that very night pressed Kinnosuke to agree, whether he liked it or not, and had the informal wedding cup with Osen rushed through at once.
