About this work

An exchange of less than ten lines between a pocket watch that has fallen behind a chest of drawers and a rat that laughs at it. A children's story Yumeno Kyūsaku wrote for a newspaper, whose one line — that anything which works only when nobody is looking, or only when somebody is, is a thief either way — goes into an adult's chest as well.

Summary

A pocket watch fell down behind a chest of drawers and went on ticking away by itself. A rat found it and laughed. "How silly. Nobody's watching, so what are you keeping on going for?" The pocket watch answered: "Because I keep going even when nobody's watching, I'm useful whenever anybody does look." And it went on: "Anything that only works when nobody's watching, or only when somebody is, is a thief either way." The rat, ashamed, slunk away — that is the whole of this tale, less than ten lines long. But it is exactly its shortness that lets the pocket watch's words reach the reader straight on. Because it keeps going the same whether it is watched or not, it can be relied on whenever it is needed. To work only when watched, or to work furtively only when unwatched, are, in the end, both the same kind of "thief." A piece written for a children's newspaper column, but its lesson bites hardest on grown-ups.
