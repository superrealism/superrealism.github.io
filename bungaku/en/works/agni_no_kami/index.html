About this work

A story for boys Akutagawa published in the children's magazine Akai Tori in 1921. The setting is Shanghai. Taeko, a Japanese girl carried off in what amounts to a sale of persons, is held by a vicious old Indian woman and used as a medium to call down Agni, the god of fire. Endō, a Japanese who goes in to save her, is brought to the edge of disaster — and then, unexpectedly, the power of Agni works. An adventure story full of foreign colour and suspense, unusual in Akutagawa's work.

Summary

On the upper floor of a dim house in Shanghai lived a vicious-looking old Indian woman. Passing herself off as a fortune teller, she was in truth a terrible woman who practised sorcery, and used Taeko, a Japanese girl carried off by traffickers in Hong Kong, entirely as she pleased, as a medium to call down the fire god Agni. Endō, a Japanese man worried for Taeko's safety, schemes to get her back. He plans to have Taeko pretend that the god Agni has possessed her and deliver a false oracle to the old woman, then use the opening this creates to escape. But when the night actually comes, Taeko genuinely falls into a state of possession, and Endō's scheme begins to fall apart. Just when all seems lost — before anyone realizes it, the old woman lies dead, pierced through by a dagger, and Taeko is safely rescued. "It was the god Agni, who came here tonight, who killed that old woman." Endō, still holding Taeko in his arms, murmurs this solemnly. An unusual adventure story of Akutagawa's, set in a foreign land, drawing a girl's peril and the mysterious power of a god.

Characters

  • Taeko　A Japanese girl who is carried off and made into a spirit medium
  • Endō　A Japanese man trying to save Taeko
  • The old Indian woman　A sorceress who controls Taeko

The opening (original text)

A certain street in Shanghai, China. On the upper floor of a certain house, dim even in the daytime, a vicious-looking old Indian woman was talking eagerly with a man who looked to be an American merchant. "The truth is, I've come again this time to ask you for a fortune, ma'am —" The American said this as he lit a fresh cigarette.

The ending (original text)

Endō's eyes moved from the old woman's corpse to Taeko's face. It was in this instant that Endō finally understood — that tonight's scheme had failed, and yet, for that very failure, the old woman had died and Taeko had been safely won back; that the workings of fate were a strange and wondrous thing. "I didn't kill her. It was the god Agni, who came here tonight, who killed that old woman." Endō, still holding Taeko in his arms, whispered this solemnly.
