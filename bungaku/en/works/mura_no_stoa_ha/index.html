About this work

A short story Makino Shin'ichi published in Shinchō in 1928. By a pond with fallen leaves floating on it, Taruno sells off his household goods one by one while ruling red lines in books on the reality of abstract ideas. A light caricature of Greek enthusiasm and self-consciousness spinning in the air, from the world of what is called the Greek Makino; the irony of calling a village dabbler in philosophy a Stoic holds this author's peculiar comedy and pathos together.

Summary

Beside a garden pond with a great many fallen leaves floating on it, Taruno is leaning back in a rattan chair and sunning himself. Though the thick book on his knees slips off, he does not even try to pick it up; he leaves the page headed "The reality of the abstract idea" turning over in the light breeze, and sleeps with his mouth open, looking up into the empty air. The carp that once swam so easily have all been sold off, and the pond has become as quiet and as foolish a sight as an athletic ground in the frost-withered season. Next he might sell the garden trees; that stone lantern seems a thing that might as well be there or not — and while he was enjoying such reckonings he became thoroughly comfortable and fell asleep. Eating up the family property while he reads books of philosophy, he is looked on by the villagers as an oddity, and feels not the least discomfort at it himself. His wife and his friend Hayashi come and go and are drawn into his way of living. "Well then, Aki-chan, see to the chicken" — but Hayashi, who goes off in high spirits, is not going to buy a chicken at all.

Characters

  • Taruno　The village "Stoic," who reads books of philosophy while selling off the family goods
  • Hayashi　Taruno's friend, who comes and goes in high spirits
  • Taruno's wife　Who puts up with her husband's way of living

The opening (original text)

I Beside a garden pond with a great many fallen leaves floating on it, Taruno was leaning back in a rattan chair and sunning himself. He was looking up at the sky at his ease, like a man with no concern for what is about him, given over to some pleasant memory; but perhaps he had fallen asleep at some point, for when the thick book that had lain face down on his knees slipped with a noise to his feet, he did not even try to pick it up.

The ending (original text)

"Well then, Aki-chan, see to the chicken," she cried, pushing up the sleeves of her shirt. "Right! That is just where I was going, so — I shall be back in ten minutes; get things ready..." came Hayashi's spirited voice from outside the entrance, growing distant. It need hardly be added that Hayashi was not going to buy a chicken.
