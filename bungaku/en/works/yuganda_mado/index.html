About this work

On a rainy day she watches from the corner of the room, eyes sharp, as her sister gets out a raincoat to go out — a short story Yamakawa Masao published in Ryūsei. The man Saeki who is getting close to her sister is a fraud with a wife and children, and his intentions are obvious. For the happiness of the sister she adores, nothing that becomes of herself matters. Outside the window where she waits, having fetched a French knife from the kitchen, the landscape washed by the long rain sways and warps.

Summary

"You understand? Now be a good girl and mind the house while I am gone." She did not miss the flustered look that ran for an instant across her elder sister's face as she said it and went out. So it is true after all. My sister means to marry that man. — It is nearly three months since a man named Saeki began to call. The regular visits; the look in his eyes at her sister, of a man being relied upon; his too-kind, ingratiating manner toward the younger sister. She has followed them, too, and knows that they talk earnestly and secretly at a coffee shop in front of the station. And there is that apologetic, pained expression her sister shows whenever she mentions him. ... In the corner of the dim room she went on weeping aloud. She looks at her own face in the window. A gloomy face, dark and lifeless. A plain face that does not resemble in the least her fair, tall, beautiful sister. And though she is twenty-three, a hard flat chest like an undeveloped schoolgirl's. If I had been a beauty like my sister, my capable sister would not have been left single until today, at twenty-six, and a bad man like Saeki would never have come near her. That man has a wife and a child. She saw it yesterday, from this window. It is the burden of me that has been in the way of a match, and he has taken advantage of that weakness and that impatience. ... It is all my fault. So for the sake of my dear sister I do not care what becomes of me. Today I shall show her the proof of it. She ran to the kitchen and hid a sharp French knife under her arm and went to the window, and went on waiting for the two of them to appear in the scene that ran and warped in the rain.

Characters

  • She　The younger sister, twenty-three
  • The elder sister　Twenty-six. Fair, tall, beautiful and capable
  • Saeki　The man who calls regularly on the elder sister

The opening (original text)

The rain that had fallen since morning is wetting the window. In the dim room of the flat, she watched from the corner with sharp eyes as her elder sister took out a raincoat and quickly made ready to go out. "You understand? Now be a good girl and mind the house while I am gone. I shall be back at once." So said her sister. She does not answer. But her sister had grown quite used to a younger sister like that. She went straight to the door.

Yuganda mado by Yamakawa Masao (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"No, I do not think she has noticed that yet. But her symptoms seem to have grown much worse of late; last night she wept the whole night through." "I see. In the rainy season that sort of illness worsens rapidly.... In any case, the way she looks at me lately is not normal. She is plainly on her guard." "Do you think, then, that my sister ought to be put into a hospital? ... We are only the two of us, and somehow it seems so pitiful...." "I understand very well how you feel. But I think the time has come for you to make up your mind.... Well, let us look in today and settle it definitely."
