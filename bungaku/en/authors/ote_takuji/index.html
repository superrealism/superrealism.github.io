Biography

Born in 1887 at a hot-spring inn in Isobe, Usui District, Gunma Prefecture. After graduating in English literature from Waseda University he joined the advertising department of the Lion Toothpaste Company (Kobayashi Shōten) and spent his life as an ordinary company employee, remaining unmarried. Contributing to Kitahara Hakushū's magazine "Zambōa," he won recognition and was regarded as an outstanding talent alongside Hagiwara Sakutarō and Murō Saisei, yet he never published a poetry collection in his lifetime. The dense, symbolic, sensuous world of his language became known only after his death, through "Aiiro no Hikigaeru" ("The Indigo Toad"), published through the efforts of Hagiwara Sakutarō and others. He died of tuberculosis in 1934 at the age of 46.
