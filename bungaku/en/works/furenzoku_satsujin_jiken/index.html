About this work

Sakaguchi Ango's orthodox detective novel, serialized from 1947 and awarded the Detective Writers Club Prize, now the Mystery Writers of Japan Award. A run of murders among the literary men gathered at an old house in the mountains, told with a challenge to the reader inserted along the way. A monument of Japanese orthodox mystery.

Summary

In the summer of the twenty-second year of Shōwa, "I" am invited by the poet Utagawa Kazuma to pass a summer at his old family house in the mountains. Those gathered at the Utagawa house are poets and novelists, their wives and their mistresses — a company of the most vulgar human relations, whirling with jealousy and desire. Before long, in this closed house, a murder occurs. One inexplicable death follows another. Who is the criminal, and why are the murders "discontinuous"? Doctor Kose takes up the puzzle in the detective's part. In the middle of the story the author, Sakaguchi Ango, inserts a "challenge to the reader," pressing him with the claim that all the clues have been given and daring him to name the true criminal. Orthodox detective logic, precisely built up against a disordered drama of love and hate — a long novel of Sakaguchi Ango's, which won the Detective Writers Club Prize and is famous as a representative work of the Japanese orthodox mystery.

Characters

  • I　A writer invited to the Utagawa house
  • Utagawa Kazuma　The poet who invited me

The opening (original text)

1. Human relations of the utmost vulgarity It was the end of June in the twenty-second year of Shōwa. Answering a summons from Utagawa Kazuma, I met him at a small restaurant called the Tsubohei in Nihonbashi. "The fact is — it is an abrupt and outlandish request, but I want you to spend a summer at my house." Kazuma's house was in the mountains, in a place of the utmost inconvenience: one got off the train, rode a bus some six ri along a mountain road, and after getting off the bus had to walk nearly another ri.
