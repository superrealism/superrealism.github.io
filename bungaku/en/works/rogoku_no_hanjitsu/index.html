About this work

A short story published in 1924 in the newly founded Bungei Sensen. Hayama set down almost exactly his own experience of being arrested for labour organizing and held as an unconvicted prisoner in Nagoya prison. On the day of the Great Kantō Earthquake, in the violent shaking that reached the distant Nagoya jail, the warders fled, the doors were not opened, and the accused were left shut in their cells. Through the narrator's anger as he kicks at the door, cutting his feet on glass, and demands an answer from the chief warder, it marks how lightly the lives of those under authority are treated. A piece at the starting point of his work.

Summary

On September 1st, 1923, 'I' was in a cell at Nagoya Prison. Arrested for labor organizing, I had spent several months awaiting trial. Gripping the iron bars of the high window and gazing out at a pumpkin field, I felt keenly how even the wind and the light had been taken from me. Past noon, a violent tremor struck. Every defendant cried out together, begging that the doors be opened, but the guards, who ordinarily made their rounds so often, were nowhere to be seen. Thinking he would rather be crushed than left helpless, 'I' hurled myself against the door and kicked at the wooden wall, cutting my foot on the glass cover of a fallen light bulb; I thrust my bleeding foot through the meal slot and waved it. Still no one came. At last, from beyond the iron-barred window, from outside the wall, a comrade's speech reached our ears, and cheers rose up through the whole prison. Only after three hours did the chief guard finally appear, saying, 'We knew it was nothing serious, so we didn't let you out.' Then why, 'I' demanded, did all of you run away? — as a doctor cleaned my foot with hydrogen peroxide. When Yamada, a tinsmith and comrade, came to visit, his report confirmed that the chief guard and the others had in fact fled to the yard. On the way back from that visit, 'I' sat down in the corridor and would not move. 'Let me see the warden.' As long as I had not been sentenced to death, I would not go back into my cell.

Characters

  • I (Hata)　A former sailor arrested for labor organizing, held awaiting trial at Nagoya Prison
  • The chief guard　A prison official who insists he knew all along the earthquake would pass without harm
  • Yamada　A tinsmith and comrade who comes to visit, and tells 'me' that the chief guard and the others had fled

The opening (original text)

One — On September 1st, 1923, I was in Nagoya Prison. Lunch in prison comes early. By eleven o'clock, one is already licking one's lips, feeling, as always, that same familiar, deep and bitter regret at how meager the prison's food portions are.

Rōgoku no hanjitsu by Hayama Yoshiki (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

On the way back from the visiting room, I sat down on the packed-earth floor of the corridor. — Let me see the warden. Whatever anyone said to me, I would not move. — This isn't Utsunomiya, but is there anyone fool enough to crawl in under a hanging ceiling? You all ran away, didn't you. As long as I haven't been sentenced to death, there is no way I'm going back in. I held my ground.

Rōgoku no hanjitsu by Hayama Yoshiki (Aozora Bunko)
