The Legends of Tōno

In the mountain villages of this country, in places deeper still than Tōno, there must be countless further legends of mountain gods and mountain folk. I wish only that they be told, and that they make the people of the plains shudder.

The closing of the preface. A declaration thrown down before city dwellers who know nothing of mountain folk and gods, it is known as the line that announces the starting point of Japanese folklore studies.

These tales were all heard from Mr. Sasaki Kyōseki, a native of Tōno. From around February of last year, the forty-second year of Meiji, he would visit me now and then in the evenings and tell them, and I wrote them down. Kyōseki is no skilled storyteller, but he is a sincere man. I, too, have written them down just as I felt them, without adding or taking away a single word.

The opening of the preface. Recording that he wrote down the tales of Sasaki Kizen (Kyōseki), a native of Tōno, without adding or removing a single word, it declares the author's stance as a recorder of folk tradition.
