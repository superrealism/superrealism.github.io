Biography

Born in 1902 in Tanikō Village, Kamiiso District, Hokkaido (now Hokuto City). He began writing while a student at Keio University, and in 1925 his story "Kage" ("Shadow") won a prize in "Shinseinen," the gateway magazine for detective writers, launching his literary career. He went on to work as an editor for "Shinseinen" at the publisher Hakubunkan, and was known as a modernist dandy who came to work in a morning coat and silk hat. Specializing in short stories rich in fantasy and wit, he left works such as "Androgynos no Sue" ("The Descendants of the Androgyne"). His older brother was the fellow detective writer Watanabe Keisuke. In 1930, on his way back from commissioning a manuscript from Tanizaki Jun'ichirō, the taxi he was riding in collided with a train, and he died suddenly at the age of 27.
