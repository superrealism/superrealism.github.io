About this work

A short story published in Bungei Sensen in 1926 by Hayama Yoshiki, a writer of proletarian literature. Matsudo Yosan, a labourer emptying cement barrels at the site of a power station on the Kiso river, finds a letter inside one of them: a mill girl has lost her lover in an accident with the crusher, and slipped the note into a barrel hoping to learn where the man — crushed and turned into cement — has gone. Brutal labour, and the human grief beneath it, drawn in a way that strikes at the chest. A representative short story of the proletarian school.

Summary

Matsudo Yosō spends the whole day emptying cement barrels at the site of a power station on the Kiso river, with Mount Ena in view. His head and the space beneath his nose are covered gray, and in eleven hours he has not a moment to clean out his nose — brutal labor. Toward quitting time, a small wooden box comes out of a barrel he empties. On the way home to the tenement, thinking of nothing but a drink, he suddenly remembers the box he shoved into his belly-apron. Breaking it open against a stone, he finds inside a letter wrapped in rags. "I am a factory girl who sews cement bags at the N Cement Company." The writer is a woman who has just lost her lover in an accident; he had the work of feeding stones into the crusher. Her lover fell into the crusher together with a great stone, and was ground up with the stone into a thin red stone, and was crushed to powder and burned and became fine cement. Bones and flesh and soul, all of it into cement. She wrapped her letter in a rag of her lover's work clothes and slipped it into that barrel. "I cannot bear to see my lover become the corridor of a theater or the wall of some great mansion." "Are you a laborer? If you are a laborer, take pity on me and write to me." Having read the letter to the end, Yosō, in the boiling uproar of his children, drains his drink at a gulp and shouts: "I want to get blind drunk. And then I want to smash everything to pieces." "And what would become of the children?" says his wife. And he saw, in his wife's swollen belly, a seventh child. A famous short story of proletarian literature, drawing sharply the brutal reality of the laborer and the human sorrow at the bottom of it.

Characters

  • Matsudo Yosō　A laborer emptying cement, who spends the whole day emptying barrels at the site of a power station
  • The factory girl　The writer of the letter. She sews cement bags at the N Cement Company, and has lost her lover in an accident with the crusher
  • The factory girl's lover　A young man of twenty-six whose work was feeding stones into the crusher. He was crushed in the accident and became cement
  • His wife　Yosō's wife, carrying their seventh child

The opening (original text)

Matsudo Yosō was emptying cement. The rest of him was not much to look at, but the hair of his head and the space beneath his nose were covered gray with cement. He wanted to poke a finger up his nostril and clear away the concrete that was stiffening his nose hairs like reinforced concrete; but to keep up with the concrete mixer, which spat out ten cubic feet a minute, he had not a moment to get his finger anywhere near his nose.

The ending (original text)

"I want to get blind drunk," he shouted. "And then I want to smash everything to pieces." "Blind drunk and running wild, indeed — and what would become of the children?" So his wife said. And he saw, in his wife's swollen belly, a seventh child.

Famous passages

Are you a laborer? If you are a laborer, please, take pity on me and write back.

The closing of a letter found inside a cement barrel. The plea of a girl who lost her lover to a crushing machine strikes directly at both the laborer who opens the barrel and the reader.
