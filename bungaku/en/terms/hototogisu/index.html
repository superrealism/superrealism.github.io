What is Hototogisu?

Category
Society / Group

A haiku magazine founded in January 1897 in Matsuyama by Masaoka Shiki's friend Yanagihara Gokudō (its title at founding was written in a different, cursive orthography). The name derives from Shiki's haiku pen name, "Shiki" (hototogisu, the lesser cuckoo). The following October, in 1898, publication moved to Tokyo, and once Shiki's disciple Takahama Kyoshi took over its management, it developed into the central magazine for haiku and prose grounded in the principle of shasei (sketching from life). At Kyoshi's urging, Natsume Sōseki contributed "Wagahai wa Neko de Aru" ("I Am a Cat," 1905) and "Botchan" (1906), both of which were published in this magazine — it is also known as the stage for Sōseki's debut as a writer. After Shiki's death, Kyoshi took over as its head, and through the Taishō era and into the early Shōwa era it reigned for a long time as the most powerful magazine of the conservative haiku world, championing objective sketching and the poetic celebration of birds and flowers.
