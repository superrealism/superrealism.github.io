About this work

A novel Yamamoto Shūgorō published in Shin Seinen in 1946. Asakawa Raita, a young man home from the front with the grin of a mischievous boy. It draws in a bright, warm hand how he sets out with Fumiko, who has been in a convent, for Hokkaido, where lilac blooms. A piece carrying a hope of renewal just after the defeat.

Summary

A tall young man in a military uniform, broad-shouldered, walks down the slope at Azabu Katamachi with a striding, distinctive gait. On his bag and his knapsack alike, written large and bold, is the name "Asakawa Raita" — and the young man's face, an open, mischievous-boy's grin that matches his name exactly, belongs to Asakawa Raita, come home alive from the front. At the house he visits, he speaks to the parrot by the entrance: "Well now, you're still alive, are you." A middle-aged woman, pressing a hand to her chest, greets him: "Oh, Rai-san, you're safe!" The story unfolds warmly around Raita, who has survived the war and come home. In time Raita decides to begin a new life together with Fumiko, who has been staying in a convent. "Lilac likes cold places, you know — once we get to Hokkaido, I'll make it bloom for sure" — carrying that promise, to make the lilac bloom, in his heart. Taking the arm of Fumiko, who has changed into a plain dress, Raita strides out through the convent gate. To Raita, who laughs wryly that his old habit — the one that got his wristwatch stolen — hasn't left him yet, Fumiko's bright laughter answers, fading away into the pine grove. Bathed in the light of early spring, a bush warbler sings out across the budding branches of a larch tree. A piece of Yamamoto's, drawing brightly and warmly a hope for renewal in the years right after the defeat.

Characters

  • Asakawa Raita　A young man demobilized from the front
  • Fumiko　The woman Raita brings with him

The opening (original text)

A tall young man, broad-shouldered, in a military uniform, came walking down the slope at Azabu Katamachi with a striding, very distinctive gait. He held a bag in one hand, and a large, bulging knapsack hung from his right shoulder; on both the bag and the knapsack, written large and bold, without a hint of restraint, was scrawled the name Asakawa Raita.

Hanasakanu rira by Yamamoto Shūgorō (Aozora Bunko)

The ending (original text)

"Lilac likes cold places, you know — once we get to Hokkaido, I'll make it bloom for sure." Twenty minutes later, taking the arm of Fumiko, now changed into a plain dress, Raita strode out through the convent gate. ...Bathed in the light of early spring, which had at last climbed to the height of the sky, the cry of a bush warbler could be heard passing from branch to branch of the budding larch.
