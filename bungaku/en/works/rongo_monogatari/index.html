About this work

One of Shimomura Kojin's central works, serialized in the magazine Gendai in 1938. Taking words from the Analects as cores, it recasts the exchanges of Confucius and his disciples as a small story apiece. In his preface the author says that the Analects is a book of heaven and at the same time a book of earth, and that Confucius spoke the words of heaven in the voice of earth. By digging up the weakness and vanity of pupils such as Zilu, Yan Hui and Zigong, it is built so that readers notice their own weakness and ugliness: Kojin's thinking as an educator shows plainly.

Summary

"The Analects is a 'book of heaven' and at the same time a 'book of earth'" — beginning so in his preface, the author defines Confucius as a man who walked the earth doggedly all his life and came to speak the words of heaven. There was no mystery in him and no miracle; he spoke of heaven with the voice of earth. Most of his disciples, by contrast, could in the end speak only the words of earth, and some of them committed even the falsehood of trying to speak the words of earth with the ring of heaven. There lay their weakness, and that weakness is one that human beings hold in common. This book takes the feeling roused by reading such words of the Analects and makes each of them into a small story. Zilu, hot-blooded and scolded by his master; Yan Yuan, who dwells in poverty and delights in the Way; Zigong, clever and fond of judging people; young men who fall with their purpose half accomplished — the disciples struck by a word of their master, or struck and still not enlightened, are drawn one after another as stories in a plain modern style. At the end the original text of the Analects on which each is based is appended, so that one may read back and forth between the story and the original.

Characters

  • Confucius　The master who speaks the words of heaven with the voice of earth
  • Zilu　A hot-blooded disciple, often scolded by his master
  • Yan Yuan　Confucius's beloved disciple, who dwells in poverty and delights in the Way
  • Zigong　A disciple of ready speech, fond of passing judgment on people

The opening (original text)

Preface The Analects is a "book of heaven" and at the same time a "book of earth." Confucius is a man who walked the earth doggedly all his life and came to speak the words of heaven. He spoke the words of heaven, but there was in him no mystery and no miracle. He was, one may say, a man who spoke the words of heaven with the voice of earth.

The ending (original text)

5. The Master said, "There is no one who knows me." Zigong said, "Why should it be that no one knows the Master?" The Master said, "I do not murmur against Heaven; I do not lay blame upon men. I study what is below and reach to what is above. He who knows me — is it not Heaven?" (Xianwen chapter)
