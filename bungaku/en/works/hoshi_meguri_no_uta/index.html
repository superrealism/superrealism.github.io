About this work

A song for which Miyazawa Kenji wrote both words and music. Beginning with the scorpion and its red eye, it sings the constellations — Scorpius, Orion, the Great and Little Bears — as they wheel through the night sky, in Kenji's own richly coloured words. A clear piece of lyric verse.

Summary

"The scorpion with the red eye, / the eagle with its wings spread, / the little dog with the blue eye, / the coil of the snake of light." A beloved song about the constellations, for which Miyazawa Kenji wrote both the words and the music. Scorpio with its red eye, Aquila with its wings spread, Canis Minor with its blue eye, the coiled snake of light — the constellations shining in the night sky are sung out in colors and images that could be no one's but Kenji's. Orion sings high and lets fall the dew and the frost; the cloud of Andromeda takes the shape of a fish's mouth. Where the foot of the Great Bear is stretched five times to the north, above the forehead of the Little Bear, is "the mark of the sky's turning" — the pole star. A short but beautiful song, in which the clear lyricism and the longing for the heavens of a Kenji who loved the universe are crystallized. One of his representative poems, sung fondly in his fairy tales and stories as well.

Famous passages

The scorpion with its red eye the eagle with wings spread wide the little dog with its blue eye the coiled serpent of light.

A song of Kenji's own composition, also sung in “Night on the Galactic Railroad.” Calling up the constellations by color and shape alone, it turns the night sky itself into a children's song.
