What is Han-Shizenshugi (Anti-Naturalism)?

Category
Literary Movement

A literary-historical term grouping together, under a single name, the writers and schools that, each in their own way, kept their distance from the Naturalism that dominated the literary establishment at the end of the Meiji era. It was not a unified movement but a general term for those dissatisfied with Naturalism's leaning toward the exposure and confession of fact. Natsume Sōseki and Mori Ōgai, maintaining an intellectual composure that viewed life from a height, were called the Yoyūha (School of Leisure) or Kōtōha (School of Detachment); Nagai Kafū and Tanizaki Jun'ichirō's Aesthetic school pursued a sensual, decadent beauty divorced from morality; and Mushanokōji Saneatsu, Shiga Naoya, and Arishima Takeo's Shirakaba school proudly proclaimed humanism and the affirmation of individuality. The slightly later Shinshichōha (New Thought school, or New Realism) of Akutagawa Ryūnosuke and Kikuchi Kan likewise drew a line against Naturalism by re-examining reality with an intellectual eye. But "anti-Naturalism" is a name given later by literary history for the sake of clarity, and these writers did not gather under a single banner themselves. The period from the end of the Meiji era into the Taishō era, when these currents stood side by side, was the season in which modern Japanese literature bloomed most variously, and most of the main streams of later literature branch off from here.
