Biography

Born in Okazaki, Aichi Prefecture, in 1896 and raised in Kyoto. Guided to Tokyo by his cousin, the painter Yamamoto Kanae, he became a Western-style painter who won award after award at the exhibitions of the Japan Art Institute. Known for his fondness for a deep madder red (garance) and for a style in which vitality and decadence stood back to back, he also shone in poetry and fiction. He left the stories "Akuma no Shita" ("The Devil's Tongue") and "Satsujin Gyōja" ("The Murderous Ascetic"), and the posthumously compiled poetry collection "Kaita no Utaeru" ("Kaita Sings"). Sunk in wandering and drink, he let a case of pneumonia worsen and died in 1919 at the age of 22.
