Biography

Born in 1898 to a farming family in Sakai Village, Minami-Tama District, Tokyo Prefecture (now Machida City). He studied English at the Tokyo Higher Normal School and taught English at middle schools in Gotemba and Hyōgo. He was baptized as a Christian while a student, and thereafter, with the Christian faith as the core of his life, he continued to write poems composed of nothing but short, clear words. In his lifetime he published only one poetry collection, "Aki no Hitomi" ("Autumn Eyes"). Struck by tuberculosis, he died in 1927 at the age of 29, leaving behind his two young children and his wife, Tomi; his unpublished manuscripts were brought into the world by his wife's hand as "Mazushiki Shinto" ("A Poor Believer"). His unadorned, prayer-like poems have steadily gained readers since his death and are still quietly read today.
