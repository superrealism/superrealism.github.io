What is Kannen Shōsetsu (Ideas Novel)?

Category
Form / Genre

A term for a body of fiction that, around 1895, in the aftermath of the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95), sought to put forward the contradictions of society or the author's own ideas as its subject; the critics Shimamura Hōgetsu and Tsubouchi Shōyō are said to have coined the name. Izumi Kyōka, who had studied under Ozaki Kōyō as a member of the Ken'yūsha, burst into the literary establishment under this label with "Yakō Junsa" ("The Night-Duty Policeman," April 1895, in "Bungei Kurabu"), depicting the conflict between rules and human feeling, and "Gekashitsu" ("The Surgery," June of the same year), depicting a love that transcends class. Kawakami Bizan, likewise of the Ken'yūsha, is also counted among the representative writers of the ideas novel for "Shokikan" ("The Clerk," February of the same year, in "Taiyō"), which contrasted a vulgar man on the make with an idealist content in poverty. Though it was for a time highly regarded as a literature that raised the author's own ideas, it later drew criticism as no more than a device for capturing readers' attention, and it declined once Kyōka turned, in the early 1900s, toward a style of the fantastic and mysterious.
