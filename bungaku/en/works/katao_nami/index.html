About this work

A very short piece in classical prose that Oguri Fūyō published at twenty-one, contributed in July 1896 to a special number of Bungei Kurabu published for the relief of the tsunami victims; it takes as its background the great Sanriku tsunami that struck the coast that June. On the night of the Boys' Festival in a fishing village, the young fisherman Senta confesses feelings he cannot suppress to Oteru, a married woman he once saved from drowning. As Oteru refuses, trying to hold the line proper to a wife, the ground suddenly shakes and a wave a thousand fathoms high comes out of the dark and swallows the village. To the two of them clinging to a single plank a drowning man clutches, and Oteru is forced to an ultimate choice. In gorgeous elegant prose learned in the Ken'yūsha, it draws vividly the instant decision of a woman standing between duty and love, and the ironic fate that waits.

Summary

On the night of the Boys' Festival, while their fellow fishermen grew merry with festive sake in the thatched hut, a young man and woman slipped away and walked along the shore. The man was the fisherman Senta; the woman, the married Oteru. Once they had rounded the headland and the firelight of the hut had vanished from view, Senta at last confessed what lay in his heart: that ever since he had once saved Oteru from drowning in the sea, he had never been able to forget the softness of her body, and had come to this beach every day since, brooding on his feelings. But Oteru had a proper husband, one Genzō. Though her heart wavered at being so sought after, Oteru held up her status as a married woman like a shield, and firmly refused him, saying that were a woman with a husband to do such a thing, both of them would become criminals who could not go on living. Senta, resigning himself with the words, "There's simply nothing to be done about it in this world," pressed her again and again to be joined with him in the next life instead. Just as Oteru was about to answer, the earth began to sway, and with a roar like a hundred thunderclaps, a tsunami came surging in through the darkness. As everything was swallowed by the raging flood, the two of them clung adrift to a single wooden door-panel. Then a man crying "Save me!" caught hold of the panel as well; its weight tipped the board, and Senta fell into the water, barely managing to cling to its edge. Oteru took hold of Senta's hand to pull him up, but the man behind her seized her sleeve, crying out in a voice full of resentment, "Are you going to kill me?" Trembling as though struck by lightning, Oteru at last let go of Senta's hand, hauled the man behind her to safety, and then flung herself into the churning water, wound both arms about the neck of the drifting Senta, and sank with him down into the depths of the waves. The next day, a door-panel bearing a man with barely a breath of life left in him washed up on the peak of a small island. The very man Oteru had saved was none other than her own husband, Genzō.

Characters

  • Senta　A young fisherman of the village. Ever since saving Oteru from drowning, he has carried a single-minded devotion to her, now that she is a married woman
  • Oteru　A married woman whose life Senta once saved. Though her heart wavers, she tries to keep faith with her husband
  • Genzō (Gen-sama)　Oteru's husband. On the night of the tsunami, he is swept away clinging to a door-panel and is saved by Oteru

The opening (original text)

The long rains of the deutzia-blossom season had at last cleared, and though the sky still lay overcast in patches, through the gaps in the clouds here and there a hazy fifth-night moon hurried on, ever westward. As they had done through ages past, the crossing swells of male wave and female wave rose and fell along the shore, and beyond the line where the surf climbed the sand, a single thatched hut stood, its form alone marking it out. Since evening, fisherman friends had gathered there together, growing merry on festive sake for the Boys' Festival — some singing, some dancing, some stamping their feet fit to break through the floorboards — and amid that confusion of noise, a young man and woman had slipped quietly away to the water's edge. "Is there something you want? Well, Senta-sama?" the woman asked first, gently, in a lovely voice. But Senta gave no answer, and, head bowed, walked on aimlessly, his feet crunching through the sand, glancing back now and again toward the hut as though something had just occurred to him.

The ending (original text)

"Y-you're going to kill me! M-me — you're going to kill me!" At the man's voice, thick with resentment, Oteru's whole body trembled violently, as though struck by lightning — but at last, resolved, she let go of the hand she had held, Senta's hand. Even as she hauled the man behind her up to safety, she flung herself with a great splash into the roiling, upturned water, wound both arms around the neck of the drifting Senta, and the two of them sank down, down, into the depths of the vast, churning, muddied waves. The next day, a single door-panel bearing a man with barely a breath of life left drifted ashore atop a small island. The one it carried was Oteru's own husband, Genzō.
